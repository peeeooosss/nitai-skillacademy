# Mission 2: Talking to AI — Your First Conversations

---
### Module 1 · You Do Not Need Special Words

Many people worry they will say the wrong thing to AI. You will not. AI understands ordinary, everyday language.

Think of it as talking to a helpful person. If they do not understand, you simply explain again in another way.

## Learning Objectives

- Talk to AI in plain language
- Let go of the fear of mistakes
- Re-phrase when needed
- Treat AI as a conversation

## Plain Language Works

- No special words are needed
- Write or speak as you normally do
- Mistakes are fine, just try again
### Why
- AI is built to understand people
- Fear stops people from starting
- Conversation improves the answer

## If It Does Not Understand

- Say it a different way
- Give an example
- Ask it to explain simply
### The rule
- Talk normally
- Re-phrase freely
- Keep the conversation going

## The Plain-Talk Prompt

```text
I am new to AI and worried about saying the wrong thing.
Reassure me in simple words and show me 3 examples of
ordinary questions I can ask.
```
- Simple reassurance
- Three example questions
- Everyday language

## Key Takeaways

- AI understands ordinary, everyday language
- No special words or code are needed
- Mistakes are fine - just re-phrase
- Treat it like a conversation with a person
- If it does not understand, say it a different way

## Practice Challenge

1. Ask AI the same question in two different ways
2. See how the answers change
3. Notice which wording worked better

**Example Quiz**

1. What language does AI understand?
- A) Everyday language
- B) Only code
- C) Only English
- D) Special words

**Answer:** A — AI understands ordinary, everyday language.

2. What if AI does not understand?
- A) Re-phrase it
- B) Give up
- C) Type code
- D) Restart the phone

**Answer:** A — Simply say it a different way until it understands.

3. Are mistakes a problem?
- A) No, just try again
- B) Yes, you break it
- C) Yes, you pay
- D) Yes, it stops working

**Answer:** A — Mistakes are fine; AI keeps working and you can re-phrase.

4. What is AI like?
- A) A conversation with a person
- B) A calculator
- C) A robot
- D) A puzzle

**Answer:** A — Treat AI like a conversation with a helpful person.

### Module 2 · Step 1: Ask a Clear Question

The clearer your question, the better the answer. A vague question gets a vague reply; a specific one gets something useful.

You do not need fancy words - just say what you want, and add a little detail.

## Learning Objectives

- Ask clear, specific questions
- Add useful detail
- Avoid vague requests
- Get better answers

## Clear Questions

- Say exactly what you want
- Add one or two details
- Ask one thing at a time
### Why
- Clarity improves the answer
- Details make it relevant to you
- One question is easier to answer

## Vague vs Clear

- Vague: tell me about food
- Clear: give me a dinner recipe using rice and tomatoes
- Vague: help with my child
- Clear: explain fractions to a 9-year-old simply
### The rule
- Be specific
- Add a detail or two
- One question at a time

## The Clear-Question Prompt

```text
I want to ask better questions. Here is my question [paste].
Rewrite it to be clearer and more specific, then answer it
for me simply.
```
- A clearer version
- A simple answer
- A better way to ask

## Key Takeaways

- Clear questions get better answers
- Say exactly what you want
- Add one or two useful details
- Ask one thing at a time
- You do not need fancy words, just clarity

## Practice Challenge

1. Take one vague question you have
2. Rewrite it clearly with a detail
3. Ask AI and compare the answer

**Example Quiz**

1. What gets a better answer?
- A) A clear, specific question
- B) A vague question
- C) No question
- D) A long speech

**Answer:** A — Clear, specific questions produce more useful answers.

2. What should you add?
- A) One or two details
- B) Nothing
- C) Ten details
- D) A poem

**Answer:** A — A detail or two makes the answer relevant to your situation.

3. How many things should you ask at once?
- A) One
- B) Ten
- C) Five
- D) None

**Answer:** A — Ask one thing at a time for a focused answer.

4. Do you need fancy words?
- A) No, just clarity
- B) Yes, always
- C) Only English
- D) Only code

**Answer:** A — You only need clear, everyday words, not fancy language.

### Module 3 · Step 2: Give AI Context About You

AI gives better help when it knows a little about you: your family, your home, your needs, and your language.

Adding context is like telling a friend your situation before asking for advice.

## Learning Objectives

- Give AI useful context
- Get answers suited to you
- Share only what is safe
- Improve relevance

## What Context Helps

- Who you are and your daily life
- The people you care for
- Your language and region
- Your budget or limits
### Why
- Context makes answers fit you
- General answers help less
- A little detail goes far

## Sharing Safely

- Share situations, not private numbers
- Never give IDs or bank details
- Keep names out when you can
### The rule
- Context, yes; private data, no
- Describe, do not identify
- Stay safe while sharing

## The Context Prompt

```text
I am a homemaker with [describe your situation: children,
city, budget, language]. With this in mind, suggest [task].
Do not ask for private details.
```
- Your situation described
- A suited suggestion
- No private data shared

## Key Takeaways

- Context makes AI answers fit your life
- Tell it about your family, home, and needs
- Mention your language and budget
- Share situations, never private numbers or IDs
- A little context goes a long way

## Practice Challenge

1. Write a short description of your situation
2. Ask one question with that context
3. Compare it to a question without context

**Example Quiz**

1. Why give AI context?
- A) To get answers that fit you
- B) To show off
- C) To confuse it
- D) To waste time

**Answer:** A — Context helps AI give answers suited to your situation.

2. What context helps?
- A) Your family, home, and needs
- B) Your OTP
- C) Your password
- D) Your bank details

**Answer:** A — Details about your daily life make answers more relevant.

3. What should you never share?
- A) Private numbers and IDs
- B) Your city
- C) Your language
- D) Your budget

**Answer:** A — Never share private numbers, IDs, or bank details.

4. How much context is needed?
- A) A little
- B) Everything
- C) None
- D) A whole book

**Answer:** A — A little context goes a long way in improving answers.

### Module 4 · Step 3: Ask for the Right Format

You can ask AI to give the answer in the shape you want: a list, a table, steps, a short paragraph, or a simple explanation.

Asking for the right format makes the answer easier to use and understand.

## Learning Objectives

- Ask for the format you want
- Choose lists, steps, or tables
- Make answers easy to use
- Understand format requests

## Choosing a Format

- A list for quick points
- Numbered steps for a process
- A table to compare things
- A short paragraph for reading
### Why
- The right shape is easier to use
- Tables compare at a glance
- Steps keep you from missing anything

## How to Ask

- Say the format in your question
- Ask for simple words
- Ask for short answers if you prefer
### The rule
- Ask for the format you need
- Keep it simple
- Request short or detailed as you like

## The Format Prompt

```text
Give me [task] as a numbered step-by-step list in simple
words, no more than 8 steps. Use short sentences I can
follow easily.
```
- The format you asked for
- Simple words
- Short, followable steps

## Key Takeaways

- You can ask AI for the format you want
- Lists, steps, tables, and paragraphs are all possible
- The right format makes an answer easier to use
- Ask for simple words and short sentences
- Say the format in your question

## Practice Challenge

1. Ask the same question as a list and as steps
2. Try asking for a table
3. Choose the format you like best

**Example Quiz**

1. What can you ask AI for?
- A) The format you want
- B) Only text
- C) Only numbers
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — You can ask for lists, steps, tables, or paragraphs.

2. Which format is best for steps?
- A) Numbered steps
- B) A poem
- C) A table
- D) A picture

**Answer:** A — Numbered steps keep a process in order and easy to follow.

3. Which format compares things?
- A) A table
- B) A list
- C) A paragraph
- D) A story

**Answer:** A — A table compares options at a glance.

4. How should you ask?
- A) Say the format in your question
- B) Guess
- C) Say nothing
- D) Use code

**Answer:** A — Mention the format you want directly in your question.

### Module 5 · Step 4: Improve the Answer by Talking Back

AI answers are a starting point, not the end. You can talk back: ask it to change, shorten, simplify, or try again.

This back-and-forth is how you turn a rough answer into exactly what you need.

## Learning Objectives

- Improve answers by replying
- Ask for changes and simpler words
- Keep the conversation going
- Get exactly what you need

## Talking Back

- Say what you want changed
- Ask for simpler or shorter
- Ask it to try again differently
### Why
- The first answer is rarely perfect
- You are in charge of the result
- Small changes make it fit you

## Useful Follow-Ups

- Make it simpler
- Make it shorter
- Give me more examples
- Change the tone to be warmer
### The rule
- Keep asking until it fits
- Be specific about changes
- Do not settle for confusing answers

## The Follow-Up Prompt

```text
Here is your last answer [paste]. Please make it simpler,
shorter, and warmer, and add one everyday example. Keep the
same information.
```
- Simpler and shorter
- Warmer tone
- One everyday example

## Key Takeaways

- AI answers are a starting point, not the end
- Talk back to change, shorten, or simplify
- Be specific about what you want changed
- Keep asking until the answer fits you
- You are in charge of the result

## Practice Challenge

1. Take one AI answer and ask for it simpler
2. Then ask for a warmer tone
3. Compare the first and final versions

**Example Quiz**

1. What is an AI answer?
- A) A starting point
- B) The final truth
- C) A mistake
- D) A rule

**Answer:** A — Treat AI answers as a starting point you can improve.

2. How do you improve an answer?
- A) Talk back and ask for changes
- B) Delete it
- C) Give up
- D) Restart the phone

**Answer:** A — Reply to AI and ask it to change, shorten, or simplify.

3. What follow-up helps?
- A) Make it simpler
- B) Make it harder
- C) Make it longer
- D) Make it confusing

**Answer:** A — Asking for simpler, shorter, or warmer answers improves them.

4. Who is in charge of the result?
- A) You
- B) The AI
- C) The phone
- D) The app

**Answer:** A — You stay in charge and keep refining until it fits your needs.

### Module 6 · Step 5: Checking the Answer Before You Trust It

AI can sometimes be wrong, especially with numbers, dates, health, and money. Always check important answers.

This lesson teaches simple ways to check before you act on an AI answer.

## Learning Objectives

- Check AI answers before trusting them
- Know what needs extra care
- Use a second source
- Act safely on answers

## When to Be Careful

- Health and medicine
- Money and legal matters
- Numbers, dates, and addresses
- Anything that affects your family
### Why
- AI can sound confident and still be wrong
- Important decisions need care
- Checking protects you

## How to Check

- Ask a second source or a person
- Ask AI to show where the answer comes from
- Use your own common sense
### The rule
- Check before you act
- Ask a real person for big decisions
- Trust your instinct

## The Checking Prompt

```text
Here is an AI answer I am unsure about [paste]. List what I
should verify, where I could check it, and what could go
wrong if the answer is incorrect.
```
- What to verify
- Where to check
- Risks if wrong

## Key Takeaways

- AI can be wrong even when it sounds confident
- Be extra careful with health, money, and legal matters
- Check important answers with a second source
- Ask a real person for big decisions
- Checking before you act protects your family

## Practice Challenge

1. Take one important AI answer
2. List what you should verify
3. Check it with a second source

**Example Quiz**

1. Can AI be wrong?
- A) Yes, even when confident
- B) No, never
- C) Only at night
- D) Only in winter

**Answer:** A — AI can sound confident and still be incorrect.

2. Which needs extra care?
- A) Health and money
- B) Recipes
- C) Stories
- D) Weather

**Answer:** A — Be extra careful with health, money, and legal matters.

3. How do you check?
- A) Use a second source
- B) Trust it fully
- C) Ignore it
- D) Ask the same app

**Answer:** A — Verify important answers with a second, independent source.

4. Who should you ask for big decisions?
- A) A real person
- B) The AI only
- C) Nobody
- D) A stranger online

**Answer:** A — Ask a trusted real person for important decisions.
