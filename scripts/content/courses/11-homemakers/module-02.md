# Mission 2: Talking to AI — Your First Conversations

AI understands plain language, but a few simple habits make its answers dramatically better. This mission teaches you the magic of a good "ask" — the skill called prompting.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand why the way you ask AI matters.
- Learn the simple Recipe: Role + Task + Details.
- Rewrite one weak question into a strong one.
- Get AI to explain something in simpler words when you do not understand.

## The Core Idea

Think of AI as a very eager assistant who wants to help but has no idea what you already know. If you ask "recipes", you may get a huge complicated list. If you ask "three simple recipes a beginner can cook with rice, eggs and tomatoes, using the appliances I own — a stove and a pressure cooker", you get something perfectly useful.

This clearer way of asking is called writing a **prompt**. Anyone can learn it. There are three ingredients.

### The Recipe: Role + Task + Details

1. **Role** — tell AI who it should pretend to be.
   - "Act as a friendly home cooking teacher…"
   - "Act as a patient math tutor for children…"

2. **Task** — say exactly what you want.
   - "Write a weekly dinner plan…"
   - "Give me 5 fun ways to teach fractions…"

3. **Details** — add what matters to you.
   - "…for a family of four with two picky eaters."
   - "…for an 8-year-old who finds maths boring."
   - "…in under 200 words, in simple language."

### Example: The Same Question, Two Ways

Weak: *"Vegetable recipes."*

Strong: *"Act as a home cooking teacher. Give me 3 easy vegetarian dinner recipes with a list of ingredients I can write for the market. We're a family of four and I have one hour. Simple language please."*

Which answer would you rather cook from? Exactly.

### When You Don't Understand the Answer

Two magic phrases every new user must learn:

- **"Explain that in simpler words."**
- **"Explain it to me like I'm learning this for the first time."**

Most AI apps understand these perfectly and give you a second, clearer version. Asking for simpler language is a sign of good prompting, not a lack of skill.

### Asking in Your Own Language

These tools work in Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, and many Indian languages. If you are more comfortable, ask in your mother tongue: *"मुझे आज रात के लिए 3 आसान रेसिपी बताओ"* works just as well as English. The AI matches your language and still gives a complete answer.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 – The recipe problem (revisited).** Moon tried the weak question first — "recipes" gave her a complicated list with ingredients she did not have. She used the Recipe — "Act as a home cooking teacher, give me 3 simple dinners for my family of 4 in easy words, one hour only" — and got three practical meals she could cook that same evening.

**Example 2 – The budget problem.** Instead of asking "how to save money", Lakshmi asked: *"Act as a friendly money advisor. Now let's plan a simple monthly budget for a family of four living in a small town, income 25,000 rupees per month. Use a table with Food, House, School, Savings."* She received a clear table she could print and follow.

**Example 3 – The angry-bird problem.** For her own curiosity, Sunita typed: *"Explain what a computer is, to me like I am a homemaker who never studied computers. Keep it very simple."* The answer began "A computer is like a powerful office friend that never sleeps…" — and she understood for the first time.

## Key Takeaways

- Prompting is just asking well: Role + Task + Details.
- The same question can produce a useless or a brilliant answer — the difference is how you ask.
- "Explain in simpler words" is your best friend. Never hesitate to use it.
- Ask in your own language — these tools respond well to Indian languages.
- Practice rewriting one weak question per day; within a week it becomes automatic.

## Practice Challenge

**Task:** Build one strong prompt for each of these three real situations.

1. **Cooking:** "Act as a home cooking teacher. Plan 3 dinner options for a family of 4 on a tight budget, using a stove and pressure cooker only. Easy words."
2. **Kids:** Ask AI to explain one thing your child is learning, in a fun way.
3. **Money or letters:** Ask AI to plan a monthly budget table, or draft a polite letter you need to send.

For each one, note: what details did you add, and how did the answer change from what you expected?
