# Mission 6: Teaching AI to Your Community — Your AI Didi Roadmap

You now have a skill thousands of others need: the ability to use AI for daily life. This final mission turns you into an **AI Didi** — a woman who teaches her community, earns respect, and builds a future she chooses. It also maps your long-term learning path.

## Learning Objectives

- Run a 30-minute beginner AI session for your family or neighbours.
- Explain AI's basics to others with confidence and zero jargon.
- Design at least one community activity you can organise near home.
- Build your next-year learning roadmap with AI's help.

## The Core Idea

In every community there are women who need exactly what you learned in Missions 1–5: how to talk to AI, plan with AI, and earn with AI. Teaching them is valuable for three reasons:

1. **You learn twice** — teaching something is the best way to truly master it.
2. **You build standing** — an AI Didi is consulted, respected, and trusted locally.
3. **You create income paths** — classes, community charging, consultancy, and trainer work all emerge from teaching.

### How to Run a 30-Minute First Session

Keep it simple. Your first session has four beats:

1. **The one-sentence hook (2 min):** "AI is a helper on your phone that understands our language and answers instantly. Today I'll show you three things it can do for your home."
2. **The live demo (10 min):** Open one AI app. Ask IT the questions your audience brings — a recipe, a budget, a homework explanation. They watch the magic happen in their own language.
3. **Hands-on pairs (10 min):** Give everyone 5 minutes to ask AI their own question on their own phones. Walk around and encourage: "There is no wrong question."
4. **The promise (8 min):** Ask each woman to write ONE task she will try with AI this week. Collect the promises — next session starts from what they tried.

### Who to Teach First

- **Home circle:** your own family and mother's group.
- **Neighbourhood:** kitchen-table groups of 4–6 women — intimate, comfortable, safe.
- **Community spaces:** anganwadi centres, school parent meetings, temple/mosque/church groups, self-help groups (SHGs), anganwadi worker networks.

### Design One Community Activity

Pick a format that fits your situation:

- **The Sunday Kitchen Class** — a weekly meet in your home, 5 women, bring-your-own-phone.
- **The SHG AI Hour** — propose a recurring slot at your self-help group: "Budget month with AI", "Our SHG's WhatsApp marketing with AI", "Selling our products online safely".
- **The Festival Project** — help the women's group make a collective festival-sweets order book using AI, taught live.
- **The School-Parent Evening** — ask your child's school to give you 30 minutes at a parent meeting to teach responsible phone use with AI.

### Your Long-Term Roadmap

Now plan the next three chapters with AI:

- **Next 3 months:** hold four sessions (start with family, then 3 community formats); collect the success stories of women who earn or save with what they learned.
- **Next 6 months:** decide whether teaching is a passion to grow — look into official skill courses: **crowdfunding-free free courses, SWAYAM, nasscom, GOI digital skilling portals, and NGO programs for women's digital literacy** — and choose one certificate.
- **This year:** document your journey — one WhatsApp status a week of a woman's win — and begin to earn as a trusted local AI trainer while always following the safety rules you learned.

### Your Power Is Your Discipline

The AI tools will change; the *skill* of asking well, checking answers, and helping others will not. You are not learning about a machine. You are learning to lead — in your kitchen, your neighbourhood, and your future.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 – The kitchen class.** Rani hosted five neighbours for chai, biscuit, and AI. One neighbour asked how to keep track of three children's school fees; by the end she had a budget table on her phone, made together. The class became a weekly habit.

**Example 2 – The SHG win.** At her self-help group, Usha introduced an AI hour. Together they built a festival order book for their collective pickle-making. The group sold out its festive stock in two weeks and asked her to run the AI hour monthly.

**Example 3 – The respected Didi.** After her school-parent evening, the teacher asked Kavita to help two other mothers set up AI for their kids' homework. Her WhatsApp statuses about their wins began to build her name as the neighbourhood AI Didi.

## Key Takeaways

- As an AI Didi, you teach what you lived: ask, check, improve, save.
- A 30-minute session with one live demo and hands-on practice is enough to change someone's week.
- Self-help groups, schools, and kitchen tables are your classrooms — you don't need a hall or a certificate to start.
- Every session you give makes your skill stronger and your reputation deeper.
- Official skilling (GOI digital literacy programs, women's skilling NGOs) can turn teaching into a structured profession next year.
