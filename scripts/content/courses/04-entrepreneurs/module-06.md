# Mission 6: Lean Ops — Marketing, Finance & Legal with AI

## Learning Objectives
- Set up a month-one marketing engine on a tiny budget
- Manage startup finance (income, tax, payments) with AI
- Handle first-customer contracts and privacy basics cleanly
- Automate admin before it eats your founder time
- Stay honest about AI boundaries in law, tax, and finance

---

## Lean Ops = Systems Before They Hurt

Good operations aren't bureaucracy — they're the shoulder strapped under the weight of growth. In this mission you set up marketing, finance, and legal "rails" so your first customers don't break you.

## Marketing: the Month-One Engine (Small Budget, Daily Habits)

Before scale, focus on one channel that works enough:

- **Channel of one:** pick the single channel where your target already gathers (from Mission 2: community, LinkedIn, local business groups, marketplace). One channel done daily beats six channels done weekly.
- **Content rhythm:** 3 posts/week of proof, not announcements: customer/problem mini-stories, sample outputs, how-you-did-it notes. Repurpose one good piece into 3 formats.
- **Outreach:** 10 targeted conversations/day (DM, email, comment) — missions 2's energy, now aimed at sellers.
- **Tracking:** a simple sheet: content sent / replies / customers. Stop what doesn't convert in 2 weeks (with enough attempts to be fair).

> "Build a 30-day marketing plan for [offer] on a ₹0–2,000 budget: 1 channel, 3 weekly post ideas (story, proof, how-to), daily outreach script, and a 2-week kill-switch for what isn't working."

## Finance: Know Every Rupee (AI Assists, You Decide)

Startup finance is boring until it's fatal. Set up:

- **Separate account:** business money away from personal (even a small one). Clean from day one.
- **The toolsheet:** income in / costs out / tax reserved (set aside ~30% in high-tax locales — check your rules).
- **Bookkeeping with AI:** upload monthly statements (redact), ask:
> "Categorise these transactions into income, tool costs, fees, and personal; flag irregular spending and money I haven't accounted for. Suggest 2 things to automate."
- **Payments:** online (UPI/Razorpay/Stripe-style) + invoicing template with GST/invoice# where required.

**AI honesty rule:** AI gives drafts and summaries, never final tax/legal opinions in your jurisdiction. Verify with local rules, an accountant, or a paid hour of a professional — the cost of an error dwarfs the fee.

## Legal & Contracts: The First-Customer Safety Net

Keep it lean but real:

- **The simple contract:** for your first service/fulfilment deals, use a plain template: scope, price, deadlines, who owns what, liability clause, cancellation. AI drafts, you adapt, and (for anything with cash on the line) spend on 1 hour of professional review.
> "Draft a one-page client agreement for [offer]: scope, price [P], deadlines, deliverables, late permissions, and a 'no guarantees of revenue/results' clause. Keep it plain-language, no legalese."
- **Privacy basics:** if you collect customer data, one honest privacy notice; be transparent about any AI processing (your customers will test it).
- **Your own protection:** separate entity (when revenue warrants), invoice always, don't sign liability you don't have to.

## Automate Admin Before It Autopilots You

Every repetitive founder task is an AI-automation candidate:

- Welcome/onboarding emails to new customers
- Invoice reminders and payment follow-ups
- FAQ and first-line support for common questions
- Reporting/updates for your own dashboard

> "List admin tasks my startup repeats weekly and score each by frequency × time. For the top 3, suggest a no-code automation (tool + trigger + steps) and the 'human still in the loop' checkpoint."

Automate only after you've done the task manually 3 times — you can't automate what you don't understand.

## The "AI Does Operations" Boundaries

- **Never let AI:** sign documents, own liability, give final legal/tax/medical advice, or decide compliance for you.
- **Always keep:** human review of anything with consequence, version control on contracts, and receipts for everything.
- **The polite check:** "This is a draft for your review, not advice." Use that line honestly.

## Key Takeaways

- Marketing: one channel, proof content, daily outreach, 2-week kill-switch.
- Finance: separate account, toolsheet, invoice, 30% tax reserve; AI categorises, you decide.
- Legal: plain contract + privacy basics + professional review when cash is on the line.
- Automate admin only after manual reps 3 times; keep a human checkpoint.
- AI drafts and summarises; it never owns legal/tax/finance decisions.

## Practice Challenge

1. **Channel decision:** pick your ONE channel and write your 30-day marketing plan + daily outreach script.
2. **Finance setup:** open the separate account, create your income/costs/tax toolsheet, and draft your first invoice + a recurring AI categorisation workflow.
3. **Contract step:** draft your client agreement with AI, note the clauses you'll have professionally reviewed before first big deal.
4. **Automation pick:** score your top 3 repetitive admin tasks; map the no-code automation for #1 with its human checkpoint.
