# Mission 3: AI-First Business Model Design

## Learning Objectives
- Design a lean business model on one page with AI
- Choose and defend a pricing strategy
- Set up cashflow awareness before you spend
- Decide build, buy, or AI-automate for every function
- Write unit economics for your first revenue goal

---

## The Business Model Is Your Operating Manual

Before building anything, decide how money moves. A one-page model prevents building an unfundable gadget. AI compresses the framing work — you still make the calls.

## Step 1: One-Page Business Model (AI-Assisted)

Use a lean canvas or this PYRAMID summary:

- **Customer** — who pays? (from Mission 2)
- **Problem** — what hurts them? (their words)
- **Solution** — what do you sell, and how does AI make you cheaper/faster/sharper?
- **Revenue** — how money comes in (price model below).
- **Costs** — what it actually takes to deliver.
- **Channel** — where they find you.
- **Edge** — why you vs alternatives.

Prompt:
> "Help me fill a one-page business model for [idea] [paste validation notes]. Cover customer, problem, solution, revenue, costs, channel, edge. Ask me 5 clarifying questions instead of guessing. Then draft the one-pager."

The rule: **results format.** Write it small and legible; you'll test it in Mission 8.

## Step 2: Pick a Pricing Strategy (Not a Guess)

Four sane options for a lean startup:

1. **One-time (product/gig):** simple, needs constant new customers.
2. **Subscription (monthly):** predictable cash, must keep proving value.
3. **Usage-based (pay-per-result):** ties price to value; good for automation/fulfilment.
4. **Package/retainer (services):** fastest cash for skill-based offers.

Pricing questions:

> "For [solution] serving [customer], compare one-time vs subscription vs usage pricing with realistic example prices in [currency/market]. Which fits my revenue goal of [₹X]/month given [how often they'd use it]? List the risks of each."

**Pricing truths:** anchor tests beat opinions ("Willing to pay?" doesn't measure); the easiest price to charge is the one you can explain in one sentence; raising later is hard, starting lower but clearly framed is fine.

## Step 3: Cashflow Before You Spend

Startups die from cashflow, not profits. Do this before ANY spend:

- **Map the 12-month mini-cashflow:** likely income months vs the fixed costs (tools, ads, payments, your time).
- **Never default on tools you can't stop.** Prefer pay-as-you-go for AI/API beyond the free tier.
- **Your runway rule:** decide now — "If I have zero revenue by [month X], I reassess." Agreeing the stop-condition in advance keeps emotions out of finance.

> "Draft a simple 12-month cashflow table for my model: expected income (realistic low case) vs costs. Which 3 months are riskiest and what triggers me to cut?"

## Step 4: The Build / Buy / AI-Automate Decision

For each function (website, payments, support, marketing, operations) decide:

- **BUILD** if it gives real moat (your core product logic).
- **BUY** if it's commodity (payments, hosting, email).
- **AUTOMATE** if it's rule-based and repetitive (workflows, vetting, follow-ups, reporting).

> "For my one-pager, suggest a build / buy / automate split for: website, payments, customer support, marketing, admin. Justify each. Flag where 'buy' saves the most hours."

**Frugality check:** your fixed costs in month 1 should be nearly zero in the best case. Tools you can't cancel on a say-once budget are features, not friends.

## Step 5: Unit Economics for Your First Goal

Unit economics = one sale's truth: **price − variable cost of delivering that one sale = margin.**

> "If my price is [P], and cost to deliver one unit is [C] (tools, time, payment fees), what's my margin? How many units/month at that margin hit my minimum viable cashflow need?"

Set the milestone: **First 3 paying customers** (not 100). From those 3 you learn price, delivery cost, and channel truth. Everything scales from a working unit.

## Key Takeaways

- A one-page model (customer, problem, solution, revenue, costs, channel, edge) is your operating manual.
- Pick one pricing model and price where behaviour was observed (pre-orders, competitor prices), not opinions.
- Map cashflow before spending; set a stop-condition in advance.
- Build the moat, buy the commodity, automate the repetitive.
- Win 3 paying customers — unit economics, not scale, comes first.

## Practice Challenge

1. **Draft your one-page model** with AI (results format). Share it with 3 people and improve based on their "what's missing?" answers.
2. **Price decision:** choose your pricing strategy, write your one-sentence "why this price" explanation, and validate with your Mission 2 customers.
3. **Cashflow + stop-condition:** build the 12-month table; write down your revenue reassessment trigger.
4. **Build/Buy/Automate table:** complete the split for 5 functions with reasons.
5. **First-goal math:** compute margin on your price and the number of customers needed for your monthly target.
