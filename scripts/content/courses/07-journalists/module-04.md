### Module 1 · AI for Multilingual and Social-First Journalism

Most audiences now meet a story on a phone, in a feed, often in another language. AI helps you reach them by translating, transcribing, and reformatting one story into many.

The rule is reach without distortion: AI expands distribution, but a human must check every translation and every claim before it reaches an audience.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the multilingual, social-first opportunity
- Use AI to expand reach across formats and languages
- Keep accuracy when reformatting
- Never publish an unchecked translation

## Reach Without Distortion

- Audiences are mobile-first and multilingual
- One story can become text, audio, and video
- AI speeds reformatting and translation
### The rule
- Expand reach, never distort meaning
- A human checks every translation
- Accuracy outranks speed

## The Multilingual Toolkit

- Transcription for audio and video
- Translation for local-language audiences
- Reformatting for social platforms
### Why
- One story serves many formats
- Local languages expand the audience
- AI removes the repetitive work

## The Reach Prompt

```text
I have this story [paste]. Give me a plan to adapt it for
audio, video, and social, plus a translation brief for
[language]. Note the checks a human must do on each.
```
- A plan across formats
- A translation brief
- Human checks at each step

## Key Takeaways

- Audiences are mobile-first and multilingual
- AI expands one story into many formats and languages
- Expand reach without distorting meaning
- A human must check every translation
- Accuracy outranks speed in every reformat

## Practice Challenge

3. List the human checks for each

### Module 2 · Step 1: Script for the Ear, Not the Eye

Audio and video scripts are written for the ear. Short sentences, plain words, and a clear through-line beat elegant written prose.

AI can adapt your text for the ear, but read every line aloud before you record.

## Learning Objectives

- Write scripts for the ear
- Adapt written copy for audio and video
- Keep sentences short and clear
- Read aloud before recording

## Writing for the Ear

- Short sentences, one idea each
- Plain words a listener understands instantly
- A clear through-line from start to finish
### Why
- Listeners cannot re-read
- Complex sentences confuse the ear
- Clarity keeps attention

## Adapting Your Copy

- Turn dense paragraphs into spoken beats
- Replace written transitions with spoken cues
- Mark pauses and emphasis
### The rule
- Read every line aloud
- Cut anything you stumble on
- Let the voice carry the story

## The Script Prompt

```text
Adapt this story [paste] into a 90-second audio script
for the ear: short sentences, plain words, a clear arc,
and pause markers. Keep all facts exactly as written.
```
- A defined length
- Written for the ear
- Facts unchanged

## Key Takeaways

- Audio and video scripts are written for the ear
- Use short sentences and plain words
- Keep a clear through-line
- Read every line aloud before recording
- Cut anything you stumble on

## Practice Challenge

1. Adapt a story into a 90-second audio script
2. Read it aloud and mark the stumbles
3. Cut and rewrite until it flows

**Example Quiz**

1. What are audio scripts written for?
- A) The ear
- B) The eye
- C) The page
- D) The archive

**Answer:** A — Scripts for audio and video are written for the ear.

2. What sentence style works best?
- A) Short sentences, one idea each
- B) Long, complex sentences
- C) Dense paragraphs
- D) Technical jargon

**Answer:** A — Short, plain sentences are easier for listeners to follow.

3. What should you do before recording?
- A) Read every line aloud
- B) Publish immediately
- C) Add more facts
- D) Skip the script

**Answer:** A — Reading aloud reveals stumbles to cut before recording.

4. What happens to facts when adapting?
- A) They stay exactly as written
- B) They can change
- C) They are improved
- D) They are removed

**Answer:** A — Adapting for the ear must keep all facts exactly as written.

### Module 3 · Step 2: Turn One Story into Many Formats

One well-reported story can become a text article, a short video, an audio clip, a social thread, and a newsletter item. AI makes the reformatting fast.

The reporting is done once; the reformatting is mechanical - and every version must stay true to the original.

## Learning Objectives

- Reformat one story into multiple versions
- Match each format to its platform
- Keep facts consistent across versions
- Avoid over-repetition

## The Formats

- Long-form article
- Short vertical video
- Audio clip or podcast segment
- Social thread and newsletter item
### Why
- Audiences live on different platforms
- One story reaches more people
- Reformatting is fast with AI

## Matching Format to Platform

- Social wants the hook in the first line
- Video wants movement and captions
- Audio wants a spoken arc
- Newsletters want a personal framing
### The rule
- Same facts, different shape
- Fit the platform's habits
- Do not just copy-paste

## The Reformat Prompt

```text
Turn this verified story [paste] into: a social thread,
a 30-second video script, an audio clip intro, and a
newsletter item. Keep facts identical in all versions.
```
- Four formats at once
- Facts identical
- Platform-appropriate shapes

## Key Takeaways

- One story can become article, video, audio, social, and newsletter
- Match each format to its platform's habits
- Keep facts identical across every version
- Reformatting is mechanical; reporting is done once
- Do not just copy-paste between platforms

## Practice Challenge

1. Turn a story into four formats

3. Adapt each to its platform

### Module 4 · Step 3: Auto-Transcribe, Subtitle, and Translate

Transcription, subtitles, and translation are the biggest time-savers AI offers a newsroom. They also carry real accuracy risks: accents, names, and jargon are where errors hide.

Treat every automatic transcript as a draft that needs checking, especially for quotes that will be published.

## Learning Objectives

- Use AI to transcribe, subtitle, and translate
- Check transcripts for accuracy
- Handle names, accents, and jargon
- Publish only checked quotes

## The Workflow

- Record the interview or event
- Auto-transcribe with an AI tool
- Auto-generate subtitles for video
- Translate for local-language audiences
### Why
- It turns hours into minutes
- Subtitles widen reach
- Translation serves more readers

## Where Errors Hide

- Names and places are frequently wrong
- Accents and audio quality cause errors
- Technical jargon is often garbled
### The rule
- Check every quote against the audio
- Fix names before publishing
- Never publish an unchecked quote

## The Transcript Prompt

```text
Here is a transcript [paste]. Identify likely transcription
errors in names, numbers, and jargon, and list the passages
I must verify against the original audio before quoting.
```
- Flag likely errors
- Focus on names and numbers
- Verify against audio

## Key Takeaways

- AI speeds transcription, subtitling, and translation
- Every transcript is a draft needing checks
- Names, accents, and jargon are where errors hide
- Check every quote against the original audio
- Never publish an unchecked quote

## Practice Challenge

1. Auto-transcribe a short recording
2. Check names and numbers against the audio
3. List the corrections you made

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the biggest AI time-savers?
- A) Transcription, subtitles, and translation
- B) Headlines
- C) Ethics
- D) Verification

**Answer:** A — These three turn hours of work into minutes.

2. Where do transcription errors hide?
- A) Names, accents, and jargon
- B) Silences
- C) The intro
- D) The outro

**Answer:** A — Names, accents, and jargon are frequently garbled.

3. What must you check before quoting?
- A) The quote against the original audio
- B) The headline
- C) The word count
- D) The source's mood

**Answer:** A — Check every quote against the audio before publishing.

4. How should you treat a transcript?
- A) As a draft needing checks
- B) As final copy
- C) As a source
- D) As a fact

**Answer:** A — Every automatic transcript needs human checking.

### Module 5 · Step 4: Visuals and Thumbnails That Stay Honest

Visuals and thumbnails drive clicks, but they must stay honest. An image that implies something the story does not support is misinformation, even if the headline is accurate.

Use AI to generate ideas and layouts, never to fabricate a scene presented as real.

## Learning Objectives

- Create honest thumbnails and visuals
- Avoid misleading imagery
- Label AI-generated visuals
- Keep images true to the story

## Honest Visuals

- The image must match the story's claim
- Do not imply what the story does not support
- Prefer real photos from the reporting
### Why
- Visuals carry meaning fast
- Misleading images are misinformation
- Trust depends on honesty

## Thumbnails and AI Images

- Thumbnails may be designed, not fabricated
- Label any AI-generated image
- Never present a generated scene as a real event
### The rule
- Design yes, fabricate no
- Label synthetic visuals
- Real reporting photos first

## The Visual Prompt

```text
Suggest 5 honest thumbnail concepts for this story [paste]
using real reporting photos. For each, note the caption and
whether any element would need an AI-generated label.
```
- Concepts from real photos
- Captions included
- Label synthetic elements

## Key Takeaways

- Visuals and thumbnails must stay honest
- The image must match the story's actual claim
- Design thumbnails, never fabricate events
- Label any AI-generated visual
- Prefer real reporting photos

## Practice Challenge

1. Review the visuals on your last story
2. Check each matches the story's claim
3. Label any synthetic elements

**Example Quiz**

1. What must a visual do?
- A) Match the story's actual claim
- B) Exaggerate for clicks
- C) Show a different event
- D) Hide the point

**Answer:** A — An image that implies more than the story supports is misinformation.

2. What is allowed in a thumbnail?
- A) Design, but not fabrication
- B) Fabricated events
- C) Fake scenes
- D) Misleading edits

**Answer:** A — You may design thumbnails but never fabricate events.

3. What must AI-generated images have?
- A) A label
- B) No caption
- C) A fake source
- D) A byline

**Answer:** A — Any AI-generated visual must be clearly labelled.

4. Which images come first?
- A) Real reporting photos
- B) Generated images
- C) Stock images
- D) Screenshots

**Answer:** A — Prefer real photos from your reporting over generated ones.

### Module 6 · Step 5: The Ethics of Synthetic Media

Synthetic media - AI-generated voices, images, and video - can be used responsibly or abused. Journalists must know the line and hold it.

The core principle: never present synthetic media as authentic, and never use it to deceive an audience.

## Learning Objectives

- Define synthetic media and its risks
- Apply the no-deception principle
- Label synthetic content clearly
- Recognise deepfakes and their harm

## What Synthetic Media Is

- AI-generated or altered voices, images, and video
- Includes deepfakes and voice clones
- Can be benign or deeply harmful
### The risk
- It can impersonate real people
- It can fabricate events
- It erodes trust in all media

## The No-Deception Principle

- Never present synthetic media as authentic
- Label clearly when it is used
- Do not use it to mislead the audience
### The rule
- Authenticity is the baseline
- Labels protect the audience
- Deception is never justified

## The Ethics Prompt

```text
Explain the ethical rules for using synthetic media in
journalism: when it may be used, how to label it, and the
cases where it must never be used. Give examples.
```
- When it is allowed
- How to label it
- When it is forbidden

## Key Takeaways

- Synthetic media includes AI voices, images, video, and deepfakes
- Never present synthetic media as authentic
- Label synthetic content clearly
- Deception is never justified in journalism
- Synthetic media can erode trust in all media

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your outlet's rule for synthetic media
2. List when it may and may not be used
3. Draft a labelling standard

**Example Quiz**

1. What is synthetic media?
- A) AI-generated or altered voices, images, and video

- D) Live interviews

**Answer:** A — Synthetic media includes deepfakes and voice clones.

2. What is the core principle?
- A) Never present synthetic media as authentic
- B) Use it freely
- C) Hide the label
- D) Trust the tool

**Answer:** A — Authenticity is the baseline - never present synthetic media as real.

3. What must synthetic content have?
- A) A clear label
- B) A fake byline

- D) A sponsor

**Answer:** A — Label synthetic content clearly so the audience is not deceived.

4. Is deception ever justified?
- A) No
- B) Sometimes
- C) For good causes
- D) When it gets clicks

**Answer:** A — Deception is never justified in journalism.
