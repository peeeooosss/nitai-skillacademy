### Module 1 · The Journalist's Non-Negotiables

Some principles do not bend, whatever the tool. Truth, independence, fairness, and accountability are the journalist's non-negotiables.

AI changes how work gets done; it never changes these duties. Anchor every AI decision to them.

## Learning Objectives

- State the journalist's non-negotiables
- Apply them to AI use
- Keep independence from tools and sources
- Stay accountable for every output

## The Core Principles

- **Truth:** publish only what you have verified
- **Independence:** serve the audience, not the tool or the source
- **Fairness:** represent people and views accurately
- **Accountability:** own and correct your mistakes
### Why
- They are the basis of public trust
- Tools change; principles do not
- Every AI decision answers to them

## Applying Them to AI

- Truth: verify every AI claim
- Independence: AI is a tool, not an authority
- Fairness: watch AI's built-in bias
- Accountability: you sign off, not the AI
### The rule
- Principles outrank convenience
- No deadline excuses deception
- The byline is human

## The Principles Prompt

```text
For a journalist using AI, explain how to apply truth,
independence, fairness, and accountability to daily tasks.
Give a concrete example of each.
```
- One example per principle
- Applied to daily work
- Anchored in trust

## Key Takeaways

- Truth, independence, fairness, and accountability are non-negotiable
- AI changes how work is done, not these duties
- AI is a tool, not an authority
- You sign off - the byline is human
- No deadline excuses deception

## Practice Challenge

2. Give an AI example of each
3. Post them at your desk

2. What is AI in relation to these principles?
- A) A tool, not an authority

**Answer:** A — AI is a tool that must answer to the journalist's principles.

- A) A human

**Answer:** A — The byline is human - you own the output.

4. Does deadline excuse deception?
- A) No

- D) For breaking news

**Answer:** A — No deadline excuses deception.

### Module 2 · Disclosure: When and How to Tell Readers

Transparency builds trust. When AI has materially shaped a story, readers often have a right to know - and your outlet may require it.

Disclosure is about material use, not every keystroke. Decide what your audience needs to know to trust the work.

## Learning Objectives

- Decide when disclosure is required
- Write clear disclosure language
- Follow your outlet's policy
- Build reader trust through transparency

## When to Disclose

- When AI materially shaped the reporting or output
- When synthetic media is used
- When your outlet's policy requires it
### Why
- Readers have a right to know how news is made
- Transparency separates journalism from propaganda
- Trust is the asset

## How to Disclose

- Plain, specific language
- State what AI did and what a human checked
- Place it where readers will see it
### The rule
- Be specific, not vague
- Name the human oversight
- Never bury the disclosure

## The Disclosure Prompt

```text
Write a short, plain-language disclosure for a story where
AI was used to [task]. State what AI did, what a human
verified, and keep it under 3 sentences.
```
- Plain and specific
- Name the human check
- Keep it short

## Key Takeaways

- Disclose when AI materially shaped a story
- Disclosure is about material use, not every keystroke
- Use plain, specific language
- State what AI did and what a human checked
- Never bury the disclosure

## Practice Challenge

1. Write a disclosure for a story where you used AI
2. State the task and the human check
3. Check it against your outlet's policy

**Example Quiz**

1. When is disclosure required?
- A) When AI materially shaped the story
- B) Every keystroke
- C) Never
- D) Only for images

**Answer:** A — Disclose material use of AI in shaping the output.

2. What should disclosure language be?
- A) Plain and specific
- B) Vague
- C) Legalistic
- D) Hidden

**Answer:** A — Use plain, specific language that readers understand.

3. What should disclosure name?
- A) What AI did and what a human checked
- B) The tool's version
- C) The API key
- D) The vendor price

**Answer:** A — State the AI's role and the human verification.

4. Where should disclosure go?
- A) Where readers will see it
- B) In a hidden file
- C) On page ten
- D) Nowhere

**Answer:** A — Place it visibly - never bury the disclosure.

### Module 3 · Accuracy, Bias, and the Verification Duty

AI can reproduce and amplify bias at scale. It learns from data that carries historical patterns, and it can present those patterns as neutral fact.

The verification duty is stronger, not weaker, when AI is involved: check claims, question framing, and seek out the voices AI might overlook.

## Learning Objectives

- Recognise how AI carries bias
- Strengthen verification when using AI
- Seek out underrepresented voices
- Avoid amplifying biased framing

## How Bias Enters

- Training data carries historical patterns
- AI presents patterns as neutral
- Underrepresented voices get left out
### Why
- Bias at scale harms audiences
- Neutrality is not the same as fairness
- Journalists must correct for it

## The Verification Duty

- Check every claim, especially familiar ones
- Question the framing AI suggests
- Actively seek missing voices
### The rule
- Familiarity is not proof
- Framing is a choice
- Inclusion is part of accuracy

## The Bias-Check Prompt

```text
Review this draft [paste] for potential bias: whose voices
are missing, what framing is assumed, and what claims need
verification. Suggest fairer alternatives.
```
- Find missing voices
- Surface assumed framing
- Suggest fairer options

## Key Takeaways

- AI reproduces and amplifies bias from its training data
- AI presents patterns as neutral fact
- Strengthen verification when AI is involved
- Question the framing AI suggests
- Inclusion is part of accuracy

## Practice Challenge

1. Review a draft for missing voices
2. Question one framing assumption
3. Add a source that was overlooked

**Example Quiz**

1. Where does AI bias come from?
- A) Historical patterns in training data
- B) Nowhere
- C) The user only
- D) The platform

**Answer:** A — AI learns patterns from data that carries historical bias.

2. What does AI present patterns as?
- A) Neutral fact
- B) Opinion
- C) Bias
- D) Fiction

**Answer:** A — AI can present biased patterns as if they were neutral fact.

3. What is part of accuracy?
- A) Inclusion of missing voices
- B) Speed
- C) Style
- D) Length

**Answer:** A — Seeking underrepresented voices is part of accurate reporting.

4. What should you do with AI-suggested framing?
- A) Question it
- B) Accept it
- C) Publish it
- D) Ignore bias

**Answer:** A — Framing is a choice - question what AI assumes.

### Module 4 · Privacy, Sources, and Sensitive Data

Sources trust journalists with their identity and their safety. Uploading that material to an AI tool can break that trust in ways you cannot undo.

The rule is simple: anonymise first, process second, and when unsure, do not upload at all.

## Learning Objectives

- Protect source identity and safety
- Anonymise data before AI processing
- Follow data-protection rules
- Know when not to upload

## The Trust at Stake

- Sources may face retaliation or harm
- Their identity is their risk
- Uploading material can expose them
### Why
- Trust is the foundation of reporting
- A breach can endanger people
- It cannot be undone

## Anonymise First

- Remove names, locations, and identifying details
- Strip metadata from files
- Use tools approved by your outlet
### The rule
- Anonymise before processing
- Follow data-protection rules
- When unsure, do not upload

## The Privacy Prompt

```text
List the steps to anonymise interview material before
using an AI tool: what to remove, how to strip metadata,
and the cases where material must not be uploaded at all.
```
- What to remove
- How to strip metadata
- When not to upload

## Key Takeaways

- Sources trust journalists with their identity and safety
- Anonymise data before any AI processing
- Strip metadata from files
- Follow your outlet's data-protection rules
- When unsure, do not upload

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your anonymisation checklist
2. Practise stripping metadata from a sample file
3. List the material you would never upload

**Example Quiz**

1. Why protect source identity?
- A) They may face retaliation or harm
- B) It is polite
- C) It is required
- D) It saves space

**Answer:** A — Source identity is their risk, and a breach can endanger people.

2. When should you anonymise?
- A) Before AI processing
- B) After publishing
- C) Never
- D) Only if asked

**Answer:** A — Anonymise first, process second - trust cannot be undone.

3. What should you strip from files?
- A) Metadata
- B) The story
- C) The quotes
- D) The headline

**Answer:** A — Strip metadata that could identify a source.

4. What if you are unsure about uploading?
- A) Do not upload
- B) Upload anyway
- C) Ask the AI
- D) Upload a sample

**Answer:** A — When unsure, do not upload sensitive material.

### Module 5 · Copyright, Attribution, and Plagiarism

AI can reproduce text and images that belong to others. Using AI output does not transfer ownership or erase copyright.

Journalists must attribute sources, respect copyright, and never pass off AI-generated or copied work as original reporting.

## Learning Objectives

- Respect copyright in AI output
- Attribute sources correctly
- Avoid plagiarism
- Understand ownership limits

## Copyright Basics

- AI output can reproduce others' work
- Using AI does not transfer ownership
- Images and text have rights holders
### Why
- Infringement carries legal risk
- It damages credibility
- Respect for rights is professional

## Attribution and Plagiarism

- Always credit the original source
- Never pass off copied work as your own
- AI-generated text is not your reporting
### The rule
- Attribute everything borrowed
- Plagiarism is a firing offence
- Verify the origin of any text you use

## The Copyright Prompt

```text
For this draft [paste], list any passages or images that
may raise copyright or attribution issues, and how to
credit or replace them correctly.
```
- Flag copyright risks
- Suggest attribution
- Suggest replacements

## Key Takeaways

- AI output can reproduce others' copyrighted work
- Using AI does not transfer ownership
- Always attribute borrowed material
- Never pass off copied or AI work as original reporting
- Verify the origin of any text you use

## Practice Challenge

1. Review a draft for attribution gaps
2. Flag any passages with unclear origin
3. Credit or replace them

**Example Quiz**

1. What does using AI do to ownership?
- A) Nothing - it does not transfer ownership
- B) It transfers it to you
- C) It removes copyright
- D) It waives rights

**Answer:** A — Using AI output does not transfer ownership or erase copyright.

2. What must you do with borrowed material?
- A) Attribute it
- B) Hide it
- C) Sell it
- D) Ignore it

**Answer:** A — Always credit the original source of borrowed material.

3. What is plagiarism?
- A) Passing off copied work as your own
- B) Citing a source
- C) Using quotes
- D) Attributing work

**Answer:** A — Plagiarism is a serious breach - attribute everything you borrow.

4. Is AI-generated text your reporting?
- A) No
- B) Yes
- C) Sometimes
- D) If edited

**Answer:** A — AI-generated text is not original reporting and must not be passed off as such.

### Module 6 · Your Newsroom AI Policy (One Page)

A one-page AI policy turns good intentions into daily practice. It tells everyone what is allowed, what is checked, and who is accountable.

Write it once, review it often, and make it usable at deadline.

## Learning Objectives

- Draft a one-page AI policy
- Cover use, verification, and disclosure
- Assign accountability
- Keep it usable at deadline

## What the Policy Covers

- Allowed and forbidden uses
- Verification and disclosure rules
- Source and data protection
- Who is accountable
### Why
- Policy turns principles into practice
- It protects staff and audiences
- It settles questions before deadline

## Making It Usable

- One page, plain language
- Concrete examples, not abstractions
- Review and update as tools change
### The rule
- Short enough to read at deadline
- Specific enough to follow
- Reviewed regularly

## The Policy Prompt

```text
Draft a one-page AI policy for a newsroom: allowed and
forbidden uses, verification, disclosure, source protection,
and accountability. Plain language, usable at deadline.
```
- One page
- Covers all five areas
- Plain and practical

## Key Takeaways

- A one-page AI policy turns intentions into practice
- Cover allowed uses, verification, disclosure, and protection
- Assign clear accountability
- Use plain language and concrete examples
- Review and update as tools change
