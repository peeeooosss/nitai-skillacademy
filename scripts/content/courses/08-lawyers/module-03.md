### Module 1 · What AI Can and Cannot Do in Contract Review

AI is genuinely useful in contract review: it reads fast, extracts terms, and flags patterns. But it does not understand your client's commercial context or the negotiation history.

Use AI to find and organise; keep judgment, strategy, and sign-off with the lawyer.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand AI's role in contract review
- Know what AI cannot judge
- Use AI for extraction and flagging
- Keep strategy and sign-off human

## What AI Can Do

- Extract defined terms, dates, and obligations
- Flag common risk clauses
- Compare against a playbook or precedent
### Why
- Reading speed is AI's strength
- Pattern-matching finds outliers
- Consistency improves

## What AI Cannot Do

- Judge commercial context and intent
- Weigh negotiation history
- Decide what risk is acceptable
- Sign off on the contract
### The rule
- AI extracts and flags
- The lawyer judges and decides
- The client owns the commercial call

## The Review Role Prompt

```text
For contract review, explain what an AI tool can reliably
extract and flag, and what must remain a lawyer's judgment.
Give examples of each.
```
- What AI can extract
- What stays human
- Examples of each

## Key Takeaways

- AI reads fast and extracts terms and obligations
- AI flags common risk clauses and outliers
- AI cannot judge commercial context or negotiation history
- The lawyer decides what risk is acceptable
- AI extracts and flags; the lawyer judges and signs off

## Practice Challenge

1. List what AI can extract from a contract
2. List the judgments only a lawyer can make
3. Note where you would double-check AI output

**Example Quiz**

**Answer:** A — AI reads fast, extracts terms, and flags patterns.

2. What can AI not judge?
- A) Commercial context and negotiation history
- B) Defined terms
- C) Dates
- D) Obligations

**Answer:** A — Commercial context and history require human judgment.

3. Who decides acceptable risk?
- A) The lawyer, with the client
- B) The AI
- C) The vendor

**Answer:** A — Risk tolerance is a human, commercial decision.

4. Who signs off on the contract?
- A) The lawyer
- B) The AI
- C) The vendor
- D) The tool

**Answer:** A — AI extracts and flags; the lawyer signs off.

### Module 2 · Step 1: The Review Checklist

A contract review is only as good as its checklist. A structured checklist ensures nothing important is missed, every time.

AI can help you build and apply the checklist, but the checklist itself reflects your practice's standards.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a contract review checklist
- Cover commercial and legal terms
- Apply the checklist consistently
- Improve it over time

## What the Checklist Covers

- Parties, term, and termination
- Payment, delivery, and performance
- Liability, indemnity, and warranties
- Dispute resolution and governing law
### Why
- Checklists prevent missed terms
- Consistency reduces risk
- They speed up review

## Applying It Consistently

- Work through every item for every contract
- Record where terms are absent
- Flag deviations from your standard
### The rule
- Absence is a finding too
- Flag every deviation
- Review the checklist regularly

## The Checklist Prompt

```text
Build a contract review checklist for [contract type] in
[jurisdiction]: commercial and legal terms to check, with
the standard position and the risk if missing.
```
- Commercial and legal terms
- Standard positions
- Risk if missing

## Key Takeaways

- A review is only as good as its checklist
- Cover parties, term, payment, liability, and dispute resolution
- Work through every item for every contract
- Absence of a term is a finding too
- Flag every deviation from your standard

### Module 3 · Step 2: Flagging Risk Clauses

Certain clauses carry outsized risk: unlimited liability, broad indemnities, auto-renewal, unilateral variation, and one-sided termination. AI helps you find them fast.

Flagging is the start, not the end. Each flag needs a lawyer's assessment and a negotiation position.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify high-risk clause types
- Use AI to find them quickly
- Assess each flag
- Set a negotiation position

## High-Risk Clauses

- Unlimited or uncapped liability
- Broad or one-way indemnities
- Auto-renewal and unilateral variation
- One-sided termination rights
### Why
- These clauses shift risk sharply
- They are easy to miss in long contracts
- They drive negotiation

## From Flag to Position

- Assess the actual exposure of each flag
- Decide the acceptable position
- Prepare fallback language
### The rule
- A flag is not a conclusion
- Assess exposure
- Prepare a fallback

## The Risk-Flag Prompt

```text
Review this contract [paste] and flag risk clauses:
liability, indemnity, renewal, variation, termination.
For each, quote it, explain the risk, and suggest a
negotiation position.
```
- Quote each clause
- Explain the risk
- Suggest a position

## Key Takeaways

- High-risk clauses include unlimited liability and broad indemnities
- Also watch auto-renewal, unilateral variation, and one-sided termination
- AI helps find them fast in long contracts
- A flag is not a conclusion - assess the exposure
- Prepare a negotiation position and fallback

## Practice Challenge

1. Run a risk-flag review on a contract
2. Assess the exposure of each flag
3. Write your negotiation position for the top three

**Example Quiz**

1. Which is a high-risk clause?
- A) Unlimited liability
- B) Governing law
- C) Notice address
- D) Defined terms

**Answer:** A — Unlimited liability, broad indemnities, and one-sided terms carry outsized risk.

2. What does AI help you do?
- A) Find risk clauses fast
- B) Decide acceptable risk
- C) Sign the contract
- D) Advise the client

**Answer:** A — AI flags clauses quickly; you assess and decide.

3. What is a flag?
- A) A prompt for assessment, not a conclusion
- B) A final decision
- C) A signature
- D) A fee

**Answer:** A — A flag starts the assessment; it is not a conclusion.

4. What should you prepare for negotiation?
- A) A position and fallback language

**Answer:** A — Prepare a negotiation position and fallback language for each risk.

### Module 4 · Step 3: Comparing Against Your Playbook

A negotiation playbook records your standard positions and fallbacks for each clause. AI can compare a contract against the playbook and report every deviation.

The playbook turns review from a judgment exercise into a fast, consistent comparison.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a negotiation playbook
- Compare contracts against it with AI
- Report deviations clearly
- Keep the playbook current

## The Playbook

- Standard position for each key clause
- Acceptable fallback positions
- Deal-breakers
### Why
- It speeds every negotiation
- It keeps positions consistent
- It trains juniors

## Comparing with AI

- Feed the playbook and the contract
- Ask for a deviation report
- Rank deviations by importance
### The rule
- Every deviation gets reported
- Rank by risk and importance
- The lawyer decides the response

## The Playbook Prompt

```text
Compare this contract [paste] against my playbook [paste].
Report every deviation, quote the clause, state my standard
position, and rank the deviations by importance.
```
- A deviation per clause
- Standard position stated
- Ranked by importance

## Key Takeaways

- A playbook records standard positions and fallbacks
- AI compares a contract against the playbook fast
- Report every deviation, ranked by importance
- The lawyer decides the response to each deviation
- Keep the playbook current as the market shifts

## Practice Challenge

**Answer:** A — AI compares the contract to the playbook and reports deviations.

### Module 5 · Step 4: Obligations, Dates, and Deadlines

Contracts create obligations with dates: payments, deliveries, notices, renewals, and termination windows. Missing a date can cost a client dearly.

AI can extract every obligation and date into a tracker, but you verify the extraction against the text.

## Learning Objectives

- Extract obligations and dates
- Build an obligation tracker
- Set reminders for key dates
- Verify the extraction

## Why Dates Matter

- Payments, deliveries, and notices have deadlines
- Renewal and termination windows are easy to miss
- A missed date can mean a lost right
### Why track them
- Obligations outlive the signing
- Deadlines drive client risk
- A tracker prevents costly misses

## Building the Tracker

- Extract each obligation with its date and party
- Note the consequence of missing it
- Set reminders ahead of each date
### The rule
- Every obligation gets a row
- Note the consequence
- Reminders go to the client where appropriate

## The Obligations Prompt

```text
Extract every obligation and date from this contract [paste]
into a table: party, obligation, date or trigger, and the
consequence of a miss. Flag anything ambiguous.
```
- Party, obligation, date, consequence
- Flag ambiguity
- Verify against the text

## Key Takeaways

- Contracts create dated obligations that outlive signing
- Missing a date can mean losing a right
- Extract obligations into a tracker with consequences
- Set reminders ahead of each key date
- Verify the extraction against the contract text

## Practice Challenge

1. Extract the obligations and dates from a contract
2. Build a tracker with consequences
3. Set reminders for the key dates

**Example Quiz**

1. Why do dates matter?
- A) Missing one can mean losing a right

- C) They are optional
- D) They impress clients

**Answer:** A — Dated obligations like renewals and notices carry real consequences.

2. What goes in the tracker?
- A) Party, obligation, date, consequence
- B) Only the parties
- C) Only the price
- D) Only the term

**Answer:** A — Each obligation gets a row with its date and consequence.

3. What should you set for key dates?
- A) Reminders ahead of each date

4. What do you do with the extraction?
- A) Verify it against the text
- B) Trust it
- C) File it
- D) Sign it

**Answer:** A — Always verify the extracted obligations against the contract text.

### Module 6 · Step 5: The Lawyer's Sign-Off

AI can review a thousand contracts, but only a lawyer can sign off. The sign-off is the professional act that carries responsibility.

The sign-off pass confirms that flags were assessed, deviations resolved, and the final document matches the client's instructions.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the lawyer's sign-off duty
- Confirm flags were assessed
- Confirm deviations were resolved
- Match the document to client instructions

## The Sign-Off Duty

- Only a lawyer can approve the contract
- The sign-off carries responsibility
- AI cannot assume that duty
### Why
- Clients rely on professional judgment
- Regulators hold lawyers accountable
- The signature is human

## What the Sign-Off Confirms

- Every AI flag was assessed
- Every deviation was resolved or accepted
- The document matches the client's instructions
### The rule
- No flag left unassessed
- No deviation left unexplained
- No document signed blind

## The Sign-Off Prompt

```text
Prepare a sign-off checklist for a contract reviewed with AI:
confirm each flag was assessed, each deviation resolved, and
the document matches client instructions. Keep it short.
```
- Flags assessed
- Deviations resolved
- Instructions matched

## Key Takeaways

- Only a lawyer can sign off on a contract
- The sign-off is a professional act carrying responsibility
- Confirm every AI flag was assessed
- Confirm every deviation was resolved or accepted
- Never sign a document blind
