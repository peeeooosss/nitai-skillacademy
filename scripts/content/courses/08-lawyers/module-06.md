# Mission 6: Building an AI-Enabled Legal Practice

---
### Module 1 · The AI-Enabled Practice Mindset

An AI-enabled practice uses AI where it adds leverage - research, drafting, review, admin - while keeping judgment and relationships human.

The mindset is not about replacing lawyers. It is about giving lawyers back the hours that matter.

## Learning Objectives

- Adopt the AI-enabled practice mindset
- Identify where AI adds leverage
- Keep judgment and relationships human
- Commit to deliberate adoption

## Leverage, Not Replacement

- AI adds leverage to existing work
- It does not replace judgment or relationships
- It gives back hours for higher-value work
### Why
- The practice of law is judgment and trust
- AI handles the repetitive layer
- Deliberate adoption beats hype

## Where AI Adds Leverage

- Research and document review
- Drafting and summarising
- Admin, scheduling, and records
### The rule
- Start where AI is proven
- Keep humans on judgment
- Measure the hours returned

## The Mindset Prompt

```text
For a small law practice, identify where AI adds real
leverage and where it does not. Give a short list of tasks
to start with and the boundaries to keep.
```
- Where AI helps
- Where it does not
- Boundaries to keep

## Key Takeaways

- An AI-enabled practice uses AI for leverage, not replacement
- AI handles repetitive work; humans keep judgment and trust
- Start where AI is proven: research, review, drafting, admin
- Measure the hours returned
- Deliberate adoption beats hype

## Practice Challenge

1. List tasks where AI adds leverage in your practice
2. List tasks that must stay human
3. Choose one task to start with

**Example Quiz**

1. What does an AI-enabled practice do?
- A) Uses AI for leverage while keeping judgment human
- B) Replaces lawyers
- C) Ignores AI
- D) Automates everything

**Answer:** A — AI adds leverage; judgment and relationships stay human.

2. What does AI handle?
- A) Repetitive work like research, review, drafting, admin
- B) Judgment
- C) Relationships
- D) Ethics

**Answer:** A — AI handles the repetitive layer of legal work.

3. What should you measure?
- A) The hours returned
- B) The fee
- C) The font
- D) The word count

**Answer:** A — Measure the hours AI gives back to higher-value work.

4. What beats hype?
- A) Deliberate adoption
- B) Speed
- C) Cost
- D) Volume

**Answer:** A — Adopt AI deliberately, starting where it is proven.

### Module 2 · Step 1: Map Your Workflows

Before automating, map how work actually flows through your practice: intake, matter setup, research, drafting, review, and closure.

Mapping reveals where AI fits and where it does not, so adoption is targeted rather than random.

## Learning Objectives

- Map your practice workflows
- Identify AI-suitable steps
- Spot bottlenecks and repetition
- Prioritise where to start

## The Workflow Map

- Intake and conflict checks
- Matter setup and documents
- Research, drafting, and review
- Closure and billing
### Why
- Mapping shows the whole process
- It reveals repetition
- It targets AI where it helps

## Finding the Fits

- Mark repetitive, rule-based steps
- Mark steps needing judgment
- Prioritise the highest-volume repetition
### The rule
- Automate repetition
- Keep judgment human
- Start with the biggest win

## The Mapping Prompt

```text

intake to closure. Mark each step as AI-suitable (repetitive)
or human (judgment), and rank the AI opportunities.
```
- Steps from intake to closure
- AI-suitable vs human
- Ranked opportunities

## Key Takeaways

- Map workflows before automating anything
- Cover intake, matter setup, research, drafting, review, closure
- Mark repetitive, rule-based steps as AI-suitable
- Keep judgment steps human
- Start with the highest-volume repetition

## Practice Challenge

### Module 3 · Step 2: Automate the Repetitive

Repetitive work - intake forms, document assembly, reminders, records - is where automation returns the most hours for the least risk.

Automate one workflow at a time, test it, and keep a human check on the output.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify automation candidates
- Automate one workflow at a time
- Keep human checks in place
- Measure the time saved

## Candidates for Automation

- Intake forms and conflict check reminders
- Document assembly from templates
- Deadline and follow-up reminders
- Records and matter reporting
### Why
- These are high-volume and rule-based
- Errors are easy to spot
- Hours are reclaimed weekly

## One at a Time

- Automate a single workflow
- Test it before trusting it
- Keep a human check on output
### The rule
- Test first
- Human check stays
- Measure the hours saved

## The Automation Prompt

```text
Design an automation for [repetitive workflow] in a law
practice: the trigger, the steps, the output, and the human
check. Keep it simple and safe.
```
- Trigger, steps, output
- A human check
- Simple and safe

## Key Takeaways

- Repetitive work is where automation returns the most hours
- Candidates include intake, document assembly, reminders, records
- Automate one workflow at a time
- Keep a human check on every automated output
- Measure the hours saved

## Practice Challenge

3. Measure the hours it saves

### Module 4 · Step 3: Build Your Prompt and Precedent Library

An AI-enabled practice runs on reusable assets: prompts that work and precedents that are vetted. Build both and every matter gets faster and more consistent.

The library is a practice asset, like a forms book. Treat it as part of your professional capital.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a prompt library
- Maintain a vetted precedent library
- Organise assets by matter type
- Keep the library current

## Two Libraries

- **Prompt library:** prompts for recurring tasks
- **Precedent library:** vetted clauses and documents
- Both organised by matter type
### Why
- Reuse saves time and ensures consistency
- Vetted assets reduce risk
- Juniors get a head start

## Organising the Library

- Group by matter type and task
- Tag with jurisdiction and date
- Version and review regularly
### The rule
- Only vetted assets go in
- Tag jurisdiction and date
- Review as law changes

## The Library Prompt

```text
Help me build a prompt and precedent library for [practice
area]: categories, tagging fields, and a review schedule.
Include a template for saving prompts with context.
```
- Categories and tags
- A review schedule
- A saving template

## Key Takeaways

- An AI-enabled practice runs on reusable assets
- Build a prompt library and a vetted precedent library
- Organise both by matter type and task
- Tag assets with jurisdiction and date
- Review the library as the law changes

## Practice Challenge

1. Start a prompt library for recurring tasks
2. Collect vetted precedents by matter type
3. Set a review schedule

**Example Quiz**

- D) A case library and a statute library

**Answer:** A — Prompts for recurring tasks and vetted precedents form the library.

2. How should assets be organised?
- A) By matter type and task
- B) By date

- D) Randomly

**Answer:** A — Group assets by matter type and task for quick reuse.

3. What should assets be tagged with?
- A) Jurisdiction and date
- B) Font

**Answer:** A — Tag assets with jurisdiction and date to keep them reliable.

4. When should the library be reviewed?
- A) As the law changes
- B) Never

**Answer:** A — Review the library as the law changes to keep it current.

### Module 5 · Step 4: Pricing, Efficiency, and Client Value

AI changes the economics of legal work. If a task that took five hours now takes one, billing by the hour alone can punish efficiency.

Focus on client value: price for outcomes and expertise, and pass on some of the efficiency as better service.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand how AI changes legal economics
- Consider value-based pricing
- Pass efficiency to clients
- Keep trust through transparency

## The Economics Shift

- AI reduces time on repetitive tasks
- Hourly billing can penalise efficiency
- Clients increasingly expect the benefit
### Why
- Value comes from expertise, not hours
- Efficiency should serve clients too
- Transparency builds trust

## Pricing for Value

- Consider fixed fees and value-based pricing
- Communicate what AI changes about cost
- Pass on part of the efficiency
### The rule
- Price for value, not just time
- Be transparent with clients
- Keep quality the priority

## The Pricing Prompt

```text
Explain how AI efficiency affects pricing models for a
law practice: hourly vs fixed vs value-based, and how to
communicate the change to clients fairly.
```
- Compare pricing models
- Communication approach
- Fairness to clients

## Key Takeaways

- AI changes the economics of legal work
- Hourly billing can penalise efficiency
- Price for value and expertise, not just time
- Pass on part of the efficiency to clients
- Be transparent about what AI changes

## Practice Challenge

1. Review your pricing models
2. Decide how to share AI efficiency
3. Draft a client communication

**Example Quiz**

1. What does AI change?
- A) The economics of legal work
- B) The law
- C) The ethics
- D) The courts

**Answer:** A — AI reduces time on repetitive tasks, changing the economics.

2. What can hourly billing do?
- A) Penalise efficiency
- B) Reward quality
- C) Reduce risk
- D) Increase trust

**Answer:** A — If a task takes less time, hourly billing can punish efficiency.

3. What should you price for?
- A) Value and expertise
- B) Hours only
- C) Volume only
- D) Cost only

**Answer:** A — Price for value and expertise rather than time alone.

4. What should you do with efficiency?
- A) Pass part on to clients
- B) Keep it secret
- C) Hide it
- D) Ignore it

**Answer:** A — Share part of the efficiency with clients and be transparent.

### Module 6 · Step 5: The 90-Day Adoption Plan

Adoption works best as a plan, not a scramble. A 90-day plan moves from one proven use case to a governed, measured practice.

Start small, prove value, then expand - with governance keeping pace.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a 90-day AI adoption plan
- Start with one proven use case
- Expand with governance
- Measure value and risk

## The Three Phases

- **Days 1-30:** one use case, one tool, measure the hours
- **Days 31-60:** expand to a second workflow, set governance
- **Days 61-90:** formalise policy, train the team, review
### Why
- Small starts reduce risk
- Proven value justifies expansion
- Governance keeps it safe

## Measuring Value and Risk

- Track hours saved and quality
- Track errors and near-misses
- Adjust the plan from evidence
### The rule
- Measure both value and risk
- Expand on evidence
- Governance keeps pace

## The Adoption Prompt

```text
Build a 90-day AI adoption plan for a law practice: one
use case in month 1, expansion and governance in month 2,
policy and training in month 3. Include metrics for value
and risk.
```
- One use case to start
- Governance in month 2
- Metrics for value and risk

## Key Takeaways

- Adoption works best as a 90-day plan
- Start with one use case and measure the hours
- Expand with governance keeping pace
- Formalise policy and train the team
- Measure both value and risk, and adjust from evidence

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your 90-day adoption plan
2. Choose the first use case and metrics
3. Set governance milestones

**Example Quiz**

1. How should adoption start?
- A) With one use case and one tool
- B) With everything at once
- C) With a big budget
- D) With a new team

**Answer:** A — Start small with one proven use case and measure the hours.

2. What happens in month 2?
- A) Expansion with governance
- B) Nothing
- C) A full rollout
- D) A rebrand

**Answer:** A — Expand to a second workflow while setting governance.

3. What happens in month 3?
- A) Policy, training, and review
- B) A holiday
- C) A merger
- D) A fee rise

**Answer:** A — Formalise policy, train the team, and review progress.

4. What do you measure?
- A) Value and risk
- B) Only value
- C) Only risk
- D) Neither

**Answer:** A — Track both hours saved and errors or near-misses.
