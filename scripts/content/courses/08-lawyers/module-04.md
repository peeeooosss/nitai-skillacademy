# Mission 4: AI as a Case Tool: Fact Patterns & Research

---
### Module 1 · From Facts to Issues: Building the Frame

Legal analysis begins with facts. The skill is moving from a messy fact pattern to a clear set of legal issues - and AI can help you organise, but not decide.

Build the frame: chronology, parties, and the questions that follow. Everything else in a case flows from this frame.

## Learning Objectives

- Move from facts to legal issues
- Build a chronology and party map
- Frame the questions for research
- Keep analysis grounded in facts

## Facts First

- Assemble the facts before the law
- Build a chronology of events
- Map the parties and their relationships
### Why
- Facts determine which law applies
- A clear chronology reveals gaps
- Party relationships shape claims

## Framing the Issues

- Turn the facts into legal questions
- Separate the issues from each other
- Identify what facts are missing
### The rule
- One issue at a time
- Note the facts each issue needs
- Research follows the issues

## The Framing Prompt

```text
From these facts [paste], build a chronology, a party map,
and a list of legal issues to research. For each issue,
note the facts needed and the facts still missing.
```
- Chronology and party map
- Issues with needed facts
- Missing facts flagged

## Key Takeaways

- Legal analysis begins with facts, not law
- Build a chronology and a party map first
- Turn the facts into discrete legal issues
- Note the facts each issue needs and what is missing
- Research follows the framed issues

## Practice Challenge

1. Take a fact pattern and build a chronology
2. Map the parties
3. List the legal issues and missing facts

**Example Quiz**

1. What comes first in analysis?
- A) The facts
- B) The law
- C) The conclusion
- D) The fee

**Answer:** A — Facts determine which law applies and frame the issues.

2. What should you build first?
- A) A chronology and party map
- B) A memo
- C) A brief
- D) A fee note

**Answer:** A — A chronology and party map organise the fact pattern.

3. How should issues be handled?
- A) One at a time, with the facts each needs
- B) All at once
- C) After research
- D) Without facts

**Answer:** A — Separate issues and note the facts each requires.

4. What does research follow?
- A) The framed issues
- B) The fee
- C) The client's mood
- D) The weather

**Answer:** A — Research follows the legal issues framed from the facts.

### Module 2 · Step 1: Organise the Fact Pattern

A disorganised fact pattern hides the issues. Organising it - chronologically, by party, and by transaction - reveals the structure of the case.

AI can help you reorganise a messy narrative into usable tables and timelines, which you then verify.

## Learning Objectives

- Organise facts chronologically and by party
- Build timelines and tables
- Spot contradictions and gaps
- Verify the organisation against the source

## Ways to Organise

- Chronology: what happened when
- Party map: who did what to whom
- Transaction map: the deals and their terms
### Why
- Structure reveals the case
- It exposes contradictions
- It highlights gaps

## Finding Contradictions and Gaps

- Look for dates that conflict
- Look for events without evidence
- Look for missing links in the chain
### The rule
- Contradictions are leads
- Gaps need evidence
- Verify against the source

## The Organisation Prompt

```text
Organise this fact pattern [paste] into a chronological
timeline, a party map, and a transaction map. Flag any
contradictions, gaps, or events lacking evidence.
```
- Timeline, party map, transaction map
- Flag contradictions and gaps
- Verify against source

## Key Takeaways

- Organise facts chronologically, by party, and by transaction
- Structure reveals the case and exposes contradictions
- Contradictions and gaps are leads to chase
- AI can reorganise a messy narrative fast
- Verify the organisation against the source

## Practice Challenge

1. Organise a fact pattern three ways
2. Flag contradictions and gaps
3. Verify the timeline against the source documents

**Example Quiz**

1. Which three ways organise facts?
- A) Chronology, party, transaction
- B) Length, tone, font
- C) Fee, cost, price
- D) Draft, edit, file

**Answer:** A — Chronology, party map, and transaction map structure the case.

2. What do contradictions reveal?
- A) Leads to chase
- B) The conclusion
- C) The fee
- D) The verdict

**Answer:** A — Contradictions are leads that may change the analysis.

3. What do gaps need?
- A) Evidence
- B) Guesswork
- C) Assumptions
- D) A fee

**Answer:** A — Gaps in the chain need evidence before you rely on them.

4. What do you do with the organisation?
- A) Verify it against the source
- B) Trust it
- C) File it
- D) Publish it

**Answer:** A — Verify AI's organisation against the underlying documents.

### Module 3 · Step 2: Issue Spotting with AI

Issue spotting is where junior lawyers learn and where AI can help - as a checklist of possibilities, not a conclusion. AI can suggest issues you might miss; you decide which are live.

Treat every suggested issue as a candidate to test against the facts and law.

## Learning Objectives

- Use AI to generate candidate issues
- Test issues against the facts
- Prioritise the live issues
- Avoid chasing irrelevant issues

## Generating Candidates

- Ask for all possible issues on the facts
- Include issues across practice areas
- Ask which facts support each issue
### Why
- AI suggests what you might miss
- Breadth first, then depth
- You filter to the live issues

## Testing and Prioritising

- Test each issue against the facts
- Rank by strength and relevance
- Discard issues without factual support
### The rule
- Candidates, not conclusions
- Facts decide which are live
- Focus on the strong issues

## The Issue-Spot Prompt

```text
From these facts [paste], list all potential legal issues,
with the facts supporting each and the area of law. Then
rank them by strength. Treat all as candidates to verify.
```
- All potential issues
- Supporting facts and law
- Ranked by strength

## Key Takeaways

- AI can suggest candidate issues you might miss
- Treat every suggestion as a candidate to test
- Test each issue against the facts and law
- Rank issues by strength and relevance
- Discard issues without factual support

## Practice Challenge

1. Ask AI for candidate issues on a fact pattern
2. Test each against the facts
3. Rank and discard the unsupported

**Example Quiz**

1. What does AI provide in issue spotting?
- A) Candidate issues to test
- B) Final conclusions
- C) Binding rulings
- D) Verdicts

**Answer:** A — AI suggests candidates; you decide which are live.

2. What decides if an issue is live?
- A) The facts and law
- B) The AI's confidence
- C) The fee
- D) The client's wish

**Answer:** A — Test each candidate against the facts and the law.

3. How should issues be ranked?
- A) By strength and relevance
- B) Alphabetically
- C) By page
- D) Randomly

**Answer:** A — Rank issues by strength and relevance to focus the work.

4. What do you do with unsupported issues?
- A) Discard them
- B) Argue them anyway
- C) File them
- D) Publish them

**Answer:** A — Discard issues without factual support.

### Module 4 · Step 3: Research the Issues

With the live issues framed, research becomes targeted. AI helps you find the authorities for each issue; you verify and read them.

Research follows the issue list, not a vague topic. That focus is what makes it efficient.

## Learning Objectives

- Research each framed issue
- Find authorities per issue
- Verify and read the authorities
- Record the research

## Targeted Research

- One issue at a time
- Find the authorities for that issue
- Note how each authority applies to the facts
### Why
- Focus prevents wasted research
- Authorities map to issues
- Application is the lawyer's work

## Finding and Reading Authorities

- Ask AI for leading authorities per issue
- Verify each in a database
- Read the key cases in full
### The rule
- Verify every authority
- Read before relying
- Record the citation

## The Research Prompt

```text
For this issue [issue] on these facts [paste], list the
leading authorities and how each applies. Mark all as
unverified leads and note which I should read in full.
```
- Authorities per issue
- Application to the facts
- Flag what to read in full

## Key Takeaways

- Research each framed issue one at a time
- Find authorities per issue, not by vague topic
- Verify every authority and read the key cases
- Note how each authority applies to the facts
- Record the citation for every authority

## Practice Challenge

1. Research one issue from your list
2. Find and verify the authorities
3. Note their application to the facts

**Example Quiz**

1. How should research proceed?
- A) One issue at a time
- B) All issues at once
- C) By topic
- D) Randomly

**Answer:** A — Targeted research follows the framed issue list.

2. What do you do with each authority?
- A) Verify and read it
- B) Cite it directly
- C) Trust the summary
- D) Skip it

**Answer:** A — Verify every authority in a database and read the key cases.

3. What is the lawyer's work?
- A) Applying authority to the facts
- B) Listing citations
- C) Finding leads
- D) Formatting

**Answer:** A — Application of law to facts is the lawyer's core work.

4. What do you record?
- A) The citation for every authority

**Answer:** A — Record the citation for every authority you rely on.

### Module 5 · Step 4: Build the Argument Outline

An argument outline sets out each point, its authority, and its application to the facts. AI can scaffold the outline; you supply the reasoning.

The outline is where the case takes shape: issue, rule, application, conclusion.

## Learning Objectives

- Build an argument outline
- Use issue-rule-application-conclusion
- Attach authorities to each point
- Order points by strength

## The IRAC Structure

- **Issue:** the question
- **Rule:** the applicable law
- **Application:** the law applied to the facts
- **Conclusion:** the result
### Why
- It is the standard reasoning structure
- It makes arguments clear
- It exposes weak points

## Ordering and Authorities

- Attach the authority to each point
- Order points by strength
- Lead with the strongest argument
### The rule
- Every point needs authority
- Strongest first
- Weak points get addressed, not hidden

## The Outline Prompt

```text
Build an argument outline for [issue] using IRAC: each point
with its rule, application to these facts [paste], authority,
and conclusion. Order by strength.
```
- IRAC per point
- Authority attached
- Ordered by strength

## Key Takeaways

- An argument outline sets out each point, authority, and application
- Use issue, rule, application, conclusion
- Attach an authority to every point
- Order points by strength and lead with the strongest
- Address weak points rather than hiding them

## Practice Challenge

1. Build an IRAC outline for your case
2. Attach authorities to each point
3. Order the points by strength

**Example Quiz**

**Answer:** A — IRAC is the standard legal reasoning structure.

2. What must every point have?

**Answer:** A — Attach an authority to every point in the outline.

3. How are points ordered?
- A) By strength, strongest first
- B) Alphabetically

- D) Randomly

**Answer:** A — Lead with the strongest argument and order by strength.

4. What do you do with weak points?
- A) Address them
- B) Hide them
- C) Delete them

**Answer:** A — Address weak points rather than hiding them.

### Module 6 · Step 5: Anticipate the Other Side

A strong case anticipates the counterarguments. AI is useful for generating the other side's best points, so you can prepare a response.

Steel-man the opposition: ask for the strongest version of their argument, not a straw man.

## Learning Objectives

- Anticipate counterarguments
- Generate the strongest opposing case
- Prepare responses
- Strengthen your own argument

## Why Anticipate

- The other side will make their best case
- Anticipating strengthens your position
- It prepares you for the hearing
### Why
- Surprises are costly
- Preparation wins arguments
- It tests your reasoning

## Steel-Manning the Opposition

- Ask for the strongest opposing argument
- Identify the facts that support them
- Prepare a response to each point
### The rule
- Steel-man, do not straw-man
- Address the strongest points
- Test your own case against them

## The Opposition Prompt

```text
Act as opposing counsel. Give the strongest arguments against
my position on [issue] with these facts [paste]. Then list
the best response to each. Be rigorous, not a straw man.
```
- Strongest opposing case
- Facts supporting them
- Best responses

## Key Takeaways

- A strong case anticipates the counterarguments
- Ask AI for the strongest version of the opposing case
- Steel-man the opposition rather than straw-manning
- Prepare a response to each opposing point
- Test your own reasoning against their best case

## Practice Challenge

1. Ask AI for the strongest opposing argument
2. List your responses to each point
3. Revise your outline where their case is stronger

**Example Quiz**

1. Why anticipate counterarguments?

**Answer:** A — Anticipating the other side strengthens your case and preparation.

2. What should you ask AI for?
- A) The strongest opposing argument

- D) A summary

**Answer:** A — Ask for the strongest version of the opposing case.

3. What is steel-manning?
- A) Presenting the strongest opposing case

**Answer:** A — Steel-manning means presenting the opposition at its strongest.

4. What do you prepare for each opposing point?

- D) A footnote

**Answer:** A — Prepare a response to each of the strongest opposing points.
