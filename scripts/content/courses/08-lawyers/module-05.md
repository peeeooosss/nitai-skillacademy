### Module 1 · The Duty of Confidentiality

Confidentiality is one of the lawyer's oldest duties. It survives the engagement and covers almost everything a client tells you.

AI tools create new ways to breach it accidentally. The rule: assume anything you paste could be disclosed, and act accordingly.

## Learning Objectives

- State the duty of confidentiality
- Understand the risk AI tools create
- Protect client information in workflows
- Know the exceptions

## The Duty

- Confidentiality covers almost everything the client tells you
- It survives the end of the engagement
- It applies to staff and tools alike
### Why
- Trust is the foundation of the relationship
- Breach can harm the client and your practice
- Regulators enforce it

## AI's New Risk

- Pasting client data into a tool may disclose it
- Free tools may train on your input
- Data can be stored or reviewed
### The rule
- Assume pasted data could be disclosed
- Use approved tools only
- When unsure, do not paste

## The Confidentiality Prompt

```text
Explain the duty of confidentiality for lawyers and the
specific risks AI tools create. List practical steps to
protect client information when using AI.
```
- The duty and its scope
- AI-specific risks
- Practical protections

## Key Takeaways

- Confidentiality covers almost everything a client tells you
- The duty survives the end of the engagement
- AI tools create new, accidental breach risks
- Assume anything pasted could be disclosed
- Use approved tools only; when unsure, do not paste

## Practice Challenge

1. List the client information you handle
2. Identify where AI tools could expose it
3. Write your protection steps

**Example Quiz**

1. What does confidentiality cover?
- A) Almost everything the client tells you
- B) Only secrets
- C) Only documents
- D) Only fees

**Answer:** A — Confidentiality covers nearly all client information and survives the engagement.

2. What risk do AI tools create?
- A) Accidental disclosure of client data
- B) No risk
- C) Only cost
- D) Only delay

**Answer:** A — Pasting client data into a tool can disclose it.

3. What should you assume about pasted data?
- A) It could be disclosed
- B) It is private
- C) It is encrypted
- D) It is deleted

**Answer:** A — Assume anything you paste could be disclosed, and act accordingly.

4. When unsure about a tool?
- A) Do not paste
- B) Paste anyway
- C) Paste a sample
- D) Ask the AI

**Answer:** A — When unsure, do not paste client information.

### Module 2 · Privilege and AI Tools

Legal professional privilege protects certain communications from disclosure. Whether AI tool use preserves privilege depends on the tool, the vendor, and the jurisdiction.

Privilege is easily lost by careless tool use. Understand the rules before you rely on them.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand legal professional privilege
- Know how AI use can affect privilege
- Assess vendor data practices
- Protect privilege in workflows

## What Privilege Protects

- Confidential legal advice communications
- Litigation communications in some contexts
- It can be lost by waiver or disclosure
### Why
- Privilege protects the client's position
- Loss can be damaging
- Rules vary by jurisdiction

## AI and Privilege

- Third-party tools can affect privilege
- Vendor data practices matter
- Jurisdictions differ on AI and privilege
### The rule
- Check vendor data handling
- Use tools that preserve confidentiality
- Get advice where uncertain

## The Privilege Prompt

```text
Explain how using AI tools may affect legal professional
privilege, what vendor data practices matter, and the steps
to protect privilege. Note that rules vary by jurisdiction.
```
- How AI affects privilege
- Vendor practices to check
- Protection steps

## Key Takeaways

- Privilege protects confidential legal advice communications
- Privilege can be lost by careless tool use
- Vendor data practices affect whether privilege is preserved
- Jurisdictions differ on AI and privilege
- Get advice where privilege questions are uncertain

## Practice Challenge

1. Review your tool's data practices
2. Note how it handles confidentiality
3. Flag privilege questions for advice

**Example Quiz**

**Answer:** A — Privilege protects confidential legal advice communications.

2. How can privilege be lost?
- A) By careless tool use or waiver

**Answer:** A — Careless disclosure or waiver can destroy privilege.

3. What matters about a vendor?

### Module 3 · Data Protection and Client Consent

Data protection law adds another layer: personal data has its own rules, and clients may need to consent to certain processing.

Understand what data you hold, why, and whether AI processing requires consent or a safeguard.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand data protection duties
- Identify personal data in your files
- Assess when consent is needed
- Apply safeguards

## Data Protection Basics

- Personal data has its own legal rules
- You must have a lawful basis to process it
- Clients may need to consent to some processing
### Why
- Breach carries regulatory penalties
- It protects client rights
- It overlaps with confidentiality

## Consent and Safeguards

- Identify the personal data you hold
- Determine the lawful basis
- Apply safeguards like minimisation and anonymisation
### The rule
- Minimise the data you process
- Anonymise where possible
- Get consent where required

## The Data Protection Prompt

```text
For a law practice using AI, explain data protection duties:
what counts as personal data, when consent is needed, and
the safeguards to apply. Note jurisdictional variation.
```
- What is personal data
- When consent is needed
- Safeguards to apply

## Key Takeaways

- Personal data has its own legal rules beyond confidentiality
- You need a lawful basis to process personal data
- Clients may need to consent to certain processing
- Minimise and anonymise data where possible
- Breach carries regulatory penalties

## Practice Challenge

1. Identify the personal data in a sample file
2. Determine the lawful basis for processing
3. List the safeguards you will apply

**Example Quiz**

**Answer:** A — Data protection law governs personal data separately from confidentiality.

2. What do you need to process personal data?

**Answer:** A — Processing personal data requires a lawful basis.

3. What safeguard reduces risk?

### Module 4 · Vendor Risk and Tool Approval

Every AI tool is a third party with access to your data. Vendor risk assessment decides which tools are safe enough for client work.

Approve tools deliberately: know where data goes, how it is stored, and what the vendor commits to.

## Learning Objectives

- Assess vendor risk
- Evaluate data handling and security
- Approve tools deliberately
- Review vendors over time

## What to Assess

- Where data is stored and processed
- Whether inputs train the model
- Security, retention, and deletion
- The vendor's contractual commitments
### Why
- Vendors are a data risk
- Poor practices can breach confidentiality
- Contracts define liability

## Approving Tools

- Require no-training and deletion commitments
- Prefer enterprise tiers with safeguards
- Document the approval decision
### The rule
- No client data without approval
- Document why a tool is approved
- Re-review periodically

## The Vendor Prompt

```text
List the questions a law firm should ask when assessing an
AI vendor: data location, training use, retention, security,
and contractual commitments. Note what answers are acceptable.
```
- Data, training, retention, security
- Contractual commitments
- Acceptable answers

## Key Takeaways

- Every AI tool is a third party with access to your data
- Assess data location, training use, retention, and security
- Require no-training and deletion commitments
- Document the approval decision
- Re-review vendors periodically

## Practice Challenge

1. Assess one AI tool you use
2. Ask the vendor's data-handling questions
3. Document whether it is approved for client data

**Example Quiz**

1. What is a vendor risk?

- D) A filing

**Answer:** A — Every tool is a third party that may access client data.

2. What commitment should you require?
- A) No training on your data and deletion
- B) A discount
- C) A logo
- D) A demo

**Answer:** A — Require no-training and deletion commitments from vendors.

3. What should you document?
- A) The approval decision and why
- B) The fee
- C) The weather
- D) The font

**Answer:** A — Document why a tool is approved for client work.

4. When should you re-review a vendor?
- A) Periodically
- B) Never

- D) Only after a breach

**Answer:** A — Re-review vendor risk periodically as practices change.

### Module 5 · Your Firm's AI Use Policy

A firm AI policy turns governance into daily practice: what tools are approved, what data may be used, and who supervises.

Write it clearly, keep it short, and make it enforceable.

## Learning Objectives

- Draft a firm AI use policy
- Define approved tools and data rules
- Set supervision and review duties
- Keep it current

## What the Policy Covers

- Approved tools and prohibited uses
- What data may and may not be entered
- Verification and review requirements
- Who supervises AI use
### Why
- Policy makes governance practical
- It protects the firm and clients
- It settles questions in advance

## Making It Enforceable

- Plain language and concrete examples
- Clear consequences for breach
- Regular training and review
### The rule
- Short enough to be read
- Specific enough to be followed
- Reviewed as tools change

## The Policy Prompt

## Key Takeaways

- A firm AI policy turns governance into daily practice

- Review the policy as tools change

### Module 6 · Accountability and Supervision

The buck stops with the lawyer. AI can assist, but a qualified lawyer must supervise and take responsibility for every output.

Supervision means reviewing the work, not just running the tool. Set who reviews what, and how.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand supervisory responsibility
- Set review duties for AI output
- Keep accountability human
- Train the team on supervision

## The Responsibility

- A qualified lawyer supervises all AI output
- The firm is accountable for the work
- AI cannot assume responsibility
### Why
- Clients rely on professional judgment
- Regulators hold lawyers accountable
- Supervision protects quality

## Setting Review Duties

- Define who reviews which outputs
- Require verification before reliance
- Document the review
### The rule
- Every output has a reviewer
- Verification precedes reliance
- Review is documented

## The Supervision Prompt

```text
Design a supervision framework for AI use in a law firm:
who reviews what, verification requirements, documentation,
and training. Keep accountability with qualified lawyers.
```
- Who reviews what
- Verification and documentation
- Training and accountability

## Key Takeaways

- A qualified lawyer must supervise every AI output
- The firm is accountable for the work product
- Define who reviews which outputs
- Verification must precede reliance
- Document the review and train the team

## Practice Challenge

1. Map who reviews which AI outputs
2. Write your verification requirement
3. Plan a supervision training session

**Example Quiz**

1. Who supervises AI output?
- A) A qualified lawyer
- B) The vendor
- C) The AI
- D) The client

**Answer:** A — A qualified lawyer must supervise and take responsibility for AI output.

2. What must precede reliance?
- A) Verification
- B) Filing
- C) Billing
- D) Signing

**Answer:** A — Verify AI output before relying on it.

3. What should be documented?
- A) The review
- B) The fee
- C) The font
- D) The weather

**Answer:** A — Document the review to show supervision occurred.

4. Can AI assume responsibility?
- A) No
- B) Yes
- C) Sometimes
- D) If approved

**Answer:** A — Accountability stays with qualified lawyers, not AI.
