### Module 1 · Three Sources, One Lens

Farming advice is only as good as the information behind it. The best habit combines three sources: **official advisories**, **farm apps**, and **general AI assistants**. No single source is enough - official data is reliable but scattered; AI is instant but needs checking. Together they cover both speed and trust.

AI is a lens: it takes data - weather, crop conditions, your description - and turns it into simple, actionable advice for your farm. The official sources supply the truth; AI translates it into a decision you can act on.

## Learning Objectives

- Name the three sources of good farming advice
- Explain why no single source is enough
- Describe AI as a lens that turns data into decisions
- Combine sources for reliable, actionable advice

## The Three Sources

- **Official advisories** - governments and institutions publish zone-specific crop and weather advisories
- **Farm apps** - free apps designed for Indian agriculture (Kisan Suvidha, Pusa Krishi, mKisan, IMD apps, district agri portals)
- **General AI assistants** - interpret the above and answer your plain-language questions instantly
### Why all three
- Official data is reliable but scattered across many places
- AI is instant but needs checking against official facts
- Together they give you both speed and trust

## AI as a Lens

```text
AI turns data into a decision:
1. Input: weather forecast + soil type + your crop description
2. AI interprets: what it means for YOUR field
3. Output: a plain-language decision - sow, spray, harvest, or hold
4. You verify: check the official source before acting
AI is the lens, not the source of truth.
```
- The lens focuses scattered information into one clear decision
- You still verify important decisions with an official
- The three sources together produce advice you can trust

## No Single Source Is Enough

- Relying on AI alone risks acting on an unverified claim
- Relying on official data alone means hunting through scattered portals
- Relying on farm apps alone misses the flexibility of a conversation
### The practical combination
- Use AI to understand and plan; use official sources to confirm
- Use farm apps for the official advisories themselves
- The combination is the habit that keeps your farm safe and informed

## Key Takeaways

- Three sources: official advisories, farm apps, and AI
- No single source is enough - combine for speed and trust
- AI is a lens that turns data into actionable decisions
- Official data is reliable but scattered; AI is instant but needs checking
- AI for understanding, officials for confirmation

### Module 2 · Weather That Speaks Your Language

Weather apps give raw forecasts; AI gives decisions. Instead of hunting through a dashboard of numbers, you ask a plain question and get a plain answer: will there be rain days or heat days, and how should that change your spraying schedule?

AI turns the forecast into decisions - when to sow, spray, harvest, or hold - for YOUR area, not a state-level summary. The key is to ask about your taluk or district and your specific crop.

## Learning Objectives

- Ask for weather in simple language for your own area
- Turn a forecast into a decision: sow, spray, harvest, or hold
- Use weather questions to plan spraying and harvest timing
- Explain why area-specific weather matters more than state summaries

## From Forecast to Decision

- Ask: 'For [your taluk/district], this week's weather - will there be rain days or heat days? How should this affect my spraying schedule?'
- Ask: 'It looks like thunderstorms in three days. Should I harvest the paddy before or wait?'
- Ask: 'This monsoon seems weak this year. Which crops cope better with light rain?'
### Why decisions beat data
- A forecast of '80% rain Thursday' becomes 'spray Tuesday morning instead'
- AI translates the numbers into the action your field needs
- Always ask about your area - a state summary is too broad

## The Area-Specific Weather Prompt

```text
The weekly weather question:
Give me this week's weather picture for [your district],
in simple language, and what it means for my [crop].
Then ask:
- Which day is best for spraying?
- Should I harvest now or wait?
- Any heat or rain risk I should prepare for?
```
- Run this every Sunday as your weekly check
- Save the answer in your notes
- Verify important decisions with the official weather advisory

## Planning Spraying and Harvest

- Spray timing matters: rain can wash away a costly treatment
- Ask AI to pick the best day and safest time around the forecast
- For harvest, ask whether rain in three days means cut now or wait
### The verification step
- Confirm the forecast with an official source like IMD or Kisan Suvidha
- Check the dose and product with your KVK before spraying
- The AI decides the timing; the official confirms the treatment

## Key Takeaways

- AI turns weather forecasts into decisions, not dashboards
- Ask about your district, not a state-level summary
- Weather questions plan spraying, harvest, and sowing timing
- Run the weekly weather check every Sunday
- Verify forecast and treatment with official sources

### Module 3 · Soil: Know Your Land

Soil decides what grows, how much water you need, and how fertiliser behaves. Knowing your soil type changes almost every farming decision, yet many farmers have never had it explained in simple words. AI can do exactly that - and turn it into a crop plan.

Learn your soil type - black (regur), red, alluvial, loam, sandy, or saline - by observing where it gets sticky in rain, how fast it dries, and how water drains. Then ask AI what that means for your land.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify your soil type by simple observation
- Ask AI to explain soil terms like pH, N-P-K, and organic matter
- Match crops to your soil and water situation
- Treat the official soil health card as the authority

## Knowing Your Soil Type

- Observe: where does it get sticky in rain? How fast does it dry? How does water drain?
- Types: black (regur), red, alluvial, loam, sandy, saline
- Ask AI: 'How do I test my soil simply as a small farmer? What do pH, N-P-K, and organic matter mean - in simple words?'
### Why soil matters
- Soil decides what grows, how much water you need, and how fertiliser behaves
- Knowing your type changes almost every decision
- It also tells you which crops to avoid

## From Soil to Crop Choice

```text
The soil-to-crop question:
1. Describe your soil to AI (sticky when wet? dries fast? drains well?)
2. Ask: what does this soil type mean for sowing and fertiliser?
3. Ask: what grows best in [your soil] with [your water]?
4. Compare 2-3 crop options
5. Confirm with your official soil health card
```
- Example: 'What grows best in red soil with good drainage and no canal water?'
- The official soil health card is the authority - AI explains it
- Match crops to both soil type and water availability

## The Soil Health Card

- Your agri department issues a soil health card - it is the official truth about your soil
- AI explains what the card's pH, N-P-K, and organic matter values mean in simple words
- Use the card plus AI to plan fertiliser without waste
### The cost saving
- Matched fertiliser reduces waste and cost
- Organic matter advice can improve water retention
- A little soil knowledge pays back every season

## Key Takeaways

- Soil decides what grows, water needs, and fertiliser behaviour
- Identify your soil by stickiness, drying speed, and drainage
- Ask AI to explain pH, N-P-K, and organic matter in simple words
- Match crops to soil type and water availability
- The official soil health card is the authority - AI explains it

### Module 4 · Building a Season Crop Plan With AI

A good season plan answers five questions: **what crop, what land, what cost, what care, what risk.** AI can produce a first draft of all five in minutes, comparing 2-3 realistic options side by side. Then your judgement and the agri officer's verification turn the draft into a plan.

The key is to ask for a comparison, not a single recommendation, and to follow up until you have a usable plan. The follow-up questions - about fertiliser, pests, and care - are what turn one answer into a full season plan.

## Learning Objectives

- Run a season-plan prompt that compares 2-3 crop options
- Ask for investment, labour, yield, profit, water, and risk
- Use follow-up questions for fertiliser and pest plans
- Write a one-page season plan

## The Season-Plan Prompt

```text
Act as an agricultural advisor for [district], [season].
I have [acres] of [soil type] land with [irrigation].
What should I sow this season? Compare 2-3 options:
investment, labour, expected yield, profit, water need,
and risks. Keep it practical for a small farmer.
```
- Asking for a comparison gives you real choices, not one guess
- The six factors cover money, effort, water, and risk together
- Practical-for-a-small-farmer keeps the advice realistic

## Follow-Up Questions

- After the first answer, ask: 'What fertiliser plan for option 2?'
- Ask: 'What are the main pests for this crop and the first sign to watch?'
- Ask: 'What is the water schedule for this crop through the season?'
### Why follow-ups matter
- One answer covers crop choice; follow-ups cover the whole season
- They turn a recommendation into a care calendar
- Do not stop at the first reply - the plan is in the follow-ups

## The One-Page Season Plan

- Record four things: crop choice, input cost, care calendar, top risk
- Keep it to one page - a plan you can glance at is a plan you will follow
- Take it to your agri officer for confirmation before you sow
### The habit to build
- Ask with details, get the decision, verify officially, then act
- A written plan beats a remembered one
- Reuse last year's plan as the starting draft this year

## Key Takeaways

- Ask for a comparison of 2-3 options, not one recommendation
- The prompt covers investment, labour, yield, profit, water, and risk
- Follow-up questions build fertiliser, pest, and water plans
- Write a one-page plan: crop, cost, care calendar, top risk
- Verify the plan with your agri officer before sowing

### Module 5 · The Check-Twice Rule

Never apply pesticides, fertilisers, or treat disease based on one AI answer alone. This is the single most important safety habit in the whole course - a wrong dose can cost a whole season, and a wrong product can harm your family and your soil.

The rule in one line: **AI for speed and understanding; official sources for the final yes.** Cross-check with your local KVK (Krishi Vigyan Kendra), district agriculture office, or a trusted agronomy app before applying anything.

## Learning Objectives

- Apply the check-twice rule before any chemical or treatment
- Identify your official verification sources (KVK, agri office, apps)
- Confirm product names, doses, and withdrawal periods
- Save your local KVK or agri officer's number

## The Rule in One Line

- **AI for speed and understanding; official sources for the final yes**
- Cross-check with KVK, the district agriculture office, or a trusted agronomy app
- Confirm product names, doses, and withdrawal periods before applying
### Why a single answer is not enough
- AI can be wrong, outdated, or too general for your field
- A wrong dose can cost a whole season
- The check-twice rule is cheap; a mistake is expensive

## Using Photo Tools for Disease

```text
The safe disease-check flow:
1. Take a clear photo of the affected plant part
2. Use a photo-based advisory tool (plantix-style or official)
3. Ask AI to explain the likely cause and options
4. Confirm with KVK or agri officer for serious outbreaks
5. Apply the confirmed treatment at the confirmed dose
```
- Photo tools trained on crop images help detect disease
- AI explains the options in simple words
- An expert confirms before you treat a serious outbreak

## Saving Your Verification Line

- Write your local KVK or agri officer's number into your phone notes now
- Save the Kisan Call Centre number too: 1800-180-1551
- Use them to verify any AI advice about money or chemicals
### The habit that protects every season
- Never act on one AI answer for chemicals
- Confirm the final decision with an official
- The rule is simple, and it saves crops, money, and health

## Key Takeaways

- Never act on one AI answer for pesticides or fertilisers
- AI for speed and understanding; officials for the final yes
- Confirm product names, doses, and withdrawal periods
- Use photo tools for disease, then confirm with an expert
- Save your KVK or agri officer's number in your phone

### Module 6 · From Forecast to Field Decisions

Putting it all together, an AI-powered crop season runs on one repeated habit: **ask with details, get the decision, check with an official source, then act.** Every stage of the season uses the same habit - before sowing, during growth, and at spraying and harvest time.

Before sowing you combine the weather outlook, soil confirmation, and an AI crop comparison, then discuss with the agri officer. During growth you ask about leaf colour, pest signs, and irrigation, confirming serious treatments with an official. At spraying and harvest you plan around the forecast and verify doses.

## Learning Objectives

- Apply the repeated habit across the whole season
- Plan before sowing using weather, soil, and crop comparison
- Monitor during growth with AI and official confirmation
- Plan spraying and harvest around the forecast

## Before Sowing

- Get the seasonal weather outlook for your area
- Confirm your soil type and its implications
- Run the AI crop comparison for 2-3 options
- Discuss the plan with your agri officer before sowing
### Why preparation pays
- A good sowing decision sets up the whole season
- Weather, soil, and crop choice are connected decisions
- The agri officer's confirmation closes the loop

## During Growth and at Harvest

```text
The season-long habit:
1. Before sowing: weather + soil + crop comparison -> officer
2. During growth: ask AI about leaf colour, pests, irrigation
3. On a pest alert: photo + AI + official confirmation
4. At spraying: plan the day and time around the forecast, verify dose
5. At harvest: ask now-or-wait using the forecast
Ask with details, get the decision, check officially, then act.
```
- The same habit repeats at every stage
- Serious treatments always get an official confirmation
- AI handles the timing and understanding; officials confirm

## The Repeating Habit

- Each step is the same: ask with details, get the decision, check with an official source, then act
- Write the habit somewhere visible - it is the whole method
- By the end of the season, the habit runs itself
### What you gain
- Better sowing dates and forecast-driven spraying
- Fewer losses from washed-away treatments or missed alerts
- A safer, more informed farm year

## Key Takeaways

- The season runs on one habit: ask, decide, verify, act
- Before sowing: weather + soil + crop comparison + officer
- During growth: AI for monitoring, officials for serious treatments
- At spraying and harvest: plan around the forecast, verify doses
- AI turns weather and soil data into decisions; you confirm officially
