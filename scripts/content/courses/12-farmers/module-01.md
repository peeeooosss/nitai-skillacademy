### Module 1 · AI Is a Helper, Not a Replacement
AI (Artificial Intelligence) is a helper on your phone that understands questions in plain language and answers instantly. Think of it as a very knowledgeable field assistant who has studied weather data, thousands of crop advisories, market price reports, and government schemes - and can talk to you in your own language.

You talk to it through a **chat assistant**: an app where you type or speak "What crop should I sow in this soil in October?" and it gives you a considered answer in seconds.

**The golden rule:** AI gives suggestions, not commands. **You know your land, your season, and your budget.** Use AI's information as advice to check against your own experience and official sources - especially for money matters.

**Try it now:** Think of one farming question you asked someone last month. That is exactly the kind of question you can ask AI today.

### Module 2 · Five Ways AI Already Helps Indian Agriculture
AI is already working across Indian agriculture, quietly and everywhere:

1. **Weather & sowing advice** - apps and voice assistants warn about rain or heat and suggest the right sowing time.
2. **Crop & pest alerts** - AI scans crop photos for disease or pest damage and recommends action.
3. **Best market prices** - daily mandi price information, so you know which mandi pays best for your produce.
4. **Government schemes** - help finding and understanding schemes like PM-KISAN, crop insurance, and subsidies.
5. **Fertiliser & soil guidance** - plans matched to your soil and crop, reducing waste and cost.

These are not future promises - they are services available today on a phone in your pocket.

### Module 4 · Special Farm Apps and General AI Assistants
There are two ways to use AI on your farm:

- **Special farm apps** - like Kisan Suvidha, Pusa Krishi apps, and crop-specific advisory services. You will learn to choose carefully in Mission 2.
- **General AI assistants** - like ChatGPT, Gemini, or a voice assistant, answering your own free-form questions about your farm.

Both have value. Special apps are built for farming - they often carry official advisory and photo-diagnosis features. General assistants are flexible - they will answer almost anything you ask, from "what is this leaf spot?" to "compare soybean and pulses for my district."

The **smart approach:** use both - special apps for official, verified information; general AI for immediate plain-language answers and planning help.

### Module 5 · Asking AI About Your Own Farm
The quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question. A good farming question to AI includes the who, what, where:

- **Who/What:** your crop and its stage (e.g., "my chilli crop is flowering")
- **Where:** your district, soil type, and water availability
- **When:** the season or time of year
- **What you noticed:** leaf colour, pest, weather, price

**Weak question:** "What should I do?"
**Strong question:** "In the second week of October, after the rains, what should I sow in black cotton soil in my district? I have canal water."

The more context you give, the more useful the advice. This is the single most important habit in this whole course.

**Try it now:** Take one of your three questions and add your district, soil, and season to it. Practice makes the habit.
