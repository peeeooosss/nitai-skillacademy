### Module 1 · The One-Page Farm Plan

This final mission brings the whole course together into one practical habit: planning your farm year with AI. A one-page farm plan captures what you will grow, your rough costs, your care calendar, and your main risks - and it becomes the document you return to every month.

A plan you write once and never open is useless. A plan you review every month changes your season. The aim is a living page, not a perfect document.

## Learning Objectives

- Draft a one-page farm-year plan
- Cover crops, costs, care calendar, and risks
- Set a monthly review habit
- Keep the plan short enough to actually use

## What the Plan Covers

- **What you will grow** - crops and area per plot
- **Rough costs** - seed, fertiliser, labour, water
- **Care calendar** - key dates for sowing, spraying, harvesting
- **Main risks** - weather, pests, price, water
- **Your decisions** - what you will do if each risk happens
### One page only
- A plan you can glance at is a plan you will follow
- Detail belongs in your records, not the plan
- Short and used beats long and ignored

## Drafting With AI

```text
Draft my one-page farm plan for this year:
1. Crops and area per plot
2. Rough input cost per crop
3. Key dates: sowing, spraying, harvesting
4. Main risks and my response to each
5. Keep it to one page and simple language
```
- AI organises your knowledge into a clear page
- Correct anything that does not fit your reality
- Verify the risky parts with your agri officer

## The Monthly Review

- On a fixed date each month, open the plan and check progress
- Update dates, costs, and risks as reality changes
- Ask AI to help replan if something big changes
### The habit that matters
- A monthly review keeps the plan alive
- Small corrections prevent large losses
- By year's end you have a real record of decisions

## Key Takeaways

- A one-page plan covers crops, costs, care calendar, and risks
- Draft it with AI, then verify risky parts with your agri officer
- Keep it short enough to actually use
- Review it on a fixed date every month
- A living plan beats a perfect document

### Module 2 · The Month-by-Month Calendar

A farm year has a rhythm: preparation, sowing, growth, protection, harvest, and selling. When you lay your tasks on a month-by-month calendar, the year stops being a series of emergencies and becomes a sequence you can prepare for.

AI can turn your one-page plan into a month-by-month checklist. Each month you see what is coming, what to ask about, and what to verify before you act.

## Learning Objectives

- Lay tasks on a month-by-month calendar
- Ask AI to build the calendar from your plan
- Identify the key action for each month
- Use the calendar to prepare instead of react

## The Rhythm of the Year

- **Preparation** - soil, seed, tools, finances
- **Sowing** - dates, seed rate, spacing
- **Growth** - fertiliser, irrigation, weeding
- **Protection** - pest and disease watch, weather alerts
- **Harvest** - timing, labour, storage
- **Selling** - price check, routes, records
### Why the rhythm helps
- Each stage has its own priorities
- Knowing the stage prevents panic decisions
- The year becomes predictable

## Building the Calendar

```text
Turn my one-page farm plan into a month-by-month checklist:
1. For each month, list the key farm actions
2. Note what to ask AI about that month
3. Note what to verify officially that month
4. Mark the busiest weeks so I can plan labour
```
- AI lays your tasks out across the year
- Each month gets a clear focus
- The busiest weeks are flagged in advance

## Preparing Instead of Reacting

- Look at next month's tasks at the end of this month
- Prepare inputs, labour, and questions early
- Follow up on anything the calendar flags
### The compounding effect
- Preparation lowers cost and stress
- You stop missing windows and dates
- A calm, planned season is a profitable one

## Key Takeaways

- The year has a rhythm: prepare, sow, grow, protect, harvest, sell
- Ask AI to build a month-by-month checklist from your plan
- Each month gets key actions, questions, and checks
- Look at next month's tasks before it begins
- Preparation replaces panic decisions

## Practice Challenge

1. Ask AI to turn your plan into a month-by-month calendar
2. Mark the busiest weeks for labour planning
3. Check next month's tasks this month

2. What does the calendar help you do?

**Answer:** A — The calendar lets you see what is coming and prepare early.

3. What should each month's checklist include?

**Answer:** A — Actions, questions for AI, official checks, and busy weeks.

**Answer:** A — Preparation lowers cost and stress and stops you missing windows and dates.

### Module 3 · Your AI Check-In Routine

The final skill of the course is a simple weekly and monthly routine with AI. This is what turns everything you have learned into an automatic habit - a short check-in that keeps your farm, your money, and your knowledge moving forward.

The weekly check-in covers weather, crop, and market. The monthly check-in reviews the plan, records, and schemes. Together they take under half an hour a week and pay back many times over.

## Learning Objectives

- Run a weekly weather/crop/market check-in
- Run a monthly plan and records review
- Combine AI with official verification every time
- Make the routine automatic

## The Weekly Check-In

- **Weather** - this week's forecast and what it means for your tasks
- **Crop** - any leaf, pest, or growth changes to ask about
- **Market** - the price trend for anything you plan to sell
- Ask each with voice, in your own language
### Keep it short
- Three questions, ten minutes
- Same day each week so it becomes automatic
- Note anything that needs official verification

## The Monthly Check-In

```text
My monthly AI check-in:
1. Open my one-page farm plan - what changed?
2. Ask AI to review my voice-note records and totals
3. Check any scheme dates or applications pending
4. Verify anything important with the agri officer
5. Update the plan for next month
```
- The monthly check-in keeps the plan alive
- Records and schemes get regular attention
- Verification stays part of the routine

## Making It Automatic

- Fix the same day and time for each check-in
- Use voice so it never feels like paperwork
- Every important answer gets an official confirmation
### The whole course in one habit
- Ask with details, get the decision, check officially, then act
- Weather, soil, market, schemes, records, and business all connect
- The routine is the real skill you carry forward

## Key Takeaways

- Weekly check-in: weather, crop, and market
- Monthly check-in: plan, records, schemes, and verification
- Use voice in your own language to keep it easy
- Verify important answers officially every time
- The routine is the real skill you carry forward

## Practice Challenge

### Module 4 · Reviewing Last Season With AI

The most underused farm skill is looking back. Most farmers repeat the same costly mistakes because last season's lessons were never written down or reviewed. AI makes a season review quick and honest: what worked, what failed, and what to change.

You do not need perfect data. A few notes, a rough cost, and your memory are enough to start. AI asks the right questions and helps you see the patterns.

## Learning Objectives

- Run a season review with AI
- Identify what worked and what failed
- Find the costliest mistake to avoid
- Turn lessons into changes for next season

## The Season-Review Prompt

```text
Help me review my last crop season.
I grew [crop] on [acres]. My main costs were [costs].
The main problems were [problems]. Results: [result].
Ask me questions to find what worked and what failed,
and suggest 3 changes for next season.
```
- AI asks questions you might not think to ask yourself
- It helps you separate bad luck from bad decisions
- Three changes keep the lesson actionable

## Finding the Costliest Mistake

- Look for the single change that would have saved the most
- Common ones: late sowing, wrong seed, missed pest alert, bad price timing
- Ask AI: 'What was likely my most expensive mistake, and why?'
### Why one mistake
- Fixing one big mistake beats fixing ten small ones
- It gives you a clear priority for next season
- It is easier to remember and act on

## From Review to Change

- Write the 3 changes at the top of your new one-page plan
- Set a reminder to check them mid-season
- Tell someone - a family member or your agri officer
### The honest-review habit
- A review without honesty changes nothing
- Blaming only the weather hides your own fixable errors
- Reviewing every season compounds your skill

## Key Takeaways

- Review last season with AI - what worked and what failed
- Find the single costliest mistake to fix first
- Ask AI for 3 changes for next season
- Write the changes at the top of your new plan
- An honest review compounds your skill every year

## Practice Challenge

1. Run the season-review prompt for your last crop
2. Identify your costliest mistake
3. Write 3 changes at the top of your new plan

2. What should you look for in the review?
- A) The single change that would have saved the most

- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — Fixing one big mistake beats fixing ten small ones.

3. How many changes should you aim for?
- A) Three
- B) Ten

**Answer:** A — Three changes keep the lesson actionable.

4. What does an honest review do?

### Module 5 · Building Your Farm Records

Records are the memory of your farm business. Without them, every season starts from zero. With them, every season starts from experience. AI turns the scattered notes and voice memos you have been keeping into a simple, organised record.

You do not need accounting software. A phone note, a notebook, or a simple table is enough - as long as you keep it going.

## Learning Objectives

- Organise farm records with AI
- Track costs, yields, and prices over time
- Keep records simple and consistent
- Use records for better decisions

## What to Record

- **Costs** - seed, fertiliser, labour, water, transport
- **Yields** - per plot, per crop
- **Prices** - what you sold for, when, and where
- **Observations** - weather events, pest outbreaks, what worked
### Simple is sustainable
- A messy record kept is worth more than a perfect one abandoned
- Use voice notes if writing is hard
- Consistency matters more than detail

## Organising With AI

```text
Organise my farm records:
1. Here are my notes for this season: [paste or dictate]
2. Make a table: date, plot, activity, cost, yield, price
3. Total my costs and my income
4. Show my profit per plot and per crop
```
- AI turns scattered notes into a clean table
- Totals reveal your true profit per plot
- The table becomes your baseline for next year

## Using Records to Decide

- Compare this year's costs and yields with last year's
- See which plot or crop earns the most
- Share the records when asking for a loan or scheme
### The long-term value
- Records prove your track record to lenders and buyers
- They turn farming from guesswork into a business
- Each year's records make the next year better

## Key Takeaways

- Record costs, yields, prices, and observations
- Simple and consistent beats perfect and abandoned
- Ask AI to organise notes into a table
- Use records to compare years and find your best plot
- Records turn farming from guesswork into a business

## Practice Challenge

1. Gather this season's notes and ask AI to organise them

3. Set a simple weekly habit for recording

### Module 6 · Teaching Your Family and Neighbours

AI skills multiply when shared. The final module of this course is about passing on what you have learned - to your family, your neighbours, and your village group. Teaching is also the best way to strengthen your own understanding.

You do not need to be an expert. You need to show three simple habits: voice questions, verification, and the monthly check-in. Anyone with a phone can learn them.

## Learning Objectives

- Teach voice questions to family members
- Share the check-twice and golden money rules
- Start a village learning group
- Reinforce your own skills by teaching

## What to Teach First

- **Voice questions** - tap the mic and speak in your own language
- **Verification** - check important answers with officials
- **Golden money rules** - schemes are free, never share OTPs
- **The monthly check-in** - plan, records, and schemes
### Keep it simple
- Three habits are enough to start
- Show, do not lecture
- Let them try with their own questions

## Running a Village Learning Group

```text
A simple village AI learning session:
1. Gather a few farmers with phones
2. Show one voice question each - weather, crop, market
3. Practise the check-twice rule on a real question
4. Share the golden money rules and the Kisan Call Centre number
5. Agree a monthly check-in day together
```
- Learning together keeps everyone accountable
- Real questions make the session useful
- One session can help the whole group

## Teaching Strengthens You

- Explaining a habit makes you understand it better
- Answering questions reveals your own gaps
- A group shares discoveries and warnings
### The ripple effect
- One farmer's skill becomes the village's skill
- The group protects each other from scams
- Shared knowledge is the strongest harvest

## Key Takeaways

- Teach three habits first: voice, verification, money rules
- Show, do not lecture - let learners try their own questions
- Run a simple village learning session
- Agree a shared monthly check-in day
- Teaching strengthens your own skills and protects the group
