### Module 1 · Know Today's Price Picture

Farmers have historically decided what to sell, where, and to whom with thin information. AI compresses days of phone calls and mandi visits into a ten-minute planning session. But the point is not that AI gives you prices - it is that AI helps you understand the prices you look up.

Use official price information first: **eNAM (National Agriculture Market)**, state mandi portals, and the Agmarknet mandi price system. AI does not have live mandi data by itself, so treat its answers as analysis and verify actual figures on the official apps.

## Learning Objectives

- Use official price sources before AI
- Ask AI to explain price trends and mandi differences
- Understand AI's role: analysis, not live prices
- Verify actual figures officially before deciding

## Official Sources First

- **eNAM** - the National Agriculture Market, an online trading platform
- **State mandi portals** - daily prices from your state's markets
- **Agmarknet** - the national mandi price system
### AI's role
- AI does not have live mandi data by itself
- It helps you understand the prices you look up
- Treat its answers as analysis questions, then verify the figures

## Asking AI to Interpret Prices

```text
The price-interpretation question:
1. Look up today's price for [crop] on eNAM/Agmarknet/state portal
2. Ask AI: what is the recent trend of [crop] prices in [my mandi]
   and [nearby mandi]?
3. Ask: what causes the difference - season, supply, or quality grades?
4. Ask: which selling route should I consider?
5. Verify the actual figures officially before negotiating
```
- AI explains why prices move; official sources give the numbers
- A trend understanding improves your bargaining position
- Never quote an AI number as if it were today's mandi price

## From Price to Plan

- The goal is a ten-minute planning session, not hours of calls
- Compare your mandi with one nearby and note the difference
- Use the understanding to decide where and when to sell
### The habit
- Look up official price, then ask AI to explain
- Verify before you negotiate
- Record what you find - your own history becomes an asset

## Key Takeaways

- Use official price sources first: eNAM, state portals, Agmarknet
- AI does not have live prices - it explains the data you look up
- Ask AI about trends and why mandis differ
- Verify actual figures officially before negotiating
- A ten-minute planning session replaces days of phone calls

### Module 2 · Why Prices Differ

Prices differ for clear reasons, and understanding them helps you decide where and when to sell. The four main reasons are quality and grades, season and supply, mandi versus eNAM, and distance and transport. Once you see why prices differ, you can choose your selling route instead of accepting the first offer.

Ask AI to compare your selling options with real costs, then verify today's mandi price on the official app before negotiating. This turns a price-taker into a price-maker.

## Learning Objectives

- Name the four reasons prices differ
- Compare selling routes: local mandi, eNAM, direct
- Account for real costs like transport, commission, and grading
- Verify today's price before negotiating

## The Four Reasons

- **Quality & grades** - better graded produce earns more; grading matters
- **Season & supply** - harvest glut lowers prices; off-season raises them
- **Mandi vs eNAM** - eNAM auctions across districts for transparent discovery; local mandis are cash-fast but often lower
- **Distance & transport** - a mandi far away may pay more but cost more to reach
### Why it helps you
- You stop accepting the first offer
- You can choose the route that nets you the most
- Understanding beats guessing

## Comparing Selling Routes

```text
Compare selling my [crop] at [local mandi] vs eNAM vs direct:
1. What price does each likely offer?
2. What are the real costs - transport, commission, waiting, grading?
3. How much time does each take?
4. When does each route make sense?
5. Which gives the best net return for my distance?
```
- Ask AI to lay out the trade-offs with real costs
- Verify the mandi price for today on the official app
- Choose the route that fits your time and distance

## Verify Before You Negotiate

- Always verify today's mandi price on the official app before negotiating
- Compare with the eNAM price and note the grading requirement
- A small test lot where the comparison is close reduces risk
### The result
- Knowing the true price improves your bargaining position
- Even a partial application of this habit helps
- Your price history becomes your best future asset

## Key Takeaways

- Prices differ by quality/grades, season/supply, route, and distance
- eNAM auctions across districts for transparent discovery
- Compare routes with real costs: transport, commission, waiting, grading
- Verify today's price before negotiating
- Your price history becomes your best future asset

### Module 3 · Find Your Government Schemes

Schemes change every year and differ by state, so AI cannot give you perfect facts - but it is brilliant at **finding and explaining** candidates for you to verify. This module shows how to ask for a scheme list and how to double-check it before applying.

Expect candidates like PM-KISAN (direct income support), PMFBY (crop insurance), KCC (Kisan Credit Card), state input subsidies, and eNAM registrations. Then do the double check at the official portal or agri office before applying.

## Learning Objectives

- Ask AI for schemes matched to your situation
- Recognise common scheme candidates like PM-KISAN and PMFBY
- Double-check eligibility and dates officially
- Record every application number and keep copies

## The Scheme-Finding Prompt

```text
List government schemes a [small/marginal] farmer with [acres]
of [crop/orchard] in [state] can apply for this year.
For each: what it gives, who is eligible, and how to apply.
Mark which are most practical for me.
```
- The more detail you give, the more relevant the candidates
- Ask AI to explain each scheme in simple words
- Mark the ones that fit your land and crop

## Common Scheme Candidates

- **PM-KISAN** - direct income support instalments
- **PMFBY / crop insurance** - premium support against crop loss
- **KCC (Kisan Credit Card)** - loan support at subsidised rates
- **State input subsidy schemes** - seeds, fertiliser, machinery, drip
- **eNAM / mandi modernisation registrations**
### What to expect
- AI lists likely schemes with eligibility and how to apply
- Some may not apply to you - that is why you verify
- The list is a starting point, not a final answer

## The Double Check

- Visit the official portal (PM-KISAN, PMFBY, your state agri website) or the agri office
- Confirm eligibility and dates before applying
- Record every application number and keep copies of all papers
### Why the double check matters
- Scheme rules and dates change every year
- AI cannot guarantee current facts
- Official confirmation protects your application

## Key Takeaways

- AI finds and explains scheme candidates - it cannot guarantee facts
- Common candidates: PM-KISAN, PMFBY, KCC, state subsidies, eNAM
- Double-check eligibility and dates at the official portal or agri office
- Record every application number and keep copies
- The AI list is a starting point; officials confirm

### Module 4 · The Golden Money Rules

Money advice deserves the strictest checks. The five golden rules in this module protect families every harvest season - they cover verification, scams, privacy, records, and free help. Write them in your phone notes and keep them there permanently.

The most important rule of all: government schemes are free to apply. Anyone charging a fee to 'help you apply' is a scam. The second: never share OTPs or bank details over the phone, even from a caller claiming to be a government officer.

## Learning Objectives

- State the five golden money rules
- Recognise scheme-application scams and fee-charging frauds
- Protect OTPs and bank details from phone fraud
- Know that your agri officer is free expert help

## The Five Golden Rules

- **Verify on official sources** before acting - AI analysis is a plan, not proof
- **Never pay anyone to 'help you apply'** for a scheme - government schemes are free to apply
- **Never share OTPs or bank details** over the phone, even from a 'government officer' caller
- **Record every application number** and keep copies of all papers
- **Ask your agri officer** - they are paid to help you with exactly this
### Why these rules exist
- They protect families every harvest season
- Schemes are free to apply - anyone charging is a scam
- A moment of caution prevents a season of loss

## Spotting the Scam

```text
How to spot a scheme scam:
1. They ask for a fee to 'process' or 'guarantee' a scheme -> scam
2. They ask for an OTP or bank details over the phone -> scam
3. They promise 'instant scheme money' -> scam
4. They pressure you to act immediately -> scam
5. Real schemes: free to apply, verified at the official portal or office
When in doubt, hang up and ask your agri officer.
```
- A caller promising instant money and asking for an OTP is a fraud
- The correct response is to hang up and report the number
- Your agri officer can confirm any scheme's authenticity

## Keeping Records and Getting Help

- Record every application number immediately in a notebook or phone note
- Keep copies of applications, acknowledgement slips, passbook, and Aadhaar where relevant
- Ask your agri officer or KVK - they are free expert help
### The free-help principle
- You never need to pay for scheme help
- The agri office is paid to assist you
- Good records turn applications into real money

## Key Takeaways

- Verify on official sources before acting on money advice
- Schemes are free to apply - anyone charging a fee is a scam
- Never share OTPs or bank details, even from an 'officer'
- Record every application number and keep copies
- Your agri officer is free expert help

### Module 5 · The Mandi Decision Walkthrough

A complete money decision combines prices, options, and verification. This module walks through the five steps: check the official price, ask AI to compare selling routes, verify findings, decide with a small test lot, and record the result.

Even a partial application of this habit improves your bargaining position. You will know what a good deal looks like before you are standing at the mandi gate.

## Learning Objectives

- Run the five-step mandi decision walkthrough
- Start with today's official price
- Use a small test lot when routes are close
- Record results to build your own price history

## The Five Steps

- **Step 1:** check the official price for your crop today (eNAM/Agmarknet/state portal)
- **Step 2:** ask AI to compare selling routes - local mandi vs eNAM vs direct - with real costs: transport, commission, waiting, grading
- **Step 3:** verify findings with the official app or a call to the agri officer
- **Step 4:** decide with facts - one small test lot where the comparison is close
- **Step 5:** record the result in your notes - your own price history becomes your best asset
### Why the order matters
- Official price first keeps the decision grounded
- The AI comparison adds the real costs most people forget
- Verification and recording make it a habit, not a one-off

## The Small Test Lot

```text
When the comparison is close, test small:
1. Sell one small lot through the new route (e.g., eNAM)
2. Note the real costs and the actual price received
3. Compare with what the local mandi offered the same day
4. Learn the route with low risk before committing the full harvest
5. Record the result in your price history
```
- A test lot lets you learn without risking the whole crop
- Real experience beats any prediction
- Grow the route only after the test proves it

## Your Price History

- Record every result: date, route, costs, price received
- Over time this becomes your own price history
- Your price history improves every future decision
### The compounding benefit
- Next season you start from experience, not zero
- You can see which route pays best for your crop and distance
- Your records become a bargaining asset

## Key Takeaways

- Five steps: official price, AI comparison, verify, test small, record
- Start with today's official price on eNAM/Agmarknet/state portal
- Use a small test lot when routes are close
- Record every result to build your own price history
- Your price history is your best future asset

### Module 6 · Schemes, Numbers, and Records

Getting money from schemes is a paperwork discipline. The difference between farmers who receive scheme benefits and those who do not is usually not eligibility - it is records, dates, and follow-up. This module covers the habits that turn an application into actual money.

Apply yourself or with your agri officer, free of charge. Note every application number immediately, keep copies of all papers, respect the dates, and follow up politely after a reasonable wait.

## Learning Objectives

- Apply for schemes free of charge, yourself or with your officer
- Note every application number immediately
- Keep copies of all papers
- Respect scheme windows and follow up politely

## The Paperwork Discipline

- **Apply yourself or with your agri officer** - free of charge, never through a fee-charging agent
- **Note every application number** - write it down immediately, in a notebook or phone note
- **Keep copies of all papers** - applications, acknowledgement slips, passbook, Aadhaar where relevant
- **Respect the dates** - schemes have windows; missing them costs a year
- **Follow up politely** - after a reasonable wait, ask the officer for status
### Why records matter
- An application number is your proof and your tracking tool
- Copies settle any dispute quickly
- Dates are the most common reason schemes are missed

## The Record-Keeping Flow

```text
For every scheme application:
1. Confirm eligibility at the official portal or office
2. Apply free of charge (yourself or with the officer)
3. Write the application number immediately in your notes
4. Keep copies of every paper and acknowledgement
5. Note the expected decision date, then follow up politely
```
- The flow takes minutes and protects months of waiting
- A recorded number makes follow-up easy
- Good records turn applications into real money

## Following Up Politely

- After a reasonable wait, ask the officer for the status
- A polite follow-up is normal and expected
- Keep your records handy when you follow up
### The quiet income increase
- Claiming schemes you are entitled to is a real income boost
- PM-KISAN instalments can be claimed year after year
- The paperwork discipline is what makes the money arrive

## Key Takeaways

- Apply free of charge, yourself or with your agri officer
- Note every application number immediately
- Keep copies of all papers and acknowledgements
- Respect scheme windows - missing them costs a year
- Follow up politely after a reasonable wait
