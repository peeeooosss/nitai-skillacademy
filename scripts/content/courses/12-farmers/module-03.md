# Mission 3: Market Prices & Government Schemes via AI

Money decisions — what price to accept, what schemes to claim — are where farmers lose or gain most. This mission trains you to use AI to understand prices and find schemes you can actually get.

## Learning Objectives

- Get the current mandi price picture for your crop across nearby mandis.
- Understand why prices differ (quality, season, supply, mandi vs eNAM) well enough to decide where to sell.
- Use AI to find government schemes for your situation and how to apply for them.
- Follow the golden safety rule: never trust money advice without official verification.

## The Core Idea

Farmers historically decide what to sell, where, and to whom with thin information. AI compresses days of phone calls and mandi visits into a ten-minute planning session. But because *money is involved*, checks are non-negotiable.

### Step 1 – Know Today's Price Picture

- Use official price information first: **eNAM (National Agriculture Market)**, state mandi portals, and the Agmarknet mandi price system.
- Ask AI to interpret: *"What is the recent trend of [crop] prices in [my mandi] and [nearby mandi]? What causes the difference — season, supply, or quality grades?"*
- Remember: AI does not have live mandi data by itself — treat its answers as analysis questions, and verify actual figures on official apps/portals.

### Step 2 – Decide Where & How to Sell

Compare your options with AI's help:

- **Local mandi** — cash-fast, but you take whatever the auction gives.
- **eNAM online auction** — transparent price discovery across districts, but needs registration and digital comfort.
- **Direct to processor/retailer** — sometimes better prices for quality produce, but harder to find and negotiate.
- **Farm gate** — saves transport; check what the local buyer offers against mandi prices.

Ask: *"Compare selling my [crop] at [local mandi] vs eNAM vs direct. What are the real costs — transport, commission, waiting, grading — and when does each make sense?"* Then **verify the mandi price** for today on the official app before negotiating.

### Step 3 – Find Your Government Schemes

Schemes change every year and differ by state, so AI can't give you perfect facts — but it is brilliant at *finding and explaining* candidates to verify.

Ask: *"List government schemes a [small/marginal] farmer with [acres] of [crop/orchard] in [state] can apply for this year. For each: what it gives, who is eligible, and how to apply. Mark which are most practical for me."*

Expect candidates like:

- **PM-KISAN** — direct income support instalments.
- **PMFBY / crop insurance** — premium support against crop loss.
- **KCC (Kisan Credit Card)** — loan support at subsidised rates.
- **State input subsidy schemes** — seeds, fertiliser, machinery, drip.
- **eNAM / mandi modernisation registrations.**

Then do the "double check": visit the official portal (PM-KISAN, PMFBY, your state agri website) or the agri office to confirm eligibility and dates before applying.

### Step 4 – The Golden Money Rules

1. **Verify on official sources** before acting — AI analysis is a plan, not proof.
2. **Never pay anyone to "help you apply"** for a scheme. Government schemes are free to apply; anyone charging a fee is a scam.
3. **Never share OTPs or bank details** over the phone, even from a "government officer" caller.
4. **Record every application number** and keep copies of all papers.
5. **Ask your agri officer** — they are paid to help you with exactly this.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 – The mandi decision.** Instead of selling straight to the local mandi, Meera compared rates: her AI session laid out eNAM's likely advantages but also the grading and digital steps. She verified eNAM's current price on the app, tried one online auction with a small lot, and got ₹2.60/kg more than the local mandi had offered the same morning.

**Example 2 – The scheme catch.** Dattatray had never claimed PM-KISAN. AI's scheme list reminded him, the agri office confirmed his eligibility, the form took twenty minutes with the officer, and his account began receiving the instalments — a quiet but real income increase.

**Example 3 – The scam warning.** A caller promised "instant scheme money" and asked for an OTP during "verification". Balu remembered the golden rules, hung up, and reported the number. This single habit protects families every harvest season.

## Key Takeaways

- Price decisions are better with a market analysis (mandi vs eNAM vs direct) — verify actual figures officially.
- AI is excellent at finding and explaining schemes; only official portals and officers confirm them.
- Schemes are free to apply — anyone charging a fee is a scam. Never share OTPs.
- Keep application numbers and paper records of everything you submit.
- Your agri officer and KVK are free expert help; involve them before big money decisions.
