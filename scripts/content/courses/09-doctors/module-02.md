# Mission 2: Clinical Documentation with AI

---
### Module 1 · Documentation as Care, Not Admin

Clinical documentation is not just paperwork; it is the record that drives safe care, communication, and continuity. Poor notes cause harm.

AI can speed documentation, but the record must stay accurate, complete, and clinically sound.

## Learning Objectives

- Treat documentation as part of care
- Understand why accuracy matters
- Use AI without lowering quality
- Keep the record clinically sound

## Documentation Is Care

- Notes drive decisions and continuity
- They communicate across teams
- They protect patients and clinicians
### Why
- Poor notes cause harm
- Clear records improve safety
- Accuracy is a clinical duty

## Using AI Safely

- AI drafts, clinician verifies
- Never let AI invent clinical facts
- Keep the record complete and honest
### The rule
- Accuracy over speed
- Verify every clinical detail
- The record must be true

## The Documentation Prompt

```text
Explain how AI can assist clinical documentation in
[setting] without reducing accuracy, listing safe uses and
the verification steps a clinician must perform.
```
- Safe documentation uses
- Verification steps
- Accuracy safeguards

## Key Takeaways

- Documentation is part of care, not just admin
- Notes drive decisions, continuity, and team communication
- Poor documentation causes harm
- AI drafts; the clinician verifies every clinical detail
- The record must always be accurate and complete

## Practice Challenge

1. Review one of your recent notes for clarity
2. Identify where AI could safely assist
3. List the details you must always verify

- A) Poor notes cause harm
- B) It looks neat

**Answer:** A — Inaccurate notes can lead to unsafe care.

3. What does AI do in documentation?
- A) Drafts, while you verify

**Answer:** A — AI drafts and the clinician verifies every detail.

4. What must the record always be?
- A) Accurate and complete

**Answer:** A — The clinical record must be true, accurate, and complete.

### Module 2 · Step 1: Structure the Note Before You Dictate

A note is easiest to write when its structure is already decided. Pick a standard format before you start dictating or drafting.

AI can format your content into the structure you use, so the note is consistent and complete.

## Learning Objectives

- Choose a standard note structure
- Structure before you write
- Keep notes consistent
- Use AI to format

## Choosing a Structure

- Use your standard format (for example SOAP or a specialty template)
- Know the sections before you start
- Keep the same order every time
### Why
- Structure prevents missing sections
- Consistency aids the whole team
- AI formats reliably into a given structure

## Structuring with AI

- Give AI the sections you need
- Ask it to place content correctly
- Review that nothing is misplaced
### The rule
- Structure first
- Same format every time
- Review the placement

## The Structure Prompt

```text
Format the following clinical content into a [SOAP] note
with sections [list]. Do not add clinical facts. Flag any
section where information is missing.
```
- Content in correct sections
- No invented facts
- Missing sections flagged

## Key Takeaways

- Decide the note structure before you write
- Use your standard format every time
- Structure prevents missing sections
- AI can format content into the structure you specify
- Always review that content is placed correctly

## Practice Challenge

1. Write down your standard note structure
2. Format one note with AI into that structure
3. Check for missing sections

**Example Quiz**

1. When should you decide the structure?
- A) Before you write
- B) After you write
- C) Never
- D) During sign-off

**Answer:** A — Deciding the structure first keeps the note complete.

2. What does structure prevent?
- A) Missing sections
- B) Billing
- C) Exams
- D) Referrals

**Answer:** A — A standard structure ensures no section is forgotten.

3. What does AI do with structure?
- A) Formats content into it
- B) Invents facts
- C) Signs off
- D) Diagnoses

**Answer:** A — AI places your content into the structure you specify.

4. What must you always review?
- A) That content is placed correctly
- B) The font
- C) The date
- D) The logo

**Answer:** A — Review that AI has not misplaced content between sections.

### Module 3 · Step 2: Turn Raw Notes into a Clear Note

Raw notes are messy: fragments, abbreviations, and shorthand. Turning them into a clear clinical note is a task AI handles well.

The key is to keep every clinical fact, invent nothing, and improve clarity.

## Learning Objectives

- Turn raw notes into clear text
- Preserve every clinical fact
- Never let AI invent details
- Improve clarity and completeness

## From Raw to Clear

- Expand abbreviations carefully
- Organise fragments into full sentences
- Keep all clinical facts
### Why
- Clarity aids the care team
- Invented facts are dangerous
- Every detail matters clinically

## The Golden Rule

- Do not add what was not written
- Flag anything ambiguous for review
- Keep the clinician's meaning
### The rule
- Preserve facts
- Invent nothing
- Flag ambiguity

## The Raw-Notes Prompt

```text
Turn these raw clinical notes into a clear, complete note
[paste]. Preserve every clinical fact, invent nothing, and
flag anything ambiguous with [FLAG].
```
- Clear, complete note
- Facts preserved
- Ambiguity flagged

## Key Takeaways

- AI turns raw notes into clear clinical text
- Preserve every clinical fact
- Never let AI invent or assume details
- Flag anything ambiguous for review
- Keep the clinician's original meaning

## Practice Challenge

1. Convert one set of raw notes with AI
2. Check every fact is preserved
3. Mark and resolve any flags

**Example Quiz**

1. What does AI do with raw notes?
- A) Turns them into clear text
- B) Invents details
- C) Deletes them
- D) Signs them

**Answer:** A — AI expands fragments into a clear, complete note.

2. What must be preserved?
- A) Every clinical fact
- B) Only the plan
- C) Only the diagnosis
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — All clinical facts must be preserved when clarifying notes.

3. What must AI never do?
- A) Invent details
- B) Organise text
- C) Expand abbreviations
- D) Improve clarity

**Answer:** A — AI must never add facts that were not in the original notes.

4. What happens to ambiguous content?
- A) It is flagged
- B) It is deleted
- C) It is guessed
- D) It is ignored

**Answer:** A — Flag ambiguous content for clinician review rather than guessing.

### Module 4 · Step 3: Summarising the Patient History

A good history summary captures what matters for the current decision: relevant past events, conditions, medications, and context.

AI can condense a long record into a summary, but it must not drop clinically important details or blur chronology.

## Learning Objectives

- Summarise patient history with AI
- Keep clinically relevant detail
- Preserve chronology
- Avoid losing important facts

## What a Summary Needs

- Relevant conditions and events
- Current medications and allergies
- Chronology that makes sense
### Why
- Decisions depend on the right details
- Chronology affects reasoning
- Missing facts can mislead

## Keeping It Safe

- Specify what must always be included
- Ask AI to keep dates and doses exact
- Check nothing important was dropped
### The rule
- Never drop allergies or medications
- Keep dates and doses exact
- Review against the source

## The History Prompt

```text
Summarise this patient history for a [specialty] clinician
[paste]. Keep all conditions, medications, allergies, and
dates exact. Do not omit clinically relevant events.
```
- Conditions and events kept
- Medications and allergies exact
- Dates preserved

## Key Takeaways

- A history summary must capture what matters for the decision
- Never drop allergies, medications, or relevant events
- Keep dates and doses exact
- Preserve a sensible chronology
- Review the summary against the source record

## Practice Challenge

1. Summarise one history with AI
2. Check allergies and medications are exact
3. Compare the summary to the source

**Example Quiz**

1. What must a summary capture?
- A) What matters for the decision
- B) Only the diagnosis
- C) Only recent visits
- D) Everything verbatim

**Answer:** A — Focus the summary on clinically relevant detail for the decision.

2. What must never be dropped?
- A) Allergies and medications
- B) Old notes
- C) Addresses
- D) Insurers

**Answer:** A — Allergies and medications are safety-critical and must be kept.

3. What must stay exact?
- A) Dates and doses
- B) Formatting
- C) Font
- D) Headings

**Answer:** A — Dates and doses must be preserved exactly to avoid error.

4. What should you check?
- A) The summary against the source
- B) The colour
- C) The length
- D) The spelling of the title

**Answer:** A — Always verify the summary against the original record.

### Module 5 · Step 4: Referral Letters and Discharge Summaries

Referral letters and discharge summaries are hand-offs: their quality decides how well the next clinician can act.

AI can draft them from your notes, but they must be accurate, complete, and clear about what you need.

## Learning Objectives

- Draft referral letters with AI
- Write effective discharge summaries
- Make the ask clear
- Keep hand-offs accurate

## The Hand-Off

- Referral: why, what you need, relevant history
- Discharge: course, medications, follow-up, safety net
- Clear ask and clear plan
### Why
- Hand-offs affect patient safety
- Clarity speeds the next step
- Missing details cause delays

## Drafting with AI

- Give AI the reason and the ask
- Include essential history and results
- Review tone and accuracy
### The rule
- State the ask clearly
- Include safety-critical detail
- Clinician reviews before sending

## The Hand-Off Prompt

```text
Draft a [referral letter / discharge summary] from these
notes [paste]. Include the reason, relevant history, the
ask or plan, and safety-critical details. For clinician review.
```
- Reason and ask stated
- Relevant history included
- Safety-critical detail included

## Key Takeaways

- Referrals and discharges are hand-offs that affect safety
- State the reason and the ask clearly
- Include relevant history and safety-critical detail
- Discharge summaries need medications and follow-up
- A clinician reviews every draft before sending

## Practice Challenge

1. Draft one referral letter with AI
2. Draft one discharge summary
3. Check the ask and plan are clear

**Example Quiz**

1. What are referrals and discharges?
- A) Hand-offs
- B) Private notes
- C) Billing items
- D) Optional

**Answer:** A — They are hand-offs whose quality affects the next clinician's care.

2. What must be clear?
- A) The ask or plan
- B) The font
- C) The logo
- D) The letterhead

**Answer:** A — State clearly what you need or what the plan is.

3. What must a discharge include?
- A) Medications and follow-up
- B) Only the diagnosis
- C) Only the dates
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — Discharge summaries need medications, follow-up, and safety nets.

4. Who reviews before sending?
- A) The clinician
- B) The AI
- C) The vendor
- D) The patient

**Answer:** A — A clinician reviews and approves every draft before it is sent.

### Module 6 · Step 5: The Clinician's Review and Sign-Off

No AI-assisted document leaves your hands without your review and sign-off. This is where responsibility is fixed and errors are caught.

The review is not a formality; it is the final safety check before the record or letter is used.

## Learning Objectives

- Review AI-assisted documents
- Verify facts and completeness
- Sign off deliberately
- Fix errors before use

## The Review

- Check every clinical fact
- Confirm completeness against the source
- Check the ask, plan, and safety details
### Why
- The sign-off fixes responsibility
- Errors must be caught here
- The record must be trustworthy

## Signing Off

- Never sign what you have not read
- Correct errors at the source
- Only sign when fully satisfied
### The rule
- Read every word
- Verify against source
- Sign deliberately

## The Review Prompt

```text
Act as a clinical review checklist for this AI-assisted
document [paste]: list the facts to verify, completeness
checks, and any ambiguity to resolve before sign-off.
```
- Facts to verify
- Completeness checks
- Ambiguities to resolve

## Key Takeaways

- No AI-assisted document is used without clinician review
- Check every clinical fact against the source
- Confirm completeness, ask, plan, and safety details
- Never sign what you have not fully read
- Sign-off fixes responsibility and is the final safety check

## Practice Challenge

1. Review one AI-assisted document line by line
2. Correct any errors at the source
3. Confirm you are satisfied before signing

**Example Quiz**

1. What must happen before a document is used?
- A) Clinician review and sign-off
- B) Nothing
- C) A second AI check
- D) An email

**Answer:** A — A clinician reviews and signs off before any document is used.

2. What do you check?
- A) Every clinical fact
- B) Only the title
- C) Only the date
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — Verify every clinical fact against the source record.

3. What should you never do?
- A) Sign what you have not read
- B) Read carefully
- C) Verify facts
- D) Correct errors

**Answer:** A — Never sign a document you have not fully read and verified.

4. What does sign-off fix?
- A) Responsibility
- B) The font
- C) The price
- D) The format

**Answer:** A — Sign-off fixes clinical responsibility for the document.
