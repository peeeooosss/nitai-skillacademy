# Mission 2: Prompt Engineering for Academics

## Learning Objectives
- Write structured prompts using a professional framework
- Chain prompts to research, draft, then refine academic work
- Control format, tone, depth, and audience in outputs
- Design one reusable prompt template per assignment type
- Critique and improve your own prompts systematically

---

## Why Prompting Is a Core Academic Skill

Prompting is how you give precise instructions to AI. For students, it's the difference between a vague essay draft and a structured, citation-ready starting point. Think of it as **writing a brief** — the same skill your professors use when they set assignments.

## The 4-Part Professional Prompt Framework

For academic use, the REC framework gets upgraded to **SCoPE**:

1. **S — System / Role.** "Act as an examiner in [subject] setting a 2nd-year essay prompt."
2. **C — Context.** Give background: your course, module, rubric, word count, deadline, and what you already know.
3. **o — Objective.** State the exact deliverable: "an outline", "a counter-argument", "3 research questions".
4. **P — Parameters.** Format, length, tone, structure, and constraints ("academic tone", "≤ 300 words", "numbered list", "no jargon").
5. **E — Example.** Show one sample of the output you're expecting (few-shot).

### Weak prompt ❌
> "Write an essay about climate change."

### SCoPE prompt ✅
> "Act as a 2nd-year environmental science examiner. Context: my module is 'Climate Policy'; the essay prompt is 'Evaluate carbon pricing vs regulation.' I have notes on EU and Indian policy. Objective: produce a structured outline (intro, 3 arguments, counter-argument, conclusion) with one citation suggestion each. Parameters: academic tone, 400 words, bullet points under each section. Example format: 'I. Introduction — [angle] — [key source: author, year]'."

## The Research → Draft → Refine Chain

Never ask for the final essay in one shot. Chain three passes:

1. **RESEARCH** — "Give me 5 academic debates and 3 landmark papers on [topic]."
2. **DRAFT** — Feed the research back: "Using the sources above, draft the intro and argument 1."
3. **REFINE** — "Now strengthen argument 2, add a counter-argument from [school/author], and tighten the conclusion to 3 lines."

Each pass keeps **you in control** and produces better, more differentiated work.

## Controlling Outputs Like a Pro

- **Tone:** "formal academic tone", "accessible first-year tone".
- **Depth:** "master's-level nuance", "undergrad survey depth".
- **Structure:** "IMRaD structure", "PEE paragraphs (Point, Evidence, Explain)".
- **Format:** "Markdown with headings", "table with 2 columns", "300 words ± 10%".
- **Constraint:** "no internet assumptions — say what needs verifying."
- **Negation:** "avoid the word 'revolutionizing'; no AI hype language."

## Build a Reusable Prompt Bank

Your biggest ROI: once per assignment type, build a template you reuse all semester. Save them in your notes app.

**Template — Essay:** SCoPE as above. **Template — Report:** add data sources + methodology section + citations. **Template — Literature summary:** "10 papers, 2 lines each: contribution / limitation / relevance to my question." **Template — Problem set help:** "Do NOT solve. Give the first-step hint. Await my answer, then check it."

## Self-Critique Loop (The 30-Second Upgrade)

After every AI response, ask yourself:

- Did I give enough **context**?
- Was the **objective** precise or fuzzy?
- Is the **format** what I need?
- Did I include an **example**?

Then re-ask with one improvement. Prompt engineering is iterative — your second prompt is always your best prompt.

## Key Takeaways

- Use **SCoPE**: System, Context, Objective, Parameters, Example.
- Chain **Research → Draft → Refine**; never one-shot the final product.
- Control tone, depth, structure, and format explicitly.
- Build a **prompt bank** of reusable academic templates.
- Iterate: 30 seconds of self-critique dramatically improves results.

## Practice Challenge

1. **Upgrade a prompt:** Take one weak prompt you recently used for studies and rewrite it with full SCoPE. Save both versions.
2. **Run the chain:** Use Research → Draft → Refine on one real course topic. Save the three outputs and note how quality improved each pass.
3. **Bank two templates:** Create a reusable "Essay" and "Literature summary" template in your notes, ready for your next two assignments.
4. **~30-second critique:** Pick an AI answer from today and improve it with one context addition — then show the before/after to a study partner.
