## Why This Course Matters for YOU

Whether you're studying engineering, commerce, medicine, arts, or law — AI is now a standard tool in every field. It's not a fancy extra anymore; it's how work gets done. By learning it now, you get an edge:

- **Studies:** faster research, better assignments, smarter revision.
- **Portfolio:** projects that impress recruiters during internships and placements.
- **Career:** roles are increasingly "AI-augmented" — your skills plus AI beat AI alone.

But this course is not about replacing your thinking with AI. It's about **augmenting** it — working *with* AI the way a pilot works *with* an autopilot: you stay in command.

## What AI Really Is (A College-Level Honest View)

- **AI** is the broad field of systems that perform tasks requiring intelligence: understanding language, images, data, and decisions.
- **Machine Learning (ML)** is how AI learns — from data and examples, not hand-written rules.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs)** like GPT, Claude, and Gemini are trained on huge text corpora to generate and understand language.
- **Generative AI** creates new content: text, code, images, audio, video.
- **Agents & Automation** chain AI actions into workflows that do real work.

## What AI Can NOT Do (Be Honest, It Matters)

- It **hallucinates** — confidently states false information. Always verify.
- It has a **knowledge cutoff** — it may not know recent events without tools.
- It **doesn't "know" your context** — your college, your assignment rubric, your deadline.
- It **won't make you learn** — passive copy-pasting creates "fluent ignorance" that shows up in interviews and exams.

Your university years are about building judgment. AI is the sharpest tool you'll ever own for that — but only if **you** supply the judgment.

## Getting Set Up (Week 1 Toolkit)

Create accounts (free tiers are fine) for **one or two** of each:

- **Chat assistant:** ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
- **Research helper:** Google Scholar + one AI research tool.
- **Coding companion:** GitHub Copilot (student tier) or an AI code assistant.
- **Note organisation:** Notion/Obsidian + an AI note-taker.

Then **calibrate one tool** this week: ask it 5 questions from your major and rate how useful the answers are. This builds your "trust meter" — knowing when AI is reliable and when to check.

## The Academic Rule (Non-Negotiable)

- Follow **your university's** AI policy. Many allow AI with disclosure; some restrict it.
- Cite AI use like any source when required (your department's guide governs; more in Mission 4).
- Never submit AI output as your own assessed work unless permitted.
- When in doubt — **ask your professor**. Transparency is a feature, not a bug.

## Key Takeaways

- AI fluency is now a baseline skill across every discipline.
- College is about judgment — you command the tool, the tool never commands you.
- LLMs hallucinate and have cutoffs: treat output as a draft to verify, never gospel.
- Follow your institution's AI policy and disclose honestly.
- Your 10-mission path ends in a portfolio-worthy project.
