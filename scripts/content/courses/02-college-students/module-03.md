### Module 1 · Research: Where AI Helps (and Where It Must Not)

AI is a research **accelerator**, not a research **source**. Get this boundary wrong and your literature review will collapse the moment someone questions a source. Get it right, and AI saves you days while your research stays defensible.

The boundary is simple: AI can discover, summarise, and roadmap your reading. It must never stand in for the primary sources you will cite, the discovery of specific papers, or the judgment of what is rigorous in your field. Those stay yours.

## Learning Objectives

- Classify tasks as safe or unsafe for AI in research
- Use AI for discovery, summarising, and roadmapping
- Explain why AI must never find the papers you cite
- Keep judgment about rigour as a human responsibility

## What AI Is Great At

- **Discovery:** finding debates, key authors, and related topics you did not know existed
- **Summarising:** compressing a paper into its core argument in a few minutes
- **Roadmapping:** building a reading list in the order your brain needs it
### How discovery looks in practice
- Prompt for the key debates and author names in a topic before you search
- Use summaries to triage what deserves your full attention
- Let AI order a reading list by dependency, not by accident

## What AI Must Never Do

```text
The red lines in research:
1. Facts you will cite - read the primary source yourself
2. Finding specific papers - AI can invent plausible titles
3. Judgment - what is rigorous vs shallow in your field
```
- Never let AI pick the exact papers for your reference list
- Never trust an AI description over the actual paper
- Rigor judgment stays with you and your supervisor

## The Accelerator, Not Source, Mindset

### Why the boundary protects you
- An AI-suggested paper you have never opened is a liability in a viva
- The accelerator mindset keeps AI as a time-saver, not a truth-source
### How to stay on the right side
- Every fact you carry forward must trace to a source you can open
- Use AI output as pointers, then verify each one yourself
- Speed comes from the workflow, not from skipping verification

## Key Takeaways

- AI accelerates research; it never substitutes for sources
- Safe uses: discovery, summarising, and reading roadmaps
- Unsafe uses: citing, finding specific papers, and rigor judgment
- AI can invent titles - ground truth is the search engine and database
- The accelerator mindset protects you in vivas and defences

### Module 2 · Step 1: Sharpen Your Research Question

A fuzzy question gives fuzzy research. Before any search, sharpening your research question is the highest-value step - and AI is an excellent sparring partner for it. A good question is **specific, answerable, and bounded**: who or what, where, and when.

The trick is to let AI stress-test your draft rather than just approve it. Ask it for reasons your question is too broad or vague, then for a sharper version. You will be surprised how often the critique is right - and how much clearer your reading list becomes.

## Learning Objectives

- Define what makes a research question specific, answerable, and bounded
- Sharpen a broad topic into a bounded research question
- Use AI to stress-test your question's scope
- Explain why a sharp question accelerates the whole review

## Sharp Versus Broad

- **Too broad:** 'What are the effects of social media?'
- **Sharper:** 'How does passive social-media use affect first-year students' exam performance in Indian universities, 2020-2025?'
### The three tests
- Specific: does it pin the variables?
- Answerable: can evidence actually address it?
- Bounded: does it name who, where, and when?

## Stress-Test With AI

```text
The stress-test prompt:
Give me 5 reasons my research question is
too broad or vague, then suggest a sharper version.
```
- Ask for critique, not approval - that is where the value is
- Compare the AI's sharper version against your own revision
- Combine both into your final scope before searching

## Why Sharpness Wins

### Sharp questions make searchable keywords
- A bounded question yields clear keywords, operators, and filters
- Searches converge instead of returning everything
### Sharp questions make grading easier
- Markers reward a question that meets your timeframe
- Your evidence gathering becomes targeted, not scattered
- Sharpen first; every later step moves faster

## Key Takeaways

- Strong questions are specific, answerable, and bounded
- Broad topics are the start, never the end of question-sharpening
- AI stress-testing beats AI approval for improving scope
- Sharp questions make keyword search and evidence gathering efficient
- Sharpen the question before any search effort

### Module 3 · Step 2: Build a Search Strategy

A sharp question needs a sharp search strategy. This means extracting keywords and synonyms, combining them with Boolean operators, and adding the filters that pin your scope. The AI can draft the search strings - but you must run them yourself.

The division of labour is the whole point: **the AI suggests, the database confirms**. You never cite a paper the search engine did not actually show you. That single habit keeps your reference list honest.

## Learning Objectives

- Extract keywords and synonyms from a research question
- Build Boolean search strings with AND, OR, and quotes
- Add scope filters such as publication site and year range
- Explain why you must run searches yourself, not trust AI results

## From Question to Keywords

- 'passive use' expands to 'passive consumption', 'scrolling', 'binge use'
- Each concept in the question contributes one keyword cluster
### The expansion rule
- List synonyms and near-synonyms for every key term
- Try both formal and colloquial phrasing your field uses
- Keep the list in your notes so searches stay reproducible

## Boolean Operators and Filters

```text
Example search string:
"passive use" AND exam AND (university OR college)
```
- **AND** narrows: all terms must appear
- **OR** broadens within a concept cluster
- **Quotes** force the exact phrase to appear
- Add scope: site: for Google Scholar, or a year range

## The AI Suggests, the Database Confirms

### Why running it yourself matters
- A paper that appears only in AI output is not real until a database shows it
- Never cite a paper the search engine did not actually return
### The workflow to use
- Have AI draft three Boolean strings from your keywords
- Run them in Google Scholar and your library database
- Keep only the top papers your own searches actually returned

## Key Takeaways

- A search strategy starts from keyword and synonym extraction
- AND, OR, and quotes make your search strings precise
- Site and year filters pin the scope of your question
- The AI drafts strings; you run them in real databases
- Never cite a paper the database did not actually show you

## Practice Challenge

1. Extract keyword clusters for your sharpened research question
2. Ask AI to draft three Boolean strings, then run them in two databases
3. Save the top five real papers your own searches returned

**Example Quiz**

### Module 4 · Step 3: Read and Annotate With AI

Reading a dense paper is a skill AI can compress - without replacing the reading. The method is a two-pass loop: a framing summary that gives you the shape of the paper, then a critical pass that lists strengths and weaknesses for you to check against the text itself.

The crucial habit is to annotate your own notes after a summary, never to paste the AI's summary into your notes as 'read'. This sketch-first, then verify loop lets you triage ten papers in the time you used to spend on two.

## Learning Objectives

- Use a framing prompt to summarise a paper's core structure
- Run a critical second pass on strengths and weaknesses
- Annotate your own notes after reading a summary
- Explain how the loop triages many papers quickly

## Framing Prompt First

- **Prompt:** 'I am reading this paper for {purpose}. Summarise in 200 words: core claim, method, key finding, limitation'
- The purpose line makes the summary targeted to your need
- The four parts map exactly to what a reviewer must know
### Why framing beats open summarising
- Open requests return generic blurbs
- Framed requests return what your review actually needs

## Second Pass: Strength and Weakness

```text
The critical second-pass prompt:
List the 3 strongest and 3 weakest
points of this paper.
```
- Then check them against the abstract and discussion yourself
- Weaknesses you spot yourself are the ones you will remember
- Compare your own reading to the AI's list before trusting it

## Annotate Your Own Notes

### Sketch first, then verify
- Write the summary in your own words, citation attached
- Never paste an AI summary into notes as if you read the paper
### Why the loop wins
- Triaging ten papers becomes affordable in one sitting
- The papers that survive triage get deep, verified reading
- Your notes stay honest and viva-proof

## Key Takeaways

- The framing prompt captures claim, method, finding, and limitation
- A critical second pass lists strengths and weaknesses to verify
- Annotate your own notes after a summary - never paste as 'read'
- The sketch-first, verify loop triages ten papers fast
- Your notes stay honest and defensible throughout

### Module 5 · The Verification Rule, Non-Negotiable

Every fact you carry forward must survive one rule: **trace it to a source you can open**. This is the verification rule, and it is not about distrusting tools - it is about research hygiene. Violating it is how AI-generated literature reviews collapse under one easy question in a viva.

The practical checklist is short: read the abstract and conclusion of any paper before trusting a summary of it, re-find exact quotes in the original, trust databases over AI summaries when they conflict, and attach the link plus source line to every note from day one.

## Learning Objectives

- State the verification rule in your own words
- Read the abstract and conclusion before trusting any summary
- Re-find exact quotes and numbers in the original text
- Attach link and source lines to every note from day one

## The Rule and Its Checklist

- **Rule:** trace every fact to a source you can open
- Read the **abstract and conclusion** of any paper before trusting an AI summary
- Re-find exact quotes and numbers in the original text
- Trust **databases and the paper itself** over any AI summary on conflict
### The habit that makes it painless
- Attach the link plus source line the moment you make a note

## Why AI Summaries Slip

```text
Common summary errors to catch:
1. AI paraphrases and invents digits
2. AI merges findings across papers
3. AI infers a sample size wrongly
4. AI imports context from another source
```
- Paraphrase and invented digits are the classic failure
- Each must be checked against the actual paper
- The abstract and conclusion are the fastest verification points

## The Hygiene That Defends You

### Verification is a workflow, not an event
- Make the link-plus-source line automatic in every note
- Batch-verify before writing, not at the last minute
### What it buys you
- A review that survives a viva, a reviewer, and a supervisor
- Confidence that every number in your draft is real
- Speed, because verified notes are reusable across assignments

## Key Takeaways

- The verification rule: trace every fact to a source you can open
- Read the abstract and conclusion before trusting any summary
- AI paraphrases and invents digits - re-find exact quotes yourself
- Databases and the paper beat AI summaries when they conflict
- Link plus source lines from day one make verification painless

### Module 6 · Manage References Like a Pro

Reference management is 90 percent habit and 10 percent tool. Start with Zotero (free) or a similar manager early - it costs minutes now and saves days later. The AI joins in only for safe tasks: converting formats you already hold and drafting the citation narrative.

The rule that keeps you safe is the same as everywhere else: the AI formats what you verify, and it never generates your bibliography from scratch. The DOI and URL must come from the real item.

## Learning Objectives

- Set up a reference manager early in your course
- Use AI to convert reference formats safely
- Use AI to draft citation narratives you then verify
- Re-check citations against your department's style guide

## Manager First

- Use **Zotero** (free) or a similar manager from week one
- Capture every source with its link and metadata as you find it
### Why the habit beats the tool
- The tool only helps if you capture as you go
- Fixing a messy reference set at the end costs days

## What AI Can Safely Do

```text
Safe AI reference tasks:
1. Formatting 6 references into APA 7th style
2. Keeping the author order intact
3. Drafting the citation narrative sentence
4. Flagging items you still must verify
```
- Prompt: 'Turn these 6 references into APA 7th style, keeping author order'
- The DOI and URL must always come from the real item
- You verify; it formats

## The Final Style Check

### Format disputes are the easiest marks to lose
- Re-check with your department's style guide before submission
- Citation styles differ subtly between disciplines and years
### What a tidy reference set buys you
- The difference between great research and great research you can defend
- A clean export that survives reviewers and submission systems
- A reusable library for the rest of your degree

## Key Takeaways

- Reference management is 90 percent habit, 10 percent tool
- Capture every source in a manager from the moment you find it
- AI converts formats you hold; it never generates a bibliography
- DOIs and URLs always come from the real item
- Re-check against your department's style guide before submitting
