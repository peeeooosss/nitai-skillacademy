# Mission 3: AI for Research & Literature Review

## Learning Objectives
- Build a research question and search strategy with AI
- Use AI to plan a literature review without skipping real sources
- Summarise, compare, and synthesise papers like a reviewer
- Detect AI hallucinations and verify every source citation
- Manage references with AI-supported workflows

---
### Module 1 · Research: Where AI Helps (and Where It Must Not)
AI is a research **accelerator**, not a research **source**. Use it for:

- **Discovery:** finding debates, key authors, and related topics you didn't know existed.
- **Summarising:** compressing a paper into its core argument in a few minutes.
- **Roadmapping:** building a reading list in the order your brain needs it.

Never use it for:

- **Facts you will cite** — you must read the primary source yourself.
- **Finding specific papers** — AI can invent titles; search engines and databases are ground truth.
- **Judgment** — what is rigorous vs shallow in your field is not something to outsource.

**Example Quiz**

1. Where does AI NOT belong in research?
- A) Facts you will cite directly without reading the source
- B) Discovery of debates to explore
- C) Summarising dense papers
- D) Building a reading roadmap

**Answer:** A — Primary sources are ground truth for anything you will cite.

2. Why must you never let AI find the exact papers for citation?
- A) It can invent plausible titles
- B) It is slow
- C) It is expensive
- D) It needs your login

**Answer:** A — AI can fabricate references — search engines and databases are ground truth.

3. What can AI do reliably in research?
- A) Compress a paper into its core argument
- B) Guarantee a paper's validity
- C) Provide your syllabus
- D) Score your peers

**Answer:** A — Summarising for triage is reliable; judgment about rigour stays yours.

### Module 2 · Step 1: Sharpen Your Research Question
A fuzzy question gives fuzzy research. Sharpen yours before any search:

- **Too broad:** "What are the effects of social media?"
- **Sharper:** "How does passive social-media use affect first-year students' exam performance in Indian universities, 2020-2025?"

A strong research question is **specific, answerable, and bounded** (who/what, where, when). Prompt the AI to stress-test yours: *"Give me 5 reasons my research question is too broad or vague, then suggest a sharper version."* You'll be surprised how often it's right.

**Example Quiz**

- B) What are the effects of social media?
- C) Is social media good?
- D) Social media and exams

**Answer:** A — Sharp questions are specific, answerable and bounded.

- B) Only the buzzwords
- C) As many commas as possible
- D) A yes/no with one-word scope

3. How can AI stress-test your research question?
- A) Ask it to list vagueness and suggest sharper versions
- B) It cannot help at all
- C) Ask it to confirm your first draft
- D) Ignore it completely

**Answer:** A — AI's critique plus your judgement sharpens scope.

### Module 3 · Step 2: Build a Search Strategy
Turn your question into a search strategy:

1. Extract **keywords** and synonyms: "passive use" → "passive consumption", "scrolling", "binge use".
2. Combine with **AND / OR / "quotes"**: `"passive use" AND exam AND (university OR college)`.
3. Add the **what** (AND "exam performance") and the **where** (site: for Google Scholar, or the year range).
4. Ask AI to draft the search strings, but **run them yourself** in Google Scholar and your library database.

The AI suggests; the database confirms. You never cite a paper the search engine didn't actually show you.

### Module 4 · Step 3: Read and Annotate With AI
Reading a dense paper is a skill AI can compress — without replacing the reading:

1. **Framing prompt:** *"I'm reading this paper for {purpose}. Summarise in 200 words: core claim, method, key finding, limitation."*
2. **Second pass:** *"List the 3 strongest and 3 weakest points."* — then check them against the abstract and discussion.
3. **Annotate your own notes** after reading the summary — never paste AI's summary into your notes as "read".

This "sketch first, then verify" loop lets you triage 10 papers in the time you used to spend on two.

### Module 5 · The Verification Rule (Non-Negotiable)
Every fact you carry forward must survive this rule: **trace it to a source you can open.**

- Read the **abstract and conclusion** of any paper before trusting an AI summary of it.
- Re-find exact quotes and numbers in the original text — AI loves to paraphrase and invent digits.
- Trust **databases and the paper itself** over any AI "summary" when there's a conflict.
- Keep your research notes with the **link + source line** attached from day one.

This rule is not about distrusting tools — it's about research hygiene. Violating it is how AI-generated literature reviews collapse under one easy question in a viva.

### Module 6 · Manage References Like a Pro
Reference management is 90% habit, 10% tool:

- Use **Zotero** (free) or a similar manager early — costs minutes now, saves days later.
- Use AI to **convert** formats: *"Turn these 6 references into APA 7th style, keeping author order."* You verify, it formats.
- Use AI to **draft the citation narrative** — but the DOI/URL must come from the real item.
- Re-check with your department's style guide before submission; format disputes are the easiest marks to lose.

A tidy reference set is also the difference between "great research" and "great research you can defend".

## Real-World Examples

- A business student pre-scans 20 marketing papers with AI, picks the six worth reading, and reads those fully.
- A literature student asks AI to draft Boolean search strings, then runs them in the library database himself.
- An engineering team keeps a shared Zotero library; AI converts a messy export into one consistent citation style.
- A nursing student catches an AI 'summary' that mis-stated a study's sample size by re-reading the methods section.

## Key Takeaways
- AI organises research thinking; real scholars + databases supply sources.
- Sharpen questions and searches with AI, then search yourself.
- Use AI for triage, comparison tables, and synthesis — always keep final judgment.
- Verify every citation (DOI + open the paper). Assume hallucination until proven.
- Reference managers + clean annotations = half the review written in advance.

## Practice Challenge

4. **Set up Zotero/Mendeley:** Add those 5 papers with clean metadata and export one APA 7 reference correctly.
