### Module 2 · AI-Assisted Interview Practice

Rehearse with AI like a real drill, not a script. The routine has three layers: build a question bank for your target role, simulate a live interviewer who gives feedback after each answer, and record your best answers under a sixty-second limit.

Then complete the loop with one real human mock. AI covers volume; a human covers the awkward, real parts. Volume plus authenticity equals confidence.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a twenty-question bank for your target role
- Simulate a live interviewer with feedback
- Record and time your practice answers
- Complete the routine with a human mock

## Build the Question Bank

- **Prompt:** 'Give me 20 likely questions for a {role} internship interview, including 5 behavioural and 3 technical about my own project'
- Include behavioural, technical, and project-follow-up questions
- Anchor the technical ones to your actual project
### Why your project
- Your project is the one area you control completely
- Interviewers probe it because it is real

## Simulate Like a Drill

```text
The simulation prompt:
Interview me one question at a time.
After each answer, give brief feedback
and the next question.
```
- One question at a time builds poise
- Immediate feedback fixes answers as you go
- The drill layers real feedback at each stage

## Volume Plus Authenticity

### The recording step
- Record your best answers and practise under sixty seconds each
### The human mock
- Finish with a friend, mentor, or professor
- AI gives volume; humans cover the awkward, real parts
- Both kinds of practice build different skills
- Authentic delivery beats a memorised script

## Key Takeaways

- Build a question bank of twenty with behavioural and technical mix
- Simulate one question at a time with feedback
- Record answers and time them under sixty seconds
- Complete with one real human mock
- Volume plus authenticity equals confidence

## Practice Challenge

1. Generate your twenty-question bank with AI

3. Book a human mock with a friend or mentor this week

### Module 3 · Presenting Your Project Live

A live demo beats a rehearsed speech. Present in a crisp structure: a sixty-second pitch of the problem, your build, the result, and what you would do next; walk the core flow live; show one thing that broke and how you fixed it; and end with an invitation question.

Practise the pitch three times out loud with a timer, present it to a friend first, and let your honesty rules from Mission 9 pay off in full during live Q&A.

## Learning Objectives

- Structure a sixty-second project pitch
- Walk the core flow live
- Show one failure and its recovery
- Close with an invitation question

## The Sixty-Second Structure

- **The problem** - the pain the project solves
- **Your build** - what you made
- **The result** - the measurable outcome
- **What next** - the direction you would take it
### The order rule
- Purpose, build, result, next step - live and tight

## Walk the Core Flow

```text
The live-walk rules:
1. Walk the one flow that always works
2. Do it live, never a recording
3. Show one thing that broke and how you fixed it
4. End with an invitation to go deeper
```
- Failure-and-recovery is a strength signal
- Live code is the most credible proof of all

## Close With an Invitation

### The invitation frame
- End with: 'Want to see how I would scale it?'
- Questions invite the conversation you want
### Why it works
- A question keeps the discussion in your court
- It shows hunger and direction, not just completion
- Live Q&A is where your honesty rules pay off

## Key Takeaways

- Present problem, build, result, and next step in sixty seconds
- Walk the core flow live - never on a recording
- Show one failure and its recovery as a strength
- Close with an invitation question
- Live Q&A is where honesty rules pay off

### Module 4 · The Ethics Framework: Your Permanent Guide

Carry one framework that answers any AI-ethics question. Five words - **transparency, ownership, fairness, verification, judgment** - filter almost every dilemma professors and hiring managers throw at students. Memorise them and run any scenario through them.

Transparency means saying how AI helped. Ownership means you stay accountable for what you produce. Fairness means not letting AI amplify bias. Verification means tracing every fact to a source. Judgment means AI suggests and the human decides.

## Learning Objectives

- Name the five words of the ethics framework
- Explain each word in one line
- Run a dilemma through the filter in ten seconds
- Defend the framework in one sentence

## The Five Words

- **Transparency:** when AI helped, say how
- **Ownership:** you remain accountable for what you produce with AI
- **Fairness:** do not let AI amplify bias in data or decisions
- **Verification:** every fact and number you present is traced to a source
- **Judgment:** AI suggests; the human decides
### The one-sentence defence
- 'AI suggests, humans decide, and everything is verified and disclosed.'

## Run Any Dilemma Through It

```text
The ten-second filter:
1. Am I being transparent about AI?
2. Do I own this output?
3. Is it fair to everyone affected?
4. Can I verify every fact?
5. Did a human make the call?
```
- A fast personal filter beats slow rulebook dependence
- Nearly every dilemma fails or passes on one of these

## Why Five Words Beat a Rulebook

### The memory advantage
- Five words travel; rulebooks stay on the shelf
- The filter works in the middle of a conversation
### Its scope
- It answers prompts, projects, tools, and submissions
- The same filter scales from a homework answer to a product
- It is your permanent guide, not a course-only rule

## Key Takeaways

- The framework is transparency, ownership, fairness, verification, judgment
- Transparency says how AI helped; ownership keeps you accountable
- Fairness checks bias; verification traces every fact
- Judgment means AI suggests and the human decides
- Five words travel where a rulebook cannot

### Module 6 · Your Next Ninety Days (After Graduation)

Graduation is not the end of the learning loop - the skills compound if you keep them fed. The next ninety days are the launch of a lifelong skill stack, and the plan is deliberately simple: keep shipping, join a community, hold a learning budget, and re-run the ethics filter.

Students who keep the loop alive after the course outpace everyone who stopped at the certificate. Start your next ninety days on purpose, not by accident.

## Learning Objectives

- Plan the three blocks of your next ninety days
- Commit to a weekly learning budget
- Re-run the ethics filter on new tools
- Treat ninety days as a deliberate launch

## The Three Blocks

- **Weeks 1-4:** keep shipping one small thing a month; update your portfolio with each
- **Months 2-3:** join one community - alumni, open source, or a LinkedIn niche group - and make one visible contribution
- **Set a learning budget:** two hours a week on one AI skill beats five hours once a month
### The compounding rule
- Consistent small shipping compounds the skills

## Learning Budget Over Marathon

```text
The cadence test:
A. 2 focused hours every week
B. 5 hours once a month
Which builds more skill?
A - weekly wins every time
```
- Weekly consistency beats rare bursts
- One skill at a time, two hours, every week

## Keep the Compass On

### Re-run the ethics filter
- Apply the five words to every new tool before adoption
- Adoption should pass the same five-word test
### Why the loop outlasts the course
- The filter scales from prompts to whole platforms
- Shipping keeps the portfolio alive and honest
- Your ninety days are a launch, deliberately started

## Key Takeaways

- Weeks 1-4 ship one small thing monthly
- Months 2-3 join a community and contribute visibly
- Two focused hours weekly beat a five-hour monthly spree
- Re-run the ethics filter on every new tool
- The next ninety days are a deliberate launch
