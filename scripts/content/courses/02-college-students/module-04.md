# Mission 4: AI for Assignments, Reports & Citations

## Learning Objectives
- Use AI to draft, structure, and improve assignments responsibly
- Apply citation formats correctly with AI assistance
- Write clean academic prose that stays in your own voice
- Refine by rubric: align AI output to marking criteria
- Know exactly when disclosure of AI use is required

---
### Module 1 · The Assignments Workflow: Plan → Draft → Rubric → Polish
The professional way to use AI on assessed work is a **4-stage pipeline**:

1. **Plan** — use AI to interrogate the brief and build an outline (never to "write the essay").
2. **Draft** — write section drafts with AI as a co-writer, you staying in command of the argument.
3. **Rubric** — run the official rubric against your draft and fix the gaps the marker will score.
4. **Polish** — edit for clarity, flow, and voice.

This pipeline keeps your work *yours* while making it dramatically better. Each stage is a different prompt and a different mindset — you're the project manager of the assignment.

- A) Plan → Draft → Rubric → Polish

### Module 2 · Stage 1 & 2: Outline Then Section Drafts
**Outline first, always.** Prompt: *"Given this brief, propose a 5-section structure with a one-line thesis for each section, and flag the 2 sections where I'll need evidence."* You revise it to match your course material.

Then draft **section by section**, pasting your own notes into each prompt: *"Using my notes in quotes below, draft section 2 as an argument, not an encyclopaedia entry. Add a topic sentence and a transition to the next section."*

Drafting in slices keeps the AI on-topic and keeps your voice in control — never one giant "write my essay" prompt.

### Module 3 · Stage 3: Rubric-Driven Refinement
The rubric is the exam paper — and the AI can read it too:

1. **Paste the rubric** + your draft.
2. Prompt: *"Score this draft against each rubric row out of 10. For the two lowest rows, rewrite the relevant part to meet the top-band description."*
3. Review its changes skeptically — only accept edits that genuinely improve the argument.

This turns "polishing" from guesswork into a checklist. Students who run this loop reliably stop leaving easy points on the table — structure, use of evidence, and depth of analysis.

### Module 4 · Stage 4: Polish Like an Editor
Polish is not the same as rewriting. Run targeted passes:

- **Clarity:** *"Cut every sentence that adds length without meaning. Keep the academic tone."*
- **Flow:** *"Improve transitions between paragraphs 3 and 4 without changing facts or my voice."*
- **Redundancy:** *"Find repeated ideas across sections and consolidate."*

Keep your own sentences. The goal is a text that *sounds like you, but sharper*. Run each pass on sections, not the whole document, so your identity doesn't blur into AI-generic prose.

### Module 5 · Citations: AI Formats, YOU Verify
Generative AI is a **formatter**, never a source. Its citation rule is simple:

- Use it to **convert** references you already hold into the required style (APA/MLA/Chicago/Harvard).
- **Never** ask it to "find references" and trust the result — it will invent plausible-looking DOIs (one of the most common AI-citation disasters).
- **Verify every entry** against the real item or your reference manager before submission.

A reference manager like Zotero (Mission 3) makes this painful task a two-minute job and de-risks your entire reference list.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is AI's citation superpower?
- A) Formatting references you already hold
- B) Inventing DOIs you can trust
- C) Locating rare books
- D) Choosing your topic

**Answer:** A — Format conversion is safe; invented references are a top hazard.

2. What is the #1 AI-citation disaster to avoid?
- A) Trusting AI to 'find' references
- B) Using Zotero
- C) Alpha ordering
- D) Double spacing

**Answer:** A — It will paste plausible-looking plausible DOIs that do not exist.

3. Why verify every entry against the real item?
- A) Only verified entries survive submission and review
- B) To fill time
- C) Because journals demand it
- D) For page count

**Answer:** A — Verification is the only thing between your list and a fabrication.

### Module 6 · Voice, Integrity & Disclosure
Assessed work should read as **yours**. Three habits protect you:

- **Draft from your own notes**, use AI as editor — the argument and evidence stay yours.
- **Disclosure, when required:** state how AI helped (grammar check, outline brainstorm, reference formatting) in a short note. Follow your university's policy exactly.
- **Double-check submissions:** run your final text past the relevant checks if your course requires them, and keep your AI-usage log.

In interviews, recruiters ask *how* you used AI — good integrity is a differentiator, not a confession. Transparency beats evasion, always.

## Real-World Examples

- A management student runs her professor's rubric over every draft and stops losing marks on structure.
- A history student uses AI to outline, then writes each paragraph from her own primary-source notes.
- A final-year team converts 40 references to one consistent style in 20 minutes via AI plus Zotero.
- A CS student attaches a one-line 'AI used for grammar and outline' note to each lab report.

## Key Takeaways
- Workflow: **Plan → Draft → Rubric → Polish → Cite+Disclose**.
- Draft section-by-section from YOUR notes; keep >70% of prose in your voice.
- Grade your own drafts against the rubric before submitting.
- Never trust AI-generated references — format real ones, verify all.
- Disclose AI use whenever in doubt; it protects you academically.

## Practice Challenge
1. **Brief to checklist:** Take your current or next assignment brief and ask AI to turn it into a 5-point requirements checklist. Keep it pinned.
2. **Rubric pre-flight:** Against your assignment's rubric, get AI to grade one of your *older* drafts and list 3 fixes. Apply those to your current work.
3. **Voice rewrite:** Pick one AI-drafted paragraph and rewrite it so it sounds like you. Compare. Note which ideas you changed.
4. **Disclosure note:** Write your standard "AI use disclosure" sentence for this semester and confirm it matches your department policy.
