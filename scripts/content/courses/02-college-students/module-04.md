# Mission 4: AI for Assignments, Reports & Citations

## Learning Objectives
- Use AI to draft, structure, and improve assignments responsibly
- Apply citation formats correctly with AI assistance
- Write clean academic prose that stays in your own voice
- Refine by rubric: align AI output to marking criteria
- Know exactly when disclosure of AI use is required

---

## The Assignments Workflow: Plan → Draft → Rubric → Polish

Treat AI like a writing tutor, not a ghostwriter. A five-stage workflow:

1. **PLAN** — Understand the brief: "List the 5 requirements of this brief and turn them into a checklist."
2. **DRAFT** — Outline first, then section-by-section drafting with your own material.
3. **RUBRIC** — Align output to the marking criteria before you write.
4. **POLISH** — Improve flow, clarity, and precision — not word count padding.
5. **CITE + DISCLOSE** — Real citations, honest AI-use note.

## Stage 1 & 2: Outline Then Section Drafts

- **Outline prompt:** "Given this brief [paste], produce an outline: thesis, 3 sections, each with 2 supporting points and the evidence I plan to use." Draft the thesis **yourself** — it's the heart of your grade.
- **Section-by-section:** Never ask for the whole essay. Ask for one section at a time and feed it YOUR notes, lecture slides, and sources: "Using my notes below, draft the methods section in 250 words."
- **Voice protection:** after drafting, put the section in your own words from your outline. If you can't re-explain it, you don't own it yet — and it will show.

## Stage 3: Rubric-Driven Refinement

Professors grade against criteria. So should you:

- "Here's the marking rubric [paste]. For each criterion, tell me what a high-scoring submission must include, and what common errors lose marks."
- "Grade my draft against this rubric: 1–5 per criterion, with one concrete fix each." Then fix, re-ask, iterate.

This turns AI into a **pre-submission examiner** — one of the highest-leverage moves in this course.

## Stage 4: Polish Like an Editor

- "Tighten this paragraph by 20% without losing meaning."
- "Replace every weak pass ('it can be seen that') with stronger wording — suggest edits inline."
- "Check flow between section 2 and 3: identify the 2 biggest logical jumps."
- "Rewrite this sentence 3 ways: simple, academic, and concise. I'll choose."
- **Never** ask AI to "make it longer" — length padding is the fastest way to bury your real argument and trigger educator suspicion.

## Citations: AI Formats, YOU Verify

- **Never trust AI-generated references.** Use your reference manager and your library.
- Use AI to format real references: "Format these details in APA 7 / IEEE / Harvard" — then compare with your style guide.
- In-text: keep the citation *next to the fact* as you write, not added later. AI can help you place them: "Where should a citation for [claim] go in this paragraph?"
- If your university allows AI assistance: use the specific disclosure format your department requires.

## Voice & Academic Integrity

- **Yours-to-AI ratio:** aim for >70% of every sentence being your own construction. AI drafts, you rewrite.
- **Plagiarism tools** flag AI patterns (uniform sentence rhythm, over-perfect structure). Deviate: add your examples, your phrasing, your roughness.
- **Turnitin-style AI detection** is inconsistent — the real defense is honesty and genuine ownership, not "beating the detector."

## When Disclosing Is Required

- **Always** if your syllabus says so, or if AI materially helped generate content.
- Short note style: "AI was used to brainstorm and refine drafts; all analysis and writing are my own. AI-assisted drafting was used with [professor's] permission." (Adapt to your department's format.)
- **When in doubt, ask the professor.** This one habit saves more students than any prompt trick.

## Key Takeaways

- Workflow: **Plan → Draft → Rubric → Polish → Cite+Disclose**.
- Draft section-by-section from YOUR notes; keep >70% of prose in your voice.
- Grade your own drafts against the rubric before submitting.
- Never trust AI-generated references — format real ones, verify all.
- Disclose AI use whenever in doubt; it protects you academically.

## Practice Challenge

1. **Brief to checklist:** Take your current or next assignment brief and ask AI to turn it into a 5-point requirements checklist. Keep it pinned.
2. **Rubric pre-flight:** Against your assignment's rubric, get AI to grade one of your *older* drafts and list 3 fixes. Apply those to your current work.
3. **Voice rewrite:** Pick one AI-drafted paragraph and rewrite it so it sounds like you. Compare. Note which ideas you changed.
4. **Disclosure note:** Write your standard "AI use disclosure" sentence for this semester and confirm it matches your department policy.
