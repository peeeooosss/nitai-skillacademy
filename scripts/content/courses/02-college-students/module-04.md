### Module 1 · The Assignments Workflow: Plan to Rubric to Polish

The professional way to use AI on assessed work is a **four-stage pipeline**: Plan, Draft, Rubric, Polish. Each stage is a different prompt and a different mindset - you stay the project manager of the assignment, and the work stays yours.

Plan uses AI to interrogate the brief and build an outline. Draft writes section by section with you in command of the argument. Rubric runs the official marking criteria against your draft. Polish edits for clarity, flow, and voice. Slicing the stages is what keeps your work your own.

## Learning Objectives

- Name the four stages of the assignment workflow
- Explain what each stage asks of the AI
- Describe why the pipeline protects ownership of your work
- Apply the pipeline to your current assignment

## The Four-Stage Pipeline

1. **Plan** - use AI to interrogate the brief and build an outline, never to write the essay
2. **Draft** - write section drafts with AI as co-writer while you command the argument
3. **Rubric** - run the official rubric against your draft and fix the gaps
4. **Polish** - edit for clarity, flow, and voice
### Why the order holds
- Every earlier stage feeds the next
- Skipping Plan makes Draft wander; skipping Rubric loses easy marks

## You Are the Project Manager

```text
The project manager mindset:
1. You define the brief and the argument
2. You decide which AI help to accept
3. You gate every AI edit against your own judgment
4. You own the final submission
```
- The AI is a fast contractor; you are the manager
- Each stage has a different prompt and a different goal
- Ownership stays with you because every decision is yours

## Why the Pipeline Beats One-Shot Essays

### The 'write my essay' trap
- A single prompt hands your thinking to the tool
- Generic AI prose and lost marks follow instantly
### The pipeline difference
- Your argument and evidence stay yours at every stage
- The work is dramatically better because each pass has one clear job
- You can explain every section - which is what markers test

## Key Takeaways

- The workflow is Plan, Draft, Rubric, Polish
- Each stage is a different prompt and a different mindset
- You are the project manager; AI is the fast contractor
- Slicing stages protects ownership of your work
- The pipeline keeps your voice and makes the work dramatically better

### Module 2 · Stage 1 and 2: Outline Then Section Drafts

Stage one is **outline first, always**. A short prompt turns the brief into a five-section structure with a one-line thesis per section and the places where you will need evidence. You revise that outline against your course material before any drafting begins.

Stage two drafts **section by section**, pasting your own notes into each prompt. Drafting in slices keeps the AI on-topic and keeps your voice in control - never one giant 'write my essay' prompt.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a five-section outline from a brief with AI
- Flag the sections where evidence is needed
- Draft section by section from your own notes
- Explain why slice drafting beats one-shot essays

## Outline First, Always

- **Prompt:** 'Given this brief, propose a 5-section structure with a one-line thesis for each, and flag the 2 sections where I will need evidence'
- Revise the AI's structure to match your course material
- The outline is the skeleton; all drafting hangs on it
### How to revise it
- Swap AI generic titles for your own phrasing
- Delete sections your brief does not need
- Mark the evidence-heavy spots before drafting

## Section-by-Section Drafts

```text
The section draft prompt:
Using my notes in quotes below, draft section 2
as an argument, not an encyclopedia entry.
Add a topic sentence and a transition to the next.
```
- Paste your own notes into the prompt so the AI drafts from them
- Ask for argument structure, not summary
- One section per prompt keeps the result on-topic

## Why Slice Drafting Wins

### Control over drift
- Small prompts cannot wander into generic AI prose
- Each section gets the exact treatment it needs
### Control over voice
- Your notes in the prompt keep the draft reflecting your argument
- You edit section by section instead of facing a wall of text
- Argument structure is what markers score in analysis banding

## Key Takeaways

- Outline first, always - revise the AI structure against your material
- Flag the evidence-heavy sections before drafting
- Draft section by section from your own notes
- Slice drafting prevents drift into generic AI prose
- Argument structure beats summary in marker banding

### Module 3 · Stage 3: Rubric-Driven Refinement

The rubric is the exam paper - and the AI can read it too. Stage three pastes the rubric and your draft side by side and asks the AI to score each row, then to repair the two weakest bands. This turns polishing from guesswork into a checklist.

Students who run this loop reliably stop leaving easy points on the table - structure, use of evidence, and depth of analysis improve every time. The discipline is to review the AI's repairs skeptically and accept only what genuinely improves the argument.

## Learning Objectives

- Use the rubric as a scoring checklist against your draft
- Ask AI to repair the two lowest bands
- Review AI rubric-repairs skeptically
- Explain why this loop stops lost marks

## Rubric-First Setting

- **Paste the rubric** and your draft into the same conversation
- The AI can only score what it can see side by side
- Keep the rubric's exact wording so rows match
### The extra advantage
- The rubric defines what 'good' means before you polish
- You target the bands a marker will actually score

## Score Then Repair

```text
The score-then-repair prompt:
Score this draft against each rubric row out of 10.
For the two lowest rows, rewrite the relevant part
to meet the top-band description.
```
- Scoring every row exposes the gaps
- Repair targets only the two weakest bands
- This keeps scope small and changes reviewable

## Skeptical Review

### The gate in the loop
- Review the AI's changes skeptically; accept only genuine improvements
- Rubric help is guidance; your judgment gates it
### What the loop reliably fixes
- Structure, use of evidence, and depth of analysis
- The marks students most often leave on the table
- Polishing becomes a checklist instead of a guess

## Key Takeaways

- The rubric is the exam paper - and the AI can read it too
- Paste rubric and draft side by side and score every row
- Repair only the two lowest bands to keep scope small
- Review AI repair skeptically; accept only real improvements
- The loop stops structure, evidence, and depth marks being lost

### Module 4 · Stage 4: Polish Like an Editor

Polish is not the same as rewriting. It is a set of targeted passes - clarity, flow, and redundancy - each run on sections rather than the whole document so your identity does not blur into AI-generic prose. The goal is a text that sounds like you, but sharper.

Run each pass with a specific prompt. Cut sentences that add length without meaning, improve transitions between paragraphs without changing facts, and consolidate repeated ideas across sections. Then keep your own sentences where they already work.

## Learning Objectives

- Run clarity, flow, and redundancy passes
- Explain why polish targets sections, not whole essays
- Keep your own sentences while sharpening the text
- Judge which AI polish edits to accept

## The Three Passes

- **Clarity:** 'Cut every sentence that adds length without meaning. Keep the academic tone'
- **Flow:** 'Improve transitions between paragraphs 3 and 4 without changing facts or my voice'
- **Redundancy:** 'Find repeated ideas across sections and consolidate'
### The pass discipline
- One pass at a time with one specific instruction
- Each pass has a different goal and a different acceptance test

## Polish Sections, Not the Whole

```text
Why section-scoped polish:
1. It prevents your identity blurring into generic AI prose
2. It keeps each change tiny and reviewable
3. It lets you keep your strongest sentences intact
4. It makes the last pass fast
```
- Whole-document passes homogenise the voice
- Small scoped passes sharpen without flattening
- You accept or reject each suggestion with full context

## Keep Your Voice

### The editor's goal
- A text that sounds like you, but sharper
- Polish fixes the surface; the argument and voice stay yours
### The acceptance rule
- Accept only edits that genuinely improve sound or clarity
- Reject changes that make it sound like someone else
- Run each pass on sections so identity never blurs

## Key Takeaways

- Polish is targeted passes: clarity, flow, redundancy
- Run passes on sections, not the whole document
- Section scope prevents your identity blurring into generic prose
- Accept only edits that genuinely sharpen your voice
- The goal is a text that sounds like you, only sharper

### Module 5 · Citations: AI Formats, YOU Verify

Generative AI is a **formatter**, never a source. Its citation rule is simple: use it to convert references you already hold into the required style, never to find references from scratch, and verify every entry against the real item before submission.

Asking AI to 'find references' and trusting the result is one of the most common AI-citation disasters - it will invent plausible-looking DOIs that do not exist. A reference manager makes verification a two-minute job and de-risks the entire reference list.

## Learning Objectives

- Use AI to convert references you already hold
- Explain why AI must never find references
- Verify every entry against the real item
- Use a reference manager to de-risk citations

## The Formatter Rule

- Use AI to **convert** references you hold into APA, MLA, Chicago, or Harvard
- Formatting is safe because the source is already real
- The output is a starting point for your verification, not the end
### What makes it safe
- You hold the item; the AI only changes its clothes
- Author order and titles must survive the conversion

## The Find-References Disaster

```text
How the disaster happens:
1. You ask AI to find references on a topic
2. It returns plausible titles with DOIs
3. Some DOIs do not exist
4. One examiner check destroys the list
```
- Never trust AI-found references for your bibliography
- Search engines and databases are the only ground truth
- Quiz your tool once and you will see it happens

## Verify With a Manager

### The verification step
- Check every entry against the real item or your reference manager
- A manager points to the stored source; you confirm the details
### Why this saves you
- Verification becomes minutes, not hours
- Your reference list survives submission and review
- The manager records what you actually read and used

## Key Takeaways

- AI is a citation formatter, never a source
- Convert references you already hold - never find them fresh
- Trusting AI-found DOIs is the number one citation disaster
- Verify every entry against the real item or your manager
- A reference manager makes verification a two-minute job

### Module 6 · Voice, Integrity and Disclosure

Assessed work should read as **yours**. Three habits protect you: draft from your own notes and use AI as editor, disclose your use exactly as your policy requires, and double-check submissions with your course's checks while keeping an AI-usage log.

In interviews, recruiters ask how you used AI - good integrity is a differentiator, not a confession. Transparency beats evasion, always, because a disclosed and verified assignment is a defensible assignment.

## Learning Objectives

- Draft from your own notes with AI as editor
- Disclose AI use exactly as your policy requires
- Keep an AI-usage log for every submission
- Explain why transparency is a differentiator

## Keep Your Voice in the Work

- **Draft from your own notes**, use AI as editor
- The argument and evidence stay yours even as AI sharpens the surface
- Run your final text past the relevant checks if your course requires them
### The ownership test
- Can you explain each section without the AI?
- If yes, the work is yours; if no, it is not ready

## Disclosure Done Right

```text
The disclosure habit:
1. Read your university's policy for the assessment
2. Note how AI helped in the submission
3. Keep the AI-usage log updated
4. Follow the policy exactly, even the strict parts
```
- State how AI helped: grammar check, outline brainstorm, or formatting
- Follow your university's policy exactly
- When in doubt, disclose more, not less

## Integrity as an Advantage

### Why transparency wins
- Recruiters ask how you used AI - honest answers stand out
- A clean AI-usage line reads as maturity and control
### The interview payoff
- Good integrity is a differentiator, not a confession
- Your log gives you concrete stories for interviews
- Defensible work protects your degree and your reputation

## Key Takeaways

- Draft from your own notes; AI is the editor, not the author
- Disclose AI use exactly as your policy requires
- Keep an AI-usage log for every submission
- Run the relevant checks before you submit
- Integrity is a differentiator - transparency beats evasion
