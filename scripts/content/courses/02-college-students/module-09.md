# Mission 9: Portfolio Building for Internships

## Learning Objectives
- Design a portfolio that tells one clear story about you
- Select and frame your best projects with a narrative
- Use AI to polish, structure, and review your portfolio
- Write project case studies in the STAR format
- Make your portfolio easy to find and quick to scan

---
### Module 1 · Your Portfolio Is a Story, Not a Folder
Recruiters spend seconds — your portfolio must tell a **story in that time**. A good portfolio answers three questions instantly:

- **Who you are** (one line: "Final-year B.Com student who automates the boring parts of finance work").
- **What you've made** (2-4 strong, real projects — quality over quantity).
- **What you can do** (proof in screenshots, demos, and results).

Every item should have one sentence of *impact*: what it does, who used it, and what result it produced. That one-sentence value line is the whole game.

**Example Quiz**

1. What three questions must a portfolio answer in seconds?
- A) Who you are, what you've made, what you can do
- B) Where you study, your marks, your hobbies
- C) Your typing speed, your apps, your theme
- D) Your fees, your plans, your city

**Answer:** A — Signal, not size, wins the first three seconds.

2. Why does quality over quantity matter?
- A) Two strong projects beat a folder of half-dones
- B) More is always better
- C) Quantity fills pages
- D) All projects are equal

**Answer:** A — Curated strength demonstrates judgment.

3. What does a one-line 'impact' sentence do?
- A) States what it does, who used it, and the result
- B) Fills space
- C) Replaces the README
- D) Explains the code

**Answer:** A — Impact lines let reviewers grasp value instantly.

### Module 2 · The 3-Section Portfolio Plan
Structure minimises effort and maximises signal:

1. **About** — 3 sentences: who, what you build, and what you're looking for.
2. **Projects** — your top 2-4 builds, each with a 30-second scannable summary.
3. **Proof** — one link to your best artifact, your credits/badges, and a clean contact line.

Host it simply: GitHub README (with images), a free Notion page, or a one-page site. **A clean simple surface beats a feature-heavy half-finished one.**

**Example Quiz**

1. Which is the correct 3-section structure?
- A) About, Projects, Proof
- B) Hobbies, Photos, Fonts
- C) Education, Fees, Books
- D) Team, Office, Plan

**Answer:** A — A clean minimal structure maximises signal.

2. Where can you host a one-page portfolio free?
- A) Notion, GitHub README, or a one-page site
- B) Only a paid server
- C) A print book
- D) A group chat

**Answer:** A — A clean simple surface beats a half-finished heavy one.

3. What belongs in 'Proof'?
- A) Links, credits/badges, and a clean contact line
- B) A list of dream jobs
- C) Your daily schedule
- D) Screenshots of code

**Answer:** A — Proof is your best artifact plus a clear way to reach you.

### Module 3 · The STAR Case Study Frame
For each project, write a mini case study using **STAR**:

- **Situation:** the problem you chose (and why it was real).
- **Task:** what you set out to make happen.
- **Action:** what you actually did — build, test, iterate.
- **Result:** measurable outcome ("cut my research prep from 4 hrs to 1", "used by 12 classmates in a week").

AI is great at drafting STAR summaries from your reflection notes (Mission 8). Draft with AI, then read it out loud — if it doesn't sound like you, rewrite until it does.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does the STAR frame structure?
- A) Situation, Task, Action, Result
- B) Steps, Tests, Advice, Rules
- C) Source, Time, Area, Role
- D) Skills, Titles, Awards, References

**Answer:** A — STAR turns a project into a portable case narrative.

2. Why does the Result need a measurable outcome?
- A) Numbers like '4 hrs → 1 hr' convince instantly
- B) It looks scientific
- C) It is a compulsion
- D) AI requires numbers

**Answer:** A — Measured results convert effort into proof.

3. After AI drafts your STAR summary, what must you do?
- A) Read it aloud and rewrite until it sounds like you
- B) Paste it verbatim
- C) Trim it to a sentence
- D) Auto-post it

**Answer:** A — Your voice survives only if you revise it.

### Module 4 · Using AI to Draft, Not Fabricate
Your portfolio is where honesty pays off most — because interviews will verify it:

- Use AI to **polish wording and structure** your own projects. It can't invent experience.
- **Never claim** projects, internships, or skills you don't have; it unmasks instantly in a technical Q&A.
- Be specific about **your role in team projects**; "we built" invites questions about *you*.
- If you used AI, say *how* — "AI for code scaffolding and drafts; I own the design and testing" is a strength, not a caveat.

A portfolio that survives scrutiny is worth more than a brilliant one that collapses under one question.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the core honesty rule for portfolio text?
- A) Never claim projects or skills you don't have
- B) Borrow a friend's project
- C) Describe future work as done
- D) Use generic terms only

**Answer:** A — Unverifiable claims unmask instantly in technical Q&A.

2. How should you state your role in a team project?
- A) Precisely — your part, not 'we built everything'
- B) As 'we' for everything
- C) Skip it entirely
- D) Claim sole credit

**Answer:** A — Precision shows ownership and survives scrutiny.

3. How is AI use best framed on a portfolio?
- A) As scaffolding you directed; you own design and testing
- B) Hidden from view
- C) As the sole author
- D) Never mentioned

**Answer:** A — Honest AI roles read as a strength, not a caveat.

### Module 5 · Make It Scannable in 30 Seconds
Assume the reader has 30 seconds — design for it:

- **Top of each project page:** one-line elevator pitch, one screenshot, one demo link, and 3 bullets (what/why/result).
- **Formatting:** short paragraphs, headings, bullet lists — no walls of text.
- **Actionable contact:** "Find me: [email] / LinkedIn" (or a print-friendly PDF).

Then loop: **show it to a friend and mentor and time them.** If they can't answer "what does this person do?" in 30 seconds, cut or move things. That feedback loop is how portfolios mature.

**Example Quiz**

1. What belongs at the top of each project page?
- A) One-line pitch, one screenshot, one demo link, 3 bullets
- B) Your résumé
- C) Ten screenshots
- D) A long changelog

**Answer:** A — Scannable tops let reviewers decide fast.

2. How do you test 30-second scannability?
- A) Time a friend or mentor reading it
- B) Read it to yourself
- C) Count the words
- D) Ask AI to review it

**Answer:** A — Real timers reveal what to cut.

3. Which formatting rule keeps a portfolio scannable?
- A) Headings, bullets and short paragraphs
- B) Walls of text with headers
- C) Small grey fonts
- D) One giant paragraph

**Answer:** A — Structure is how 30-second reviews find the signal.

### Module 6 · Honesty Rules That Protect You
The rules that keep your portfolio an asset, not a liability:

- **Every project exists and works** — the recruiter can run it or you can demo it live.
- **Every claim is verifiable** — screenshots only of real product output.
- **Metrics are real** — "12 users" means 12 actual users.
- **Your role is accurate** — no inflated solo credit on group work.

One unwritten rule under them all: *exaggeration in the portfolio is a test of honesty, and the interview is the check.* Clean honesty is the cheapest competitive advantage you have.

## Real-World Examples

- A final-year student turns his week-long flashcard tool into a STAR case study with real usage numbers.
- A commerce student's one-page portfolio passes a '30-second scannability' test with a placements mentor.
- An engineering student openly notes 'AI: code scaffolding; Me: architecture + testing' - and it impresses interviewers.
- A first-generation graduate uses AI to polish, not invent, her project summaries and lands a referral.

## Key Takeaways
- Portfolio = one clear story, answerable in 30 seconds.
- 2–3 projects, STAR-framed, all on-message.
- AI drafts and reviews; you verify every claim.
- Scannable cards + live demo + honest AI-use line beat essay-length pages.
- Launch early, gather 30-second feedback, tighten in 2 rounds.

## Practice Challenge
1. **Story sentence:** Write your "I solve ___ with ___" line. Test it on two people: does it instantly describe you?
2. **Pick 2–3 projects** from school/labs/this course, and write STAR mini case studies (AI-assisted, then edited by you).
3. **Build or rebuild** your portfolio one-pager with scannable cards, hero line, and contact, and host it somewhere shareable.
4. **Honesty audit:** Re-read every claim and mark ✓ if you can back it with a demo, data, or repo. Remove or soften anything not backed.
5. **30-second test:** have someone browse your portfolio for 30 seconds and say your story back to you.

- A) It exists, works, and you can demo it live
