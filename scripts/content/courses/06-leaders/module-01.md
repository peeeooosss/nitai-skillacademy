# Mission 1: The AI Landscape for Business Leaders

## Learning Objectives

- See AI as a leadership topic, not a technology topic
- Understand the four capabilities behind most AI products
- Map where AI creates value: efficiency, experience, and growth
- Cut through hype and vendor language with clear questions
- Leave with a 30-day plan for your own AI education

---
### Module 1 · AI Is a Leadership Topic

Many leaders hear 'AI' and think it is an IT decision. It is not - it is a strategy, cost, talent, and risk decision wearing a technical coat. Who uses it, where, on what data, and under what controls are leadership calls.

You do not need to build AI to lead it. You need to ask the right questions and hold the people who answer accountable.

**The leader's stance:** understand enough to set direction, fund the right things, and govern the risks - not to write code.

**Example Quiz**

1. Why is AI a leadership topic, not just IT?
- A) It involves strategy, cost, talent, and risk decisions
- B) IT should own it all
- C) It is only about coding
- D) It has no business impact

**Answer:** A — AI decisions - use cases, data, controls, budget - are strategy and risk calls for leaders.

2. What does a leader need to do about AI?
- A) Set direction, fund what matters, govern risk
- B) Learn to code
- C) Personally audit every model
- D) Appoint one engineer to decide

**Answer:** A — Leaders set direction, fund the right things, and govern risk - not build models.

3. What do you not need to do as an AI leader?
- A) Understand enough to ask good questions
- B) Build AI yourself
- C) Hold teams accountable for results
- D) Consider risk and budget

**Answer:** B — You lead AI by asking strong questions and governing - building it is optional.

### Module 2 · The Four Capabilities Behind AI

Almost every AI product you will evaluate reduces to four capabilities:

1. **Classification** - sorting things into categories (spam, intent, risk level).
2. **Prediction** - forecasting (demand, churn, maintenance needs).
3. **Generation** - creating text, images, code, and video from prompts.
4. **Retrieval** - finding the right information fast (search, Q&A over your documents).

When a vendor says 'AI-powered', ask which of these four is doing the work. The honest answer determines what it can actually do for your business.

**Example Quiz**

1. Which of these is one of the four AI capabilities?
- A) Classification, prediction, generation, retrieval
- B) Colour, speed, memory, price
- C) App, cloud, phone, tablet
- D) None of these

**Answer:** A — Almost every AI product reduces to classification, prediction, generation, or retrieval.

2. Why should you ask which capability a product uses?
- A) It tells you what the product can really do
- B) It proves the vendor is rich
- C) It affects the logo
- D) It is a legal requirement

**Answer:** A — The honest capability answer reveals real business utility vs marketing.

3. What capability makes a chatbot able to answer over your documents?
- A) Retrieval
- B) Classification
- C) Prediction
- D) Generation alone

**Answer:** A — Q&A over your documents works through retrieval - find the right information.

### Module 3 · Three Places AI Creates Value

Value concentrates in three places. Keep every business case in one of them:

1. **Efficiency** - do the same work faster or cheaper (automation, drafting, triage).
2. **Experience** - serve customers better (personalisation, faster answers, smoother journeys).
3. **Growth** - enter new markets, products, or pricing (new offers, insight-led strategy).

If a proposal is not clearly in one of these, keep asking until it is. Value you cannot name, you cannot fund.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the three value buckets?
- A) Efficiency, experience, growth
- B) Speed, size, colour
- C) Cost, ego, fashion
- D) Pilot, demo, blog

**Answer:** A — Value concentrates in efficiency (faster/cheaper), experience (better serving), or growth (new business).

2. Which bucket is 'chatbot answers 80%% of support tickets'?
- A) Efficiency
- B) Growth
- C) Fashion
- D) None

**Answer:** A — Offloading repetitive support is an efficiency (and partly experience) play.

3. Why must every business case sit in a named bucket?
- A) Value you cannot name, you cannot fund
- B) It looks good in slides
- C) Vendors require it
- D) It shortens meetings

**Answer:** A — Named value keeps funding disciplined and outcomes measurable.

### Module 4 · Cutting Through the Hype

Vendors use words loosely: 'AI-native', 'machine learning', 'large language models'. Your defence is a short set of clear questions:

1. **What exactly does it do today, for whom, at what accuracy?** (not what it will do).
2. **What data does it need from us, and what will it touch?**
3. **What is the measurable business outcome and timeframe?**
4. **What happens when it fails - who is liable, how do we recover?**

Hype survives vague questions. It does not survive specific ones. Ask these four and the room gets honest fast.

**Example Quiz**

1. Which question undercuts vendor hype fastest?
- A) What exactly does it do today, for whom, at what accuracy?
- B) When was the company founded?
- C) Do you have a mobile app?
- D) What font is in the deck?

**Answer:** A — Ask for today's capability, audience, and accuracy - not future promises.

2. Which question covers risk and failure?
- A) What happens when it fails and who is liable?
- B) What colour is the brand?
- C) How many employees do you have?
- D) Is there a demo video?

**Answer:** A — Failure handling - liability and recovery - separates real products from pitch decks.

3. What should a business outcome include?
- A) A measurable result and a timeframe
- B) A logo
- C) A hashtag
- D) A thank-you slide

**Answer:** A — Fund only measurable outcomes with timeframes; vague promises are not business cases.

### Module 5 · Classification vs Generative - What Your Business Needs

Know the two conversations you keep hearing about:

- **Predictive/classification AI** - reliable, narrow, proven: pricing, fraud, demand, sorting. Great ROI today, boring to pitch.
- **Generative AI (LLMs)** - flexible, broad, dramatic: drafting, search, assistants, code. Fast to deploy, needs more controls.

Many businesses should start with the boring one where the numbers are clear, and pilot the dramatic one where the use case is safe.

**Rule of thumb:** senior leaders usually overheat on generative and under-value the classification wins right under their nose.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is predictive/classification AI good at?
- A) Pricing, fraud, demand, sorting - reliable and proven
- B) Making jokes
- C) Designing logos
- D) Vague promises

**Answer:** A — Predictive/classification AI delivers proven, high-ROI wins in pricing, fraud, and demand.

2. What is generative AI best for?
- A) Drafting, search, assistants, code - flexible and fast
- B) Guaranteed business decisions
- C) Cheap predictions at scale
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — Generative AI shines at drafting, search, assistants, and code - with more controls needed.

3. Where do many leaders misplace their attention?
- A) Overheating on generative and under-valuing the classification wins
- B) Focusing only on boring problems
- C) Ignoring digitalisation
- D) Funding nothing

**Answer:** A — Leaders often overvalue the dramatic and miss the proven classification wins.

### Module 6 · Your First 30 Days as an AI Leader

You do not need a strategy document on day one. Your first 30 days:

1. **Learn** - fifteen minutes a day on one capability and one business use case.
2. **Ask** - interview two teams about their most repetitive, costly, or error-prone work.
3. **Audit** - list the disorganised data you already hold (it is your hidden asset).
4. **Observe a demo** - ask one vendor the four hype-cutting questions in person.
5. **Choose a pilot** - one small, safe, measurable use case to run in 90 days.

Thirty days of this beats a year of reading AI newsletters.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is step one of a leader's first 30 days?
- A) Learn fifteen minutes a day on capabilities and use cases
- B) Buy every AI product
- C) Rewrite the org chart
- D) Hire an AI officer

**Answer:** A — Sustained small learning beats weekend sprints and newsletter subscriptions.

2. What should you audit early?
- A) The disorganised data you already hold
- B) The office snack budget
- C) Vendor logos
- D) Your email signatures

**Answer:** A — Existing data is the hidden asset your use cases will run on.

3. How should your first pilot be scoped?
- A) Small, safe, and measurable over 90 days
- B) Company-wide from day one
- C) Unfunded and informal
- D) A full vendor race

**Answer:** A — Pick one small, safe, measurable pilot - it builds evidence and confidence.

## Real-World Examples

**The capability question —** A CEO asked an 'AI recruiting platform' which of the four capabilities did the work. The honest answer - retrieval plus classification of CVs - clarified exactly what it could and could not promise, and the deal was right-sized.
**The value bucket test —** A retail board forced every one of twelve AI proposals into efficiency, experience, or growth. Nine were cut in the first pass because the value could not be named - and the three remaining got funded properly.
**The 30-day audit —** An operations leader spent 30 days learning and interviewing teams, then found that a small demand-forecasting pilot (classification AI) saved more than three flashy generative pilots combined.

---

## Key Takeaways

- AI is a strategy, cost, talent, and risk decision - a leadership topic.
- Four capabilities (classification, prediction, generation, retrieval) explain almost every AI product.
- Keep business cases in one of three buckets: efficiency, experience, growth.
- Ask the four hype-cutting questions and the room gets honest.
- Predictive AI is proven and underestimated; generative AI is dramatic and needs controls.
- Your first 30 days: learn, ask teams, audit data, see a demo, pick one small pilot.
