# Mission 3: Evaluating AI Tools, Vendors & Build vs Buy

## Learning Objectives

- Define needs and scoring criteria before meeting any vendor
- Understand the build vs buy vs integrate decision clearly
- Ask the vendor questions that reveal capability, not razzle-dazzle
- Compare costs honestly: seats, usage, migration, and exit
- Use a simple scorecard to decide and defend the choice
- Run a real evaluation on one tool you are considering

---
### Module 1 · Define Needs Before Vendors

Never invite a vendor to define your problem - they will define it in their favour. Write your needs first:

1. **The job** - what business outcome must the tool move?
2. **The user** - who uses it, how often, how skilled?
3. **The constraints** - budget, data rules, security, integration, timeline.
4. **The measures** - how you will know it worked (reduce X by Y in Z time).

Then shop. Evaluation is scored against your criteria, not against the vendor's claims.

**Example Quiz**

1. Why define needs before meeting vendors?
- A) So the vendor does not shape the problem for you
- B) Vendors dislike clarity
- C) It is good etiquette
- D) It avoids paperwork

**Answer:** A — Define the job, user, constraints, and measures before any vendor shapes them.

2. What is the first element of a needs statement?
- A) The business outcome the tool must move
- B) The brand name
- C) The discount
- D) The demo style

**Answer:** A — Start with the outcome - the job the tool must do for the business.

3. How should you know if it worked?
- A) By defined measures - reduce X by Y in Z time
- B) By the vendor's word
- C) By aesthetics
- D) By usage alone

**Answer:** A — Predefine the measurable outcome and timeframe before you buy.

### Module 2 · Build vs Buy vs Integrate

Three honest routes, not two:

- **Buy** - a mature product off the shelf. Fastest, proven, but limited fit.
- **Integrate** - wire proven AI services (APIs from large providers) into your existing systems. Most organisations' sweet spot.
- **Build** - train or host your own models. Requires the deepest data, talent, and cost - rarely justified at the start.

**The trap to avoid:** 'Let's build our own LLM.' Unless your data moat and scale are exceptional, build is a vanity project dressed as strategy.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the three honest routes?
- A) Buy, integrate, build
- B) Buy, rent, borrow
- C) Cloud, app, desktop
- D) Hire, train, fire

**Answer:** A — The real decision is buy, integrate (APIs into your systems), or build.

2. What is most organisations' sweet spot?
- A) Integrate - proven AI services wired into existing systems
- B) Build everything in-house
- C) Buy five overlapping tools
- D) Wait and watch

**Answer:** A — Integrating proven AI APIs is the fastest path to business value for most firms.

3. When is building your own model justified?
- A) Rarely - only with an exceptional data moat and scale
- B) Always at the start
- C) Never, for any reason
- D) Whenever it is trendy

**Answer:** A — Build needs an exceptional data moat and scale - otherwise it is vanity IT.

### Module 3 · Vendor Questions That Reveal Truth

A short, sharp question set outperforms any demo:

1. 'What exactly does it do today, at what measured accuracy?'
2. 'Which of the four capabilities does the product use?'
3. 'What data does it touch, where does it live, who can see it?'
4. 'What does a successful deployment look like at month 3 and month 12?'
5. 'What happened in your last three failed deployments - and why?'

The last question is the quiet killer. Vendors talk freely about failures they have learnt from, and their answer tells you their true maturity.

**Example Quiz**

1. Which question asks about today's real capability?
- A) What exactly does it do today, at what measured accuracy?
- B) What are your brand colours?
- C) Do you offer discounts?
- D) When was the website launched?

**Answer:** A — Demand real, current, measured capability - not roadmaps and demos.

2. Why ask about the last three failed deployments?
- A) It reveals true maturity and honest self-awareness
- B) It is a trick with no value
- C) It makes vendors angry
- D) It wastes meeting time

**Answer:** A — How a vendor speaks about failures reveals far more than polished demos.

3. What should a successful deployment look like at month 3?
- A) Measurable business outcomes, not just using the tool
- B) Everyone attending training
- C) The tool installed
- D) A new dashboard

**Answer:** A — Month-3 success is about measurable outcomes the business can see.

### Module 4 · Compare Costs Honestly

The sticker price is the smallest number on the invoice. Cost compare includes:

- **License** - per seat, per usage, or per outcome.
- **Migration** - data, integration, and change effort.
- **Training** - who learns it, at what cost, for how long.
- **Running** - infrastructure, prompts, and maintenance after go-live.
- **Exit** - how hard is it to leave, export data, and cancel?

**The test:** ask for total cost of ownership over three years and an exit plan in writing. Most vendor comparisons fail on those two alone.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the smallest number on most invoices?
- A) The sticker price
- B) The exit fee
- C) Training cost
- D) Migration cost

**Answer:** A — License price is the headline; migration, training, running, and exit cost the real money.

2. Why ask for a written exit plan?
- A) It reveals true cost and lock-in risk
- B) It is a legal formality
- C) It is unused paper
- D) It slows deals usefully

**Answer:** A — An exit plan in writing reveals migration cost and how hard it is to change later.

3. What horizon should cost comparison use?
- A) Three-year total cost of ownership
- B) The first month only
- C) The demo month
- D) No horizon at all

**Answer:** A — Three-year TCO captures license, migration, training, running, and exit.

### Module 5 · The Vendor Scorecard

Score every candidate against the same write-up, with weights you chose before seeing demos:

| Criterion | Weight | Vendor A | Vendor B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business impact | 30%% | 8 | 5 |
| Fit with our systems | 25%% | 6 | 9 |
| Data & security | 20%% | 9 | 7 |
| Total cost (3yr) | 15%% | 7 | 8 |
| Evidence & track record | 10%% | 8 | 6 |

Weights make the decision transparent and defensible. When the board asks 'why this one?', you show the scorecard, not an opinion.

**Example Quiz**

1. Why weight the scorecard criteria first?
- A) It makes decisions transparent and defensible
- B) It fills time
- C) It impresses vendors
- D) It guarantees the cheapest price

**Answer:** A — Pre-chosen weights make the choice transparent and answerable to anyone.

2. What does a good scorecard carry?
- A) Business impact, fit, data/security, cost, evidence
- B) Logos, fonts, colours
- C) Only price
- D) Only marketing claims

**Answer:** A — Score on business impact, system fit, data and security, three-year cost, and evidence.

3. How do you defend the choice to a board?
- A) Show the weighted scorecard
- B) Cite the best demo
- C) Name-drop the vendor
- D) Quote the discount

**Answer:** A — The weighted scorecard is the defensible answer to 'why this one?'.

### Module 6 · Run a Real Evaluation This Week

Close the mission with a live evaluation on one tool you are genuinely considering:

1. **Write needs** - one outcome, one user, constraints, measures.
2. **Score** - against your weights, before the demo.
3. **Demo with questions** - ask the five truth questions live.
4. **Cost** - get three-year TCO and exit terms in writing.
5. **Decide and record** - scorecard + a one-paragraph rationale.

Real evaluations are how you learn to buy AI well; the first one changes the way you will see every future demo.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the first step of a real evaluation?
- A) Write needs: outcome, user, constraints, measures
- B) Book the biggest vendor
- C) Ask for a discount
- D) Watch a showroom demo

**Answer:** A — Write your needs on paper before any demo shapes the decision.

2. When should you score candidates?
- A) Before the demo, against pre-chosen weights
- B) After the best pitch
- C) At the end of the quarter
- D) Never

**Answer:** A — Scoring before the demo keeps vendors from moving your criteria.

3. What closes an evaluation properly?
- A) A scorecard and a one-paragraph rationale
- B) A lively debate
- C) A signed PDF
- D) A vendor dinner

**Answer:** A — Record the scorecard and why - it anchors the decision in evidence.

## Real-World Examples

**The needs-first buyer —** An operations director wrote his outcome ('quote turnaround under 2 hours') before meeting vendors. With that fixed, two of four vendors disqualified themselves in the first meeting - and the chosen tool was implemented in eight weeks.
**The failure question —** A CIO asked every shortlisted AI vendor about their last failed deployment. One gave a specific, honest post-mortem; another gave vague talk. The specific one won - and their project stayed on schedule.
**The three-year TCO —** A CFO asked for three-year totals. The 'cheap' per-seat product was outweighed by migration and exit costs, and the more expensive tool won on total cost of ownership - saving the company later.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Define needs and scoring weights before any vendor shapes the conversation.
- Choose deliberately among buy, integrate (most firms' sweet spot), and build.
- Ask the five truth questions - accuracy, capability, data, outcomes, failures.
- Compare three-year total cost and demand a written exit plan.
- Weighted scorecards make decisions transparent and defensible.
- Run a real evaluation this week - it changes how you see every future demo.
