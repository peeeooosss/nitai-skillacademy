### Module 1 · Adoption Is a People Problem

The best tool fails if a terrified or confused team refuses to use it. Adoption is behaviour change before it is technology. As a leader, most of your job is setting the conditions for that change - not picking the model.

Three things decide whether change sticks: do people see why, can they try safely, and is it rewarding?

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why adoption is behaviour change
- Name the three conditions for change
- Shift your focus from tools to conditions
- Diagnose why an adoption stalled

## Behaviour Before Technology

- A team that fears or ignores a tool will not use it
- The technology can be perfect and the rollout still fail
- Adoption is a change-management problem first
### The evidence
- Failed rollouts usually trace to people, not models
- Fear and confusion beat capability
- Leaders who treat it as IT miss the real work

## The Three Conditions

```text
Three conditions for change to stick:
1. Do people see why? (the reason, in their terms)
2. Can they try safely? (low stakes, good support)
3. Is it rewarding? (time back, praise, fewer errors)
Set these conditions and behaviour follows.
```
- The why must be in the team's own terms
- Safe trial lowers the cost of experimenting
- Reward makes the new behaviour repeat

## The Leader's Job

- Set the three conditions - why, safe trial, reward
- Model the behaviour yourself
- Remove barriers quickly when they appear
### What it is not
- Not selecting every model personally
- Not writing all the prompts
- Not ignoring the people side

## Key Takeaways

- Adoption is behaviour change before it is technology
- The best tool fails if the team will not use it
- Three conditions: see why, try safely, be rewarded
- Leaders set conditions, not models
- Most failed rollouts trace to people, not tools

### Module 2 · Communicate the Why

People accept hard change when they understand why and how it affects them. Four messages matter: what is happening, why now, what it means for you, and what stays.

Deliver these repeatedly and in your own voice. One honest town hall beats ten poster campaigns.

## Learning Objectives

- Deliver the four change messages
- Put the personal impact at the centre
- Repeat the why in your own voice
- Reassure by naming what stays

## The Four Messages

- **What is happening** - the honest plan, for this year, in plain language
- **Why now** - the external pressure or opportunity forcing the move
- **What it means for you** - hours back, new skills, different work, not 'job gone'
- **What stays** - the judgement and relationships AI will not replace
### Why all four
- The personal message matters most
- The why-now creates urgency without panic
- What-stays reduces fear

## Delivering the Messages

```text
Four messages, in your own voice:
1. What is happening - the honest plan, this year, plainly
2. Why now - the pressure or opportunity
3. What it means for you - hours back, new skills, different work
4. What stays - judgement and relationships AI will not replace
Repeat them. Trust forms by repetition.
```
- Plain language beats corporate phrasing
- Repeating the why is necessary, not redundant
- Honesty about the unknown builds trust

## What Stays

- Judgement, relationships, and accountability remain human
- Naming what stays reduces the fear of replacement
- It signals that people are central, not incidental
### The effect
- People engage when they feel secure
- Security comes from clarity, not slogans
- The why lands when the 'what stays' is credible

## Key Takeaways

- Four messages: what, why now, what it means for you, what stays
- The personal message matters most to employees
- Repeat the why in your own voice - trust forms by repetition
- Name what stays to reduce fear
- One honest town hall beats ten poster campaigns

## Practice Challenge

1. Write the four messages for your AI programme
2. Deliver them in one team meeting, in your own words
3. Ask what people are still worried about

3. What does 'what stays' reassure people about?

**Answer:** A — One honest town hall in your own voice beats ten poster campaigns.

### Module 3 · Skills, Roles, and Upskilling

AI changes jobs, but mostly it reshapes them - some parts shrink, others grow. Plan for three moves: update existing roles, create AI-adjacent roles, and upskill intentionally.

**The rule:** train people for the work you are actually moving to, before you ask them to do it. Nothing kills adoption like expecting skills nobody was taught.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how AI reshapes rather than deletes jobs
- Update existing roles with AI usage
- Create AI-adjacent roles
- Train before asking for new behaviour

## Jobs Are Reshaped

- Some parts of a role shrink, others grow
- Most jobs are reshaped, not removed
- Plan for both shrinking and growing parts
### The implication
- Redesign roles rather than replace people
- Identify which tasks AI absorbs
- Identify which tasks grow in importance

## Three Moves

```text
Three workforce moves:
1. Update existing roles - add AI usage, add an hour a week of practice
2. Create AI-adjacent roles - prompt libraries, workflow owners, data stewards, AI champions
3. Upskill intentionally - define the skill per role and fund a small programme
Train to the real future work before you ask for it.
```
- An hour a week of practice normalises AI use
- AI-adjacent roles scale adoption across teams
- Funded upskilling signals seriousness

## Training Before Asking

- Define the skill per role
- Train to the work you are moving to
- Fund a small, practical programme
### Why order matters
- Skills precede adoption
- Expecting untrained people to perform new work guarantees failure
- Training is an investment, not an expense

## Key Takeaways

- AI mostly reshapes jobs - parts shrink and grow
- Update roles with AI usage and an hour a week of practice
- Create AI-adjacent roles: workflow owners, data stewards, champions
- Define the skill per role and fund upskilling
- Train before you ask for new behaviour

## Practice Challenge

1. List the tasks AI will absorb and those that will grow in one role
2. Define one AI-adjacent role or champion per team
3. Budget an hour a week of practice for the team

**Example Quiz**

1. How does AI mostly change jobs?
- A) It reshapes them - some parts shrink, others grow
- B) It deletes them entirely
- C) It never changes jobs
- D) It only adds typing

**Answer:** A — Most jobs are reshaped, not removed - plan for parts shrinking and growing.

2. What are AI-adjacent roles?
- A) Prompt libraries, workflow owners, data stewards, AI champions
- B) Only engineers
- C) Only managers
- D) Only vendors

**Answer:** A — Supporting roles like workflow owners and AI champions scale adoption.

3. When should you train people?
- A) Before you ask them to do the new work
- B) After launch, if asked
- C) When the vendor suggests it
- D) Never

**Answer:** A — Train to the real future work before launching - skills precede adoption.

4. What does an hour a week of practice do?
- A) Normalises AI use in the role
- B) Wastes time
- C) Replaces training
- D) Slows delivery

**Answer:** A — A small weekly practice habit normalises AI use and builds confidence.

### Module 4 · Incentives and Habits That Stick

Launch excitement fades unless new behaviour becomes habit. Design for the habit rather than relying on willpower or a single launch event.

**Habit engine:** cue (weekly ritual) → action (try one prompt) → reward (time saved, praised, shared). Run the engine; do not rely on motivation.

## Learning Objectives

- Design cue-action-reward habits
- Build AI into existing routines
- Reward outcomes and sharing, not tool use
- Lower friction for users

## The Habit Engine

- **Cue** - a weekly ritual that prompts the behaviour
- **Action** - an easy, low-stakes thing to try
- **Reward** - visible time saved, praise, or sharing
### Why habits beat launches
- Launch hype fades in weeks
- Habits persist without willpower
- Rituals normalise the new behaviour

## Designing the Habits

```text
Design AI habits that stick:
1. Build it into the routine - 'bring one thing you automated' in stand-up
2. Make it visible - celebrate saves and workflow wins in public
3. Reward the behaviour, not the tool - recognise outcomes and sharing
4. Lower friction - remove approval delays, give a safe sandbox
Cue -> action -> reward. Run the engine.
```
- Existing meetings are the best cue
- Public celebration is a powerful reward
- Friction kills good intentions

## Lowering Friction

- Remove approval delays for safe experiments
- Give people a sandbox to try things
- Make the first step tiny
### The principle
- Every extra step loses users
- Safe sandboxes turn curiosity into habit
- Lean approvals speed learning

## Key Takeaways

- Design cue-action-reward habits, not one-off excitement
- Build AI into standing routines
- Celebrate saves and workflow wins publicly
- Reward outcomes and sharing, not tool use
- Lower friction with sandboxes and lean approvals

## Practice Challenge

1. Design one weekly ritual that makes AI use a habit
2. Identify one approval delay you can remove
3. Plan how you will celebrate the first win publicly

**Example Quiz**

1. What makes AI adoption a habit?
- A) A routine cue, an easy action, and a visible reward
- B) A single launch event
- C) Willpower
- D) Vendor posters

**Answer:** A — Cue-action-reward rituals beat launch hype and willpower.

2. What reward drives adoption best?
- A) Outcomes and sharing - time saved, wins praised
- B) T-shirts and stickers
- C) More meetings
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — Recognise outcomes and sharing - visible wins keep the habit running.

3. How do you lower friction for users?
- A) A safe sandbox and fewer approval delays
- B) More forms
- C) Longer review queues
- D) Stricter passwords

**Answer:** A — Remove barriers to trying - safe sandboxes and lean approvals speed trial.

4. What is the best cue for an AI habit?
- A) An existing standing meeting
- B) A new weekly email
- C) A poster
- D) A memo

**Answer:** A — Building AI into an existing routine makes the cue automatic.

### Module 5 · Managing Resistance and Fear

Resistance is usually fear in disguise: 'will I lose my job?' Address it with evidence and honesty - not slogans. Silence makes the fear louder.

**Avoid the two failures:** ignoring fear (it curdles) and dismissing it ('everyone will be fine' - impossible to promise). Honesty about unknowns builds more trust than reassurance.

## Learning Objectives

- Recognise resistance as fear
- Respond with four moves
- Avoid ignoring or dismissing fear
- Build trust through honesty about unknowns

## Fear in Disguise

- Resistance usually means fear, especially of job loss
- Ignoring it makes it louder
- Dismissing it destroys trust
### The two failures
- Ignoring fear - it curdles into active opposition
- Dismissing it - 'everyone will be fine' promises what you cannot guarantee

## Four Moves

```text
Respond to resistance with evidence and honesty:
1. Acknowledge the fear - name it in meetings; silence makes it louder
2. Show the plan - who is affected, what training, what timeline
3. Prove by pilot - one team's win, shared with numbers and stories
4. Give ownership - let resistant teams choose their first use case
```
- Naming the fear defuses it
- A concrete plan replaces vague worry
- Evidence beats persuasion

## Honesty About Unknowns

- Say what you do not know yet
- Commit to what you can control - training, timeline, support
- Trust grows from honesty, not reassurance
### The result
- People engage with the real plan
- Ownership turns resistors into advocates
- The pilot converts doubt into evidence

## Key Takeaways

- Resistance is usually fear in disguise
- Four moves: acknowledge, show the plan, prove by pilot, give ownership
- Avoid ignoring or dismissing fear
- Honesty about unknowns builds more trust than reassurance
- Give resistant teams ownership of their first use case

## Practice Challenge

1. Name the top fear in your team about AI
2. Write a one-page plan covering who, training, and timeline
3. Let one resistant team choose its first use case

**Example Quiz**

1. What is most AI resistance really about?

- B) Laziness
- C) Bad software
- D) Poor Wi-Fi

**Answer:** A — Resistance is usually fear in disguise - name and address it directly.

2. How should leaders respond to resistance?
- A) Acknowledge fear, show the plan, prove with a pilot, give ownership
- B) Ignore it until it passes
- C) Promise everyone is fine
- D) Punish complaints

**Answer:** A — Evidence, plan, pilots, and ownership beat slogans and silence.

3. Why is 'everyone will be fine' a bad reassurance?
- A) It promises what you cannot guarantee and erodes trust
- B) It is too short
- C) It is too long
- D) It is too technical

**Answer:** A — Honesty about what is unknown builds more trust than blanket reassurance.

4. What turns resistors into advocates?
- A) Giving them ownership of their first use case
- B) Ignoring them
- C) Longer meetings
- D) A memo

**Answer:** A — Ownership plus a proven pilot converts doubt into evidence and advocacy.

### Module 6 · Sustaining Momentum Past Launch

Adoption is won in the quiet months two through six, not on launch day. Launch events are easy; sustained follow-through is where results are made.

Sustain it by measuring monthly, celebrating publicly, removing blockers fast, and iterating the plan each quarter.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why months 2-6 decide adoption
- Measure usage, saves, quality, and satisfaction
- Remove blockers quickly
- Iterate the plan each quarter

## Where Adoption Is Won

- Months two through six of consistent follow-through
- Not launch day, not the kickoff party
- Sustained attention determines outcomes
### Why launch fades
- Novelty wears off quickly
- Blockers accumulate without attention
- Momentum needs maintenance

## Sustaining the Momentum

```text
Sustain adoption after launch:
1. Measure monthly - usage, saves, quality, and satisfaction
2. Celebrate publicly - share wins in numbers and people's own words
3. Remove blockers fast - a weekly 'adoption shop' gives issues owners and dates
4. Iterate the plan - add the next skill or use case each quarter
```
- Monthly measurement keeps the plan honest
- Public celebration sustains motivation
- A weekly adoption shop clears friction

## The True End State

- Not 'we use AI', but 'we do better work with AI as our tool'
- Metrics are the mirror of that journey
- The prize is better results, not technology ownership
### The mindset
- Tool ownership is not the goal
- Better work and outcomes are
- Measure what changes for the business

## Key Takeaways

- Adoption is won in months 2-6, not launch day
- Measure monthly: usage, saves, quality, satisfaction
- Celebrate publicly in numbers and people's words
- Run a weekly adoption shop to remove blockers
- The end state is better work with AI as the tool

## Practice Challenge

1. Set your monthly metrics: usage, saves, quality, satisfaction
2. Schedule a weekly adoption shop
3. Define what 'better work' looks like for your team
