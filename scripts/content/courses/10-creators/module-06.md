# Mission 6: Monetising Your Audience with AI

---
### Module 1 · Audience First, Money Second

Money follows trust. Creators who put the audience first build the kind of trust that makes every monetisation option work better.

AI helps you plan monetisation, but the order matters: serve first, sell second.

## Learning Objectives

- Put audience before money
- Understand trust as an asset
- Avoid trust-destroying monetisation
- Plan monetisation in order

## Trust Comes First

- Trust is the creator's core asset
- Money follows a loyal audience
- Betraying trust ends the business
### Why
- Audiences buy from those they trust
- Trust takes time, breaks fast
- Short-term cash can cost long-term income

## Ordering Monetisation

- Grow and serve the audience first
- Add monetisation that fits the audience
- Keep the audience's interest central
### The rule
- Audience first, money second
- Only promote what helps them
- Protect trust above all

## The Order Prompt

```text
I have [X] followers on [platform] in [niche]. Suggest a
monetisation plan that puts audience trust first: what to
offer, in what order, and what to avoid.
```
- A trust-first plan
- Order of offers
- What to avoid

## Key Takeaways

- Money follows trust
- Trust is the creator's core asset
- Put the audience first and sell second
- Only promote what genuinely helps them
- Short-term cash can cost long-term income

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your audience's top three needs
2. List monetisation that serves those needs
3. List monetisation to avoid

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the creator's core asset?
- A) Trust
- B) Follower count
- C) Equipment
- D) A platform

**Answer:** A — Trust is what makes every monetisation option work better.

2. What comes first?
- A) Audience
- B) Money
- C) Ads
- D) Sponsors

**Answer:** A — Serve the audience first; money follows trust.

3. What ends the business?
- A) Betraying trust
- B) Posting daily
- C) Growing slowly
- D) Being honest

**Answer:** A — Betraying audience trust destroys the creator's core asset.

4. What should you promote?
- A) Only what helps the audience
- B) Anything paid
- C) Random products
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — Promote only what genuinely helps your audience.

### Module 2 · Step 1: Map Your Monetisation Options

Creators have many ways to earn: sponsorships, products, services, memberships, affiliate links, and digital goods. Each fits different audiences and sizes.

Map the options and pick the ones that fit your niche, audience, and stage.

## Learning Objectives

- Know the main monetisation options
- Match options to your stage
- Pick what fits your niche
- Avoid premature monetisation

## The Options

- **Sponsorships:** brands pay for placement
- **Products:** courses, templates, digital goods
- **Services:** coaching, consulting, done-for-you work
- **Memberships:** recurring community or perks
- **Affiliates:** commission on referrals
### Why
- Options fit different sizes and niches
- Diversifying reduces risk
- The right fit beats the trendy option

## Matching to Your Stage

- Small audience: services and affiliates
- Growing audience: products and sponsorships
- Loyal audience: memberships and community
### The rule
- Match option to stage
- Pick one to start
- Add others as you grow

## The Options Prompt

```text
I have [audience size] on [platform] in [niche]. Compare
sponsorships, products, services, memberships, and
affiliates for my stage, and recommend which to start with.
```
- Options compared
- A starting recommendation
- Fit for your stage

## Key Takeaways

- Creators earn through sponsorships, products, services, memberships, and affiliates
- Each option fits a different audience size and niche
- Match the option to your current stage
- Pick one to start and add others as you grow
- Diversifying income reduces risk

## Practice Challenge

1. List all monetisation options for your niche
2. Match each to your audience size
3. Pick one to start

**Example Quiz**

1. Which is a monetisation option?
- A) Sponsorships
- B) Posting daily
- C) Editing
- D) Captions

**Answer:** A — Sponsorships, products, services, memberships, and affiliates all earn.

2. What should monetisation match?
- A) Your stage and niche
- B) A trend
- C) A competitor
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — Match the option to your audience size and niche.

3. How many should you start with?
- A) One
- B) All of them
- C) Five
- D) None

**Answer:** A — Pick one option to start and add others as you grow.

4. Why diversify income?
- A) To reduce risk
- B) To confuse fans
- C) To work more
- D) To spend more

**Answer:** A — Multiple income streams reduce dependence on one source.

### Module 3 · Step 2: Sponsored Content That Keeps Trust

Sponsorships pay well, but a bad fit or hidden ad can damage trust fast. The rule is simple: only work with products you would recommend anyway, and disclose clearly.

AI helps you vet sponsors, draft pitches, and keep sponsored content in your voice.

## Learning Objectives

- Choose sponsors that fit
- Disclose sponsorships clearly
- Keep sponsored content on-voice
- Protect audience trust

## Choosing Sponsors

- Only promote products you would recommend
- Check fit with your audience and values
- Decline offers that do not fit
### Why
- Fit protects trust
- A bad product hurts you, not just them
- Saying no protects the long game

## Disclosing and Staying On-Voice

- Disclose sponsorship clearly and early
- Write the ad in your own voice
- Integrate it naturally into the content
### The rule
- Always disclose
- Only genuine recommendations
- Keep your voice

## The Sponsor Prompt

```text
A brand in [category] offered a sponsorship. Help me vet
the fit for my [niche] audience, draft a clear disclosure,
and write the ad in my [tone] voice.
```
- A fit assessment
- A clear disclosure
- An on-voice ad

## Key Takeaways

- Only work with sponsors you would recommend anyway
- Check fit with your audience and values
- Always disclose sponsorships clearly and early
- Write ads in your own voice
- Declining a bad fit protects long-term trust

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your sponsor fit criteria
2. Draft a disclosure line
3. Write one ad in your voice

**Example Quiz**

1. Which sponsors should you accept?
- A) Only ones you would recommend
- B) Any paying brand
- C) The highest bidder
- D) Random brands

**Answer:** A — Only promote products you would genuinely recommend.

2. What must you always do?
- A) Disclose the sponsorship
- B) Hide the ad
- C) Delete the post
- D) Lie

**Answer:** A — Disclose sponsorships clearly and early to keep trust.

3. How should the ad sound?
- A) In your own voice
- B) Like a robot
- C) Like the brand
- D) Like a press release

**Answer:** A — Write sponsored content in your own voice.

4. What does declining a bad fit protect?
- A) Long-term trust
- B) Short-term cash
- C) Nothing
- D) Your follower count

**Answer:** A — Saying no to bad fits protects your audience's trust.

### Module 4 · Step 3: Your Own Product or Service

Your own product or service is often the most durable income: you set the price, you own the relationship, and it can scale.

Start with the smallest thing that solves a real problem your audience already has.

## Learning Objectives

- Create your own product or service
- Start small and solve a real problem
- Validate before building big
- Scale what sells

## Product or Service

- **Product:** courses, templates, guides, tools
- **Service:** coaching, consulting, done-for-you work
- Start with what you can deliver now
### Why
- Your own offer is durable income
- You control price and margin
- Services fund products

## Starting Small

- Solve a problem the audience already has
- Validate before building big
- Improve from customer feedback
### The rule
- Start with the smallest useful thing
- Validate with real customers
- Scale only what sells

## The Offer Prompt

```text
My audience is [describe] and struggles with [problem].
Suggest 3 product or service ideas I could start small,
how to validate each, and how to price the first one.
```
- Three offer ideas
- Validation steps
- A first price

## Key Takeaways

- Your own product or service is durable income
- Start with the smallest thing that solves a real problem
- Validate with real customers before building big
- Improve from customer feedback
- Scale only what actually sells

## Practice Challenge

1. Identify one real audience problem
2. Design the smallest useful offer
3. Plan how to validate it

**Example Quiz**

2. How should you start?
- A) With the smallest useful thing
- B) With a huge course
- C) With nothing
- D) With ads

**Answer:** A — Start small and solve a problem the audience already has.

3. What should you do before building big?
- A) Validate
- B) Spend big
- C) Hire a team
- D) Buy ads

**Answer:** A — Validate the offer with real customers before scaling.

4. What should you scale?

### Module 5 · Step 4: Pricing Your Work

Pricing is a decision, not a guess. Price on the value you deliver, not on how long it took you.

AI helps you compare pricing models and communicate value without underselling.

## Learning Objectives

- Price on value, not time
- Choose a pricing model
- Communicate value clearly
- Avoid underselling

## Value-Based Pricing

- Price on the value delivered
- Compare models: one-off, subscription, tiers
- Charge what the outcome is worth
### Why
- Time-based pricing penalises skill
- Value pricing rewards results
- Clear value justifies the price

## Communicating Value

- Explain the outcome, not the features
- Use testimonials and results
- Raise prices as you improve
### The rule
- Price on outcomes
- Communicate value clearly
- Do not undersell yourself

## The Pricing Prompt

```text
Help me price [offer] for my [niche] audience. Compare
one-off, subscription, and tiered models, suggest a price
based on the outcome, and how to communicate the value.
```
- Models compared
- A suggested price
- A value message

## Key Takeaways

- Pricing is a decision, not a guess
- Price on the value you deliver, not time spent
- Compare one-off, subscription, and tiered models
- Explain the outcome, not the features
- Raise prices as your results improve

## Practice Challenge

1. List the outcomes your offer delivers
2. Compare two pricing models
3. Set your price and a value message

**Example Quiz**

1. What should pricing be based on?
- A) Value delivered
- B) Time spent
- C) Competitor price only
- D) A guess

**Answer:** A — Price on the value delivered rather than hours worked.

2. Which is a pricing model?
- A) Tiered
- B) Random
- C) Free forever
- D) None

**Answer:** A — One-off, subscription, and tiered are common models.

3. What should you explain?
- A) The outcome
- B) The features
- C) The hours
- D) The tools

**Answer:** A — Communicate the outcome and value, not just features.

4. When should you raise prices?
- A) As your results improve
- B) Never
- C) Randomly
- D) Only in a crisis

**Answer:** A — Raise prices as your skills and results improve.

### Module 6 · Step 5: The Creator Business Plan

A one-page creator business plan keeps you focused: who you serve, what you make, how you earn, and what you are building toward.

AI helps you draft and stress-test the plan so it is realistic, not just aspirational.

## Learning Objectives

- Write a one-page creator business plan
- Define audience, content, and income
- Set realistic goals
- Review and adjust the plan

## The One-Page Plan

- **Who:** the audience you serve
- **What:** the content you make
- **How:** how you earn
- **Where:** the goal you are building toward
### Why
- One page keeps focus
- A plan turns posting into a business
- Review keeps it honest

## Keeping It Realistic

- Base goals on your current numbers
- Set milestones you can measure
- Review monthly and adjust
### The rule
- One page only
- Realistic, measurable goals
- Review and adjust monthly

## The Business-Plan Prompt

```text
Help me write a one-page creator business plan: who I
serve ([audience]), what I make ([content]), how I earn
([offers]), and a 12-month goal. Keep it realistic.
```
- A one-page plan
- A 12-month goal
- Realistic and measurable

## Key Takeaways

- A one-page plan keeps you focused
- Define who you serve, what you make, and how you earn
- Set a realistic 12-month goal
- Make milestones measurable
- Review and adjust the plan monthly

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your one-page business plan
2. Set a 12-month goal
3. Schedule a monthly review

**Example Quiz**

1. How long should the plan be?
- A) One page
- B) Fifty pages
- C) Ten pages
- D) No plan

**Answer:** A — A one-page plan keeps you focused and is easy to review.

2. What should the plan define?
- A) Who, what, how, and the goal
- B) Only income
- C) Only content
- D) Only followers

**Answer:** A — Define your audience, content, income, and goal.

3. What kind of goals should you set?
- A) Realistic and measurable
- B) Impossible
- C) Vague
- D) None

**Answer:** A — Base goals on current numbers and make them measurable.

4. How often should you review?
- A) Monthly
- B) Never
- C) Yearly
- D) Daily

**Answer:** A — Review and adjust the plan monthly to stay on track.
