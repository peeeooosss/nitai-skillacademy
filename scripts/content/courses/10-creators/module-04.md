### Module 1 · Consistency Beats Virality

One viral post is luck; consistent posting is a strategy. Audiences and algorithms reward creators who show up regularly.

A calendar turns consistency from willpower into a system you follow.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand why consistency wins
- Stop chasing viral luck
- Use a calendar for consistency
- Build a sustainable pace

## Why Consistency Wins

- Algorithms reward regular posting
- Audiences build a habit of watching
- Skills improve with repetition
### Why
- Viral hits are unpredictable
- Consistency compounds
- A pace you can keep beats bursts

## Building a Pace

- Choose a frequency you can sustain
- Plan ahead with a calendar
- Protect your production days
### The rule
- Consistency over intensity
- Pick a sustainable pace
- Let the calendar decide

## The Consistency Prompt

```text
I can post [X] times a week on [platform] about [niche].
Suggest a sustainable weekly rhythm and the days to batch
and schedule, so consistency does not depend on motivation.
```
- A weekly rhythm
- Batch and schedule days
- A sustainable pace

## Key Takeaways

- One viral post is luck; consistency is strategy
- Algorithms and audiences reward regular posting
- Choose a pace you can actually sustain
- Plan ahead with a calendar
- Consistency compounds; bursts do not

## Practice Challenge

1. Choose a posting frequency you can keep
2. Pick your batch and schedule days
3. Commit for four weeks

**Example Quiz**

- A) Consistency

**Answer:** A — Consistent posting is a strategy; virality is luck.

2. What do algorithms reward?
- A) Regular posting
- B) Random posting
- C) Silence
- D) One post

**Answer:** A — Algorithms favour creators who post regularly.

3. What pace should you choose?
- A) A sustainable one
- B) The fastest possible
- C) A punishing one
- D) No pace

**Answer:** A — Pick a pace you can keep so consistency lasts.

4. What turns consistency into a system?
- A) A calendar
- B) Willpower
- C) Luck
- D) Motivation

**Answer:** A — A calendar makes consistency a system rather than willpower.

### Module 2 · Step 1: The Content Pillars

Content pillars are the three to five themes you always post about. They keep your channel coherent and your ideas flowing.

Pillars make planning easy: every post fits a pillar, and every pillar gets regular attention.

## Learning Objectives

- Define three to five content pillars
- Keep content coherent
- Balance pillars over time
- Generate ideas per pillar

## What Pillars Are

- Three to five core themes
- What your channel is known for
- The buckets every idea fits into
### Why
- Pillars keep content coherent
- They make planning fast
- They build a recognisable channel

## Using Pillars

- Assign each idea to a pillar
- Rotate pillars across the calendar
- Generate ideas per pillar with AI
### The rule
- Three to five pillars, no more
- Every post fits one
- Balance them over time

## The Pillar Prompt

```text
My channel is about [niche] for [audience]. Suggest 3-5
content pillars, and for each give 5 content ideas that
fit my [voice].
```
- Three to five pillars
- Five ideas per pillar
- In your voice

## Key Takeaways

- Content pillars are your three to five core themes
- They keep your channel coherent and recognisable
- Every post should fit a pillar
- Rotate pillars across your calendar
- Generate ideas per pillar with AI

## Practice Challenge

1. Define your three to five pillars
2. Assign ten existing ideas to pillars
3. Generate five ideas per pillar

**Example Quiz**

2. What does every post fit?

**Answer:** A — Every post should fit one of your content pillars.

3. What do pillars make fast?

**Answer:** A — Pillars make planning and idea generation fast.

4. What should pillars build?

### Module 3 · Step 2: Build a Month-Long Calendar

A month-long calendar maps every post before the month starts. You always know what is going out and when.

AI can fill the calendar from your pillars and ideas, leaving you to adjust and approve.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a month-long calendar
- Map posts to pillars and dates
- Balance formats and topics
- Plan ahead of time

## The Calendar

- Every post mapped to a date
- Each slot tied to a pillar
- A balance of formats and topics
### Why
- You always know what is next
- Balance prevents repetition
- Planning removes daily decisions

## Filling the Calendar

- Start from pillars and idea bank
- Assign posts to days and formats
- Leave room for timely content
### The rule
- Plan a month ahead
- Tie every slot to a pillar
- Keep some flexibility

## The Calendar Prompt

```text
Build a one-month content calendar for [platform] about
[niche]: 3-5 posts a week, each tied to a pillar, with
format and a working title. Leave two flexible slots.
```
- A month of posts
- Pillar per slot
- Two flexible slots

## Key Takeaways

- A month-long calendar maps every post in advance
- Tie every slot to a content pillar
- Balance formats and topics across the month
- Leave room for timely content
- Planning removes daily decisions

## Practice Challenge

1. Build a one-month calendar
2. Tie each post to a pillar
3. Mark two flexible slots

### Module 4 · Step 3: Repurpose One Idea into Ten Posts

Repurposing is the highest-leverage creator skill: one strong idea becomes a short, a long video, a carousel, a caption, a newsletter, and more.

AI makes repurposing fast by reformatting one idea into many formats, each suited to its platform.

## Learning Objectives

- Repurpose one idea into many posts
- Adapt content to each format
- Maximise reach per idea
- Use AI to reformat

## One Idea, Many Formats

- A short video from the main point
- A long video from the full story
- A carousel from the steps
- A caption or newsletter from the lesson
### Why
- One idea can reach many feeds
- Each format suits a platform
- Repurposing multiplies output

## Adapting, Not Copying

- Rewrite for each platform's habits
- Change length, hook, and format
- Keep the core idea consistent
### The rule
- Adapt, never copy-paste
- One core idea per set
- Tailor hook and format

## The Repurpose Prompt

```text
Take this idea [paste] and turn it into 10 posts: a short
script, a long outline, a carousel, a caption, and a
newsletter section. Adapt each to its platform. Voice: [tone].
```
- Ten adapted posts
- Multiple formats
- In your voice

## Key Takeaways

- Repurposing is the highest-leverage creator skill
- One idea becomes many posts across formats
- Adapt content to each platform, never copy-paste
- Keep the core idea consistent across posts
- AI reformats fast so one idea reaches many feeds

## Practice Challenge

1. Pick one strong idea
2. Turn it into five different formats
3. Adapt each to its platform

**Example Quiz**

2. What should you do with content for each platform?

**Answer:** A — Adapt hook, length, and format to each platform.

3. What stays consistent?
- A) The core idea

**Answer:** A — Keep the core idea consistent while adapting the format.

4. What does AI do in repurposing?

- D) Films

### Module 5 · Step 4: Batch Produce and Schedule

Batching means producing several posts in one session instead of one at a time. It saves setup time and protects your creative energy.

Schedule the batch so posts go out steadily while you move on to the next set.

## Learning Objectives

- Batch produce multiple posts
- Save setup time
- Schedule posts in advance
- Protect creative energy

## Why Batch

- Setup time is paid once, not per post
- Momentum makes production faster
- Fewer context switches, less fatigue
### Why
- Batching saves hours
- One setup, many posts
- Energy stays higher

## How to Batch

- Script a week's posts in one session
- Shoot them back to back
- Edit and schedule them together
### The rule
- Batch by stage, not by post
- Schedule everything you make
- Keep a buffer of finished posts

## The Batch Prompt

```text
Plan a batching session for [platform]: script 5 posts in
one sitting from these ideas [paste], then a shoot order
and an edit-and-schedule order. Keep it efficient.
```
- Script five posts
- A shoot order
- An edit and schedule order

## Key Takeaways

- Batching produces several posts in one session
- Setup time is paid once, not per post
- Batch by stage: script, shoot, edit, schedule
- Schedule everything you make
- Keep a buffer of finished posts

## Practice Challenge

1. Batch-script five posts in one sitting
2. Shoot and edit them together
3. Schedule the whole batch

**Example Quiz**

1. What is batching?
- A) Producing several posts in one session
- B) Posting several times a day
- C) Editing one post
- D) Skipping posts

**Answer:** A — Batching produces many posts in one focused session.

2. What does batching save?
- A) Setup time
- B) Money
- C) Storage
- D) Followers

**Answer:** A — Setup time is paid once rather than per post.

3. How should you batch?
- A) By stage: script, shoot, edit, schedule
- B) By random order
- C) One post at a time
- D) Never

**Answer:** A — Batch by stage to keep momentum and save time.

4. What should you keep?
- A) A buffer of finished posts
- B) An empty calendar
- C) No plan
- D) Only drafts

**Answer:** A — A buffer keeps you consistent even in busy weeks.

### Module 6 · Step 5: The Weekly Creator Review

A short weekly review keeps the system improving: what went out, what worked, and what to change next week.

Fifteen minutes a week turns posting into a learning loop instead of a grind.

## Learning Objectives

- Run a weekly review
- Review what worked
- Adjust the plan
- Improve every week

## The Review

- What went out this week
- What performed best and worst
- What to change next week
### Why
- Reviews turn output into learning
- Small weekly changes compound
- You stay in control of the plan

## What to Adjust

- Double down on what worked
- Cut or fix what did not
- Refill the idea bank
### The rule
- Review every week
- Adjust the plan, not your voice
- Refill ideas for next week

## The Review Prompt

```text
Here are this week's posts and metrics [paste]. Give me a
weekly review: what worked, what did not, and 3 changes
for next week's calendar.
```
- What worked and did not
- Three changes
- Next week's plan

## Key Takeaways

- Run a short weekly creator review
- Track what went out and what performed
- Double down on what worked and fix what did not
- Refill the idea bank for next week
- Small weekly changes compound over time

## Practice Challenge

1. Run a 15-minute review this week
2. List three changes for next week
3. Refill your idea bank

**Example Quiz**

1. How often should you review?

2. What do you track?
- A) What went out and what performed

**Answer:** A — Review what went out and how each post performed.

3. What do you do with what worked?
- A) Double down

**Answer:** A — Double down on what worked and fix what did not.

4. What should you refill?
- A) The idea bank
