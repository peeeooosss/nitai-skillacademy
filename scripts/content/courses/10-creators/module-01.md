# Mission 1: Your AI Content Engine: Setup

---
### Module 1 · What an AI Content Engine Is

An AI content engine is a repeatable system that turns ideas into published posts with AI doing the heavy lifting on the repetitive parts. It is not one tool; it is a workflow.

The goal is consistency without burnout: AI handles drafting, captions, and scheduling; you bring the voice, the face, and the judgment.

## Learning Objectives

- Define an AI content engine
- Understand what AI handles vs what you handle
- Commit to a repeatable system
- Avoid the burnout trap

## A System, Not a Tool

- A workflow from idea to published post
- AI handles repetitive steps
- You keep voice and judgment
### Why
- Consistency beats sporadic effort
- Systems prevent burnout
- One tool is not a strategy

## Your Role vs AI's Role

- You: ideas, voice, face, taste, relationships
- AI: drafting, captions, hashtags, scheduling
- You approve everything before it goes out
### The rule
- AI drafts, you decide
- Your voice is the differentiator
- Never publish unchecked AI output

## The Engine Prompt

```text
Help me design an AI content engine for my [platform]
channel about [niche]. List the steps from idea to
published post, which steps AI can do, and which stay mine.
```
- Steps from idea to post
- AI steps vs your steps
- Built around your niche

## Key Takeaways

- An AI content engine is a workflow, not one tool
- AI handles repetitive steps; you keep voice and judgment
- Consistency beats sporadic effort
- You approve everything before it goes out
- Your voice is the differentiator

## Practice Challenge

1. Write the steps from idea to published post
2. Mark which steps AI can do
3. Mark which steps must stay yours

**Example Quiz**

1. What is an AI content engine?
- A) A repeatable workflow from idea to post
- B) A single tool
- C) A scheduling app
- D) A camera

**Answer:** A — It is a system, not one tool, that turns ideas into posts.

2. What does AI handle?
- A) Repetitive steps like drafting and captions
- B) Your voice
- C) Your face
- D) Your relationships

**Answer:** A — AI handles the repetitive layer; you bring voice and judgment.

3. What must you do before publishing?
- A) Approve everything
- B) Publish instantly
- C) Skip review
- D) Trust the AI

**Answer:** A — You approve every post before it goes out.

4. What is your differentiator?
- A) Your voice
- B) The tool
- C) The algorithm
- D) The platform

**Answer:** A — Your voice and taste are what set you apart.

### Module 2 · Pick Your Platform and Niche

You cannot be everywhere at once. Pick one primary platform and one clear niche, and go deep before you spread out.

AI helps you research niches and adapt to platform norms, but the choice is strategic and yours.

## Learning Objectives

- Choose one primary platform
- Define a clear niche
- Adapt to platform norms
- Avoid spreading thin

## One Platform, One Niche

- Pick where your audience already is
- Define the specific niche you serve
- Go deep before you expand
### Why
- Focus compounds
- Algorithms reward consistency
- A clear niche attracts the right audience

## Matching Content to Platform

- Short vertical video: hooks and fast value
- Long video: depth and story
- Newsletters: personal framing
- Written posts: clarity and ideas
### The rule
- Fit the platform's habits
- Do not copy-paste across platforms
- Master one first

## The Niche Prompt

```text
My interests: [list]. Audience I want: [describe]. Give
3 platform-niche combinations with why each could work.
```
- Three combinations
- Reasons each could work
- Based on your interests

## Key Takeaways

- Pick one primary platform and one clear niche
- Go deep before you expand
- Match content to the platform's habits
- Do not copy-paste across platforms
- Master one platform first

### Module 3 · The Creator's Prompt Frame

Creator prompts work best with four parts: who you are, who you talk to, what you want made, and how it should sound. That last part - voice - is what keeps AI from sounding generic.

Use the frame **Context – Audience – Task – Voice** for every content prompt.

## Learning Objectives

- Use the creator prompt frame
- Define your audience and voice
- Write prompts that sound like you
- Save the prompts that work

## The Four Parts

- **Context:** your channel, platform, and niche
- **Audience:** who you talk to and what they want
- **Task:** the exact content to make
- **Voice:** tone, style, and words you use
### Why
- Voice is what makes it yours
- Audience focus sharpens the content
- A clear task gets a usable result

## Defining Your Voice

- Describe your tone in three adjectives
- List words and phrases you use
- List words you never use
### The rule
- Voice is non-negotiable
- Generic AI copy kills trust
- Save prompts that nail your voice

## The Voice Prompt

```text
Write a [content type] for my [platform] channel. Context:
[niche]. Audience: [who]. Voice: [three adjectives, words
I use, words I avoid]. Task: [what to make].
```
- All four parts present
- Voice defined explicitly
- One content type

## Key Takeaways

- Use Context - Audience - Task - Voice
- Voice is what stops AI sounding generic
- Describe your tone in three adjectives
- List words you use and words you avoid
- Save prompts that nail your voice

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your voice guide in three adjectives
2. List five words you use and five you avoid
3. Run one prompt with the full frame

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the four parts of the frame?
- A) Context, Audience, Task, Voice
- B) Who, What, When, Where
- C) Hook, Body, Close, CTA
- D) Plan, Shoot, Edit, Post

**Answer:** A — Context, Audience, Task, and Voice keep AI output on-brand.

2. What makes content yours?
- A) Voice
- B) The tool
- C) The length
- D) The platform

**Answer:** A — Voice is the differentiator that stops AI sounding generic.

3. How do you define your voice?
- A) Three adjectives plus words to use and avoid
- B) One word
- C) The platform
- D) The font

**Answer:** A — Describe tone in adjectives and list words to use or avoid.

4. What should you do with prompts that work?
- A) Save them
- B) Delete them
- C) Forget them
- D) Share them

**Answer:** A — Save prompts that nail your voice for reuse.

### Module 4 · Your Tool Stack (Script, Edit, Caption, Schedule)

You need four kinds of tools: scripting, editing, captioning, and scheduling. Pick one reliable option in each and learn it well.

A small, mastered stack beats a dozen apps you never open.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify the four tool categories
- Choose one tool per category
- Master a small stack
- Avoid tool overwhelm

## The Four Categories

- **Scripting:** chat assistant for hooks, scripts, captions
- **Editing:** a simple video editor with AI cuts
- **Captioning:** auto-captions and subtitles
- **Scheduling:** a calendar that posts for you
### Why
- Each solves one job
- One tool per job prevents chaos
- Mastery beats novelty

## Choosing Tools

- Prefer free or cheap to start
- Prefer tools that work on your phone
- Prefer one that exports everywhere
### The rule
- One tool per category
- Free first, upgrade later
- Learn it before adding another

## The Stack Prompt

```text
Recommend a simple creator tool stack for [platform]:
one scripting tool, one editor, one captioning tool, and
one scheduler, with free options first and why each.
```
- One tool per category
- Free options first
- Reasons for each

## Key Takeaways

- You need scripting, editing, captioning, and scheduling tools
- Choose one reliable tool per category
- Prefer free or cheap and phone-friendly options
- A small mastered stack beats a dozen apps
- Learn each tool before adding another

## Practice Challenge

1. Choose one tool for each of the four categories
2. Set them up on your phone
3. Use them for one full post

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the four tool categories?
- A) Scripting, editing, captioning, scheduling
- B) Camera, mic, light, tripod
- C) Post, story, reel, live
- D) Text, image, audio, video

**Answer:** A — You need one tool in each of the four categories.

2. How many tools per category?
- A) One
- B) Five
- C) Ten
- D) None

**Answer:** A — One tool per job prevents chaos and builds mastery.

3. Which tools should you start with?
- A) Free or cheap ones
- B) The most expensive
- C) The newest
- D) The most complex

**Answer:** A — Start free or cheap and upgrade only when needed.

4. What beats a dozen apps?
- A) A small, mastered stack
- B) More apps
- C) Newer apps
- D) Paid apps

**Answer:** A — Mastering a few tools beats collecting many.

### Module 5 · Set Up Your Content System

A content system is where ideas live and how they become posts: an idea bank, a calendar, and a production checklist.

Set it up once and posting stops depending on motivation.

## Learning Objectives

- Build an idea bank
- Set up a content calendar
- Create a production checklist
- Make posting independent of mood

## The Three Parts

- **Idea bank:** where every idea is captured
- **Calendar:** when each post goes out
- **Checklist:** the steps to produce a post
### Why
- Ideas disappear without a bank
- A calendar creates consistency
- A checklist prevents missed steps

## Setting It Up

- One place for ideas, always open
- A simple weekly calendar
- A short checklist you follow every time
### The rule
- Capture every idea
- Plan a week ahead
- Follow the checklist

## The System Prompt

```text
Help me set up a creator content system: an idea bank,
a weekly calendar template, and a production checklist
for [platform] and [niche]. Keep it simple.
```
- Idea bank
- Calendar template
- Production checklist

## Key Takeaways

- A content system is an idea bank, a calendar, and a checklist
- Capture every idea in one place
- Plan at least a week ahead
- Follow the production checklist every time
- A system makes posting independent of motivation

## Practice Challenge

1. Create your idea bank
2. Set up a weekly calendar template
3. Write your production checklist

**Answer:** A — An idea bank, a calendar, and a checklist form the system.

2. Why keep an idea bank?

- D) It saves money

**Answer:** A — A single place for ideas stops them being lost.

3. How far ahead should you plan?

4. What does a system make posting independent of?

### Module 6 · Your First Week Plan

The best way to start is a concrete first week: three to five posts, produced with your new system, using your tools.

The goal of week one is not virality - it is proving the system works and shipping consistently.

## Learning Objectives

- Plan your first week of content
- Produce three to five posts
- Prove the system works
- Build the posting habit

## The First Week

- Three to five posts is enough to start
- Use the idea bank, calendar, and checklist
- Keep each post simple
### Why
- Small volume builds the habit
- Consistency matters more than perfection
- Week one proves the system

## Producing the Posts

- Batch-script with AI
- Shoot in one session
- Edit and caption in one session
- Schedule them all
### The rule
- Batch to save time
- Ship on schedule
- Review what worked

## The Week-One Prompt

```text
Plan my first week of [platform] content on [niche]:
3-5 post ideas with hooks, a batching plan, and a schedule.
Keep it doable for a beginner.
```
- 3-5 ideas with hooks
- A batching plan
- A simple schedule

## Key Takeaways

- Start with three to five posts in week one
- Use the idea bank, calendar, and checklist
- Batch-script, shoot, edit, and schedule
- Consistency matters more than perfection
- Review what worked at the end of the week

## Practice Challenge

1. Plan three to five posts for week one
2. Batch-produce and schedule them
3. Review the results at week's end

**Example Quiz**

1. How many posts in week one?
- A) Three to five

- C) One
- D) Zero

**Answer:** A — Three to five posts build the habit without overwhelming you.

2. What should you do with production?
- A) Batch it

- C) Skip it
- D) Outsource it

**Answer:** A — Batching script, shoot, edit, and schedule saves time.

3. What matters more than perfection?
- A) Consistency
- B) Quality
- C) Length
- D) Equipment

**Answer:** A — Showing up consistently matters more than perfect posts.

4. What is the goal of week one?
- A) Prove the system and ship consistently
- B) Go viral
- C) Get sponsors
- D) Gain a million followers

**Answer:** A — Week one proves the system works and builds the habit.
