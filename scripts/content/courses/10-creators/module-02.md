### Module 1 · Where Ideas Come From

Ideas are not magic; they come from inputs. Your audience's questions, your own experience, other niches, and the news are all renewable sources.

AI is a great idea generator, but the best ideas come from your audience and your life. Feed AI those inputs and it multiplies them.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify reliable idea sources
- Use audience questions as input
- Mine your own experience
- Feed AI real inputs

## Renewable Idea Sources

- Your audience's questions and comments
- Your own experience and mistakes
- Adjacent niches and industries
- News and trends in your niche
### Why
- Sources renew themselves
- Audience questions are proven demand
- Experience is unique to you

## Feeding AI Real Inputs

- Paste real comments and questions
- Describe your own stories
- Ask for variations and angles
### The rule
- Real inputs beat generic prompts
- Your experience is the edge
- AI multiplies, you source

## The Idea-Source Prompt

```text
Here are 20 questions my audience asked [paste]. Turn them
into 20 content ideas for my [platform] channel on [niche],
with a hook angle for each.
```
- Real questions as input
- Twenty ideas
- A hook angle each

## Key Takeaways

- Ideas come from inputs, not magic
- Audience questions are proven demand
- Your own experience is unique and renewable
- Feed AI real comments and stories
- AI multiplies inputs; you source them

## Practice Challenge

1. Collect 20 audience questions
2. Add five of your own experiences
3. Generate 20 content ideas with hooks

**Answer:** A — Audience questions and your own experience are renewable sources.

- A) They are proven demand

**Answer:** A — Real questions show what your audience actually wants.

3. What should you feed AI?
- A) Real comments and stories

**Answer:** A — Real inputs produce better, more specific ideas.

4. What does AI do with inputs?

### Module 2 · Step 1: Build an Idea Bank

An idea bank is a running list of content ideas, always being filled. When you sit down to create, you never start from a blank page.

Capture every idea the moment it arrives, and use AI to expand and organise the bank.

## Learning Objectives

- Keep a running idea bank
- Capture ideas immediately
- Expand and organise with AI
- Never start from blank

## The Bank

- One place for every idea
- Capture immediately, sort later
- Keep it always accessible
### Why
- Ideas vanish quickly
- A full bank removes blank-page panic
- Sorting later keeps capture easy

## Expanding and Organising

- Turn one idea into several angles
- Group ideas by pillar or format
- Mark the strongest ones to produce
### The rule
- Capture first, judge later
- Expand with AI
- Organise by content pillar

## The Bank Prompt

```text
Expand my idea bank for [niche] on [platform]: take these
ideas [paste] and give me 5 angles, formats, and hooks
for each. Group them by content pillar.
```
- Five angles per idea
- Formats and hooks
- Grouped by pillar

## Key Takeaways

- An idea bank means you never start from a blank page
- Capture ideas immediately and sort later
- Turn one idea into several angles
- Organise the bank by content pillar
- Mark the strongest ideas to produce

## Practice Challenge

1. Start an idea bank in one accessible place

3. Expand five into multiple angles

### Module 3 · Step 2: Hooks That Stop the Scroll

The hook decides whether anyone sees the rest. On a feed, you have about two seconds to earn attention.

AI can generate dozens of hook options; you choose the one that is true, specific, and in your voice.

## Learning Objectives

- Write hooks that stop the scroll
- Use proven hook patterns
- Generate hook options with AI
- Choose truthful, on-voice hooks

## The Two Seconds

- The hook decides whether the rest is seen
- Specific beats clever
- The promise must be real
### Why
- Feeds reward early retention
- Clickbait burns trust
- A real promise keeps viewers

## Hook Patterns

- The question: 'Why does [X] happen?'
- The mistake: 'You are doing [X] wrong'
- The result: 'How I got [result] in [time]'
- The contrarian: 'Everyone says [X]; here is the truth'
### The rule
- Match the pattern to the content
- Never promise what you do not deliver
- Keep it in your voice

## The Hook Prompt

```text
Give me 15 hook options for a [platform] video about [topic]
for [audience]. Use question, mistake, result, and contrarian
patterns. Keep them truthful and in a [tone] voice.
```
- Fifteen options
- Multiple patterns
- Truthful and on-voice

## Key Takeaways

- The hook decides whether anyone sees the rest
- You have about two seconds to earn attention
- Use proven patterns: question, mistake, result, contrarian
- Never promise what you do not deliver
- Keep hooks truthful and in your voice

## Practice Challenge

1. Generate 15 hooks for one video
2. Choose the best truthful option
3. Test two hooks on the same content

**Example Quiz**

1. What does the hook decide?
- A) Whether anyone sees the rest
- B) The edit
- C) The caption
- D) The thumbnail

**Answer:** A — The hook earns the first two seconds of attention.

2. Which is a hook pattern?
- A) The mistake: 'You are doing X wrong'
- B) The summary
- C) The credits
- D) The outro

**Answer:** A — Question, mistake, result, and contrarian are proven patterns.

3. What must a hook never do?
- A) Promise what you do not deliver
- B) Be specific
- C) Be truthful
- D) Match the content

**Answer:** A — Clickbait promises that are not kept burn audience trust.

4. What should hooks stay in?
- A) Your voice
- B) Someone else's voice
- C) Jargon
- D) Silence

**Answer:** A — Keep hooks truthful and in your own voice.

### Module 4 · Step 3: Script the Short (Hook-Value-Payoff)

Short-form scripts have a simple skeleton: hook, value, payoff. The hook earns attention, the value delivers, the payoff rewards the watch.

A tight short is one idea, delivered fast, with a reason to watch to the end.

## Learning Objectives

- Use the hook-value-payoff structure
- Keep one idea per short
- Write for retention
- End with a payoff

## The Skeleton

- **Hook:** the first two seconds
- **Value:** the one useful idea
- **Payoff:** the reward or call to action
### Why
- One idea keeps it clear
- Fast delivery holds attention
- A payoff earns the watch

## Writing for Retention

- Cut every unnecessary word
- Open loops and close them
- Keep the payoff until the end
### The rule
- One idea per short
- Write the hook first
- Never bury the payoff

## The Short-Script Prompt

```text
Write a 30-second [platform] script on [topic] using
hook, value, payoff. Hook in the first line, one clear
idea, and a payoff at the end. Tone: [voice].
```
- Hook in line one
- One clear idea
- Payoff at the end

## Key Takeaways

- Short scripts use hook, value, and payoff
- Keep one idea per short
- Cut every unnecessary word
- Open loops and close them for retention
- Never bury the payoff

## Practice Challenge

1. Write a 30-second script for one idea
2. Rewrite the hook three ways
3. Read it aloud and cut filler

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the short-form skeleton?
- A) Hook, value, payoff
- B) Intro, body, outro
- C) Who, what, why
- D) Start, middle, end

**Answer:** A — Hook earns attention, value delivers, payoff rewards the watch.

2. How many ideas per short?
- A) One
- B) Five
- C) Ten
- D) As many as possible

**Answer:** A — One idea keeps the short clear and focused.

3. Where does the payoff go?
- A) At the end
- B) In the first line
- C) Nowhere
- D) In the caption

**Answer:** A — Keeping the payoff to the end earns the watch.

4. What should you do with unnecessary words?
- A) Cut them
- B) Keep them
- C) Repeat them
- D) Add more

**Answer:** A — Cutting filler keeps the pace fast for retention.

### Module 5 · Step 4: Script the Long (Story Arc)

Longer videos need a story arc, not just information. A beginning that sets up a problem, a middle that develops it, and an end that resolves it.

AI helps outline the arc and draft sections; you supply the story and the specifics.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a story arc for long content
- Structure setup, development, resolution
- Hold attention over time
- Use AI to outline and draft

## The Arc

- **Setup:** the problem and the stakes
- **Development:** the journey, turns, and detail
- **Resolution:** the outcome and the lesson
### Why
- Story holds attention longer
- Stakes create curiosity
- A lesson makes it shareable

## Holding Attention

- Tease the outcome early
- Add turns and reveals
- Re-hook at each section
### The rule
- Outline before you write
- One arc, not many
- Every section earns the next

## The Long-Script Prompt

```text
Outline a 10-minute [platform] video on [topic] as a story
arc: setup, development, resolution. Include re-hooks for
each section and a lesson at the end. Tone: [voice].
```
- A full arc
- Re-hooks per section
- A closing lesson

## Key Takeaways

- Long videos need a story arc
- Structure it as setup, development, resolution
- Stakes and turns hold attention
- Re-hook at each section
- Outline before you write

## Practice Challenge

1. Outline one long video as an arc
2. Add a re-hook to each section
3. Write the closing lesson

**Example Quiz**

1. What does a long video need?
- A) A story arc
- B) Just facts
- C) No structure
- D) Only visuals

**Answer:** A — A setup, development, and resolution arc holds attention.

2. What creates curiosity?
- A) Stakes
- B) Length
- C) Jargon
- D) Silence

**Answer:** A — Raising the stakes early makes viewers want the outcome.

3. What should you add at each section?
- A) A re-hook
- B) A sponsor
- C) A break
- D) A title card

**Answer:** A — Re-hooking at each section keeps attention over time.

4. What should you do before writing?
- A) Outline
- B) Record

- D) Edit

**Answer:** A — Outlining the arc first keeps the video focused.

### Module 6 · Step 5: The Idea-to-Script Workflow

A workflow turns a raw idea into a finished script in repeatable steps: capture, choose, outline, draft, tighten.

Following the same steps every time makes scripting fast and removes the blank-page problem.

## Learning Objectives

- Follow a repeatable script workflow
- Move from idea to script efficiently
- Use AI at each step
- Tighten before you shoot

## The Five Steps

- **Capture:** the idea into the bank
- **Choose:** pick the strongest angle
- **Outline:** hook, structure, payoff
- **Draft:** write it with AI
- **Tighten:** cut and polish for your voice
### Why
- Repeatable steps remove friction
- AI speeds drafting
- Tightening protects your voice

## Where AI Fits

- AI drafts the outline and first pass
- You supply the story and specifics
- You tighten until it sounds like you
### The rule
- Same steps every time
- AI drafts, you finalise
- Never shoot an untightened script

## The Workflow Prompt

```text
Take this idea [paste] and run my script workflow: choose
the strongest angle, outline it, draft a [length] script,
then suggest cuts to tighten it. Tone: [voice].
```
- Strongest angle chosen
- A drafted script
- Suggested cuts

## Key Takeaways

- Use a repeatable five-step script workflow
- Capture, choose, outline, draft, tighten
- AI drafts; you supply story and specifics
- Tighten until it sounds like you
- Never shoot an untightened script

## Practice Challenge

1. Run one idea through all five steps
2. Note where AI saved the most time
3. Save the workflow as a template

**Example Quiz**

- C) Hook, value, payoff, CTA, end
- D) Who, what, when, where, why

**Answer:** A — The workflow moves an idea from capture to a tightened script.

2. What does AI do in the workflow?
- A) Drafts outline and first pass
- B) Publishes
- C) Chooses your niche
- D) Films

**Answer:** A — AI drafts; you supply story and specifics and finalise.

3. What protects your voice?
- A) Tightening
- B) Publishing fast
- C) Copying
- D) Skipping the draft

**Answer:** A — Tightening until it sounds like you protects your voice.

4. What should never be shot?
- A) An untightened script
- B) A good hook
- C) A clear idea
- D) A payoff

**Answer:** A — Always tighten before you shoot to keep quality high.
