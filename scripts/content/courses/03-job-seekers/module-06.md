# Mission 6: Personal Brand, Portfolio & Offer Negotiation

## Learning Objectives

- Build a small, sharp personal brand that proves your skills

- Negotiate salary and terms with AI-prepared scripts

### Module 1 · Your Brand Is Your Proof
Nobody needs you to be famous. You need to be **findable and provable**: when someone searches your name, they should see evidence that you can do the thing you claim.

That endpoint is three things: a one-page personal site, a tidy GitHub, and a focused LinkedIn. Nothing more is needed at this stage — and attempting more delays you.

**The principle:** Brand is not about aesthetics. It's about proof. Every piece of your online presence should answer one question: "Can this person actually do what they say?"

A hiring manager who finds your name on a shortlist will Google you within 60 seconds. What they see in that search decides whether your application gets read or ignored.

**Answer:** B — When someone searches your name they should see proof you can do the thing you claim.

- A) Blog, podcast, YouTube
- B) One-page site, tidy GitHub, focused LinkedIn
- C) Certificates, degrees, cover letter
- D) Resume, references, portfolio

**Answer:** B — A one-page personal site + tidy GitHub + focused LinkedIn are sufficient.

3. What question should every brand piece answer?
- A) How many followers
- B) Can this person actually do what they say
- C) What is the price
- D) What city are they in

**Answer:** B — Every piece should answer: can this person actually do what they say?

### Module 2 · Building the Three-Proof Brand
Three pieces, built fast:

**Proof 1 — Your project page.** One-page site on Netlify, Vercel, or GitHub Pages (all free). Include: your story sentence, 2–3 STAR projects from your proof work, a 60-second demo or screenshot, and a contact CTA. Two to three hours maximum — don't rebuild the internet.

> "Draft a one-page site copy using my portfolio notes [paste]: hero line, 3 project blurbs, footer. Keep it 200 words of real content."

Then you edit, host, screenshot.

**Proof 2 — GitHub discipline.** Clean repos: README with context and screenshot, honest AI-use statement, 2+ commits per project. Messy history plus empty README signals the opposite of professional.

**Proof 3 — Content that costs 15 minutes.** One short post per week: "Made X with [tool], here's what I learned, here's the mini-repo." Regular tiny posts (build-in-public) beat one perfect essay — consistency compounds.

**The compounding effect:** By week 12, you have 12 posts, a portfolio site, clean repos, and an active LinkedIn. That's a brand that proves itself.

### Module 3 · Handling the Offer Stage
When interviews progress to offers, protect yourself:

**Reference-check the company too.** Salary bands on the web, Glassdoor-style signals, current employees on LinkedIn. Know what you're walking into.

**When asked your expected salary:** Give a researched band, not a number into the void.
> "For my profile and this market, I'm targeting [X]–[Y]. What's the band you're working with?"

Turn it into a two-way conversation. Silence after stating your range is normal — let them respond first.

**Red flags at the offer stage:** Vague job description that changes between interviews, pressure to accept immediately, no written offer letter, or salary below the advertised range. These signal problems that won't improve after you join.

**Protect your timeline:** If you have multiple processes running, let each employer know you have other interviews in progress. This is honest and creates appropriate urgency without lying.

**Example Quiz**

1. What should you do when asked your expected salary?
- A) Give one number
- B) Give a researched band and ask theirs
- C) Refuse to answer
- D) Name a huge number

**Answer:** B — A researched band turns salary into a two-way conversation: 'I'm targeting X-Y, what band are you working with?'

2. What is an offer-stage red flag?
- A) A written offer
- B) Pressure to accept immediately
- C) A salary range
- D) A clear role description

**Answer:** B — Pressure to accept immediately and vague changing role descriptions are red flags.

3. Why should you mention other in-progress interviews?
- A) To brag
- B) It is honest and creates appropriate urgency
- C) To speed up rejection
- D) It looks bad

**Answer:** B — Honesty about active processes creates appropriate urgency without lying.

### Module 4 · Negotiation: Scripts and Rules
Most negotiators lose by being unprepared, not by being rude. Build your script with AI:

> "Act as a salary negotiation coach. My offer: [details]. Market band for [role/location]: [X–Y]. I have [strengths/proof]. Give me a 3-step negotiation script: 1) positive acceptance, 2) a researched counter within the band, 3) a graceful ask on one non-salary item (learning budget, remote days, title). Keep it under 150 words and polite."

**Rules that actually work:**

- **Never accept on the spot.** "Thank you — I'd like to review the total package and respond by [day]." Always.
- **Negotiate the total package,** not just salary: bonus, learning budget, remote days, equipment, title, notice/start date.
- **One counter, then listen.** You don't need to win every dollar — you need a respectful exchange.
- **Know your walk-away number before the call.** Uncomfortable but saves regret.

**The tone:** Firm but warm. You want this job — you also want fair terms. Both can be true at the same time.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the first rule before accepting an offer?
- A) Say yes fast
- B) Never accept on the spot
- C) Ask for double
- D) Resign immediately

**Answer:** B — 'Thank you - I'd like to review the total package and respond by [day].' Always.

2. What should you negotiate besides salary?
- A) Nothing
- B) The total package: bonus, budget, remote days, title, dates
- C) Only the title
- D) Only the start date

**Answer:** B — Negotiate the total package - bonus, learning budget, remote days, equipment, title, timing.

3. When should you know your walk-away number?
- A) After the call
- B) Before the call
- C) Never
- D) After accepting

**Answer:** B — Know your walk-away number before any negotiation - uncomfortable but prevents regret.

### Module 5 · Deciding: Accept, Decline, or Walk Away
A decision framework when offers arrive:

1. **Package review:** Salary + benefits + growth + commute/remote + team quality + learning curve. Score each 1–5, weight by your personal goals.

2. **Compare to your Tier-1 story** (Mission 1): Does this role accelerate the path you chose? A good salary at the wrong role compounds into career drift.

3. **Sleep on it.** Impulse "yes" and fear-based "no" are both unreliable states. Give yourself 24–48 hours minimum.

**If you decline:** Do it graciously. A polite note now is a network asset later. "Thank you for the offer. After careful consideration, I've decided to pursue a different direction. I genuinely appreciate your time and would welcome keeping in touch."

**If you accept:** Get it in writing before you resign from anything. Verbal offers are not offers — written ones are.

**The hard truth:** Sometimes the right answer is to walk away from a good offer because it's not the right role. That takes courage but prevents costly career mistakes.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does the package review score?
- A) Salary only
- B) Salary, benefits, growth, commute, team, learning — weighted by YOUR goals
- C) Company logo
- D) Interview count

**Answer:** B — Score salary, benefits, growth, commute/remote, team quality, and learning curve, weighted to your goals.

2. How does the offer compare to your Tier-1 story?
- A) It doesn't matter
- B) Does it accelerate the path you chose?
- C) Salary decides
- D) Title decides

**Answer:** B — A good salary on the wrong path compounds into career drift — check the direction.

3. What should you do before deciding?
- A) Accept instantly
- B) Sleep on it for 24-48 hours
- C) Ask friends to decide
- D) Flip a coin

**Answer:** B — Impulse 'yes' and fear-based 'no' are both unreliable — sleep on it.

### Module 6 · The Application-to-Offer System
You now have every piece. Assemble the loop:

- **Brand** (this mission) feeds the **Job search system** (Mission 5) feeds the **Mock reps** (Mission 4) feeds the **Materials** (Mission 2) feeds the **Proof projects** (Course 02). Each piece makes the next piece better.

**Weekly rhythm:**
- 4–5 quality applications + tailored materials
- 1 brand/LinkedIn update
- 2 mock interview reps (rotate types)
- 1 funnel review

**Never break the loop during rejections.** Rejections are data; the system is the plan. A rejection means one specific thing needs fixing — not that the system failed.

**Your brag file:** Start a saved notes doc "wins.txt" — every positive reply, testimonial, mock score improvement, and lesson learned. You'll need its confidence in week 8 when rejections pile up. It's not ego — it's evidence that the system works over time.

**The commitment:** Run this loop weekly for 90 days. You're hired-ready — the reps start now.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is your brag file for?
- A) Bragging publicly
- B) Evidence the system works during rejection streaks
- C) Salary records
- D) Company contacts

**Answer:** B — wins.txt stores positive replies, testimonials, and improvements — confidence evidence when rejections pile up.

2. What keeps the application-to-offer loop working?
- A) Intensity
- B) Running it weekly even through rejections
- C) Perfect conditions
- D) Luck

**Answer:** B — The loop only works if you run it weekly, even through rejections.

3. What is the final weekly commitment?
- A) 10 apps + no mocks
- B) 4-5 tailored apps + 1 brand update + 2 mocks + 1 funnel review
- C) 1 app per month
- D) Only mocks

**Answer:** B — A sustainable weekly rhythm: 4-5 apps, 1 brand update, 2 mock reps, 1 funnel review.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 — The portfolio site.** Amit built a one-page site in two hours using AI draft copy and Netlify hosting. Three projects, a demo link, and a contact CTA. A recruiter found it through LinkedIn, bookmarked it, and called him a week later.

**Example 2 — The negotiation that worked.** Neha received an offer at ₹8 LPA. She had researched the band (₹10–14 LPA). Her counter was polite and specific: 'I'm excited about this role. Based on my research and the value I bring, could we explore ₹12 LPA? I'm also interested in the learning budget.' She got ₹11 LPA plus the budget.

**Example 3 — The brag file.** After 10 rejections, Raj almost quit. He opened his wins.txt — 4 positive replies, 2 second-rounds, improving mock scores. The data proved the system was working. Two weeks later, he got his offer.

## Key Takeaways

- Brand = findable + provable: project page, tidy GitHub, small consistent content.
- Research the company and keep an offer-time negotiating script ready.
- Never accept on the spot; negotiate the total package, know your walk-away number.
- Decide with the package-score plus Tier-1 cultural fit check, and sleep on it.
- Your application-to-offer loop only works if you run it weekly, even through rejections.
