### Module 1 · Your Brand Is Your Proof

Nobody needs you to be famous. You need to be **findable and provable**: when someone searches your name, they should see evidence that you can do the thing you claim. That is the whole definition of a personal brand in a job search - not followers, not aesthetics, but proof a stranger can verify in under sixty seconds.

The proof endpoint is three pieces: a one-page personal site, a tidy GitHub, and a focused LinkedIn. Nothing more is needed at this stage - and attempting more delays you. A hiring manager who finds your name on a shortlist will Google you within 60 seconds; what they see decides whether your application gets read or ignored.

## Learning Objectives

- Define findable and provable personal branding
- Name the three-piece proof endpoint
- Explain why brand is proof, not aesthetics
- Understand the 60-second Google test

## Findable and Provable

- **Findable:** your name surfaces as a person - not a ghost or an impostor
- **Provable:** the search results demonstrate the skills you claim
- The bar is low effort and high value: a site, a GitHub, a LinkedIn - all maintained lightly
### The 60-second test
- Interviewees are googled within 60 seconds of being shortlisted
- What the recruiter finds in that window is your brand's first impression
- Missing = forgettable; proving = strong; contradicting = fatal

## The Three-Piece Endpoint

- **One-page personal site:** your story sentence, 2-3 star projects, a demo link, a contact CTA
- **Tidy GitHub:** clean repos with README, screenshots, and 2+ commits each
- **Focused LinkedIn:** keyword-rich headline, About with proof, Featured section leading
```text
The brand audit - run once a month:
1. Search my full name - three tabs: web, LinkedIn, GitHub
2. Does the top result show my work or my absence?
3. Can a stranger name my role and one achievement in 60 seconds?
4. Are the site, GitHub, and LinkedIn consistent (same photo, same headline)?
5. Fix the weakest of the three this week
```
- More than these three pieces dilutes your effort - this set is enough

## Brand Is Proof, Not Looks

- A beautiful site with no demonstrable work is decoration, not a brand
- A plain site with a project, a number, and a demo is a brand
- Every brand piece should answer one question: 'Can this person actually do what they say?'
### The proof-first order
- Build the work first, then the presentation - never the reverse
- Each proof item you ship makes the brand writes itself
- Your weekly proof habit (Mission 3) feeds this brand automatically

## Key Takeaways

- Brand = findable + provable, not famous
- Three pieces: one-page site, tidy GitHub, focused LinkedIn
- A shortlisted candidate is googled within 60 seconds
- Brand is proof, not aesthetics
- Your weekly proof habit feeds the brand automatically

### Module 2 · Building the Three-Proof Brand

The proof brand is built from three fast pieces - a project page, disciplined GitHub, and small consistent content. Each piece is deliberately cheap to build and maintain, because the value is in showing up weekly, not in one heroic build. Two to three hours gets the site live; five lines a week keeps the content engine running.

The pieces reinforce each other: the project page shows finished work, GitHub proves the work is real and documented, and the weekly posts keep a steady trail of evidence that compounds into a genuinely strong brand by week 12.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a one-page project site in 2-3 hours
- Keep clean Git repos with README, screenshot, and commits
- Publish one 15-minute build-in-public post per week
- Explain the compounding effect of small consistent proof

## Proof 1: The Project Page

- Build a one-page site on Netlify, Vercel, or GitHub Pages - all free
- Include: your story sentence, 2-3 STAR projects from your proof work, a 60-second demo or screenshot, and a contact CTA
- Time-box it to 2-3 hours maximum - do not rebuild the internet
### The site copy shortcut
- Ask AI to draft copy from your portfolio notes: hero line, 3 project blurbs, footer - under 200 words of real content
- Edit the draft (your voice, your facts), host it, screenshot the result
- A live link beats a design that never shipped

## Proof 2: GitHub Discipline

- Clean repos: README with context and screenshot, honest AI-use statement, 2+ commits per project
- A messy history plus an empty README signals the opposite of professional
- One project with a README beats five repos with nothing to read
```text
The clean-repo checklist:
1. README: 'what this does, why I built it, how to run it'
2. Screenshot or demo link in the README
3. Honest AI-use line: 'built with AI assistance for [parts]'
4. At least 2 commits - shows process, not a pasted final file
5. Pin or star the project you want seen first
```
- The AI-use line is honesty and increasingly a plus, not a confession

## Proof 3: Content That Costs 15 Minutes

- One short post per week: 'Made X with [tool], here's what I learned, here's the mini-repo'
- Regular tiny posts (build-in-public) beat one perfect essay - consistency compounds
- The posts feed the same trail a recruiter finds when they search you
### The end-state brand
- By week 12: 12 posts, a portfolio site, clean repos, an active LinkedIn
- That is a brand that proves itself - no further effort required
- Schedule the 15 minutes with the weekly proof checkpoint and it runs itself

## Key Takeaways

- Build the one-page site in 2-3 hours - live beats perfect
- GitHub: README, screenshot, honest AI-use line, 2+ commits
- Publish one 15-minute build-in-public post weekly
- Tiny consistent posts compound better than one perfect essay
- Week 12 outcome: site + repos + posts + LinkedIn = proving brand

### Module 3 · Handling the Offer Stage

The offer stage is where a search gets won or quietly lost - not by the interview, but by how you handle the moment of decision. This module protects you with the moves that separate prepared candidates from surprised ones: reference-checking the company, giving a researched salary band, spotting offer red flags, and protecting your timeline when multiple processes run.

The offer stage is a two-way conversation, not a verdict handed down. Every move below keeps you in the driver's seat while staying honest and warm.

## Learning Objectives

- Reference-check the company before accepting
- Give a researched salary band, not a single number
- Spot offer-stage red flags
- Protect your timeline when several interviews run in parallel

## Reference-Check the Company Too

- Do your own diligence: salary bands online, Glassdoor-style signals, current or past employees on LinkedIn
- Know what you are walking into - culture, growth tempo, and turnover tell stories interviews do not
- The interview reviews you; the offer stage is where you review them
### What to check
- Advertised vs offered band - a gap is the first thing to name
- Team size and growth stage match the lifestyle you want
- One conversation with a current or past employee is worth ten pages of reviews

## The Researched Band, Not the Void

```text
When asked 'what salary are you expecting?':
1. Research the market band for role + location + level
2. Say: 'For my profile and this market, I'm targeting [X]-[Y].
     What's the band you're working with?'
3. Then stay silent - let them respond first
4. A range, not a point - the low end should still be acceptable
```
- The band turns the question into a two-way conversation, not a guess
- Silence after stating your range is normal - resist filling it

## Red Flags and the Parallel-Timeline Move

- **Red flags:** vague job description that changes between interviews, pressure to accept immediately, no written offer letter, salary below the advertised range - these signal problems that will not improve after you join
- **Parallel timelines:** if you have multiple processes running, let each employer know - honest and creates appropriate urgency without lying
- Protect your decision with time: you are allowed to say 'I need a few days to review the full package'
### Why the flags matter
- Offer-stage pressure predicts onboarding-stage chaos
- A written offer is the only offer - verbal ones evaporate
- Mentioning other processes is honesty, not a threat - most recruiters respect it

## Key Takeaways

- Reference-check the company as they checked you
- Give a researched band and ask theirs - it is a conversation
- Offer red flags: changing scope, pressure, no written offer
- Written offers only - verbal offers evaporate
- Mention parallel timelines - it is honest and builds mild urgency

## Practice Challenge

2. Write and rehearse your band-and-ask line
3. List 3 offer-stage red flags you will not ignore

### Module 4 · Negotiation: Scripts and Rules

Most negotiators lose by being unprepared, not by being rude. The difference between a candidate who gets a better package and one who accepts the first number is almost always a prepared script. AI can build that script in minutes - but you must know the rules that keep it warm, honest, and effective.

This module gives you a 3-step negotiation script, the four rules that actually work (never accept on the spot, negotiate the total package, one counter then listen, know your walk-away number), and the tone that holds firm and warm at the same time.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a 3-step negotiation script with AI
- Apply the four rules that actually work in negotiation
- Negotiate the total package, not just salary
- Hold a firm-but-warm tone throughout

## The AI-Prepared Script

```text
Act as a salary negotiation coach. My offer: [details]. Market
band for [role/location]: [X-Y]. I have [strengths/proof]. Give me
a 3-step negotiation script: 1) positive acceptance, 2) a researched
counter within the band, 3) a graceful ask on one non-salary item
(learning budget, remote days, title). Keep it under 150 words and
polite.
```
- Positive acceptance first - 'I'm genuinely excited about the role' - keeps the talk warm
- The counter stays inside the researched band - grounded, not greedy
- The third step moves to a non-salary item if salary is fixed - package, not just pay

## The Four Rules

- **Never accept on the spot.** 'Thank you - I would like to review the total package and respond by [day].' Always
- **Negotiate the total package,** not just salary: bonus, learning budget, remote days, equipment, title, start date
- **One counter, then listen.** You do not need every dollar - you need a respectful exchange
- **Know your walk-away number before the call.** Uncomfortable, but it saves regret
### Why rules beat instinct
- The on-the-spot yes is the single most expensive word in hiring
- A researched package negotiation is respected; a greedy one is remembered
- Knowing your floor before the call keeps the counter calm

## The Firm-Warm Tone

- You want this job AND you want fair terms - both can be true at once
- **Firm:** your number, your timeline, your walk-away - stated plainly
- **Warm:** enthusiasm and respect throughout - you are negotiating with your future colleague
- Rehearse the script out loud with AI playing the hiring manager before the real call
### The tone checklist
- No apologies in your language - 'I can offer' not 'sorry, but'
- No ultimatums unless you mean them - one counter, then listen
- End every exchange on 'we' - 'so we can find something that works for both of us'

## Key Takeaways

- Prepare a 3-step script: accept warmly, counter in band, ask one non-salary item
- Never accept on the spot - always review the total package
- Negotiate package, not just salary
- One counter, then listen - know your walk-away number first
- Firm and warm can coexist

## Practice Challenge

2. Write your walk-away number and non-salary ask ahead of any call
3. Run one mock negotiation with AI playing the hiring manager

### Module 5 · Deciding: Accept, Decline, or Walk Away

When offers arrive, your decision system takes over - because impulse 'yes' and fear-based 'no' are both unreliable drivers. The framework here is three moves: score the package against YOUR weighted criteria, compare the role to your Tier-1 story, and sleep on it for 24-48 hours before deciding anything.

The framework also covers the two hard moves - declining graciously and walking away from a good-but-wrong offer. Both take courage, and both protect your career from expensive mistakes that a hot decision would make.

## Learning Objectives

- Score a package against your own weighted criteria
- Compare any offer to your Tier-1 target story
- Sleep on decisions for 24-48 hours
- Decline or walk away gracefully when it is the right call

## The Package Score

- Score each factor 1-5: salary, benefits, growth, commute/remote, team quality, learning curve
- Weight each factor by YOUR personal goals - a role is good relative to your priorities
- The weighted total becomes your objective comparison across offers
### Why weighting matters
- Unweighted scores treat growth and parking as equal - they are not
- Your weights reflect your life: career velocity, family, risk appetite
- Re-weight yearly as your priorities shift - today's weights are not forever

## The Tier-1 Fit Check

- Compare the offer to your Mission 1 Tier-1 story: does this role accelerate the path you chose?
- A good salary at the wrong role compounds into career drift
- The check is short: direction up, sideways, or into a detour?
```text
The decision sequence when an offer lands:
1. Get the offer IN WRITING first - verbal offers are not offers
2. Score the package with your weights
3. Check direction against your Tier-1 story
4. Sleep on it for 24-48 hours minimum
5. Then decide - accept, decline graciously, or walk away
```
- Writing before resigning anywhere - it protects every later decision

## The Graceful Decline and the Walk-Away

- **Declining:** a polite note now is a network asset later - 'Thank you for the offer. After careful consideration, I've decided to pursue a different direction. I genuinely appreciate your time and would welcome keeping in touch'
- **Walking away:** sometimes a good offer is not the right role - that takes courage but prevents costly career mistakes
- The package score catches what momentum hides: a strong number on a wrong path
### The decision test
- Would you take this role if the salary were 20% lower?
- Does it move you toward the work you said you wanted?
- If both answers are no, walk - a wrong yes is far harder to reverse than a graceful no

## Key Takeaways

- Score salary, benefits, growth, commute, team, learning - weighted to YOU
- Check direction against the Tier-1 story - wrong-path salaries drift
- Get it in writing before resigning from anything
- Sleep on every decision for 24-48 hours
- Decline and walk-away, done warmly, are network assets

### Module 6 · The Application-to-Offer System

You now have every piece: brand, search system, mocks, materials, and proof projects. The final act is assembling them into one rotating loop - the application-to-offer system - and committing to run it weekly for 90 days. Every piece feeds the next, and the loop only works if you run it through rejections without breaking the rhythm.

The loop is the entire course condensed into a weekly beat: 4-5 tailored applications, 1 brand update, 2 mock reps, 1 funnel review - plus the brag file that keeps you honest when the streak gets ugly.

## Learning Objectives

- Assemble the application-to-offer loop from all course pieces
- Run the weekly rhythm without interruption
- Maintain a brag file as evidence during rejection streaks
- Treat rejections as the system naming one specific fix

## The Loop, Assembled

- **Brand** (this mission) feeds the **search system** (Mission 5) feeds the **mock reps** (Mission 4) feeds the **materials** (Mission 2) feeds the **proof projects** (Mission 3)
- Each piece makes the next piece better - the loop is a flywheel, not a checklist
- Missing one piece slackens every other - run the whole beat, not the favourite part
### The weekly beat
- 4-5 quality applications + tailored materials
- 1 brand/LinkedIn update, 2 mock interview reps, 1 funnel review
- Same cadence every week - the loop becomes the habit

## Never Break the Loop During Rejections

- Rejections are data - the system is the plan, and a rejection names one specific thing to fix
- 'Applied 12, 3 replies, 2 interviews, 1 second-round' is a normal funnel, not a failure
- Stop-and-start searches lose their own momentum; steady loops convert
```text
The rejection-loop routine:
1. Log the rejection in the tracker - no ceremony
2. Ask: which stage does this data point to?
3. Fix that stage (materials, targeting, mocks)
4. Run the weekly beat unchanged
5. Open wins.txt when the streak feels heavy
```
- Each rejection shrinks the gap between you and the offer stage

## The Brag File: Your Evidence

- Start a saved notes doc 'wins.txt' - every positive reply, testimonial, mock score improvement, and lesson learned
- It is not ego - it is evidence that the system works over time
- By week 8, when rejections pile up, wins.txt is the confidence data you need
### The final commitment
- Run this loop weekly for 90 days - you are hired-ready; the reps start now
- The offer arrives at the end of a repeated process, not at its peak of hope
- Your job now is to keep running the loop - everything else has been built to make it work

## Key Takeaways

- One loop: brand -> search -> mocks -> materials -> proof
- Weekly beat: 4-5 apps, 1 brand update, 2 mocks, 1 funnel review
- Rejections are data naming one specific fix
- wins.txt is the confidence evidence for hard weeks
- Run the loop weekly for 90 days - constant beats heroic

## Practice Challenge

3. Commit to your 90-day run - calendar the standing weekly slots
