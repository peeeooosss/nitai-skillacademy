### Module 1 · The 'Role + AI' Mindset

Headlines scream that AI will take jobs, but the useful reality is sharper and far more encouraging: **AI replaces tasks, not whole jobs.** From HR to banking to marketing to operations, almost every career is becoming augmented rather than automated away. The skill that wins is not fighting the change - it is riding it intentionally.

The question shifts from *'Will AI replace me?'* to *'Which version of my job uses AI most?'* The people who answer that question well move ahead of the crowd. A marketing manager still manages marketing - but now uses AI for data analysis, content drafts, and campaign optimisation. The human skill stays; the tools get sharper.

Start where you are: think of your current role or last job right now. List three tasks you do daily - then ask which of those three AI could assist with, which it could fully handle, and which should stay entirely in your hands. That quick audit is the whole mindset in miniature.

## Learning Objectives

- See the career landscape as 'role + AI' instead of 'AI replaces roles'
- Explain how AI replaces tasks within jobs, not entire jobs
- Distinguish tasks AI can assist with from tasks that need human judgment
- Apply the mindset shift to your own work immediately

## A Team Upgrade, Not a Trade-Off

- AI changes **how** a job is done, not **whether** the job exists
- The accountant gains an AI that drafts ledgers; the manager gains AI reports at 9 am
- Roles that feel threatened today often become the *easiest* to accelerate next year
- The consistent theme: **human skill + AI tooling** is the new definition of a working role
### The mistake people make
- They ask 'what will AI do to my job?'
- The better question is 'what will my job look like WITH AI?'
- The first question breeds fear; the second breeds a plan

## The Three-Task Audit

```text
The three-task audit to run on your current role:
1. List 3 tasks you do daily or weekly
2. Task A: could AI fully handle it? (repetitive, rule-based)
3. Task B: could AI assist it? (drafting, searching, analysing)
4. Task C: must YOU keep it? (judgment, trust, relationships)
5. Your upgrade plan = keep C, delegate B to AI, automate A
```
- The audit turns vague anxiety into a concrete to-do list
- Most people discover only one task in three truly needs their hands
- That discovery is the lever for applying to bigger roles

## From Fear to a Plan

- Fear is real, but it is a poor strategy - it freezes action
- The counter is specificity: name the tasks, name the tools, name the skills
- Each task you hand to AI frees hours for the work that actually grows your career
### Why the mindset wins interviews
- Interviewers now ask 'how have you used AI in your work?'
- A candidate with a three-task audit answers instantly with proof
- A candidate with fear answers with a shrug - the difference is decisive

## Key Takeaways

- AI replaces tasks inside jobs, not whole jobs
- Every role is becoming 'human skill + AI tooling'
- Run the three-task audit to find your upgrade plan
- Specificity defeats fear - name the tasks and the tools
- A 'role + AI' mindset is a demonstrable interview strength

## Practice Challenge

2. For each task, note whether AI would fully handle, assist, or require you
3. Write your own one-line mindset statement: what does 'role + AI' mean for YOUR role

### Module 2 · Four Kinds of AI-Augmented Career Paths

AI-augmented careers fall into four categories, and knowing where you fit - or want to fit - gives you a target instead of a vague ambition. Each path rewards different skills, and each has its own typical jobs. Once you can name the category you want, every resume edit and every upskilling decision becomes easier to make.

The four paths are: **Deep-Hands Roles**, where you build AI systems; **Tool-Powered Domain Roles**, where you use AI daily inside your current field; **Process & Workflow Roles**, where you automate work for teams; and **Human-Advantage Roles**, where trust and judgement sit on top of AI. They are not in competition - most careers combine elements of two.

## Learning Objectives

- Name the four kinds of AI-augmented career paths
- Match your current background to the most fitting path
- Explain why tool-powered roles are the largest pool of new jobs
- Define where human judgement becomes more valuable, not less

## Path 1: Deep-Hands Roles

- You **build** the AI - data engineering, ML engineering, prompt/agent engineering
- These roles require technical depth, and they reward it with strong salaries
- Typical moves for technical folks: engineer, data scientist, ML developer
### Who should consider it
- You enjoy building systems and debugging logic
- You are willing to invest months of structured learning
- It is the narrowest funnel but the highest barrier to entry

## Path 2: Tool-Powered Domain Roles

- You **use** AI daily inside your existing field - marketing, legal, finance, HR, ops
- This is the **largest pool of new jobs** - every domain now needs AI fluency
- Your domain expertise becomes more valuable when combined with AI skills
- Typical titles: AI-augmented analyst, digital marketer with AI workflows, ops specialist
### The multiplier idea
- A domain expert who uses AI beats a generic AI user every time
- Your existing field is not a cage - it is your differentiation
- This is the path most job seekers should lead with

## Path 3 & 4: Process and Human-Advantage Roles

- **Process & Workflow Roles:** you automate work for teams (automation specialists, AI operations). You do not build the AI - you make the teams workflows faster and smarter
- **Human-Advantage Roles:** judgement, trust, and relationships with AI underneath (management, sales, coaching, care). When AI handles routine work, human judgement becomes MORE valuable, not less
```text
Quick self-check to place yourself:
1. Do I want to build AI?  -> Deep-Hands
2. Do I want to use AI in my field?  -> Tool-Powered
3. Do I want to automate for others?  -> Process & Workflow
4. Do I want to lead relationships above the tool?  -> Human-Advantage
```
- Most careers blend two - note your primary and your secondary
- Your current experience is a runway, not a cage

## Key Takeaways

- Four paths: deep-hands, tool-powered domain, process & workflow, human-advantage
- Tool-powered domain roles are the largest pool of new jobs
- Your domain expertise becomes more valuable with AI fluency
- Human judgement grows MORE valuable when AI handles routine tasks
- Your past experience transfers - it is a runway, not a cage

## Practice Challenge

1. Place yourself: run the quick self-check and name your primary and secondary path
2. List 3 real roles in your primary path that you could pursue this year
3. Write one transfer story: what you did before maps to which new role

**Example Quiz**

1. How many kinds of AI-augmented career paths does this course describe?
- A) 4
- B) 2
- C) 5
- D) 8

**Answer:** A — Four: deep-hands, tool-powered domain, process & workflow, human-advantage.

2. Which path is the largest pool of new jobs?
- A) Tool-powered domain roles
- B) Deep-hands roles
- C) Human-advantage roles
- D) Process roles

**Answer:** A — Every domain now needs AI fluency, making tool-powered roles the largest pool.

3. In which path does human judgement become MORE valuable?
- A) Human-advantage
- B) Deep-hands
- C) Process & workflow
- D) None

**Answer:** A — When AI handles routine work, trust and judgement in management, sales, coaching, and care grow more valuable.

4. You did logistics work. What is a logical AI-augmented transition?

**Answer:** A — Domain experience transfers to automation roles - 'logistics to logistics-automation specialist'.

### Module 3 · Mapping Your Skills to a Target Role

A target role is a compass: once you have one, every application, every course, and every conversation points the same direction. But choosing a target blindly is guesswork. This module turns that guesswork into a five-step process that produces a realistic, motivating shortlist and a single 'North Star' role.

The five steps are: inventory your skills, generate a role shortlist with AI, pick a Tier-1 target, run a gap analysis, and build a 90-day plan. You have done versions of this yourself before - the difference here is that AI helps you generate options widely, while the plan helps you choose and commit narrowly.

## Learning Objectives

- Inventory your strongest 8 skills, split into hard and soft
- Generate a realistic 8-role AI shortlist spanning all four paths
- Choose one Tier-1 primary role plus a backup
- Run a gap analysis and draft a 90-day plan

## Step 1-2: Skills and the Shortlist

- **Inventory:** list your 8 strongest skills - split hard (tools, processes, languages) and soft (communication, problem solving, reliability)
- **Generate creativity:** ask AI for 8 realistic roles - 2 deep-hands, 3 tool-powered in your domain, 2 process/automation, 1 human-advantage
- Each role should come with typical tasks, a salary range, and 2 core AI skills needed
### The honesty check
- Recruiters verify claims - do not inflate your skill list
- Rank the 8 by two filters: feasible within 90 days of effort, and genuinely motivating
- A role that bores you will not survive the 90-day grind

## The Shortlist Prompt

```text
Act as a career strategist. My background: [skills, industry, experience].
Recommend 8 realistic AI-augmented roles - 2 deep-hands, 3 tool-powered
in my domain, 2 process/automation, 1 human-advantage. For each:
typical tasks, salary range, and 2 core AI skills needed.
```
- Keep the background line concrete - the more real facts, the better the options
- Ask follow-ups: 'which 2 are most realistic in a 90-day window?'
- Save the output - it becomes your shortlist to rank

## Step 3-5: Tier-1, Gaps, and the Plan

- **Tier-1 target:** choose ONE primary role (your North Star) plus one backup, and write it as: *I am transitioning from [now] to [target role] by building [2 AI skills] and [1 proof project]*
- **Gap analysis:** ask AI to list the 10 most common skills in target postings, mark which you have, and recommend the 2 cheapest gaps to close first
- **The 90-day plan:** days 1-30 learn and close gaps, days 31-60 build one proof project, days 61-90 apply with tailored materials and interview practice
### Why the one-liner matters
- It becomes your resume summary, your LinkedIn headline, and your interview story
- One clear sentence beats ten vague paragraphs
- Commit to it publicly once you are ready - accountability sharpens execution

## Key Takeaways

- Inventory 8 skills - hard and soft - before anything else
- Generate a shortlist spanning all four paths with AI assistance
- Pick ONE Tier-1 target plus a backup - your North Star
- Close the 2 cheapest gaps first, then repeat
- A 90-day plan structures learn, prove, and apply

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your 8 skills (4 hard, 4 soft) with one proof example each
2. Run the shortlist prompt and rank the 8 roles by feasibility and motivation
3. Write your Tier-1 one-liner: from [now] to [role] by building [2 skills] and [1 project]

**Example Quiz**

1. How many skills should you inventory first?
- A) 8, split hard and soft
- B) 3 strongest
- C) 20 total
- D) Only technical ones

**Answer:** A — List your 8 strongest split across hard and soft skills, honestly verified.

2. The shortlist prompt asks for how many recommended roles?
- A) 8, spanning all four paths
- B) 3
- C) 1 target only
- D) No limit

**Answer:** A — It generates 8: 2 deep-hands, 3 tool-powered, 2 process, 1 human-advantage.

3. What is a Tier-1 target?
- A) One primary 'North Star' role plus a backup
- B) The highest-salary role
- C) The hardest role to get
- D) Any role you apply to first

**Answer:** A — You choose ONE primary role to pursue plus one backup - focused direction.

4. Which gaps should you close first?
- A) The 2 cheapest gaps
- B) The hardest ones
- C) Every gap at once

**Answer:** A — Close the 2 cheapest gaps first for the fastest visible progress.

### Module 4 · Decoding Job Postings

Job postings are written to attract and filter. Read them like a recruiter would - as documents full of signals - and you will know exactly what your resume needs to say and which companies to skip. The skill of decoding a posting is a job-search skill in itself, and it takes maybe ten minutes to learn.

Every posting contains four categories of information: **must-have skills** (the non-negotiables), **nice-to-have skills** (differentiators), **salary signals**, and **red flags** (signs of disorganised hiring). Once you can extract all four, you apply with precision instead of hope.

## Learning Objectives

- Extract must-have and nice-to-have skills from any job posting
- Recognise salary and negotiation signals in a posting
- Spot red flags that signal disorganised hiring
- Turn posting analysis into resume keywords and application decisions

## The Must-Have / Nice-to-Have Split

- **Must-haves** are your resume keywords - but only use them if you genuinely have them
- **Nice-to-haves** are your differentiators - include them if you have them
- Salary bands, when present, give you negotiation anchors from Day 1
### Why the split matters
- A resume that mirrors the must-haves passes the ATS screen
- A candidate who can name the nice-to-haves they possess sounds like an insider
- The split also tells you whether to apply at all - if you miss most must-haves, this is not your role yet

## The Decoding Prompt

```text
Extract from this job description:
1. The 5 must-have skills
2. The 3 nice-to-have skills
3. The salary band, if present
4. What my resume should emphasise to match
5. Any contradictory or outdated requirements - flag them
```
- Paste the actual posting text - the more complete, the better the extraction
- Ask a follow-up: 'which 2 must-haves should my resume lead with?'
- Save the extraction for each target role - it becomes your tailoring brief

## Red Flags in Postings

- **Vague role descriptions** - the company may not know what they are hiring for
- **No salary range** when the market includes one - be cautious
- **'Fast-paced' with no specifics** - code for understaffed chaos
- **Requirements that mismatch the title** - a sign of an outdated or unedited process
### How to use the flags
- A flag is a caution, not an automatic reject - some great teams post badly
- When you interview, use the flag as a question: 'how does this team prioritise?'
- The flags protect you from joining a team that will make the job miserable

## Key Takeaways

- Must-haves are resume keywords; nice-to-haves are differentiators
- Salary bands are negotiation anchors - capture them early
- Red flags signal disorganised hiring - read them carefully
- A decoding prompt turns any posting into a tailoring brief
- Posting analysis makes your applications precise instead of hopeful

## Practice Challenge

1. Find two real job postings for your Tier-1 target role
2. Run the decoding prompt on both and list the must-have skills each
3. Compare the two lists - note the common must-haves and any red flags

**Example Quiz**

1. What should you paste into AI to analyse a job posting?
- A) The actual job description
- B) Your resume
- C) Your salary history
- D) A list of companies

**Answer:** A — Paste the job description so AI can extract must-haves, nice-to-haves, and salary band.

2. What does a red flag in a posting signal?
- A) Disorganised hiring
- B) A great company
- C) A higher salary
- D) A faster process

**Answer:** A — Vague descriptions, missing salary ranges, and contradictory requirements signal disorganised hiring.

3. What does the decoding prompt extract first?
- A) The 5 must-have skills
- B) Company culture
- C) Interview questions
- D) Your weaknesses

**Answer:** A — It lists the 5 must-have skills first, then nice-to-haves and the salary band.

4. When might a red flag NOT be a reason to skip a role?
- A) Good teams sometimes post badly - verify in the interview
- B) Never - always skip
- C) Only for startups
- D) When the salary is high

**Answer:** A — A flag is a caution, not an auto-reject - verify with real questions at interview time.

### Module 5 · Keeping Your Story Honest

Job search is a numbers game and a story game - this course gives you both. But honesty is the non-negotiable that runs underneath everything: your resume, your interviews, and your portfolio must all tell the same truthful story. Every shortcut that feels clever today becomes a credibility problem next week.

There are three rules worth internalising: do not claim AI expertise you do not have yet; keep every transferable-skills story demonstrable; and only apply where you genuinely qualify. None of these slow you down - they actually speed you up, because truthful candidates move through interviews faster and convert to offers more often.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why claiming skills you lack backfires quickly
- Build transferable-skills stories that are demonstrable, not vague
- Apply the 70% qualification threshold before applying
- Understand the mindset shift from 'starting from zero' to building on value

## Rule 1: No Fake AI Expertise

- You will be asked to demonstrate AI skills within weeks, in an interview or on the job
- Better to say 'learning in progress' honestly than to be caught exaggerating
- Honest learners earn respect; exaggerators damage their reputation in one conversation
### The safe phrasing
- 'Currently learning AI-assisted content workflows' - true and increasingly valued
- Pair every honest claim with a link or example you can show
- Build visible proof first - then the claim writes itself

## Rule 2: Demonstrable Stories

- 'I ran women's safety logistics; now I automate onboarding flows' - with proof from your proof projects
- Vague stories like 'I'm good with people' fail the first follow-up question
- A demonstrable story has a before, a change you made, and an outcome you can show
```text
The demonstrability test for any story:
1. Can you show it? (project, workflow, document)
2. Can you name the tool and the task it assists?
3. Can you state the outcome in a number?
4. If you cannot answer all three, make the story more concrete first
```
- Run this test before you put any claim on a resume

## Rule 3: The 70% Threshold

- Apply where you genuinely qualify - if you meet most must-haves, apply
- If you meet only 3 of 10 must-haves, skip it - it wastes their time and yours
- The 70% threshold keeps your focus on high-probability applications
### The mindset shift
- You are not starting from zero - your existing skills are valuable
- They just need the right AI tooling attached to them
- Career mapping gives you direction; the rest of the course gives you the proof

## Key Takeaways

- Never claim AI expertise you cannot demonstrate within weeks
- Transferable-skills stories must be demonstrable, not vague
- Apply only where you meet roughly 70% of must-haves
- Honest phrasing is increasingly valued, not penalised
- Your existing skills plus AI tooling is the story to tell

## Practice Challenge

1. Rewrite any weak claims on your current resume to be honest and demonstrable
2. Write one transfer story with the demonstrability test applied to it
3. Review your last 5 applications - did each meet the 70% threshold? Note any misses

**Example Quiz**

1. Why should you NOT claim AI expertise you do not have?
- A) You will be asked to demonstrate it within weeks
- B) AI tools check claims
- C) It is illegal
- D) Recruiters hate AI

**Answer:** A — You will be asked to show it in an interview or on the job - honesty beats being caught.

2. What makes a transferable-skills story 'demonstrable'?
- A) Proof you can show - project, workflow, measured outcome
- B) It sounds impressive
- C) It mentions many tools
- D) It is under 10 words

**Answer:** A — A demonstrable story has real proof - a project or workflow you can present.

3. When should you skip an application?

**Answer:** A — If you do not meet most must-haves, skip - chasing 1-in-10 fits wastes your best hours.

- B) Exaggerate to match the post

- D) Copy a generic phrase

**Answer:** A — Honest learning-in-progress phrasing is increasingly valued by recruiters.

### Module 6 · Your First 90-Day Career Plan

Direction without a timeline is a wish. The 90-day plan turns your career mapping into a schedule you can follow day by day, with a visible deliverable every stage. It is deliberately simple: learn for 30 days, prove for 30 days, apply for 30 days - and it fits around a full-time job, because it asks for 45 focused minutes a day, not heroic weekends.

The plan works because it is built on the compound effect - small consistent actions that become a portfolio, a pipeline, and finally interviews. The commitment contract below is not a metaphor: print it, pin it, and tick off your daily 45 minutes.

## Learning Objectives

- Structure a 90-day plan into three 30-day blocks
- Define what happens in the learn, prove, and apply phases
- Use a commitment contract to stay consistent
- Explain why consistency beats intensity in a job search

## Days 1-30: Learn and Close Gaps

- Focus on your top 2-3 A-priority skills - one track per skill
- Finish one skill completely before starting the next - no parallel dabbling
- Log progress daily in two lines max: learned X, practised Y, stuck on Z
### The discipline detail
- Use free official resources first - the tools own documentation
- Reuse the same gap list you created in Mission 1
- Every Friday, produce something visible - a config, a sample, a mini-project

## Days 31-60: Build Your Proof Project

- Take one skill you learned and build something real with it
- Options: a small automation, a content piece, a data project, a workflow improvement
- This becomes the centrepiece of your portfolio and your interview story
```text
The proof project spec:
1. Something you can show in under 60 seconds
2. Built with YOUR real data or scenario if possible
3. Documented: what it does, which tool, what you learned
4. Hosted or shareable - a link beats a screenshot
5. Honest AI-use line included - 'built with AI assistance'
```
- A small finished project beats a grand abandoned one

## Days 61-90: Apply and the Commitment Contract

- Apply to 4-5 roles per week with tailored materials - resume, cover letter, small tweaks per posting
- Your resume is rebuilt (Mission 2), interviews sharp (Mission 4), and proof project ready (Mission 5)
- **The commitment contract:** 45 minutes per day for 90 days; week 1-4 foundations, week 5-8 project, week 9-12 apply and interview; weekly proof every Friday
### Why the plan will work
- Consistency beats intensity - a daily 45 minutes outlasts a weekly 3-hour panic
- The contract makes progress visible and failure small
- Pin it where you will see it - visible goals get met

## Key Takeaways

- 90 days in three blocks: learn, prove, apply
- Days 1-30 close your A-priority gaps
- Days 31-60 deliver one proof project - your portfolio centrepiece
- Days 61-90 run 4-5 tailored applications per week
- Consistency beats intensity - pin your commitment contract
