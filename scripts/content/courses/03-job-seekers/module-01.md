# Mission 1: Career Mapping: AI-Augmented Roles That Fit You

## Learning Objectives

- See the career landscape as "role + AI" instead of "AI replaces roles"
- Identify AI-augmented roles that match your skills and experience
- Map your transferable skills into a target role shortlist
- Assess the AI skills required for your top roles
- Build a 90-day career-adaptation plan

---
### Module 1 · The 'Role + AI' Mindset
Headlines scream "AI will take jobs." The reality is more useful than the fear: **AI replaces tasks, not whole jobs — so jobs become human skill + AI tooling.** Almost every career — from HR to banking to marketing to operations — is becoming augmented.

The question shifts from *"Will AI replace me?"* to *"Which version of my job uses AI most?"* The people who answer that win the transition.

AI doesn't eliminate entire professions — it transforms how tasks are done within them. A marketing manager still manages marketing, but now uses AI for data analysis, content drafts, and campaign optimisation. The human skill stays; the tools get sharper.

**Try it now:** think of your current role or last job. List three tasks you do daily — which ones could AI assist with?

### Module 2 · Four Kinds of AI-Augmented Career Paths
AI-augmented careers fall into four categories. Knowing where you fit (or want to fit) gives you a target:

1. **Deep-Hands Roles** — you build AI systems (data engineering, ML engineering, prompt/agent engineering). These require technical depth and reward it.

2. **Tool-Powered Domain Roles** — you use AI daily inside your current field (marketing, legal, finance, HR, ops). This is the largest pool of new jobs. Your domain expertise becomes more valuable when combined with AI fluency.

3. **Process & Workflow Roles** — you automate work for teams (automation specialists, AI operations). You don't build the AI; you make the team's workflows faster and smarter.

4. **Human-Advantage Roles** — trust, judgement, and relationships, with AI underneath (management, sales, coaching, care). Human judgement becomes MORE valuable, not less, when AI handles the routine work.

**Where are you today?** Your current experience is transferable: logistics → logistics-automation specialist. Draft writing → AI-content lead. Your past work is a runway, not a cage.

### Module 3 · Mapping Your Skills to a Target Role
Five steps to find your AI-augmented target role:

**Step 1: Inventory your skills.** List 8 strongest skills — split into **hard** (tools, processes, languages, technical skills) and **soft** (communication, problem solving, reliability, stakeholder handling). Be honest; recruiters will check.

**Step 2: Generate a role shortlist with AI.** Paste this prompt:
> "Act as a career strategist. My background: [skills, industry, experience]. Recommend 8 realistic AI-augmented roles — 2 deep-hands, 3 tool-powered in my domain, 2 process/automation, 1 human-advantage. For each: typical tasks, salary range, and 2 core AI skills needed."

Rank those 8: *feasible with ≤90 days effort* + *motivating*.

**Step 3: Pick a Tier-1 target.** Choose one primary role (your "North Star") and one backup. Write it as:
> *"I'm transitioning from [now] → [target role] by building [2 AI skills] and [1 proof project]."*

**Step 4: Gap analysis.** For your Tier-1:
> "List the 10 most common skills in [target role] job postings. Mark which I have and which are gaps, and recommend the 2 cheapest gaps to close first."

**Step 5: The 90-day plan.** Days 1–30 learn and close gaps → Days 31–60 build 1 proof project → Days 61–90 apply with tailored materials and interview practice.

### Module 4 · Decoding Job Postings
Read real job posts, not guesses. Learning to decode postings is a job-search skill in itself. Recruiters write signals in postings — you need to read them.

Paste a target role's posting into AI:
> "Extract from this job description: the 5 must-have skills, the 3 nice-to-have skills, the salary band if present, and what my resume should emphasise to match. Then list any requirements the post contradicts — unrealistic or outdated ones."

**What the posting actually tells you:**

- The must-haves are your resume keywords — use them if they match your real skills.
- Nice-to-haves are your differentiators — include them if you have them.
- Salary bands (when present) give you negotiation anchors.
- Contradictions or outdated requirements signal a company that hasn't updated its process — good to know.

**Red flags in postings:** vague role description, no salary range, "fast-paced" without specifics, or requirements that don't match the job title. These signal disorganised hiring.

**Try it now:** find two real job postings for your Tier-1 target role and extract their must-have skills into a list. Compare them.

**Answer:** B — Paste the actual job description so AI can extract must-haves, nice-to-haves, and salary band.

### Module 5 · Keeping Your Story Honest
Job search is a numbers game and a story game. This plan gives you both — but honesty is non-negotiable.

**Three rules to follow:**

- **Don't claim AI expertise you don't have yet.** You'll be asked to show it within weeks. Better to be honest about learning-in-progress than caught exaggerating.

- **Your transferable-skills story must be demonstrable.** "I ran women's safety logistics; now automate onboarding flows" — with proof in Missions 4–5. Vague stories don't survive interview questions.

- **Apply where you genuinely qualify.** Applying to roles you don't meet wastes the recruiter's time and yours. The 70% threshold: if you meet most must-haves, apply. If you meet 3 out of 10, skip it.

**The mindset shift:** You're not starting from zero. Your existing skills are valuable — they just need the right AI tooling attached. The career mapping in this mission gives you the direction; the rest of the course gives you the proof.

**Example Quiz**

1. Why should you NOT claim AI expertise you don't have?
- A) AI will check
- B) You'll be asked to show it within weeks
- C) It is illegal
- D) Recruiters hate AI

**Answer:** B — You'll be asked to demonstrate AI skills within weeks — honesty beats being caught exaggerating.

2. What makes a transferable-skills story 'demonstrable'?
- A) It sounds impressive
- B) It includes proof from Missions 4-5
- C) It mentions many tools
- D) It is under 10 words

**Answer:** B — A demonstrable story has real proof behind it — like a project or workflow you can show.

3. When should you skip an application?
- A) When you meet under 70% of must-haves
- B) Always apply to everything
- C) When the salary is low
- D) Never skip

**Answer:** A — If you don't meet most must-haves, skip — chasing 1% fits wastes your best hours.

### Module 6 · Your First 90-Day Career Plan
Your 90-day plan turns direction into action. Structure it in three blocks:

**Days 1–30: Learn and Close Gaps.** Focus on your top 2–3 A-priority skills. One track per skill. Finish one skill completely before starting the next. Log progress daily — two lines max in a tracker.

**Days 31–60: Build Your Proof Project.** Take one skill you've learned and build something real with it. This could be a small automation, a content piece, a data project, or a workflow improvement. This becomes the centrepiece of your portfolio in Mission 5.

**Days 61–90: Apply with Tailored Materials.** Your resume is rebuilt (Mission 2), your interview skills are sharp (Mission 4), and your proof project is ready. Apply to 4–5 roles per week with tailored applications.

**The commitment contract:**

> I will spend 45 minutes per day for 90 days on this career transition.
> Week 1–4: foundations. Week 5–8: project. Week 9–12: apply and interview.
> Weekly proof: something visible every Friday.

Pin this where you'll see it daily. Consistency beats intensity.

**Example Quiz**

1. What happens in days 31-60 of the 90-day plan?
- A) Learn foundations
- B) Build your proof project
- C) Apply with tailored materials
- D) Take interviews

**Answer:** B — Days 31-60 are for building one proof project that becomes the centrepiece of your portfolio.

2. How many applications per week should you target in days 61-90?
- A) 1
- B) 2
- C) 4-5 tailored applications
- D) As many as possible

**Answer:** C — Days 61-90 use a sustainable 4-5 tailored applications per week cadence.

3. What is the commitment contract for?
- A) Employer agreement
- B) Personal accountability for daily practice
- C) Company policy
- D) Salary guarantee

**Answer:** B — The commitment contract (45 minutes daily, weekly proof) keeps you consistent and accountable.

## Key Takeaways

- AI replaces tasks, not jobs — every role becomes human skill + AI tooling.
- Four career paths: deep-hands, tool-powered domain, process, human-advantage.
- Map skills → shortlist 8 roles → rank → one Tier-1 target → gap analysis.
- Use a 90-day plan: learn (days 1–30), prove (31–60), apply (61–90).
- Decode real job postings; keep your story honest and demonstrable.
