### Module 1 · Prioritising Your Gaps

You know your gaps from Mission 1 - but boxes in your head do not get hired. A prioritised plan does. The single highest-leverage move in upskilling is sorting your gaps into three buckets that tell you exactly what to do first, what to do later, and what never to chase at all.

The three buckets are **Must-close (A)**, **Nice-to-have (B)**, and **Reality-checks (C)**. Sorting takes ten minutes; the clarity it creates saves you months of learning the wrong things.

## Learning Objectives

- Sort gaps into Must-close (A), Nice-to-have (B), and Reality-check (C)
- Apply the reframing rule for gaps that take more than a year
- Prioritise A-gaps that appear in 3+ target postings
- Explain why chasing C-gaps derails a reasonable career path

## The Three Buckets

- **Must-close (A):** appear in most target postings and are cheap to learn - do these first; they are the price of entry
- **Nice-to-have (B):** appear sometimes and help differentiation - do them optionally in weeks 3-4
- **Reality-check (C):** nice words that need years (like 'deep ML research') - reframe or drop them
### Why the split works
- A-gaps unlock applications; B-gaps improve offers; C-gaps mostly consume time
- Time spent on C-gaps is time stolen from A-gaps that would actually get you hired
- The buckets make your study list automatically - learn in exactly this order

## The Reframing Rule

- If a gap takes more than one year to close, pivot the target role slightly instead
- A tool-powered domain role may suit you far better than a deep-hands technical role
- Reframing is not giving up - it is choosing the shortest path to hired
```text
Reframing loop for any C-gap:
1. Name the gap ('deep ML research')
2. Ask: could I learn enough in 90 days to use it?
3. If no - ask: what role would NOT need this gap?
4. If yes - move it to bucket A and make it concrete
5. Pivot the target role toward the realistic version
```
- Reason through reframes with AI: 'what wins with my profile in 90 days?'

## Prioritising Your A-List

- Write your A-priority gaps first - the ones that appear in 3+ postings for your target role
- Within the A-list, order by cost: cheapest and highest-frequency gaps first
- The 'appears in 3+ postings' rule is your filter against learning what is not asked
### The A-list test
- Paste 3 target postings and ask AI which skills appear across all three
- Those are your true A-gaps - not the ones in your head
- Re-run the test monthly as postings evolve

## Key Takeaways

- Sort gaps into A (must-close), B (nice-to-have), and C (reality-checks)
- A-gaps that appear in 3+ postings come first
- Reframe year-long gaps by pivoting the role, not suffering the gap
- C-gaps mostly consume time - drop or reframe them
- The buckets turn your study list into a fixed order

### Module 2 · Building Your 30-Day Learning Plan

A 30-day plan is the smallest container that shows real progress: four weeks, four deliverables, one integrated portfolio piece. It converts your A-gap list into a schedule with a visible outcome every week, so you are never guessing whether the learning is working.

The plan has a clear rhythm - Week 1 foundations, Week 2 mini-project, Week 3 a second skill, Week 4 integration - and it runs on 45 deliberate minutes a day. Generate a draft with AI, then edit it until every week has one visible deliverable.

## Learning Objectives

- Generate a focused 30-day learning plan with AI
- Structure four weeks: foundations, project, skill 2, integration
- Make each week produce one visible deliverable
- Write a 30-day commitment contract for daily practice

## The Four-Week Rhythm

- **Week 1 - Foundations:** core concepts, tool setup, introductory tutorials - hands-on from day one, no passive video marathons
- **Week 2 - Mini-project:** one hands-on project that becomes a portfolio item - build something real with week 1's skill
- **Week 3 - Skill 2:** apply the same pattern to a second skill - and produce a second mini-proof
- **Week 4 - Integration:** combine all skills into one polished showcase project that feeds Mission 5
### The weekly proof rule
- Every Friday, something visible must exist
- A visible item is a config, a working sample, a small project, a notebook
- Passive consumption never counts - shipped work does

## The Plan-Generation Prompt

```text
Act as a learning designer. Given my A-gaps [list] and my
target role [role], produce a 30-day plan: each week one
focus area, 3 concrete learning tasks per day, 1 practical
mini-project per week, and free resources (official docs,
courses, communities). Prioritise hands-on practice over
passive videos.
```
- Give it your exact A-list - the quality of the plan tracks the quality of the input
- Ask follow-ups: 'which task each day is the highest ROI?'
- Edit the draft so every week ends with a named deliverable

## The 30-Day Commitment Contract

- **The contract:** I will spend 45 minutes per day for 30 days; weekly proof every Friday; ask for help before burning 2 hours stuck
- Pin it where you will see it daily - visible goals get met
- One rest day per week is part of the plan, not a violation
### Why the contract works
- It makes progress visible and failure small
- Falling behind is handled by resuming, never by cramming
- A 45-minute daily habit is sustainable next to a full-time job

## Key Takeaways

- Four weeks: foundations, mini-project, skill 2, integration
- Three concrete tasks per day, hands-on from day one
- Every week ends with something visible - proof weekly
- A commitment contract makes progress visible and failure small
- Edit your AI plan so every week has a named deliverable

### Module 3 · Finding Quality Free Resources

AI lists 'great courses' cheerfully - and most lists are mediocre or outdated. The difference between learning quickly and wasting weeks is resource selection. Filter everything through the same test: official first, community-proven, hands-on, and free or cheap for the first week.

The trap is not too few resources; it is too many. Picking one track per skill and finishing it beats collecting twenty half-watched courses - a finished track with a project is proof, while a certificate pile is not.

## Learning Objectives

- Prioritise official documentation over third-party lists
- Verify resources before committing to them
- Prefer hands-on resources over passive video
- Avoid the 'collecting 20 courses' trap

## The Filter: Official, Proven, Hands-On

- **Official sources first:** a tool's own docs and getting-started guides beat third-party listicles
- **Community proof:** a subreddit, Discord, or forum where practitioners answer questions - check that people post solutions, not just questions
- **Hands-on over passive:** anything with projects and labs beats video marathons - build from minute one
### The free-or-cheap rule
- Use official free tiers, platform free courses, and library access
- Do not pay for anything in the first week - your A-gap may shift
- Free does not mean lower quality; official docs are often the best teaching

## Verifying Before You Commit

- Ask AI: 'What does [course] actually teach? Hours? Prerequisites? Labs? Last updated?'
- Cross-check the claims - AI repeats marketing language uncritically
- Read a course's syllabus page and skim real learner reviews, not hosted testimonials
```text
The resource verification prompt:
For [course/resource], tell me:
1. Topics actually covered (from the syllabus)
2. Total hours and practical labs vs pure video
3. Prerequisites - do I meet them?
4. Last updated date - is it current for this tool?
5. Would this one course cover my A-gap or only part of it?
```
- Run this before any paid or time-heavy commitment

## The One-Track Rule

- Collecting 20 courses and finishing zero is the classic trap
- Pick **one track per skill**, finish it, ship the proof
- A single completed course with a small project beats ten certificates of half-watched videos
### Start today's action
- Choose your Week 1 skill, find one official tutorial, and complete the first hands-on module before this mission ends
- Momentum is built by finishing, not by bookmarked tabs
- A finished module is also your first Friday-proof item

## Key Takeaways

- Official documentation beats third-party lists every time
- Verify with the five-question resource prompt before committing
- Hands-on projects beat passive video marathons
- One finished track beats twenty started courses
- Use free or cheap sources for the first week

### Module 4 · Tracking Progress Honestly

Learning without tracking is hoping. A simple tracker keeps you accountable, makes progress visible, and turns two-month slumps into weekly wake-ups. The good news is that the tracker is tiny - two lines a day - because its job is momentum, not documentation.

The system is three pieces: a daily two-line log, a weekly visible-proof checkpoint, and a weekly AI recap that compresses your week into a summary and the next three actions. Together they make learning feel faster because they make progress visible.

## Learning Objectives

- Keep a daily two-line learning log
- Produce a visible proof item every week
- Run a weekly AI recap for summary and next actions
- Explain why a good tracker is tiny, not exhaustive

## The Two-Line Daily Log

- Log two lines max: 'Learned X, practised Y, stuck on Z' - takes 30 seconds
- Stuck-note is the most valuable line: it turns blockers into help requests
- No essays - the tracker is for you, not for a manager
### Why tiny wins
- A one-line habit survives; a journal system collapses by day four
- The daily log is the raw material for the weekly recap
- Two lines also make it easy to see progress compounding

## The Weekly Proof Checkpoint

- Every week, something visible must exist: a config file, a working sample, a small project, a notebook with examples
- Passive consumption is not proof - shipped work is
- The proof item becomes the raw material for your portfolio later
```text
Simplest working tracker - copy this:
Date   | Learned      | Practised     | Blockers
------ | ------------ | ------------- | --------
Day 1  | prompt tricks | 3 exercises   | none
Day 2  | tool API      | connected data| auth issue
Week 1 proof: config file working end to end
```
- Markdown tables are fine here - this is your private tracker, not a slide

## The Weekly AI Recap

- Paste your weekly log to AI - ask for a progress summary and the next 3 actions
- Reading your own compounded progress is powerful motivation
- The recap also catches drift: are you still heading toward your A-gaps?
### The compounding effect
- Week 1 the recap is short; week 4 it covers a mini-project - visible growth
- Use the recap's next-3-actions to open Monday's session
- Store recaps in the same file - a year of progress in one place

## Key Takeaways

- Track daily with two lines: learned, practised, stuck
- Produce one visible proof item every Friday
- Weekly AI recap compresses progress and names next actions
- A tiny tracker survives; an exhaustive one collapses
- Visible progress is the fuel that keeps learning going

### Module 5 · Turning Learning Into Evidence Fast

The moment you learn something, weaponise it - turn it into evidence you can show. Learning that stays in your head evaporates; learning that becomes a visible item compounds. The fastest way to build a portfolio is not to 'do a portfolio project' but to convert every skill you learn into a small piece of proof the same week you learn it.

The habit has four moves: build a use-case when you finish a tool module, save a prompt template when you learn a pattern, publish a five-line post when you solve a problem, and guarantee that every week ends with something visible.

## Learning Objectives

- Turn every finished module into a real-use-case proof
- Save reusable prompts and templates as portfolio items
- Publish short 'how I did it' posts for professional presence
- Guarantee every week ends with something visible

## The Four Moves

- **Finished a tool module?** Build one 20-minute use-case with YOUR real data or scenario - a quick automation, a data analysis, a content piece
- **Learned a prompt pattern?** Save the template you actually use - a reusable prompt is a portfolio item
- **Solved a problem?** Write a 5-line 'how I did it' post - it validates learning and builds presence
- **Every week:** guarantee one visible item - the weekly proof checkpoint
### Why 20 minutes is the sweet spot
- Long enough to be real, short enough to repeat weekly
- A use-case with YOUR data is far more convincing than a tutorial clone
- Repeat four times and you have a portfolio - not four unfinished grand plans

## The 5-Line Post Pattern

- Structure: what I made, which tool, what I learned, mini-repo or link
- Five lines is the anti-perfection guardrail - it keeps publishing weekly
- Short posts compound into a visible public record of growth
```text
The 5-line build-in-public template:
1. Made X with [tool]
2. Why: [one line - the real problem you had]
3. What I learned: [the one insight]
4. Link: [repo / demo] - honest AI-use line included
5. Next: [the next small thing you will build]
```
- One post a week is plenty - consistency beats one perfect essay

## The Compound Effect

- By week 4 you have 4 visible items - these become your portfolio in Mission 5
- They do not need to be impressive - they need to be real, documented, and yours
- The habit of sharing proof starts today, not next month
### The counter-example to avoid
- The learner who watches modules and takes certificates but ships nothing
- That learner has nothing to show an interviewer - proof ships, not watching
- Your first 'how I did it' post is the antidote - write it today

## Key Takeaways

- Turn every module into a 20-minute real-use-case proof
- Save reusable prompt templates - they are portfolio items
- Publish a 5-line 'how I did it' post weekly
- Four weekly proofs by week 4 = a real portfolio
- Proof ships; watching and collecting does not

### Module 6 · The 30-Day Commitment Contract

A plan without a contract is a wish. The 30-day commitment contract is the accountability device that makes the plan real - a single pinned statement of the daily time, the weekly proof, and the honesty rules. It converts intention into a schedule you can check against.

The contract also solves the two most common failures: falling behind (handled by resuming, not cramming) and losing the thread (handled by three weekly review questions). Both are designed to make failure small and recovery automatic.

## Learning Objectives

- Write a 30-day commitment contract you can pin daily
- Handle falling behind with the resume-don't-cram rule
- Run the three weekly review questions
- Explain why visible progress and small failures sustain motivation

## The Contract

- **The pledge:** I will spend 45 minutes per day for 30 days on [specific skill]
- **The weeks:** Week 1 foundations, Week 2 mini-project #1, Week 3 skill #2, Week 4 integration showcase
- **Weekly proof:** something visible every Friday
- **Non-negotiables:** one rest day per week; ask for help before burning 2 hours stuck
### Why specific beats general
- 'Learn AI' is not enforceable; 'complete the API module' is
- Name the specific skill in the contract - it focuses every 45 minutes
- A contract you wrote is one you can keep; a generic one fails on day 2

## Handling the Fall

- Do not try to catch up by cramming - just resume the next day
- Consistency over intensity: 45 minutes daily beats a 3-hour panic session
- The contract expects one rest day - rest is planned, not failure
```text
The fall-recovery routine:
1. Missed a day? Note it in the tracker, do not punish yourself
2. Next day: start with the smallest 10-minute task to restart
3. Ask for help if you are stuck past the 2-hour rule
4. Review the week honestly at the checkpoint - then move on
```
- The system forgives single days - week streaks are what build skill

## The Weekly Review Questions

- Ask every Friday: **what did I build or demonstrate? what is still unclear? what are my 3 priorities for next week?**
- These three questions are the difference between learning and collecting certificates
- The contract works because it makes progress visible and failure small
- The same three questions map directly onto your portfolio and interview story
### What the rhythm produces
- Each Friday you answer with a proof item and a priority list
- After 4 weeks you have 4 proofs, 12 priorities, and a real plan
- Use the same questions in your final review before applying

## Key Takeaways

- Pin a specific 45-minutes-a-day contract for 30 days
- Weekly proof every Friday is baked into the contract
- Falling behind? Resume the next day - never cram
- Three Friday questions keep learning honest
- Pen the contract with a named skill, not a vague goal
