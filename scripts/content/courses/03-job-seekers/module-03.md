# Mission 3: Skills Gap Analysis & Your 30-Day Learning Plan

## Learning Objectives

- Turn gaps into a prioritised, learnable skills map
- Build a focused 30-day upskilling plan with AI
- Find quality free learning resources (not just lists)
- Track progress and build quick proof as you learn
- Turn learning into demonstrable portfolio items fast

---
### Module 1 · Prioritising Your Gaps
You know your gaps from Mission 1. But boxes in your head don't get hired — a prioritised plan does.

Sort your gaps into three buckets:

- **Must-close (A):** Appear in most target postings; cheap to learn. Do these first. They're the price of entry.
- **Nice-to-have (B):** Appear sometimes; good for differentiation. Optional in weeks 3–4.
- **Reality-checks (C):** Nice words that need years (e.g., "deep ML research"). Reframe these — don't chase them. You don't need to become an ML researcher to get hired in AI-augmented roles.

**The reframing rule:** If a gap would take more than one year to close, pivot the target role slightly instead. A tool-powered domain role may suit you better than a deep-hands technical role.

**Try it now:** Write your A-priority gaps first — the ones that appear in 3+ job postings for your target role. These are your immediate focus.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does 'Must-close (A)' mean?
- A) A skill you can skip
- B) A gap that appears in most target postings and is cheap to learn
- C) A luxury skill
- D) A decade-long skill

**Answer:** B — A-gaps appear in most postings and are cheap to close - do them first.

2. What should you do with a 'Reality-check (C)' gap?
- A) Chase it for years
- B) Reframe it - pivot the target role slightly
- C) Ignore all learning
- D) Pay for courses

**Answer:** B — If a gap takes more than a year to close, pivot the target role instead of chasing it.

3. Why not become an ML researcher to get an AI-augmented role?
- A) It's too easy
- B) It's years of depth most roles don't need
- C) They pay less
- D) Recruiters avoid them

**Answer:** B — Most AI-augmented roles don't need deep ML research - tool-powered fluency is enough.

### Module 2 · Building Your 30-Day Learning Plan
Generate a focused 30-day plan with AI using this prompt:

> "Act as a learning designer. Given my gaps [list A] and target role [role], produce a 30-day plan: each week 1 focus area, 3 concrete learning tasks per day, 1 practical mini-project per week, and free resources (official docs, courses, communities). Prioritise hands-on practice over passive videos."

**Structure your plan:**

- **Week 1:** Foundations of the main skill. Understand core concepts, set up your tools, complete introductory tutorials. No passive video marathons — hands-on from day one.
- **Week 2:** One hands-on mini-project that becomes a portfolio item. Build something real with what you learned.
- **Week 3:** Second skill + second mini-proof. Apply the pattern again with a different skill.
- **Week 4:** Integration — combine all skills into one polished showcase project. This feeds Mission 5.

**The commitment contract:**

> I will spend 45 minutes per day for 30 days.
> Weekly proof: something visible every Friday.
> Ask for help before burning 2 hours stuck.

Pin this where you'll see it daily.

- A) Foundations, mini-project, skill #2, integration
- B) Videos, tests, certificate, job
- C) One skill all month
- D) Random topics daily

**Answer:** A — Week 1 foundations, Week 2 mini-project, Week 3 second skill, Week 4 integration showcase.

2. How many concrete learning tasks should each day have?
- A) 1
- B) 3
- C) 10
- D) No tasks - just videos

**Answer:** B — The plan prompt asks for 3 concrete learning tasks per day, hands-on first.

3. What should Week 4's integration produce?
- A) A certificate
- B) One polished showcase project combining all skills
- C) A resume
- D) A job application

**Answer:** B — Week 4 combines all skills into one polished showcase project that feeds Mission 5.

### Module 3 · Finding Quality Free Resources
AI blithely lists "great courses." Most are mediocre or outdated. Filter them:

- **Official sources first.** A tool's own docs and tutorials beat third-party listicles. If you're learning a specific tool, start with its official getting-started guide.
- **Community proof.** A subreddit, Discord, or forum where practitioners actually answer questions. Check that people post solutions, not just questions.
- **Hands-on over passive.** Anything with projects and labs beats video marathons. Build from minute one.
- **Free or cheap.** Official free tiers, platform free courses, library access. Don't pay for anything in the first week.
- **Verify before committing:** ask AI "What does [course] actually teach? Hours? Prerequisites? Labs? Last updated?" Cross-check the claims.

**The trap:** Collecting 20 courses and finishing zero. Pick **one track per skill**, finish it, ship the proof. A single completed course with a small project beats 10 certificates of half-watched videos.

**Start today:** Choose your Week 1 skill, find one official tutorial, and complete the first hands-on module before the end of this mission.

**Answer:** B — Official sources first - the tool's own docs beat third-party listicles.

2. Why prefer hands-on resources?

### Module 4 · Tracking Progress Honestly
Learning without tracking is hoping. A simple tracker keeps you accountable:

- **Log daily:** Two lines max in a tracker — "Learned X, practised Y, stuck on Z." This takes 30 seconds and keeps your momentum visible.
- **Weekly proof checkpoint:** Every week, something visible must exist: a config file, a working sample, a small project, a notebook with examples.
- **Recap with AI weekly:** Paste your weekly log to AI; it drafts a progress summary and suggests next 3 actions. Reading your own compounded progress is powerful motivation.

**What a good tracker looks like:**

| Day | What I Learned | What I Practised | Blockers |
|-----|---------------|-----------------|----------|
| 1   | Basic prompt patterns | 3 prompt exercises | None |
| 2   | Tool-specific API basics | Connected to test data | Confused on auth |

Two lines per day. No essays. The tracker is for you, not for a manager.

**Example Quiz**

- A) A full page

- C) One paragraph
- D) No log needed

**Answer:** B — Two lines per day: 'Learned X, practised Y, stuck on Z' - takes 30 seconds.

2. What must exist every Friday?
- A) A certificate
- B) Something visible - config, project, sample
- C) A study group
- D) A new course

**Answer:** B — Every week ends with a visible proof item - passive consumption isn't proof.

3. How can AI recap help your tracking?

### Module 5 · Turning Learning Into Evidence Fast
The moment you learn something, weaponise it:

- **Finished a tool module?** Build one 20-minute use-case with YOUR real data or scenario. A quick automation, a data analysis, a content piece.
- **Learned a prompt pattern?** Save the template you actually use. A reusable prompt is a portfolio item.
- **Solved a problem?** Write a 5-line "how I did it" post for your LinkedIn. It validates your learning and builds your professional presence.

**Every week should end with something visible.** Passive consumption isn't proof; shipped work is.

**The compounding effect:** By the end of 30 days, you'll have 4 visible items (one per week). These become your portfolio in Mission 5. They don't need to be impressive — they need to be real, documented, and yours.

**Start now:** Write one line about what you learned in this mission, and share it somewhere — a note, a message, a LinkedIn post. The habit of sharing proof starts today.

### Module 6 · The 30-Day Commitment Contract
Pin this commitment where you'll see it daily:

> **I will spend 45 minutes per day for 30 days on [specific skill].**
> **Week 1:** Foundations. **Week 2:** Mini-project #1. **Week 3:** Skill #2. **Week 4:** Integration showcase.
> **Weekly proof:** Something visible every Friday.
> **Non-negotiable:** 1 rest day per week; ask for help before burning 2 hours stuck.

**What to do when you fall behind:** Don't try to catch up by cramming. Just resume the next day. Consistency over intensity — 45 minutes daily beats a 3-hour panic session.

**Weekly review questions (every Friday):**
1. What did I build or demonstrate this week?
2. What's still unclear?
3. What are my 3 priorities for next week?

These three questions, asked weekly, are the difference between learning and collecting certificates. The commitment contract works because it makes progress visible and failure small.

**Example Quiz**

1. How much time is the 30-day daily commitment?
- A) 2 hours
- B) 45 minutes
- C) All evening
- D) Only weekends

**Answer:** B — 45 minutes per day for 30 days - consistency beats intensity.

2. What should you do after falling behind?
- A) Cram for 3 hours
- B) Resume the next day - consistency over intensity
- C) Quit the plan
- D) Double your rest day

**Answer:** B — Don't catch up by cramming - just resume the next day.

3. What are the weekly review questions?
- A) Three about salary
- B) What did I build, what's unclear, what are 3 next priorities
- C) Where are the courses
- D) Who liked my posts

**Answer:** B — Ask every Friday: what did I build, what's unclear, what are my 3 priorities next week.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 — The A/B/C split.** Kavita listed 12 gaps, then sorted them. Only 4 were 'must-close' (A). The other 8 were reframed or deferred. She closed her A gaps in 3 weeks, not 12 — because she focused.

**Example 2 — The proof habit.** Every Friday, Amit built one small thing — a prompt template, a mini-automation, a 5-line write-up. By week 4, he had four portfolio items and a LinkedIn post with genuine engagement. The compound effect was visible.

**Example 3 — The resource trap avoided.** Ravi almost started five courses. He picked one official tutorial, finished it, built a project, and moved on. His friend started five and finished zero — Ravi's project won the interview.

## Key Takeaways

- Prioritise gaps: A (must-close), B (nice-to-have), C (reframe — don't chase years).
- One 30-day plan: foundations → mini-project → skill #2 → integrate.
- Verify resources before committing; one finished track beats 20 started.
- Track daily, ship weekly proof, recap with AI.
- Every week produces something visible for your portfolio.
