### Module 1 · Building Your Job Search Command Center

Scattered searching burns you out because every day is a fresh negotiation with your own attention. The command center replaces that with a **daily system**: sourced, tracked, tailored, followed-up, reviewed. Once the system is running, the search becomes a habit instead of a panic - and habits compound.

The command center is three things: a tracker with the right columns, alerts that deliver 3-5 fresh roles a day instead of 50 random ones, and a fixed 45-minute routine with a job for each block of minutes.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a job-search tracker with the right columns
- Set up daily alerts that deliver quality, not volume
- Run a fixed 45-minute daily routine
- Explain why a daily system beats occasional panic

## The Tracker

- Use Sheet, Notion, or Airtable with columns: Company | Role | Link | Source | Status | Applied date | Follow-up date | Notes
- **Status** values: sourced / applied / response / interview / offer / rejected
- The tracker is the single source of truth - if it is not logged, it did not happen
### What the columns do
- Link and Source make the row re-openable without re-searching
- Follow-up date auto-reminds you at day 7-10 for the follow-up move
- Notes hold the tailored key points - resume version, contact, response

## Alerts That Filter, Not Flood

- Set up daily digests - you want 3-5 fresh relevant roles per day, not 50 random ones
- Save keyword searches that match your target role title and skills
- Review alerts once a day, at the same time, as part of the routine
```text
Good alert setup (10 minutes of work):
1. Pick 2 boards and apply filters for title + skills + location
2. Set daily digest email, not instant notifications
3. Create a saved search per target role type
4. Time-limit review to your 15-minute source block
5. Anything outside the filters does not exist to you
```
- Your attention is the asset - Alerts protect it

## The 45-Minute Routine

- **Minutes 1-15 - Source:** review alerts, find 3-5 quality matches
- **Minutes 16-30 - Tailor:** run the apply-matching pipeline for 2 of those
- **Minutes 31-45 - Apply and log:** submit applications, update tracker
- Tomorrow's structured hour beats today's panicked marathon - the routine is the system, and the system is the result
### The habit anchor
- Same time, same place, every day - anchor it to an existing habit (after coffee, before lunch)
- Miss one day? Resume tomorrow - the routine forgives single misses
- Track streak so the system is visible - visible systems keep running

## Key Takeaways

- One tracker is the single source of truth
- Alerts should deliver 3-5 quality roles a day - never a flood
- 45 minutes: 15 source, 15 tailor, 15 apply and log
- The system beats panic because it compounds
- A daily routine is a habit - anchor it and forgive single misses

### Module 2 · Automating Sourcing With AI

AI is excellent at finding and filtering roles fast - and dangerously confident about what it finds. The correct workflow is: let AI do the broad sourcing and ranking, then verify every single result yourself by clicking through to the real posting. AI files the shortcut; you own the ground truth.

The three tools in this module are search prompts that find roles, a quality filter that ranks matches, and a company-signals check that tells you if a place is worth your application at all. Run them, verify them, and apply to only the survivors.

## Learning Objectives

- Use search prompts to find roles rapidly
- Run a quality filter that ranks matches and flags red flags
- Check company signals before applying
- Always verify every AI-sourced posting manually

## Search and Quality Filter

- **Search prompt:** 'Find 5 [role] openings for a [profile] in [city/remote] on [board], each with: link, team, must-haves, salary if shown'
- **Quality filter:** 'Of these, which 3 best match my resume highlights [paste]? Rank by fit and flag any red flags - outdated stacks, unrealistic requirements'
- The filter is where your resume highlights do the ranking - AI scores against your real profile
### Why you must verify
- AI repeats stale or invented listings confidently
- Every shortlist row gets: link opens, posting still live, must-haves as written
- Verification is 2 minutes per row and converts AI suggestions into real leads

## The Company-Signals Check

- Ask AI to check hiring pages and postings for: remote policy, growth stage, tech/stack, and culture hints
- Combine AI signals with your own glance at their site and Glassdoor-style sources
- Signal checks happen BEFORE tailoring effort - do not customise for a company you would not join
```text
The sourcing-to-shortlist flow (15-minute block):
1. Run 2-3 search prompts across boards
2. Paste candidates into a quality filter - get ranked top 3
3. Run the company-signals check on the ranked 3
4. Click through and verify: live? accurate? red flags?
5. Log the true shortlist into the tracker
```
- Filter first, then apply - applying to junk is a tax on your focus

## Quality Over Volume

- Ten quality applications beat fifty generic ones
- Each quality application is a tailored resume, a real cover letter, and a tracked follow-up
- The tax on focus is real: junk applications crowd out the tailored ones you can sustain
### The repeatable lesson
- AI accelerates the finding; you own the filtering
- Your resume highlights + your judgement beat any autopilot
- Set a weekly number of quality applications - and stop there

## Key Takeaways

- AI sources fast - YOU verify everything manually
- Three tools: search prompts, quality filter, company-signals check
- Filter first, then apply - quality over volume
- Click through and confirm every posting is live and accurate
- Ten quality applications beat fifty generic ones

## Practice Challenge

### Module 3 · The Apply-Matching Pipeline

A tailored application does more than a generic one - the research says 3-5x more replies. The apply-matching pipeline is a repeatable 15-20 minute recipe that converts any serious posting into a tailored resume, a role-match brief, and a 150-word cover letter. Run it once, and every future application is faster.

The pipeline has three steps: build a five-line role brief, tailor your resume against it, then write a short cover letter that hooks from the actual posting. Nothing here invents your experience - AI matches and sharpens what is really yours.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a 5-line role-match brief from any posting
- Tailor a resume version using the brief
- Write a 150-word cover letter with hook, proof, and ask
- Verify every AI change against your real experience

## Step 1: The Role-Match Brief

```text
From this posting [paste] make a 5-line brief:
1. Must-haves
2. Nice-to-haves
3. 2 terms the recruiter likely screens for
4. What my resume should lead with
5. Any red flags in the posting wording
```
- The brief is your compass for every reservation decision
- Line 3 (screen terms) directly informs resume keywords
- Keep briefs in the tracker notes - reuse for the interview later

## Step 2: The Resume Pass

- Feed your master resume + the brief into AI -> output a tailored one-page version
- Adjust summary, reorder strengths, and mirror the posting's exact terminology
- Verify every change against your actual experience - AI sometimes embellishes
### The verification habit
- Go line by line: have I done this? Can I show it?
- Reject any claim that invented a title, date, or number
- The tailored version should differ because the ROLE differs, not the facts

## Step 3: The 150-Word Letter

- Max 150 words: a hook from the posting + 2 proof points + why now
- Ask AI to draft with 'lead with a specific connection to their product or team; end with a confident ask; no clichés'
- The pipeline takes 15-20 minutes per application once practised
### Why short letters win
- Recruiters skim letters - 150 words is the sweet spot between nothing and a novel
- A specific product tie-in proves you read the posting to the end
- Every letter should sound like you, not like a template

## Key Takeaways

- Step 1: build a 5-line role-match brief from any posting
- Step 2: tailor a one-page resume against the brief
- Step 3: write a 150-word letter - hook, 2 proofs, confident ask
- Verify every AI change line by line against real experience
- The pipeline runs in 15-20 minutes once practised

## Practice Challenge

### Module 4 · Follow-Ups That Don't Annoy

Follow-up converts silent applications into replies - and most people never send one, so the ones who do get noticed. Done right, a follow-up is a polite, specific, 80-word nudge at day 7-10 that reminds a recruiter you exist and still care. Done wrong, it is spam that costs you the opportunity it was meant to save.

This module gives you the system (one nudge, timed), the template (specific, not desperate), and the tone options (warm, direct, friendly) - plus the rule against mass-mailing identical follow-ups to fifty companies.

## Learning Objectives

- Time the single follow-up at day 7-10 of silence
- Use the 80-word template with real specifics
- Draft three tones with AI and personalise the chosen one
- Distinguish a good follow-up from an annoying one

## The Timing and Template

- **When:** if no response in 7-10 days, send ONE short, specific follow-up
- **What:** one-line reminder of the role, one line of specific interest (their product/post), one-line polite ask for timeline
- **Length:** 80 words maximum - every word beyond that dilutes
### Why timing matters
- Day 1-3 follow-ups look desperate; day 7-10 look organised
- One follow-up, then stop - silence is the recruiter's answer until it is not
- Your tracker's follow-up date column triggers the move automatically

## The Tone Menu

- Ask AI to draft three tones: warm, direct, friendly - then choose and personalise
- **Warm:** 'I really enjoyed learning about your team's [project]'
- **Direct:** 'I'm still enthusiastic about the [role] - can you share a timeline?'
- **Friendly:** short, human, low-pressure - 'Hi [name], just circling back on the [role]'
```text
A good follow-up sounds like:
1. 'Hi [name], I applied for the [role] on [date].'
2. 'I'm particularly interested in [specific detail about their
    project or company].'
3. 'Could you share an update on the timeline?' - polite, brief
```
- Personalise the winner with one fact from THEIR world - not a macro merge field

## The Annoy Map - and the Anti-Spam Rule

- **Bad follow-up:** 'Just checking in. Have you seen my application? Please respond.' - entitled, vague, zero specifics
- **Worse:** identical mass-mailing to 50 companies - targeting wins, volume loses
- **Annoy signals:** demanding, sending daily, copy-pasted without context
### The one-line win
- The best follow-up names something only you would know from their posting
- That single detail converts 'nudge' into 'this person is serious'
- Write follow-ups the same day as the application - personalise later with their updates

## Key Takeaways

- Send exactly one follow-up at day 7-10
- 80 words: reminder + specific interest + timeline ask
- Draft 3 tones with AI, then personalise the chosen one
- Never mass-mail identical follow-ups
- A specific detail from their posting is what converts the nudge

## Practice Challenge

2. Ask AI for warm, direct, and friendly drafts; personalise one
3. Set a day-7 reminder in your tracker for a live application

### Module 5 · Reviewing Your Funnel Weekly

Thirty minutes a week of funnel review is the difference between a search that repeats its mistakes and one that compounds. The funnel is the numbers: sourced, applied, replies, interviews, offers. Counting it weekly tells you exactly which stage is broken - and there is always exactly one broken stage to fix next week.

This module turns your tracker into a diagnostic tool. Each pattern - zero replies, zero after follow-ups, interviews without offers - has a specific fix. Diagnose the pattern, apply the fix, repeat.

## Learning Objectives

- Count the funnel weekly: sourced, applied, replies, interviews
- Diagnose the three common stall patterns and their fixes
- Log a one-line funnel summary each week
- Treat the funnel as the honest report of the system

## Reading the Three Stall Patterns

- **Zero replies from 10 tailored apps:** fix resume/LinkedIn (Mission 2) or widen geography/role tier - something is not getting past ATS
- **Zero responses after follow-ups:** improve targeting, not volume - you are reaching the wrong companies or wrong roles
- **Interviews but no offers:** the weak spot is Mission 4 - more mock interview reps; practise is the answer
### The one-stage rule
- The funnel nearly always has ONE weak stage pulling everything down
- Naming it focuses next week's effort - fix the stage, not the whole machine
- Re-check the same stage the following week to see the fix land

## The Weekly One-Line Log

```text
The weekly funnel summary (30-minute review):
1. Count: applied X, replies Y, interviews Z, rounds W, offers V
2. Name the weakest stage from the counts
3. Write one lesson: 'what did I learn from the numbers this week?'
4. Set 3 concrete actions that target the weak stage
5. Log it in the tracker - same day every week
```
- The log keeps you calm and honest - rejections become data points

## System Over Mood

- Rejections are data points in a system that works over time - 'Applied 12, 3 replies, 2 interviews, 1 second-round' is normal
- The funnel review replaces mood with measurement - you cannot argue with your own counts
- Adjust per week; the system is the plan, and the plan outlasts any single rejection
### The compounding value
- Ten weekly reviews build a picture no single week can show
- Your one-line logs become the interview story of a disciplined search
- Reviewing weekly is the habit that keeps the 90-day cadence alive

## Key Takeaways

- Count the funnel weekly - sourced, applied, replies, interviews
- Zero replies -> materials; zero after follow-ups -> targeting; interviews-no-offers -> mocks
- Fix the one weak stage, not the whole machine
- Log a one-line summary with a lesson each week
- The funnel replaces mood with measurement

## Practice Challenge

1. Count your funnel from your tracker - if empty, count the last 90 days
2. Name your weakest stage and pick 1-2 fixes for it
3. Write this week's one-line funnel log with a lesson

### Module 6 · The 90-Day Application Cadence

A 90-day search splits naturally into three phases: foundation and first applications, proof and momentum, then a full weekly cadence. Each phase has one job, and each sets up the next - no phase requires intensity, only consistency. This module puts the whole search together with the honesty rules that keep it defensible.

The cadence works because it is sustainable. Burnout kills more job searches than rejection does - so the system is designed to run at a pace you can keep for ninety days without hating yourself by day thirty.

## Learning Objectives

- Run the day 1-30 foundation phase
- Run the day 31-60 proof and momentum phase
- Run the day 61-90 full weekly cadence
- Apply the application honesty rules throughout

## Phases 1-2: Foundation and Proof

- **Days 1-30 - Foundation:** resume rebuilt (Mission 2), learning plan running (Mission 3), search system set up - apply to 3-4 roles per week while building skills
- **Days 31-60 - Proof and momentum:** proof project ready (Mission 3), add it to portfolio and LinkedIn, lift to 5 applications per week - mock reps paying off, interviews start coming
### Why the phases stack
- Month 1 skills make Month 2 projects real
- Month 2 proof makes Month 3 applications strong
- Each month's output is the next month's raw material

## Phase 3 and the Weekly Beat

- **Days 61-90 - Full cadence:** 5 quality applications + 1 brand update + 2 mocks + 1 funnel review per week
- This is the sustainable rhythm that carries through the offer stage
- Keep the funnel review - the cadence without review is aimlessness in rhythm
```text
The honesty rules that make the whole cadence defensible:
1. Apply only where you genuinely qualify (~70% of must-haves)
2. Never fake skills, dates, or roles - gap periods are fine and explainable
3. Never let AI ghost-write applications empty of your specifics
4. Generic letters are the first thing recruiters skip
```
- Apply the rules monthly - they protect the brand you are building

## Why Sustainability Wins

- Burnout kills more searches than rejection - the cadence is tuned to be maintainable
- A 5-app week sustained for 12 weeks beat a 30-app week collapsed on week 3
- Your future-offer moment comes from the last repeated week, not the first heroic one
- The 90-day cadence is how a system looks when it runs out of urgency and into habit

## Key Takeaways

- Day 1-30: foundation and first applications
- Day 31-60: proof project live, momentum builds
- Day 61-90: full weekly cadence of apps, brand, mocks, review
- Apply where you meet ~70% of must-haves - never fake gaps
- Sustainability beats intensity - burnout kills more searches than rejection
