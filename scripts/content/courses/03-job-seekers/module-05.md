# Mission 5: Job-Search Automation & Applications

## Learning Objectives

- Build an organised job-search system you can run daily
- Automate sourcing, tracking, and follow-ups with AI
- Write tailored applications from role to application letter
- Match your resume to each posting in minutes
- Run a productive 90-day application cadence

---
### Module 1 · Building Your Job Search Command Center
Scattered searching burns you out. This mission turns your search into a **daily system**: sourced → tracked → tailored → followed-up → reviewed.

**Set up your tracker** (Sheet, Notion, or Airtable) with columns: Company | Role | Link | Source | Status (sourced/applied/response/interview/offer/rejected) | Applied date | Follow-up date | Notes.

**Set up alerts** on job boards — daily digest, not spam. You want 3–5 fresh, relevant roles per day, not 50 random ones.

**Your daily routine** (45 minutes, same time every day):
- **Minutes 1–15:** Source — review alerts, find 3–5 quality matches.
- **Minutes 16–30:** Tailor — run the apply-matching pipeline for 2 of those.
- **Minutes 31–45:** Apply and log — submit applications, update tracker.

Tomorrow's structured hour beats today's panicked marathon. The routine is the system, and the system is the result.

### Module 2 · Automating Sourcing With AI
Use AI to find and filter roles faster — but verify everything manually.

**Search prompts:**
> "Find 5 [role] openings for a [profile] in [city/remote] on [board], each with: link, team, must-haves, salary if shown."

**Quality filter:**
> "Of these, which 3 best match my resume highlights [paste]? Rank by fit and flag any red flags in the post (e.g., outdated stacks, unrealistic requirements)."

**Company signals:**
> "Check these companies' hiring pages/roles for: remote policy, growth stage, tech/stack, and culture hints."

**Important:** Always click through to the actual posting. Don't trust AI's claims about current openings — verify the link, check the posting is still live, and read it yourself.

**Filter first, apply selectively.** Applying to junk is a tax on your focus. Ten quality applications beat fifty generic ones.

**Answer:** B — Verify links and read the actual posting before applying - don't trust AI's claims blindly.

**Answer:** B — It ranks which postings best match your resume and flags red flags like outdated stacks.

### Module 3 · The Apply-Matching Pipeline
For each serious posting, run a 3-step tailor:

**Step 1 — Role-match brief:**
> "From this posting [paste] make a 5-line brief: must-haves, nice-to-haves, 2 terms the recruiter likely screens for, and what my resume should lead with."

**Step 2 — Resume pass:** Feed your master resume + the brief into AI → output a tailored one-page version. Then verify every change against your actual experience — AI sometimes embellishes.

**Step 3 — Cover letter (short, real):** 150 words maximum. Hook from the posting + 2 proof points + why now.
> "Draft a 150-word cover letter for [role] at [company] using my resume + the posting. Lead with a specific connection to their product or team; end with a confident ask. No clichés."

**The pipeline takes 15–20 minutes per application** once you're practised. Quality tailored applications get 3–5x more replies than generic ones.

### Module 4 · Follow-Ups That Don't Annoy
Follow-up converts silent applications into replies. Most people don't follow up — the ones who do get noticed.

**System:** If no response in 7–10 days, send one short, specific follow-up.

**Template + personalisation:**
> "Subject: [Role] application — [Name] / [date applied]. One-line reminder of the role, one line of specific interest (their product/post), one-line polite ask for timeline. 80 words maximum."

Ask AI to draft three tones (warm, direct, friendly), then choose and personalise. Do NOT mass-mail identical follow-ups to 50 companies — targeting wins.

**What a good follow-up sounds like:**
> "Hi [name], I applied for the [role] on [date]. I'm particularly interested in [specific detail about their project/company]. Could you share an update on the timeline? Thank you."

**What a bad follow-up sounds like:**
> "Just checking in. Have you seen my application? Please respond."

Be specific, be polite, be brief.

### Module 5 · Reviewing Your Funnel Weekly
Every week, spend 30 minutes reviewing your tracker. Count the funnel: sourced → applied → replies → interviews.

**If zero replies from 10 tailored apps:** Fix resume/LinkedIn (Mission 2) or widen geography/role tier. Something in your materials isn't getting past ATS.

**If zero responses after follow-ups:** Improve targeting, not volume. You're reaching the wrong companies or the wrong roles.

**If interviews but no offers:** The weak spot is Mission 4 — more mock interview reps needed. Practise is the answer.

**Log one-liners weekly:**
> "Applied 12, 3 replies, 2 interviews, 1 second-round. Lessons: X."

The system keeps you calm and honest. Rejections aren't failures — they're data points in a system that works over time.

### Module 6 · The 90-Day Application Cadence
Structure your 90-day job search into three phases:

**Days 1–30: Foundation and first applications.** Your resume is rebuilt (Mission 2), your learning plan is running (Mission 3), and your search system is set up. Start applying to 3–4 roles per week while you continue building skills.

**Days 31–60: Proof and momentum.** Your proof project is ready (Mission 3). Add it to your portfolio and LinkedIn. Increase to 5 applications per week. Your mock reps are paying off — interviews start coming.

**Days 61–90: Full cadence.** 5 quality applications + 1 brand update + 2 mocks + 1 funnel review per week. This is the sustainable rhythm that carries through offer stage.

**Application honesty rules:**
- Apply only where you genuinely qualify (~70% of must-haves).
- Never fake skills, dates, or roles — gap periods are fine and explainable.
- Don't let AI ghost-write applications empty of your specifics — generic letters are the first thing recruiters skip.

The cadence works because it's sustainable. Burnout kills more job searches than rejection does.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the days 61-90 application target?
- A) 2 per week
- B) 5 quality applications per week
- C) 20 per day
- D) Only referrals

**Answer:** B — A sustainable full cadence of 5 quality applications per week.

2. When is it OK to apply to a role you don't fully meet?
- A) Always
- B) When you meet roughly 70% of must-haves
- C) Never
- D) Only for junior roles

**Answer:** B — Apply if you meet ~70% of must-haves - not for 1% fits.

3. Why does the cadence work?
- A) It is aggressive
- B) It is sustainable - burnout kills more searches than rejection
- C) It uses more channels
- D) It is automated

**Answer:** B — Sustainability is the point - burnout kills more job searches than rejection does.

## Real-World Examples

## Key Takeaways

- A 45-minute daily system (source → tailor → apply → log) beats episodic panic.
- Filter with AI, verify links, apply selectively — quality over volume.
- Tailor each application: brief → resume pass → 150-word letter.
- Follow up at day 7–10 with one short, specific note.
- Review the funnel weekly and fix the weak stage.
