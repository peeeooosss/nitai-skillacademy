### Module 1 · Why Resumes Fail Before a Human Reads Them
Recruiters and Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) skim in seconds. Your resume fails silently if it isn't: **scannable** (structure no machine fights), **relevant** (keywords a human expects), and **quantified** (achievements, not duties).

Most resumes are written as a list of duties — "Handled customer complaints." That tells a recruiter nothing about how well you did it. ATS software scans for keywords, measurable outcomes, and structural clarity. Missing any of these three means you're invisible.

**The six-second test:** A recruiter glances at your resume for six seconds before deciding to read or discard. In those six seconds, they scan: your name and contact, your current role, one or two measurable achievements, and whether the formatting is clean. If none of that stands out, it's in the reject pile.

AI speeds up the fix, but you must steer it with truth. A polished resume that exaggerates will fail in the interview — and damage your reputation.

### Module 2 · The PAR Method for Strong Bullets
Every resume bullet should follow **PAR** (Problem, Action, Result):

- **P — Problem:** the challenge you faced.
- **A — Action:** what you did to address it (include AI when true).
- **R — Result:** measured outcome with a number where possible.

**Weak (duty):** "Handled customer complaints."
**Strong (PAR):** "Resolved 40+ complex escalations/quarter, cutting repeat calls 25% by introducing a 5-step triage checklist."

**Another weak-to-strong example:**
- Weak: "Responsible for social media."
- Strong: "Grew LinkedIn engagement 180% in 3 months by posting daily AI-generated content drafts, then manually editing for brand voice."

**Prompt to transform your bullets:**
> "Rewrite each of my raw duties as PAR bullets. Keep them honest, specific, and under 18 words. Quantity where I gave real numbers; leave a [ ] placeholder where I must verify or fill a metric."

**Important:** Never let AI invent numbers. Every claim must survive the follow-up question: "Show me how you got that number." If it can't be shown, remove it.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does the P in PAR stand for?
- A) Plan
- B) Problem
- C) Practice
- D) Performance

**Answer:** B — PAR = Problem, Action, Result.

2. Which is a strong PAR bullet?
- A) Handled customer complaints
- B) Resolved 40+ escalations/quarter, cutting repeat calls 25% with a triage checklist
- C) Responsible for social media
- D) Helped with projects

**Answer:** B — A PAR bullet names the problem, action, and a measured result - not a duty.

3. What should you do with AI-generated numbers?
- A) Use them as-is
- B) Verify every number is real
- C) Round them up
- D) Delete all numbers

**Answer:** B — Never let AI invent numbers - every claim must survive 'show me how'.

### Module 3 · The 3-Pass AI Resume Workshop
Three passes with AI produce a polished, honest, targeted resume:

**Pass 1 — Match to target role.** Paste your resume plus your Mission 1 gap list:
> "Rewrite my resume to target [role]. Infer the 10 skills that role wants; emphasise them using my real experience only. Flag every place you made a stronger claim so I can verify."

**Pass 2 — Quantify and keywords.** Add ATS keywords from the job post; improve action verbs; kill weak language ("responsible for", "helped with"). Strong action verbs: delivered, launched, reduced, resolved, built, automated.

**Pass 3 — Tailor per application.** For each serious application, feed the job description and adjust. One size never fits — the best resumes are customised to the role.

**Honesty guardrails (non-negotiable):**
- Never let AI invent numbers, titles, companies, dates, or skills.
- If AI suggests stronger language, check it's still true first.
- Do not keyword-stuff skills you can't answer in an interview.
- You can note "I use AI-assisted workflows" in skills if it's true — it's increasingly valued.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is Pass 1 of the 3-pass workshop?
- A) Quantify keywords
- B) Match resume to target role
- C) Tailor per application
- D) Proofread for typos

**Answer:** B — Pass 1 matches the resume to the target role using your gap list and real experience.

2. What does Pass 3 add for each serious application?
- A) A new font
- B) Tailoring to that specific job description
- C) A second page
- D) More colours

**Answer:** B — Pass 3 tailors the resume to each application - one size never fits.

3. Is listing 'AI-assisted workflows' on a resume allowed?
- A) Never
- B) Yes, if it's true - it is increasingly valued
- C) Only for engineers
- D) Only if you built AI

**Answer:** B — You can note honest AI use in skills - it's increasingly valued, not penalised.

### Module 4 · Building a Resume That Compiles
Structure matters for both humans and ATS software:

- **Header:** Name, phone, email, city, LinkedIn, portfolio/GitHub. No photos in most markets.
- **Summary (3 lines):** Your story sentence from Mission 1, plus 3 value points with measurable outcomes.
- **Skills:** 6–10 grouped skills (tech / tools / soft). Place keywords from the target posting here.
- **Experience:** Reverse-chronological, PAR bullets, 4–6 per role. Every bullet must have a number or measurable outcome.
- **Education + Certifications + Projects.**

**Length rules:** One page for junior levels; two pages maximum otherwise.

**Formatting for ATS:** Use standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills). Avoid tables, columns, graphics, or photos — ATS parsers struggle with non-standard layouts. Save as PDF unless specifically asked for DOCX.

**Test your resume:** Paste the text into a plain-text editor. If the structure reads cleanly without formatting, an ATS will parse it correctly. If columns collapse into gibberish, you need to simplify the layout.

**Example Quiz**

1. What should never appear in a standard resume header?
- A) Name and email
- B) Photos in most markets
- C) Phone number
- D) LinkedIn URL

**Answer:** B — Photos are excluded from standard resumes in most markets.

2. How long should a junior-level resume be?
- A) One page
- B) Two pages
- C) Three pages
- D) As long as needed

**Answer:** A — One page for junior levels; two maximum otherwise.

3. What resume elements confuse ATS parsers?
- A) Standard headings
- B) Tables, columns, graphics, photos
- C) Simple bullets
- D) Plain text

**Answer:** B — Tables, columns, graphics, and photos confuse ATS parsers - keep the layout simple.

### Module 5 · LinkedIn: Building for Recruiters
LinkedIn is searchable, so it needs keywords in the right places. Recruiters search by skill and title — if those words aren't in your profile, you don't appear.

**Headline (120 chars):** Structure it as role + transition + tools.
Example: "Operations Specialist → Process Automation | AI Workflows | Data Analysis"
Don't just put "Seeking new opportunities" — that wastes your headline.

**About section:** Three short paragraphs: hook (your story), proof (2–3 achievements with numbers), and transition story (where you're heading). Ask AI for three drafts, then edit to sound like you.

**Experience:** Mirror your resume PAR bullets. LinkedIn rewards detail — expand where you can.

**Featured section:** Put your proof project (Mission 5) front and centre. This is your portfolio showcase.

**Skills:** Add the 10 skills from your target posting. Get endorsements from 3 real people — mutual endorsement is best.

**Settings:** Turn on "Open to Work" for recruiters-only visibility to stay private at current work.

**Prompt:**
> "Rewrite my headline and About for [role]. 120-char headline; About in 3 scannable paragraphs. Use my real facts only. Flag suggested keywords separately.

**Example Quiz**

- A) Name, photo, location

- C) Experience, education, skills
- D) Salary, dates, references

**Answer:** B — Headline structure: current role + transition + the tools you use - it is recruiter-searchable.

### Module 6 · The Two Readability Tests
Before you submit, run two quick tests:

**Test 1 — The Six-Second Scan.**
Show your resume to one person who knows nothing about your work. Give them six seconds to glance at it, then ask: "What am I good at? What value would I bring?" If they can't answer clearly, your top-of-page isn't working.

**Test 2 — The ATS Plain-Text Test.**
Copy your resume text into a plain-text editor (Notepad, Notes). Read it through. If the structure is clear — logical headings, readable bullets, no jumbled columns or missing pieces — an ATS will parse it correctly. If sections are garbled or the order is nonsensical, simplify your layout.

**Additional check:** Have someone proofread for typos and grammar. One spelling error in a key skill or company name can trigger a rejection — it signals carelessness.

**Combine both tests** into your application routine. They take five minutes and save you from silent failures.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does the six-second scan test actually check?
- A) Grammar
- B) Whether top-of-page value is obvious
- C) Page count
- D) Buzzwords

**Answer:** B — A stranger glances for six seconds - if they can't say what you're good at, the resume fails.

2. How do you run the ATS plain-text test?
- A) Email the resume
- B) Paste resume text into a plain-text editor and check structure
- C) Print the resume
- D) Convert to image

**Answer:** B — Pasting into plain text reveals whether the structure is ATS-readable or collapses.

3. What should you fix if sections look garbled in plain text?
- A) The content of bullets
- B) The layout - probably tables or columns
- C) The font
- D) The pronouns

**Answer:** B — Garbled plain text means non-standard layout like tables or columns. Simplify it.

## Key Takeaways

- PAR bullets beat duty bullets: Problem → Action → Result.
- Run 3 passes: match role, quantify keywords, tailor per application.
- Never let AI invent metrics or titles — verify everything.
- Build LinkedIn for search: keyword-rich headline, About with proof, Featured proof project.
- Test with six-second scanning and ATS plain-text check.
