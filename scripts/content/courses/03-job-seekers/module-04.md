### Module 1 · Your 90-Second Opening

Every interview opens with 'tell me about yourself' - and most candidates waste it on chronology. This module compresses your entire story into ninety seconds with four parts: **Now, Transition, Proof, Here**. Perfect this one answer and you set the tone for everything that follows.

The opening is drillable with AI, but it must be performed in YOUR words. Draft a version, rewrite it until it sounds like you, time it out loud, and have one human hear it. Natural and confident beats polished and rehearsed-sounding every time.

## Learning Objectives

- Structure a 90-second opening: Now, Transition, Proof, Here
- Draft it with AI but rewrite it in your own voice
- Keep it under 160 words and specific
- Practise it out loud and with a real listener

## The Four-Part Structure

- **Now** - your current situation and what you are known for (one line)
- **Transition** - why you are moving toward X, using your background (one line)
- **Proof** - one achievement or project with a number (1-2 lines)
- **Here** - why this role and company, and what you bring (one line)
### Why this order works
- It leads with the present, not a start-from-birth story
- The proof line carries the weight; Now and Here frame it
- Ninety seconds is 150-170 words at a natural speaking pace - aim under 160

## Drafting With AI, Owning With Voice

```text
Draft a 90-second 'tell me about yourself' from my resume [paste].
Structure: now / transition / proof / here. Keep it natural, specific,
and under 160 words. Then give me 2 alternative openings.
```
- The two alternatives give you a range of tones to pick from
- Rewrite the chosen draft in your own words - AI polish that is not yours sounds memorised
- Cut anything you cannot say conversationally - natural beats precise

## The Delivery Drill

- Write it in YOUR words, then time it out loud - adjust until it lands at 85-95 seconds
- Have one person hear it - their reaction is the test
- The goal is confident and specific, not rehearsed-sounding - vary the rhythm slightly each take
### The compounding rehearse
- Five takes a day for three days changes your delivery noticeably
- Record your phone's voice memo and re-listen - most people notice the filler words immediately
- Your opening is reused everywhere: interviews, networking, portfolio intros - repeat it enough to internalise

## Key Takeaways

- Structure: Now, Transition, Proof, Here
- Draft with AI, then rewrite in your own voice
- Keep it under 160 words and under 95 seconds
- Practise out loud and with one human listener
- Your opening is reusable across interviews and networking

## Practice Challenge

1. Draft your opening with AI, rewrite it in your own voice, and time it
2. Speak it into a voice memo and re-listen - cut the filler words
3. Deliver the final version to one person and note their reaction

**Example Quiz**

**Answer:** A — Now (current) -> Transition (why moving) -> Proof (achievement with number) -> Here (why this role).

### Module 2 · The AI Mock-Interview Loop

Interview skill is built by repetition - not by reading about interviews. The AI mock-interview loop gives you three session types that train every common question pattern: behavioural, technical, and case-style. Run them in a cycle, answer out loud, and let AI score and push back after each answer.

The loop exists because interviewers do not hire answers - they hire how you think under pressure. The sessions below train that thinking by simulating realistic interview conditions, one question at a time.

## Learning Objectives

- Run three AI mock session types: behavioural, technical, case
- Answer every session out loud, not in writing
- Use AI scoring and follow-ups to sharpen each answer
- Loop the three types across weeks for steady reps

## Session 1: Behavioural (STAR)

- Ask AI to interview you on 5 behavioural questions - conflict, failure, deadline, feedback, disagreement
- Answer each in STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result - 60-90 seconds each
- After each answer, get a 1-5 score on clarity, structure, specificity, plus one follow-up
### The STAR discipline
- Situation and Task in two sentences max - land in the Action fast
- Every behavioural answer ends with a Result - measurable if possible
- Scoring each answer teaches you where your answers thin out

## Sessions 2 & 3: Technical and Case

- **Session 2 - Technical:** 4 technical questions about your skill/tool stack - include 1 scenario and 1 'you are asked to build X' question; ask AI to point out where an expert would push further
- **Session 3 - Case:** one realistic 40-minute case (an open-ended business or data problem); present your approach, then let AI push back on weak spots like a hiring manager
```text
The three-session rotation (run weekly):
Week A: Behavioural (STAR) + scoring
Week B: Technical / skill-specific + expert pushback
Week C: Case / take-home style + hiring-manager pushback
Log 3 fixes after every session - see your weakest pattern shrink
```
- Rotating the types keeps your reps balanced and honest

## The Out-Loud Rule

- Written answers do not train your mouth or your confidence
- Speak answers into your phone's voice recorder if it helps - then re-listen
- Interviews are spoken sport - train the speaking, not the writing
### Making the loop a habit
- Two reps per week is the minimum that shows progress
- Same weekly slot, same three recorded answers to compare growth
- The loop pairs with Mission 4's debrief to convert reps into fixes

## Key Takeaways

- Three session types: behavioural, technical/skill, case
- Answer every session OUT LOUD - never just in writing
- Behavioural runs on STAR with a measurable result
- Case sessions build thinking under a real problem
- Two reps per week, rotated, is the minimum that moves you

## Practice Challenge

1. Run a behavioural session with 5 STAR questions and log your scores
2. Run one technical session with a 'build X' scenario
3. Run one case session and note where AI pushed back the hardest

**Example Quiz**

1. How many AI mock session types are there?

- B) 1

- D) 5

**Answer:** A — Three: behavioural (STAR), technical/skill, and case/take-home style.

2. How should you answer mock questions?
- A) Out loud
- B) In writing
- C) In bullet points
- D) Silently

**Answer:** A — Always answer out loud - written answers do not train your mouth.

3. Which session pushes back on weak spots like a hiring manager?
- A) Case / take-home style
- B) Behavioural
- C) Technical
- D) None

**Answer:** A — The case session gives a realistic problem and pushes back on weak spots like a hiring manager.

**Answer:** A — Two rotated reps per week is the minimum that shows steady progress.

### Module 3 · The Post-Session Debrief

Mock interviews without a debrief are practice without a mirror. The post-session debrief converts every session into a short, concrete fix-list: exactly three weaknesses, one strength to scale, and one drill per weakness. Repeat this after every session and the same weakness visibly shrinks across sessions - that shrinking is proof you are improving.

The debrief is built around a growing fix-list file that you read before each new session. Its purpose is honest comparison: your Session 1 rambling answers versus your Session 5 structured ones is the exact evidence you need when motivation dips.

## Learning Objectives

- Run a debrief prompt after every session
- Maintain a growing fix-list file across sessions
- Track one recurring weakness shrinking over time
- Use the file to start each new session immediately

## The 3-Fix Debrief Prompt

```text
Based on this entire interview, give me exactly 3 weaknesses to
fix and 1 strength to scale. For each weakness, give one concrete
drill to improve it this week.
```
- Exactly three - more than three and you cannot act on all of them
- Each weakness is useless without a drill - the drill is the action
- Ask a follow-up: 'rank the 3 weaknesses by interview impact'

## The Growing Fix-List File

- Keep a single file across ALL sessions - one tab, one doc, one running list
- Example: Session 1 'answered behavioural questions without structure - rambling' -> fix: practise STAR with a timer
- Example: Session 2 'gave vague technical answers - did not explain reasoning' -> fix: narrate your thought process out loud
- Example: Session 3 'same reasoning gap - still not spoken' -> fix: practise explaining before writing
### The reading ritual
- Open the fix-list before every session - start cold, not from memory
- Session begins by checking: which old weakness did I bring in?
- Tag each entry with a date so you can watch the timeline

## Visible Shrinkage

- The same weakness shrinking across sessions is the real gain of mock interviews
- Not one perfect session - visible growth across many - is what transferable skill looks like
- When one weakness plateaus, move the drill energy into the next one
### The confidence effect
- Your fix-list doubles as evidence you are improving, which recruiters sense
- Prepare a one-line interview answer about 'how you practise' - strong candidates do
- Ten sessions with debriefs beat a hundred sessions without

## Key Takeaways

- Debrief every session: 3 weaknesses, 1 strength, 1 drill each
- Keep one growing fix-list file across all sessions
- Read the file before every new session - start cold
- Watch the SAME weakness shrink - that is real progress
- Sessions without debriefs are practice without a mirror

## Practice Challenge

1. Run the debrief prompt after your most recent mock and write the 3 fixes
2. Create your fix-list file and log today's session
3. Export one weakness from your file into this week's practice plan

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the 3-fix list?
- A) 3 weaknesses to fix and 1 strength to scale
- B) Three strengths
- C) Three salary targets
- D) Three companies to apply

**Answer:** A — After every session: exactly 3 weaknesses to fix and 1 strength to scale, each with a drill.

2. What should each weakness come with?
- A) One concrete drill to improve it this week
- B) A follow-up question
- C) A new resume
- D) A mock score

**Answer:** A — Each weakness gets one concrete weekly drill so you can see it shrink.

3. How do you know practice is working?
- A) The same weakness visibly shrinks across sessions
- B) Feedbacks stay the same
- C) Scores stay constant
- D) You stop practising

**Answer:** A — Tracking the same weakness across sessions shows visible growth - that is practice working.

4. Why read the fix-list before each session?
- A) So you start cold, targeting your known weaknesses
- B) To memorise answers
- C) To delete old notes
- D) To replace drills

**Answer:** A — Starting cold against your known weaknesses is how sessions compound.

### Module 4 · The 5 Question Archetypes

Nearly every interview question compresses into five patterns. If you prepare one answer framework for each archetype, you are prepared for almost any interview - because the interviewer is asking the same five questions in different disguises. This module gives you the framework for each and the research habits that make the answers land.

The five archetypes are: weakness or mistake, behavioural 'tell me about a time', why-us, problem-solving scenarios, and your own questions for them. Master the frameworks, and the interview stops feeling like a pop quiz.

## Learning Objectives

- Name the five question archetypes
- Answer weakness and behavioural questions with STAR
- Prepare specific why-us answers from real research
- Prepare smart questions that show genuine interest

## Weakness, Behavioural, and Why-Us

- **Weakness/Mistake:** use STAR with a real failure and what you changed - never 'I work too hard'
- **Behavioural 'tell me about a time...':** STAR format, 60-90 seconds, always end with a measurable result
- **Why us / Why this role:** research the company, then give 2 specific reasons that show you did homework
### The misstep to avoid
- Generic answers read as not interested
- 'I work too hard' is the most rehearsed lie in interviews
- A real failure with a real lesson outperforms a perfect-sounding fake

## Problem-Solving and Your Questions

- **Problem-solving scenario:** talk through your approach step-by-step out loud - hiring managers hire the thinking process, not just the answer
- **Your questions for them:** always have 2-3 smart questions ready - they show genuine interest and strategic thinking
- Ask questions that show you understand their challenges - not generic ones answerable from their website
```text
Company research prompt - run before every interview:
Summarise [company]: what they do, their products, recent news
(verify it yourself!), culture signals, and 3 sharp questions I
should ask the interviewer that show real interest.
```
- Verify any 'news' the AI returns - it can be wrong or stale
- Write your 3 questions in your own words - they must sound like you

## Turning Frameworks Into Reps

- Build one framework page per archetype with examples from YOUR experience
- Run each framework through a mock interview, not just reading it
- Pair the archetypes with the mock loop so the frameworks become reflexes
### The research habit
- Always research the company before the interview - not after
- The 3 questions you ask are as judged as the answers you give
- Two specific researched reasons beat a paragraph of vague praise

## Key Takeaways

- Five archetypes cover most interview questions
- Weakness and behavioural answers run on STAR
- Why-us needs 2 specific researched reasons
- Problem-solving rewards the spoken thinking process
- Always bring 2-3 sharp questions that show real interest

## Practice Challenge

1. Write one STAR answer for weakness and one for a behavioural prompt
2. Run the company research prompt on your top target company
3. Write your 3 smart questions in your own words

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the five question archetypes?
- A) Weakness, behavioural, why-us, problem-scenario, your questions
- B) Five strengths
- C) Salary, title, team, hours, perks
- D) Name, role, company, project, reference

**Answer:** A — Weakness/mistake, behavioural STAR, why us, problem-solving scenario, and your questions for them.

2. How should you handle the 'Why us' question?
- A) With 2 specific reasons from company research
- B) With a generic answer
- C) With salary data
- D) With a joke

**Answer:** A — Research the company and give 2 specific reasons that show you did homework.

3. What does a good 'questions for them' show?
- A) Real interest and strategic thinking
- B) Confusion
- C) Negotiation skills
- D) Nerves

**Answer:** A — Smart questions show genuine interest and understanding of their challenges.

4. What should you say for a weakness/mistake question?
- A) A real failure with what you changed - via STAR
- B) 'I work too hard'
- C) 'I have no weaknesses'
- D) A fake flaw

**Answer:** A — Use a real failure and what you changed - never 'I work too hard'.

### Module 5 · Preparing for Take-Home Tasks

Many roles send a take-home assignment: analyse a dataset, build a small automation, prepare a document. These assignments look casual but are scored hard - on process, not just output. This module shows how to beat them professionally: restate scope, over-explain your method, time-box the work, and stay honest about AI.

The take-home is a filter for how you work when unsupervised. Interviewers want to see your assumptions, your thinking, and your professionalism under a deadline - not a perfect artefact produced at triple the allowed effort.

## Learning Objectives

- Restate the scope in two lines before starting
- Deliver an over-explained answer with method and next steps
- Time-box take-home work to what was asked
- Use AI to structure and polish - honestly disclosed

## Restate, Then Deliver

- **Restate scope in 2 lines** before starting - if allowed, ask one clarifying question; recruiters love the precision
- **Deliver over-explained:** assumptions, method, results, next steps - not just the answer
- **Record your process** even as 5 bullet points - interviewers ask how you approached it, and that is the real test
### The value of restating
- A 2-line restate proves you understood the brief - half of candidates do not
- A clarifying question shows professional precision and reduces your ambiguity risk
- Your process notes are the raw material for the follow-up interview

## Time-Boxing and AI Use

- **Never over-perfect.** Set a time limit based on what was asked (usually 2-4 hours). A solid 80% solution delivered on time beats a perfect solution a day late
- **Never use AI to fake the deliverable.** Use it to structure and polish your own work - and say so if asked. Honesty about AI use is increasingly valued, not penalised
```text
The take-home workflow:
1. Restate scope in 2 lines (and optionally ask 1 clarifying question)
2. Set a timer to 80-120% of the time offered
3. Do the work yourself; use AI to restructure and polish
4. Document: assumptions, method, results, next steps
5. Submit on time; add a 2-line follow-up thank-you
```
- The follow-up thank-you is one short note, not a novel

## The After-Submit Move

- Send a brief follow-up thanking them and restating your interest
- The note is short, specific, and free of apology for the work
- Keep your process notes - they are ammunition for the next interview round
### How this scores with recruiters
- On-time, over-explained, honest work signals reliability - the top trait they screen for
- A late 'perfect' submission signals the opposite set of instincts
- Your follow-up is a second touchpoint that recruiters remember

## Key Takeaways

- Restate scope in 2 lines - ask one clarifying question if allowed
- Deliver over-explained: assumptions, method, results, next steps
- Time-box to what was asked - 80% on time beats perfect late
- Never fake with AI - use it to polish your own work, disclosed
- Follow up with one short, specific thank-you note

## Practice Challenge

1. Write the 2-line scope restatement for a real or imagined take-home brief
2. Draft the 5-bullet process log template you would fill in during work
3. Write a one-paragraph after-submit follow-up note you can adapt

**Answer:** A — Restate the scope in 2 lines before starting - recruiters love the precision.

### Module 6 · Running a 90-Day Application Cadence

The job search is a system, not a random event - and a system only works when run weekly. This mission's cadence is the operating rhythm: 4-5 tailored applications, 1 brand update, 2 mock reps, and 1 funnel review, every week, without interruption. The cadence is deliberately sustainable because sustainability beats intensity in a long search.

The funnel review is the heart of the system - it tells you exactly where your search stalls, so you fix the right part instead of shouting at the whole machine.

## Learning Objectives

- Run a weekly cadence: 4-5 apps, 1 brand update, 2 mocks, 1 funnel review
- Track the funnel: sourced, applied, reply, interview, offer
- Diagnose the weak stage from funnel data
- Treat rejections as data in a working system

## The Weekly Rhythm

- **4-5 quality tailored applications** - not volume, precision
- **1 brand/LinkedIn update** - a post, a connection, a skill added
- **2 mock interview reps** - rotate the three session types from Mission 4
- **1 funnel review** - where is each application in the pipeline?
### Why the combo
- Applications feed the funnel; mocks feed conversions; the brand feed sources
- Each element has a job - and skipping one starves the machine
- The whole cadence fits between 3 and 5 hours a week

## The Funnel Review

- Track **sourced -> applied -> reply -> interview -> offer -> accepted**
- Zero replies after 10 applications: fix resume or LinkedIn (Mission 2)
- Interviews happen but no offers: more mock reps (Mission 4) - practise is the answer
```text
The weekly one-line funnel log:
Applied X, replies Y, interviews Z, rounds W, offers V.
Lessons: [one sentence on the weakest stage].
Then set 3 actions for the weakest stage next week.
```
- The log keeps the search honest and the fixes targeted

## Emotional Discipline

- Rejections are data - 'Applied 12, 3 replies, 2 interviews, 1 second-round' is a normal funnel
- Never break the loop during rejection streaks - the system keeps you calm and honest
- By day 90 you may not have the offer yet - but the system is proven and running
### The 90-day checkpoint
- You should have a working application-to-offer system
- Offers follow from consistent systems, not desperation
- If the numbers are ugly, the funnel review tells you exactly why - fix the stage

## Key Takeaways

- Weekly cadence: 4-5 apps, 1 brand update, 2 mocks, 1 funnel review
- The funnel review names the weak stage - fix the stage, not the machine
- Zero replies -> materials; interviews-nothing -> more mock reps
- Rejections are data, not verdicts
- A sustainable cadence beats intensity in any 90-day search

## Practice Challenge

2. Create your funnel tracker with stage columns
3. Run one funnel review this week and write the one-line log
