# Mission 4: AI Mock Interviews & Case Studies

## Learning Objectives

- Prepare a tight 90-second "tell me about yourself"
- Run realistic mock interviews with AI, then a human
- Use AI to debrief each practice and fix weak spots systematically
- Crack common technical and behavioural question patterns
- Prepare for take-home case or assignment-style rounds

---
### Module 1 · Your 90-Second Opening
Every interview opens with "tell me about yourself." Perfect this first — it sets the tone for everything after.

Structure it in four parts:

1. **Now** — Current situation and what you're known for (one line).
2. **Transition** — Why you're moving toward X, using your background (one line).
3. **Proof** — One achievement or project with a number (1–2 lines).
4. **Here** — Why this role and company, and what you bring (one line).

**Prompt to build it:**
> "Draft a 90-second 'tell me about yourself' from my resume [paste], structure: now / transition / proof / here. Keep it natural, specific, under 160 words. Then give me 2 alternative openings."

Write it in YOUR words — not AI's polished phrasing. Time it out loud. Have one person hear it. The goal is natural, confident, and specific — not rehearsed-sounding.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the four parts of the 90-second opening?
- A) Past, present, future, hobbies
- B) Now, transition, proof, here
- C) Name, skills, education, goal
- D) Strengths, weaknesses, examples, ask

**Answer:** B — Now (current) → Transition (why moving) → Proof (achievement with number) → Here (why this role).

2. How long should the drafted opening be?
- A) 250 words
- B) Under 160 words
- C) Exactly 100 words
- D) One paragraph minimum

**Answer:** B — Keep it under 160 words, natural and specific.

3. What should you do after drafting the opening?
- A) Submit it
- B) Write it in YOUR words and time it out loud
- C) Let AI memorise it
- D) Never practise

**Answer:** B — Rewrite in your own voice, time it, and practise out loud.

### Module 2 · The AI Mock-Interview Loop
Interview skill is built by repetition. Run three session types in a loop: practise, debrief, fix.

**Session 1 — Behavioural (STAR):**
> "Act as a senior interviewer at [company type] hiring for [role]. Ask me 5 behavioural questions one at a time: conflict, failure, deadline, feedback, disagreement. After each answer, score 1–5 on clarity/structure/specificity, then ask one follow-up."

**Session 2 — Technical / skill-specific:**
> "Ask me 4 technical questions about [role skill/tool stack]. Include 1 scenario and 1 'you're asked to build X' question. After each, point out where an expert would push further."

**Session 3 — Case / take-home style:**
> "Give me a realistic 40-minute case: [open-ended business/data problem for my role]. Wait for my approach, then push back on weak spots like a hiring manager."

**Always answer out loud.** Written answers don't train your mouth or your confidence. Speak into your phone's voice recorder if it helps.

**Example Quiz**

1. How many AI mock session types are there?
- A) 1
- B) 2
- C) 3
- D) 5

**Answer:** C — Three: behavioural (STAR), technical/skill, and case/take-home style.

2. How should you answer mock questions?
- A) In writing
- B) Out loud
- C) In bullet points
- D) Silently

**Answer:** B — Always answer out loud - written answers don't train your mouth.

3. Which session pushes back on weak spots like a hiring manager?
- A) Behavioural
- B) Technical
- C) Case / take-home style
- D) None

**Answer:** C — The case session gives a realistic problem and pushes back on weak spots like a hiring manager.

### Module 3 · The Post-Session Debrief
After EVERY session, get your 3-fix list:
> "Based on this entire interview, give me exactly 3 weaknesses to fix and 1 strength to scale. For each weakness, one concrete drill to improve it this week."

Keep a **growing fix-list file** across sessions. Track the same weakness shrinking over time — that's your practice working.

**What a fix-list looks like across sessions:**
- Session 1: "Answered behavioural questions without structure — too rambling." → Fix: practise STAR format with a timer.
- Session 2: "Gave a vague technical answer — didn't explain my reasoning." → Fix: always narrate your thought process out loud.
- Session 3: "Same issue — reasoning wasn't spoken." → Fix: practise explaining code before writing it.

The pattern shows improvement. That's the real gain of mock interviews — not one perfect session, but visible growth across many sessions.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the 3-fix list?
- A) Three strengths
- B) 3 weaknesses to fix and 1 strength to scale
- C) Three salary targets
- D) Three companies to apply

**Answer:** B — After every session: exactly 3 weaknesses to fix and 1 strength to scale, each with a drill.

2. What should each weakness come with?
- A) A follow-up question
- B) One concrete drill to improve it this week
- C) A new resume
- D) A mock score

**Answer:** B — Each weakness gets one concrete weekly drill so you can see it shrink.

3. How do you know practice is working?
- A) Feedbacks stay the same
- B) The same weakness visibly shrinks across sessions
- C) Scores stay constant
- D) You stop practising

**Answer:** B — Tracking the same weakness across sessions shows visible growth - that's practice working.

### Module 4 · The 5 Question Archetypes
Nearly all interview questions reduce to five patterns. Prepare an answer framework for each:

1. **Weakness / Mistake** — Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) with a real failure and what you changed. Never say "I work too hard."

2. **Behavioural "tell me about a time..."** — STAR format, 60–90 seconds, always end with a measurable result.

3. **Why us / Why this role** — Research the company (use the prompt below), give 2 specific reasons that show you did homework.

4. **Problem-solving scenario** — Talk through your approach step-by-step out loud. Hiring managers hire the thinking process, not just the answer.

5. **Your questions for them** — Always have 2–3 smart questions. This shows genuine interest and strategic thinking.

**Company research prompt:**
> "Summarise [company]: what they do, their products, recent news (verify!), culture signals, and 3 sharp questions I should ask the interviewer that show real interest."

Ask questions that show you understand their challenges — not generic ones easily answered from their website.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the five question archetypes?
- A) Five strengths
- B) Weakness, behavioural, why-us, problem-scenario, your questions
- C) Salary, title, team, hours, perks
- D) Name, role, company, project, reference

**Answer:** B — Weakness/mistake, behavioural STAR, why us, problem-solving scenario, and your questions for them.

2. How should you handle the 'Why us' question?
- A) With a generic answer
- B) With 2 specific reasons from company research
- C) With salary data
- D) With a joke

**Answer:** B — Research the company and give 2 specific reasons that show you did homework.

3. What does a good 'questions for them' show?
- A) Confusion
- B) Real interest and strategic thinking
- C) Negotiation skills
- D) Nerves

**Answer:** B — Smart questions show genuine interest and understanding of their challenges.

### Module 5 · Preparing for Take-Home Tasks
Many roles send a take-home assignment: analyse data, build a small automation, prepare a document. Beat it professionally:

- **Restate scope in 2 lines** before starting. If allowed, ask one clarifying question — recruiters love the precision.
- **Deliver over-explained:** assumptions, method, results, next steps. Not just the answer — show your thinking.
- **Record your process** (even 5 bullet points). Interviewers ask how you approached it — that's the real test.
- **Never use AI to fake the deliverable.** Use it to structure and polish your own work, and say so if asked. Honesty about AI use is increasingly valued, not penalised.

**The trap:** Spending too long perfecting a take-home. Set a time limit based on what was asked (usually 2–4 hours). A solid 80% solution delivered on time beats a perfect solution a day late.

**After submitting:** Brief follow-up thanking them and restating your interest. One short note, not a novel.

**Example Quiz**

1. What should you do before starting a take-home task?
- A) Guess the answer
- B) Restate scope in 2 lines and clarify if allowed
- C) Email HR
- D) Start coding immediately

**Answer:** B — Restate the scope in 2 lines before starting - recruiters love the precision.

2. What should a delivered take-home include?
- A) Just the answer
- B) Assumptions, method, results, next steps
- C) Only slides
- D) A thank-you letter

**Answer:** B — Deliver over-explained: assumptions, method, results, and next steps - not just an answer.

3. Can you use AI on a take-home task?
- A) Never
- B) Yes - to structure and polish your own work, disclosing if asked
- C) Only for design
- D) Only outside working hours

**Answer:** B — Use AI to structure and polish your own work, and say so if asked - honesty is valued.

### Module 6 · Running a 90-Day Application Cadence
The interview process is a system, not a random event. Run it with discipline:

**Weekly rhythm:**
- 4–5 quality tailored applications
- 1 brand/LinkedIn update
- 2 mock interview reps (rotate the three session types)
- 1 funnel review (what stage is each application at?)

**The funnel review is critical.** Track: sourced → applied → reply → interview → offer → accepted. If replies are zero after 10 applications, fix your resume or LinkedIn (Mission 2). If interviews happen but no offers, more mock reps are needed (this mission).

**Never break the loop during rejections.** Rejections are data. "Applied 12, 3 replies, 2 interviews, 1 second-round" — that's a normal funnel. The system keeps you calm and honest.

**The 90-day checkpoint:** By the end, you should have a working application-to-offer system. You may not have the offer yet — but the system is proven and running. Offers follow from consistent systems, not desperation.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the weekly interview rep target?
- A) 10 mocks
- B) 2 mock reps rotating the session types
- C) 1 mock per month
- D) None once employed

**Answer:** B — Weekly rhythm: 4-5 tailored apps, 1 brand update, 2 mock reps, 1 funnel review.

2. What does the funnel review track?
- A) Salary
- B) Sourced → applied → reply → interview → offer
- C) Hours spent
- D) Courses completed

**Answer:** B — The funnel shows where applications stall - the weak stage is where you fix next.

3. What should rejections be treated as?
- A) Failures
- B) Data points in a working system
- C) Reasons to quit
- D) Personal insults

**Answer:** B — Rejections are data - 'Applied 12, 3 replies, 2 interviews' is a normal funnel.

## Key Takeaways

- Perfect your 90-second opening: Now → Transition → Proof → Here.
- Run 3 mock types (behavioural, technical, case) and answer OUT LOUD.
- Debrief every session into 3 fixes; read your fix-list each new session.
- Master the 5 question archetypes + company research.
- Take-homes: restate scope, over-explain method, be honest about AI use.
