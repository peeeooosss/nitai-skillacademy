# Mission 7: AI Ethics — Privacy, Honesty, Real vs Fake

## Learning Objectives

- Protect your privacy when using AI tools
- Learn the difference between "AI made it" and "it really happened"
- Practise honest and kind use of AI
- Become a fact detective with the F.A.C.T. toolkit
- Build a personal AI Safety Code

---
### Module 1 · The AI Superpower Comes With Rules
AI is powerful — and with great power comes great responsibility. This mission is about using your AI superpower **safely, honestly, and kindly**.

Think of your AI knowledge as three protection shields you carry everywhere:

1. **Shield 1 — Privacy:** what you should never give AI.
2. **Shield 2 — Honesty:** always telling the truth about how AI helped.
3. **Shield 3 — Real vs Fake:** spotting things AI made that aren't true.

Learn to raise all three shields, and you'll be able to use AI confidently for your whole life — without ever getting hurt or hurting others.

### Module 2 · Shield 1: Protect Your Privacy
AI tools store your chats. Be careful what you type:

**Never share with AI:**
- Your full name + school name together
- Your home address, phone number, or birthday
- Photos of your face, or faces of family and friends
- Passwords, OTPs, or bank/app details (even if AI asks!)
- School login details

**Smart habits:**
- Use fake names in examples: "a student in Class 6" instead of your real name.
- Don't paste private letters or documents into AI.
- If an app asks for your photo, camera, or contacts — stop and ask a grown-up.
- Use apps that your school or parents approve.

> **Golden privacy rule:** *If you wouldn't write it on the class board, don't type it to AI.*

**Tonight's privacy audit (from your practice challenge):** look at the last few things you typed to AI and rewrite them with NO personal information. It's like cleaning your digital room — quick and protects you.

### Module 3 · Shield 2: Honesty — Always Tell the Truth About AI
Honesty means being clear when AI helped you:

- Write your schoolwork yourself, and be proud of using AI as a coach.
- If a project allows AI help, write a small line: *"I used AI to brainstorm ideas; I wrote and designed the rest myself."*
- Never print AI answers as your own on graded work without your teacher's permission.
- Never pretend an AI-made image or video is your own work.

**The honest user is never caught; the dishonest user always is.** And honesty keeps your learning real — which is the whole point.

There's a secret advantage to honesty too: the moment you say *"AI helped me here"*, you look smart and trustworthy. Teachers and leaders love students who are honest about their tools. It's a strength, not a confession.

**Try it now:** Write your own honesty line: *"In this project, AI helped me with ______, and I did ______ myself."*

### Module 4 · Shield 3: Real vs Fake — Become a Fact Detective
AI can create pictures, videos, and voices that look and sound completely real. These are called **deepfakes**. And AI can also write fake news that spreads fast.

**Famous fake traps:**
- A video with a real face and a fake voice saying something it never said.
- An amazing event photo that never happened.
- Shocking news headlines with no source.

**The Detective's Toolkit — F.A.C.T.**
- **F — Find the source.** Who made this? Is it a known, trusted site or an unknown account?
- **A — Ask more questions.** Does it match other trustworthy reports?
- **C — Check for clues.** Strange hands, blurry edges, wobbly lips, weird reflections? AI images often mess these up.
- **T — Tell a grown-up.** If something claims something shocking or asks you to share it urgently — STOP and ask an adult first.

**Bonus habit:** Use *reverse image search* (with a grown-up) to see where a picture really came from.

**Try it now:** Next time you see a shocking video or headline, whisper the four letters to yourself: F-A-C-T. Then act on it.

### Module 5 · The Kindness Rule
Words matter, even computer words:

- Don't ask AI to write mean, hateful, or scary things about anyone — not even "as a joke."
- Don't create fake images to embarrass or bully classmates. **This is serious and can hurt deeply** — never do it.
- If a friend is spreading fake content or cyberbullying, be brave and tell a trusted adult.

**Why it matters:** the person "behind the screen" is a real person with real feelings. A fake image or a mean AI text can humiliate someone for years. Kindness online is not soft — it's strength, and it protects everyone, including you.

**The kindness check:** before you share anything, ask: *"Would I be okay if this was written or shown about me?"* If the answer is no, don't send it.

### Module 6 · Your Personal AI Safety Code
Make your own 5-line Safety Code. Start with these, then add your own:

1. I will never share my full name, school, address, or photos with AI or strangers online.
2. I will always check with a grown-up before using a new app or sharing something online.
3. I will do my own schoolwork and honestly say when AI helped me.
4. I will check F.A.C.T. before believing or sharing anything surprising.
5. I will keep my AI use kind, respectful, and safe for everyone.

**Pin it somewhere visible** — your study desk, your notebook cover, or the fridge. A code you can see is a code you remember. And codes work best when you review them: every week, read your five lines and check you've kept each promise.

**Your future self will thank you.** These five lines are the difference between using AI as a superpower and being hurt by it.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 — The fake celebrity video.** A student saw a video of a famous person "saying" something rude. His mother helped him run F.A.C.T.: the account was unknown, no other site reported it, and the lips moved strangely. They found the real video — and the student learned never to believe thrilling clips instantly.

**Example 2 — The photo that fooled a class.** A viral "giant shark" photo went around a class chat. One student reverse-searched it and proved the picture was edited. Instead of laughing, the class applauded the detective — facts beat drama.

**Example 3 — The honest project.** A student proudly put the line "I used AI to brainstorm; I designed this myself" on his science poster. His teacher called it the best part of the display and pointed it out to other classes — proof that honesty wins.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Privacy: never give AI your real personal information.
- Honesty: be proud and truthful about how you used AI.
- Deepfakes exist: real-looking videos/photos can be fake — be a detective.
- Use F.A.C.T. to check anything surprising before believing or sharing.
- Kindness rule: never use AI to hurt, embarrass, or bully anyone.
