### Module 1 · What Is a Prompt?

A **prompt** is simply the message you type or say to an AI tool. It is your question, your instruction, or your request - the words you feed the machine. When you understand prompts, you control what AI does for you.

Think of AI like a new classmate who is super smart but needs you to be **clear**. If you say *'give me something about trains'*, your classmate can't tell if you want a story, facts about history, a diagram of a steam engine, or a poem about trains. So the classmate guesses - and you get a messy answer. The exact same thing happens with AI.

## Learning Objectives

- Define 'prompt' in your own words
- Explain why clear prompts give clear answers
- Give an example of a vague prompt and a clear prompt
- State the golden rule of prompts

## The Message You Send AI

- A prompt is your question, instruction, or request
- It is the exact words you feed the machine
- The same topic can become four different requests
```text
'Trains' could mean:
- a story about trains
- facts about the history of trains
- a diagram of a steam engine
- a poem about trains
```
- AI cannot read your mind - it needs you to say which one

## The Golden Rule of Prompts

### Quality in, quality out
- The quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question
### A clear prompt is like directions
- 'Go over there' is confusing; 'Turn left at the blue house' is clear
- Give AI landmarks it can follow

## Vague vs Clear in Action

- **Vague:** 'Tell me about the solar system'
- **Clear:** 'Explain the planets in 6 lines, one line each, in easy words'
### Try it with a friend
- Ask a friend a vague question, then a clear one
- Notice how different the answers are - that is exactly how AI behaves

## Key Takeaways

- A prompt is the message you type or say to the AI
- Clear prompts give clear answers - vague prompts give messy ones
- AI needs you to say exactly which one of many options you want
- The golden rule: the quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question
- Clear prompts are like directions with landmarks

### Module 2 · The REC Recipe: Role, Explain, Check

A truly great prompt has three parts. Remember the magic word **REC**. When you give the AI a role, explain exactly what you need, and check how the answer should look, you get exactly what you asked for - almost every time.

The REC recipe turns vague wishes into precise instructions. It is the same habit a chef uses: name the dish, list the ingredients, and say how to plate it. For AI, those three steps are Role, Explain, and Check.

## Learning Objectives

- Recite the three letters of REC and what each means
- Write a prompt that includes role, explain, and check
- Compare a bad prompt with a good REC prompt
- Rewrite a boring prompt using REC

## R - Role: Who Does AI Pretend to Be?

- Tell the AI who to be: *'Pretend you are a science teacher...'*
- A role changes the AI's tone, detail, and style of answer
- 'Friendly teacher' feels different from 'strict coach'
```text
Example role lines:
- 'Act as a friendly science teacher'
- 'Pretend you are my big sister'
- 'Be a patient maths coach'
```

## E - Explain: What Exactly Do You Need?

- Say exactly what you want done: *'...explain the water cycle in 5 simple steps...'*
- The more specific, the better the answer
- Name the topic, the form, and the amount
### Avoid one-word asks
- 'Explain the water cycle' is better than 'water'
- '5 simple steps' tells AI how much to write

## C - Check: How Should the Answer Look?

- Set the shape of the answer: length, format, language
- Example: *'...in 80 words, in easy English, for a Class 6 student'*
### REC in a full sentence
- 'Pretend you are a friendly science teacher. Explain the solar system in 6 simple lines - one line about the Sun, one line about each planet - in easy English. End with one fun fact'
- That one prompt has Role, Explain, AND Check - and it works beautifully

## Key Takeaways

- REC = Role, Explain, Check
- R: tell AI who to pretend to be
- E: explain exactly what you need, clearly and specifically
- C: check the answer's length, format, and language
- One REC sentence can turn a weak answer into a perfect one

### Module 3 · Good vs Bad Questions: Side by Side

Let's build a detective's eye for prompts. Compare each pair below and spot what makes the second one better. Once you see the pattern, you can improve any question you write.

All the good prompts share four qualities: they say **who** (context and role), **what you really need** (explain, plan, fix - not just 'answer'), **what NOT to do**, and they give the answer a **shape** (30 words, 3 points, a list). Learn these four, and every prompt you write improves.

## Learning Objectives

- Compare bad and good prompts and explain the difference
- Identify the four shared qualities of good prompts
- Rewrite a school question using all four qualities
- Recognise when a prompt is too vague to be helpful

## Pair 1: Homework Questions

### Bad
- 'What is the answer to question 4?'
### Good
- 'Explain how to solve this question step by step: [your question]. Don't give the final answer first - let me solve it and then check my work'
- The good version asks for a **method**, not just the result

## Pair 2: School Projects

### Bad
- 'Make a project on trees'
### Good
- 'I'm doing a Class 6 project on "Why Trees Matter". Plan it for me: an introduction, 3 main points, and a strong conclusion. Keep each point under 30 words'
- The good version gives context, structure, and length limits

## Pair 3: English Essays

### Bad
- 'Correct my essay'
### Good
- 'Here is my essay about my school. Fix my grammar and spelling, but don't change my ideas. After correcting, list the 3 mistakes I make most often'
- The good version says what to fix AND what to leave alone
```text
Four qualities of a good prompt:
1. Who (context and role)
2. What you really need
3. What NOT to do
4. A shape (30 words, 3 points)
```

## Spot the Difference

### Which pair is homework?
- Both pairs are homework - the point is HOW each asks
### The extra power of 'don't'
- Saying what NOT to do stops AI from going the wrong way
- Example: 'Don't give the final answer first - let me solve it'

## Key Takeaways

- Good prompts say WHO, WHAT, what NOT to do, and give a SHAPE
- Asking for a method beats asking for the answer
- Context like 'Class 6 project' helps AI match difficulty
- Telling AI what to avoid is as useful as telling it what to do
- Rewrite questions with these four qualities and watch answers improve

### Module 4 · Level-Up Tricks for Better Answers

Once you have mastered REC, add these extra tricks to become an AI superstar. Each one is small, but together they produce dramatically better answers. The professionals call one of these tricks 'few-shot prompting' - teaching by example.

None of these tricks are hard. They are just new habits: give an example, ask for a table, say what you don't want, chain your thinking, be polite, and add your own material. Try them one at a time and watch each answer improve.

## Learning Objectives

- Use examples to teach AI ('few-shot prompting')
- Ask for lists and tables for cleaner answers
- Tell AI what you do NOT want
- Chain steps and add your own material to prompts

## Teach by Example

- **Give an example:** *'Here is a haiku: [example]. Now write a haiku about rain'*
- Experts call this **few-shot prompting** - showing AI what 'good' looks like
### A tiny demo
- Show AI one poem, one table row, or one correct answer
- AI copies the style instead of guessing it

## Tables, DON'Ts, and Chains

- **Ask for a list or table:** *'Put the facts in a table with two columns: Planet | One cool fact'*
- Tables are easier to read and copy than long paragraphs
- **Say what you DON'T want:** *'No long words. No more than 5 sentences'*
- Removing the bad is as useful as adding the good
- **Chain your thinking:** *'First, check my maths answer. Then, if it is wrong, explain where I made my mistake'*
- Step-by-step instructions give longer, better answers

## Politeness and Your Own Material

### Be polite
- 'Please' and 'thank you' often keep AI models calm, clear, and helpful
### Add your own material
- Paste your own class notes or lines you wrote
- AI works best with YOUR content, not random internet text
```text
Trick checklist:
[] Give an example
[] Ask for a table or list
[] Say what I don't want
[] Chain the steps
[] Be polite
[] Add my own notes
```

## Key Takeaways

- Few-shot: show AI an example of the answer you want
- Tables and lists make answers clean and copy-able
- Telling AI what NOT to do prevents wrong answers
- Chaining steps breaks a request into clear mini-tasks
- Politeness and your own notes make prompts more effective

### Module 5 · The Ask → Improve → Re-ask Loop

Great AI users never accept the first answer. They **improve it** with a simple loop: ask, look at the answer, and re-ask with more detail. Every expert knows that the first version is almost never the best one - the fifth version often is.

Why does re-asking work? Because AI doesn't get tired or annoyed by your requests. You can push it and refine it as many times as you like. Each re-ask is like sharpening a pencil: your prompt edges get finer and the answer gets more useful.

## Learning Objectives

- Describe the three steps of the Ask → Improve → Re-ask loop
- Give three example re-asks that improve an answer
- Explain why the first answer is rarely the best
- Use the loop confidently on a real prompt

## The Three-Step Loop

- **Ask:** type your question
- **Look:** what's missing? What's confusing? What could be better?
- **Re-ask:** add more detail and ask again
```text
Example re-asks:
- 'That's good, but make it shorter'
- 'Add one example'
- 'Speak like you're explaining to my little sister'
- 'Change the ending to a question'
```

## Why the First Answer Is Never Enough

### The pencil-sharpening idea
- Each re-ask sharpens your prompt like a pencil point
- Edges get finer; the answer gets more useful
### AI never tires
- You can refine as many times as you like - that's a superpower
- Use it: the fifth version is often the best one

## Your First Loop Practice

- Ask an AI tool anything at all
- Re-ask twice: first 'make it more fun', then 'add one example'
- Watch the answer improve step by step
### Write it down
- Note the before and after - the difference shows your skill growing

## Key Takeaways

- Loop = Ask → Look → Re-ask with more detail
- The first answer is almost never the best - keep refining
- AI never gets tired: refine as many times as you want
- Each re-ask sharpens your prompt like a pencil
- Practise on any question: 'make it shorter', 'add one example'

### Module 6 · Common Mistakes to Avoid + Your 10-Minute Practice

Let's finish the mission by catching the classic mistakes - the ones almost every new user makes at least once. Once you can name them, you can avoid them.

Then comes your 10-minute practice session. In ten short minutes you will fix a bad prompt, test it live, and improve the answer. That practice turns everything you learned into a real skill.

## Learning Objectives

- Name five classic prompt mistakes
- Fix a bad prompt using REC
- Test a bad and a good prompt side by side
- Run the Ask → Improve → Re-ask loop twice

## The Classic Mistakes

- ❌ **Asking an unclear, one-word prompt** ('football') → tell AI *what about* football
- ❌ **Copying the answer straight into homework** → AI is for learning, remember the Three-Stop Rule
- ❌ **Giving up after one bad answer** → improve your prompt and try again
- ❌ **Thinking AI always knows the latest news** → some AI only knows what it was taught
- ❌ **Using long rambling prompts with no structure** → keep REC: role, explain, check

## Your 10-Minute Practice Session

### Fix-It (3 minutes)
- Take the bad prompt 'Tell me about the solar system' and rewrite it with REC
- Save it in your notebook
### Live Test (4 minutes)
- Ask AI the BAD prompt, write down what you get
- Then ask your GOOD prompt - compare: which is better and why?

## Improve It (3 minutes)

### The final 3 minutes
- Take the answer from your good prompt and run the loop twice
- For example: 'make it shorter', then 'add one fun fact for each planet'
- Write down how the answer changed
```text
You now talk like an AI pro:
- REC prompts, level-up tricks,
- and the improve loop at your fingertips.
```

## Key Takeaways

- Avoid one-word prompts, copying answers, and giving up early
- AI is not an automatic news source - double-check facts
- Keep prompts structured with REC
- Run a quick fix-test-improve session to build real skill
- Practice turns prompt knowledge into prompt power
