# Mission 2: Talking to AI — Asking Questions the Smart Way

## Learning Objectives
- Understand what a "prompt" is and why it matters
- Learn the three magic ingredients of a good prompt
- Write clear, specific, and polite prompts that get better answers
- Compare a bad prompt with a good prompt and see the difference
- Practise improving your own prompts

---

## What Is a Prompt?

A **prompt** is simply the message you type or say to an AI tool. It's your question, your instruction, or your request.

Think of AI like a new classmate who is super smart but needs you to be **clear**. If you say "give me something about trains," your new classmate doesn't know if you want:

- a story about trains,
- facts about the history of trains,
- a diagram of how a steam engine works, or
- a poem about trains.

So the classmate guesses — and you get a messy answer. The same happens with AI. **The quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question.**

## The Three Magic Ingredients

A great prompt has three parts. Remember the word **REC**:

1. **R — Role.** Who should the AI pretend to be? e.g., "Pretend you are a science teacher..."
2. **E — Explain.** What exactly do you need? Be specific. e.g., "...explain the water cycle in 5 simple steps..."
3. **C — Check.** What should the answer look like? Length, format, language. e.g., "...in 80 words, in easy English, for a Class 6 student."

### Bad Prompt ❌
> "Tell me about the solar system."

### Good Prompt ✅
> "Pretend you are a friendly science teacher. Explain the solar system in 6 simple lines — one line about the Sun, and one line about each planet — in easy English for a Class 7 student. End with one fun fact."

See the difference? The good prompt tells the AI **who** it is, **what** to do, **how much** to write, and **who** it is for.

## Level-Up Tips for Better Answers

Once you master REC, add these extra tricks:

- **Give an example.** "Here is an example of a haiku: ... Now write a haiku about rain." (This is called *few-shot prompting* — you teach by example.)
- **Ask for a list or table.** "Put the facts in a table with two columns: Planet | One cool fact."
- **Say what you DON'T want.** "No long words, no more than 5 sentences."
- **Chain your thinking.** "First, check my maths answer. Then, if it is wrong, explain where I made my mistake."
- **Polite counts!** "Please" and "thank you" help many AI models stay calm, clear, and helpful.

## The "Ask, Improve, Re-ask" Loop

Great AI users don't accept the first answer. They **improve it**:

1. **Ask** your question.
2. **Look** at the answer. What's missing? What's confusing?
3. **Re-ask** with more details: "That's good, but make it shorter" / "Add one example" / "Speak like you're explaining to my little sister."

This loop is the secret skill of every AI expert.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Asking an unclear, one-word prompt ("football"). → Tell AI what about football.
- ❌ Copying the answer straight into your homework. → AI is for *learning*, remember the Three-Stop Rule.
- ❌ Giving up after one bad answer. → Improve your prompt and try again.
- ❌ Thinking AI always knows the latest news. → Some AI only knows what it was taught; always double-check facts.

## Key Takeaways

- A prompt is your instruction to the AI — clear prompts give clear answers.
- Use **REC**: Role, Explain, Check.
- Add examples ("few-shot"), formats, and "what not to do" for even better results.
- Always **Ask → Improve → Re-ask** — never settle for the first answer.
- Never copy answers without understanding them.

## Practice Challenge

1. **Fix-It Mission:** Take the bad prompt *"Tell me about the solar system"* and rewrite it as a great prompt using REC. Save it in your notebook.
2. **Live Test:** Open an AI tool and ask it the **bad** prompt. Write down what you get. Then ask your **good** prompt. Compare — which is better and why?
3. **Improve It:** Take the answer from your good prompt and use the Ask-Improve-Re-ask loop twice (e.g., "make it shorter," "add one fun fact for each planet"). Write down how the answer changed.
4. **Share the trick:** Teach the REC trick to a classmate or family member and show them one example.
