### Module 1 · What Is a Prompt?
A **prompt** is simply the message you type or say to an AI tool. It's your question, your instruction, or your request — the words you feed the machine.

Think of AI like a new classmate who is super smart but needs you to be **clear**. If you say *"give me something about trains,"* your new classmate can't tell if you want:

- a story about trains,
- facts about the history of trains,
- a diagram of how a steam engine works, or
- a poem about trains.

So the classmate guesses — and you get a messy answer. The exact same thing happens with AI.

> **The golden rule of prompts: the quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question.**

A clear prompt is like giving someone directions with landmarks (turn left at the blue house) instead of just "go over there". When you give AI clear directions, it can do its best work for you.

**Try it now:** Ask a friend a vague question, then ask a clear question. Notice how the answers differ. That difference is exactly how AI works.

### Module 2 · The REC Recipe: Role, Explain, Check
A truly great prompt has three parts. Remember the magic word **REC**:

1. **R — Role.** Who should the AI pretend to be? e.g., *"Pretend you are a science teacher..."*
2. **E — Explain.** What exactly do you need? Be specific. e.g., *"...explain the water cycle in 5 simple steps..."*
3. **C — Check.** What should the answer look like? Length, format, language. e.g., *"...in 80 words, in easy English, for a Class 6 student."*

Let's see REC in action with a bad and a good prompt.

**Bad Prompt ❌**
> "Tell me about the solar system."

**Good Prompt ✅**
> "Pretend you are a friendly science teacher. Explain the solar system in 6 simple lines — one line about the Sun, and one line about each planet — in easy English for a Class 7 student. End with one fun fact."

See the difference? The good prompt tells the AI **who** it is (a science teacher), **what** to do (explain in 6 lines), **how much** to write (one line per planet), **who** it is for (Class 7), and **how** to end (fun fact). AI loves that kind of clarity.

**Try it now:** Take this boring prompt — *"Tell me about the water cycle"* — and rewrite it yourself using REC. Say it out loud before you move on.

### Module 3 · Good vs Bad Questions: Side by Side
Let's build a detective's eye for prompts. Compare each pair — can you spot what makes the second one better?

**Pair 1 — Homework**
- ❌ "What is the answer to question 4?"
- ✅ "Explain how to solve this question step by step: [your question]. Don't give the final answer first — let me solve it and then check my work."

**Pair 2 — School project**
- ❌ "Make a project on trees."
- ✅ "I'm doing a Class 6 project on 'Why Trees Matter'. Plan it for me: an introduction, 3 main points, and a strong conclusion. Keep each point under 30 words."

**Pair 3 — English**
- ❌ "Correct my essay."
- ✅ "Here is my essay about my school. Fix my grammar and spelling, but don't change my ideas. After correcting, list the 3 mistakes I make most often."

What do all the good prompts share?

- They say **who** (the context and role).
- They say **what you really need** (explain, plan, fix — not just "answer").
- They say **what NOT to do** ("don't give the final answer first", "don't change my ideas").
- They give a **shape** (30 words, 3 points, a list of mistakes).

**Try it now:** Write down one school question you ask often, then rewrite it so it shares all four of those qualities.

### Module 4 · Level-Up Tricks for Better Answers
Once you've mastered REC, add these extra tricks to become an AI superstar:

- **Give an example.** *"Here is a haiku: [example]. Now write a haiku about rain."* Teaching by example works brilliantly — experts call it *few-shot prompting*.
- **Ask for a list or table.** *"Put the facts in a table with two columns: Planet | One cool fact."* Tables are easier to read and copy.
- **Say what you DON'T want.** *"No long words. No more than 5 sentences."* Removing the bad is as useful as adding the good.
- **Chain your thinking.** *"First, check my maths answer. Then, if it is wrong, explain where I made my mistake."* Step-by-step instructions give longer, better answers.
- **Be polite.** *"Please"* and *"thank you"* help keep many AI models calm, clear, and helpful.
- **Add your own material.** Paste your own class notes or lines you wrote — AI works best with YOUR content, not random internet text.

**Try it now:** Take one prompt you wrote earlier and add TWO of these tricks. Notice how the answer changes.

### Module 5 · The Ask → Improve → Re-ask Loop
Great AI users never accept the first answer. They **improve it** with a simple loop:

1. **Ask** your question.
2. **Look** at the answer. What's missing? What's confusing? What could be better?
3. **Re-ask** with more detail:
   - "That's good, but make it shorter."
   - "Add one example."
   - "Speak like you're explaining to my little sister."
   - "Change the ending to a question."

This loop is the secret skill of every AI expert. The first answer is almost never the best one — the *fifth* version often is. Each re-ask is like sharpening a pencil: your prompt edges get finer and the answer gets more useful.

**Why this matters:** AI doesn't get tired or annoyed by your requests. You can push it and refine it as many times as you like. That's a superpower — so use it.

**Try it now:** Ask an AI tool anything. Then re-ask twice ("make it more fun" and "add one example"). Watch the answer improve step by step.

### Module 6 · Common Mistakes to Avoid + Your 10-Minute Practice
Let's finish the mission by catching the classic mistakes:

- ❌ **Asking an unclear, one-word prompt** ("football"). → Tell AI *what about* football.
- ❌ **Copying the answer straight into homework.** → AI is for *learning*, remember the Three-Stop Rule.
- ❌ **Giving up after one bad answer.** → Improve your prompt and try again.
- ❌ **Thinking AI always knows the latest news.** → Some AI only knows what it was taught; always double-check facts.
- ❌ **Using long, rambling prompts with no structure.** → Keep REC: role, explain, check.

Now your **10-minute practice session**:

1. **Fix-It (3 min):** Take the bad prompt *"Tell me about the solar system"* and rewrite it as a great prompt using REC. Save it in your notebook.
2. **Live Test (4 min):** Open an AI tool and ask it the **bad** prompt. Write down what you get. Then ask your **good** prompt. Compare — which is better and why?
3. **Improve It (3 min):** Take the answer from your good prompt and run the Ask → Improve → Re-ask loop twice (e.g., "make it shorter", "add one fun fact for each planet"). Write down how the answer changed.

Excellent — you're officially talking like an AI pro.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 — The school report.** A student prepared a history report by first asking AI to *outline* it (REC), then asking it to *explain two difficult words*, and finally re-asking to "make the conclusion punchy". Each step produced a better draft that she then rewrote in her own words.

**Example 2 — The science fair poster.** Before AI, a poster said "Photosynthesis - Important". With one REC prompt — *"Act as a science writer. Explain photosynthesis as 3 steps, each under 15 words, with one funny comparison"* — the classmate got a clear, printable summary to pair with her own drawing.

**Example 3 — The stuck math problem.** Instead of typing the question and getting the answer, a student asked *"Give me only the first step and a hint, then wait."* AI became a coach that trained her own thinking instead of doing it for her.

---

## Key Takeaways

- A prompt is your instruction to the AI — clear prompts give clear answers.
- Use **REC**: Role, Explain, Check.
- Add examples ("few-shot"), formats, and "what not to do" for even better results.
- Always **Ask → Improve → Re-ask** — never settle for the first answer.
- Never copy answers without understanding them.
