### Module 1 · The Golden Rule of Study AI

Your teachers give you homework so you can **learn** - and learning happens in *your* brain, not the AI's. So the golden rule is simple: **AI should help you learn faster - never do your homework for you.**

Compare two students. **Kopi** types his question, copies the first answer into his notebook, and calls it done. On exam day, he is lost - because the AI isn't in the exam hall to help him. **Maya** uses AI like a coach: she asks it to *explain* how to solve, does the solving herself, and then asks AI to *check* her work. She practises and gets stronger. Which one are you?

## Learning Objectives

- State the golden rule of studying with AI
- Explain why copy-pasting makes you weaker, not stronger
- Compare a copier and a coach in your own words
- Set your intent to be a Maya, not a Kopi

## The Golden Rule

### The rule in one line
- 'AI should help you learn faster - never do your homework for you'
### Why it matters
- Learning happens in YOUR brain, not the AI's
- Homework exists to train your thinking muscle

## Kopi vs Maya

### Kopi the copier
- Types a question, copies the first answer, calls it done
- On exam day he is lost - the AI is not in the exam hall
### Maya the coach-user
- Asks AI to *explain* how to solve
- Does the solving herself
- Asks AI to *check* her work - she practises and grows
```text
Copy-paste → weaker on exam day
Coach + practice → stronger every day
```

## Key Takeaways

- Golden rule: AI helps you learn faster - never does your homework
- Copy-pasting answers makes you weaker, not stronger

- Coaching (explain → do → check) beats copying every time

### Module 2 · The Copy-and-Explain Method

When you are stuck on homework, use the **copy-and-explain** method. The name can trick you - you do NOT copy the answer. You copy your *problem* into AI, and AI explains how to walk through it so YOU do the thinking.

This single study habit is called the best in the whole course. Why? Because when you struggle with a step and then find the answer, your brain remembers it far better than if you simply read it. Struggle is where learning happens.

## Learning Objectives

- Run the four steps of the copy-and-explain method
- Ask AI for the first step only, not the final answer
- Check your work with AI after solving yourself
- Explain why struggle creates stronger memory

## Step by Step

- **Step 1 - Type your problem clearly:** *'Solve this and explain each step: 3x + 5 = 20'*
- **Step 2 - Ask AI NOT to give the final answer first:** *'Don't tell me the answer. Give me the first step only, then wait'*
- **Step 3 - Solve the next step yourself** - this is where your brain grows
- **Step 4 - Ask AI to check:** *'I solved it and got x = 5. Is that correct? Show me where I might have gone wrong'*

## Why Struggle Creates Learning

### The memory effect
- Effort + success = a strong memory
- Reading an answer is weak; finding it yourself is strong
```text
Copy-and-explain in 4 lines:
1. Type the problem clearly
2. Ask for the first step only
3. Solve the rest yourself
4. Let AI check your work
```

## Try It on a Live Problem

- Take ONE question you are stuck on right now
- Run it through the four steps
### Notice the difference
- Feel how differently it feels compared to just copying the answer
- That feeling is your brain building a memory

## Key Takeaways

- Copy YOUR problem into AI - never the answer
- Ask for the first step only, then solve the rest yourself
- Use AI to check, not to provide, the solution
- Struggle plus success builds the strongest memories
- This is the single best study habit in the whole course

### Module 3 · Create Your Own Practice Tests

Tests feel scary when they surprise you. Practise with your **own questions** before the real thing. AI makes it easy to build a personal test in seconds - and self-testing is scientifically proven to boost memory more than rereading notes. That is called **active recall**.

The trick is to create the test without answers, take it yourself, then open the answer key and learn from each mistake. Let AI be your test-maker and marker, while you do the real work of thinking.

## Learning Objectives

- Design a practice test with a clear prompt
- Take the test before revealing the answer key
- Ask AI to mark your test and explain mistakes
- Plan a three-day revision week with a mini-test

## Build Your Personal Test

### The test prompt
- 'Create 8 practice questions on [topic] for Class [your class]. Three multiple choice, three short answer, two word problems. Difficulty: medium. Put them in a table. Do not give the answers yet'
### Then ask for the key
- 'Now show the answer key separately and explain the trick behind each answer'

## Why Self-Testing Wins

- Revising by *answering questions* beats re-reading notes every time
- This is **active recall** - your brain pulls the answer out by itself
- AI makes it super easy to create and mark your own tests
```text
Your week with AI:
Monday: make a 6-question mini-test
Wednesday: take it yourself
Friday: AI marks you + explains mistakes
```

## Before You Know It

- Exam day becomes 'another practice test'
- Because you've already done this exact job yourself
### The confidence effect
- Facing an exam you've pre-tested feels familiar, not frightening

## Key Takeaways

- Create your own tests with a clear prompt and no early answers
- Take the test before you open the answer key - that is active recall
- Ask AI to mark your work and explain each mistake
- Spread quizzes across the week: make, take, mark
- Pre-tested topics make exam day feel familiar and calm

## Practice Challenge

1. Build an 8-question test for your hardest subject this term.
2. Take it yourself BEFORE opening the answer key.
3. Ask AI to mark it and explain each mistake in one line.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is 'active recall'?

3. What makes exam day feel familiar?

- B) Trying something brand new

4. Why does testing beat re-reading?

### Module 4 · Summaries, Notes and Flashcards

Reading a huge chapter? Let AI turn it into study-friendly material. With one prompt you can get a summary in bullet points, an easy-English version of a hard paragraph, flashcards for memory practice, and a glossary of difficult words.

The top tip is simple: **paste your own class notes** into the prompt, not random internet text. AI works best with YOUR material - and your notes are exactly what your teacher taught you. A summary is a starting point, not the end: read it, then go back to the chapter and check you agree with every point.

## Learning Objectives

- Create a summary, plain-English version, flashcards, and glossary with AI
- Use your own class notes, not random internet text
- Check your summary against the original chapter
- Explain why summaries alone are not enough

## The Four Tools

- **Summary:** *'Summarise this chapter in 5 bullet points, each under 15 words'*
- **Plain English:** *'Explain this paragraph like I'm 10 years old'*
- **Flashcards:** *'Turn these notes into 10 flashcards. Front: question. Back: answer'*
- **Glossary:** *'Make a glossary of the 8 hardest words in this chapter with one-line meanings'*

## Use YOUR Material

- Paste your own class notes into the prompt
- AI works best with exactly what your teacher taught
### Top tip
- Avoid random internet text - it may not match your syllabus
```text
Flashcard recipe:
1. Copy your class notes
2. Ask AI for 10 flashcards (front: question)
3. Test yourself every morning
```

## Check Before You Trust

- A summary is a starting point, not the end
- Read your summary, then return to the chapter
- If a point surprises you, click back to the source
### Why this matters
- The act of checking is what makes summaries stick
- Blind trust in a summary is how wrong facts sneak in

## Key Takeaways

- AI can make summaries, plain-English versions, flashcards, and glossaries
- Always paste YOUR own class notes into the prompt
- A summary is a starting point - check it against the chapter
- Surprises in a summary mean you should check the source
- Flashcards reviewed daily move facts into long-term memory

### Module 5 · Your Daily Study-with-AI Checklist

Before you start homework, run this checklist. It trains you to use AI the safe, smart way - and after two weeks it becomes an automatic habit. This checklist is the difference between on-and-off AI use and genuine independent learning.

Each box reminds you of a principle you already know: read first, write clearly (REC), ask for explanation not answers, do the thinking yourself, check your work, and understand every line before moving on.

## Learning Objectives

- Run the six-item study checklist before homework
- Connect each checklist item to a lesson you learned
- Self-reflect when you skip a box and say why
- Turn the checklist into a daily habit in two weeks

## The Six-Box Checklist

- [ ] **I read the question myself** and tried to understand it first
- [ ] **I wrote my prompt clearly** (REC from Mission 2!)
- [ ] **I asked AI to explain**, not just answer
- [ ] **I did the real thinking and solving myself**
- [ ] **I asked AI to check my work** afterwards
- [ ] **I understood every line** before moving on

## Where to Keep It Visible

- Print it or copy it where you can see it every study session
- Your desk, your notebook cover, or the fridge - anywhere you look
### The two-week habit
- After two weeks it becomes automatic
- You become an independent learner with a powerful sidekick
```text
Habit formula:
Same list + same spot + every study
= automatic, safe AI use in 2 weeks
```

## The Honest Check

- For your next homework session, tick every box
### Skip one? Ask why
- If you skip a box, ask yourself why - that honest check keeps you on track
- A skipped box is not failure; it is feedback for tomorrow

## Key Takeaways

- Six boxes: read first, write clearly, ask for explanation, do thinking, check work, understand lines
- Keep the checklist visible in every study session
- Two weeks of use turns it into an automatic habit
- Skipping a box is honest feedback - ask yourself why
- Checklists make safe, smart AI use a daily reflex

### Module 6 · What NOT to Do (Signals of Cheating)

Know the danger signals so you never slide into them. Each one looks harmless in the moment - but together they turn AI help into AI cheating, and cheating steals your own learning.

The honest-user advantage is huge: when you do your own work and let AI coach you, you can explain *every line* of your homework to anyone - your teacher, your parents, or yourself. That confidence is worth more than any shortcut.

## Learning Objectives

- Name five signals that you are using AI as a cheat
- Explain why you are the final checker of every answer
- Describe the honest-user advantage
- Commit to explaining every line of your own work

## The Danger Signals

- ❌ **Copying AI text** into homework, essays, or projects without understanding
- ❌ **Getting an answer and never checking** if it's right
- ❌ **Using AI to write your whole assignment** at the last minute
- ❌ **Sharing personal details** (name, school, address, photos) with AI tools
- ❌ **Trusting every AI answer** - AI is smart but can still be wrong

## You Are the Final Checker

### The rule
- AI is smart but can be wrong - **you are the final checker**
- Never sign your name to work you did not verify
```text
The honest loop:
AI drafts → you understand → you verify → you submit
```
- Understanding every line is the difference between using AI and being used by it

## The Honest-User Advantage

- When you do your own work, you can explain *every line* to anyone
- Your teacher, your parents, or your classmate - no secrets to hide
### The confidence payoff
- That confidence is worth more than any shortcut
- Honest work is stress-free work - you know exactly what you did

## Key Takeaways

- Never copy AI text without understanding it
- Never trust an answer without checking it - you are the final checker
- Personal details stay OUT of AI tools
- AI can be confidently wrong - verify important answers
- Honest work earns the confidence to explain every line
