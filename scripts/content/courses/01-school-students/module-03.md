### Module 1 · The Golden Rule of Study AI
Your teachers give you homework so you can **learn** — and learning happens in *your* brain, not the AI's. So the golden rule is simple:

> **AI should help you learn faster — never do your homework for you.**

Compare two students:

- **Kopi** types his question, copies the first answer into his notebook, and calls it done. On exam day, he is lost — the AI isn't in the exam hall to help him.
- **Maya** uses AI like a coach: she asks it to *explain* how to solve, does the solving herself, and then asks AI to *check* her work. She practices and gets stronger.

Copy-pasting answers makes you **weaker**, not stronger. On exam day, the AI won't be there — so make sure the learning is in your head.

**Try it now:** Repeat the golden rule in your own words: "AI helps me learn... but AI never..." Finish both sentences.

### Module 2 · Way 1: The Copy-and-Explain Method
When you're stuck on homework, use the **copy-and-explain** method:

1. **Type your problem clearly.** e.g., *"Solve this and explain each step: 3x + 5 = 20."*
2. **Ask AI NOT to give the final answer first.** Try: *"Don't tell me the answer. Give me the first step only, then wait."*
3. **Solve the next step yourself** — this is where your brain grows.
4. **Ask AI to check:** *"I solved it and got x = 5. Is that correct? Show me where I might have gone wrong."*

This way **YOU do the thinking**, and AI is your checking partner. This is the single best study habit in this entire course.

Why it works: when you struggle with a step and then find the answer, your brain remembers it far better than if you simply read it. Struggle is where learning happens.

**Try it now:** Take ONE question you're stuck on right now, and run it through the four steps above. Notice how differently it feels compared to just copying the answer.

### Module 3 · Way 2: Your Own Practice Tests
Tests feel scary when they surprise you. Practise with your **own questions** before the real thing:

- **Prompt idea:** *"Create 8 practice questions on [topic] for Class [your class]. Three multiple choice, three short answer, two word problems. Difficulty: medium. Put them in a table. Do not give the answers yet."*
- **Then ask for the answer key:** *"Now show the answer key separately and explain the trick behind each answer."*

**Why this works:** Revising by *answering questions* (not just reading notes) is scientifically proven to make you remember more. It's called **active recall** — and AI makes it super easy.

**Plan your week with AI:**
- Make a 6-question mini-test on Monday.
- Test yourself on Wednesday.
- Ask AI to mark you and explain each mistake on Friday.

Before you know it, exam days become "another practice test" — because you've already done this exact job yourself.

### Module 4 · Way 3: Summaries, Notes and Flashcards
Reading a huge chapter? Let AI turn it into study-friendly material:

- **Summary:** *"Summarise this chapter in 5 bullet points, each under 15 words."*
- **Plain English:** *"Explain this paragraph like I'm 10 years old."*
- **Flashcards:** *"Turn these notes into 10 flashcards. Front: question. Back: answer."*
- **Glossary:** *"Make a glossary of the 8 hardest words in this chapter with one-line meanings."*

⚠️ **Top Tip:** Paste your *own class notes* into the prompt, not random internet text. AI works best with YOUR material — and your notes are exactly what your teacher taught you.

**Watch out:** A summary is a starting point, not the end. Read your summary, then go back to the chapter and check you agree with every point. If a point surprises you, click back to the source — that's the work that makes summaries stick.

### Module 5 · Your Daily Study-with-AI Checklist
Before you start homework, run this checklist — it trains you to use AI the safe, smart way:

- [ ] I read the question myself and tried to understand it first.
- [ ] I wrote my prompt clearly (REC from Mission 2!).
- [ ] I asked AI to *explain*, not just answer.
- [ ] I did the real thinking and solving myself.
- [ ] I asked AI to check my work afterwards.
- [ ] I understood every line before moving on.

Print or copy this checklist somewhere you can see it every study session. After two weeks, it becomes an automatic habit — and you'll be a genuinely independent learner who uses AI as a powerful sidekick.

**Try it now:** For your next homework session, tick every box. If you skip one, ask yourself *why* — that honest check keeps you on the right path.

### Module 6 · What NOT to Do (Signals of Cheating)
Know the danger signals so you never slide into them:

- ❌ Copying AI text into homework, essays, or projects without understanding.
- ❌ Getting an answer and never checking if it's right.
- ❌ Using AI to write your whole assignment at the last minute.
- ❌ Sharing personal details (name, school, address, photos) with AI tools.
- ❌ Trusting every AI answer — AI is smart but can still be wrong. **You are the final checker.**

**The honest-user advantage:** when you do your own work and let AI coach you, you can explain *every line* of your homework to anyone — your teacher, your parents, or yourself. That confidence is worth more than any shortcut.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 — The math turnaround.** A student who "hated" word problems used the first-step-only trick on every homework question for two weeks. Within a month she was solving them alone and asking AI only to check.

**Example 2 — The revision sprint.** Before a science test, a class made an 8-question practice test with AI on Sunday, shared answers on Tuesday, and helped each other fix mistakes. Everyone's score jumped.

**Example 3 — The flashcard fan.** A boy turned a 20-page history chapter into 12 flashcards in one evening using AI. He reviewed them on the bus each morning — and aced the chapter test without late-night cramming.

## Key Takeaways

- AI = personal study coach, not homework-answering machine.
- Use the **copy-and-explain method** to stay honest and learn more.
- Build your own **practice tests** to revise with active recall.
- Turn chapters into **summaries, flashcards, and glossaries**.
- After every AI session: check, understand, and do the thinking yourself.
