# Mission 4: AI Art & Creative Projects

## Learning Objectives

- Use AI to generate pictures, stories, and poems from a prompt
- Write detailed creative prompts that give beautiful results
- Edit and improve AI art to make it truly yours
- Understand that AI uses other artists' work and how to use it responsibly
- Create a finished creative project you can proudly share

---
### Module 1 · AI Can Create — But You Are the Artist
AI tools can create pictures, stories, poems, music, and even videos from just a prompt. This sounds like magic, but remember the golden rule:

> **AI is the paintbrush. YOU are the artist.**

The tool follows your instructions. Your imagination, your choices, and your edits make the project special. Two students with the same AI tool will make completely different projects — and both will be "theirs" in different ways.

Think of it like a cooking set: the AI gives you the ingredients, but you choose the recipe, adjust the taste, and decide how to serve it. The dish is yours because of the choices *you* made.

**Try it now:** Imagine the same prompt, "a robot in a garden", given to three friends. Describe three very different pictures they could each make. That's the "artist" part — *you*.

### Module 2 · Writing a Great Creative Prompt
Creative prompts love **extra detail**. Use this recipe for pictures:

1. **What is it?** *"A friendly robot watering flowers..."*
2. **Where and when?** *"...in a sunny school garden, in the morning..."*
3. **How does it look?** *"cartoon style, bright colours, soft shading..."*
4. **Mood?** *"peaceful and happy..."*

**Example:**
> "A friendly robot watering flowers in a colourful school garden, cartoon style, bright colours, soft shading, sunny morning, peaceful mood — square image."

And for **stories**, try:
> "Write a 3-paragraph story for a 12-year-old about a girl who finds a library that only opens at midnight. Include a surprise ending. Use fun and simple language."

Notice how the prompt gives the AI *what* (robot watering flowers), *where* (school garden), *style* (cartoon), *mood* (peaceful), and even a *shape* (square image). Every extra detail is a brush the AI uses.

**Try it now:** Invent your own picture idea and write it with all four parts. Keep it safe and kind — no scary or hurtful content.

### Module 3 · Editing & Iterating — Where Real Creativity Happens
The first result is rarely perfect. The best artists **iterate** — they improve step by step:

- "Make the robot hold a book instead of a watering can."
- "Change the colours to purple and blue."
- "Make the story 2 paragraphs longer and add a second character."
- "Give the ending a happy twist."

You can also **remix** ideas: take a poem AI wrote, change the words you don't like, add your own, and make it yours. AI is a first draft machine — the final draft is always human.

**The artist's checklist for every project:**
1. Get a first draft from AI.
2. Change at least **3 things** (details, colours, wording, characters).
3. Add **one thing AI can't** — your handwriting, your signature, a photo element, your real name on the cover.
4. Decide **why** the final version is better than draft one.

When you can say *"I decided this and that"*, you've created something that is genuinely yours.

**Try it now:** Take any AI draft you have and improve it 3 times. Save the "before" and "after" — you'll love the difference.

### Module 4 · Creative Project Ideas (Pick One)
Ready to build something real? Choose ONE of these, or invent your own:

1. **Storybook Page:** A 1-page story with an AI-generated illustration for your younger class.
2. **Poster:** An awareness poster (e.g., Save Water, Anti-Litter, Book Week) with an AI image + your own message.
3. **Poem & Sky:** A short poem about your town with an AI sky-scene background.
4. **Animation Flip Book:** AI images of a bouncing ball at 8 stages — then flip through them fast!
5. **Comic Strip:** A 4-panel comic using your own funny idea, with AI-drawn panels you edit.

For **stories and poems**, use the story prompt from earlier and then remix:
- "Change the ending."
- "Add a talking animal."
- "Make it rhyme."

The best projects combine AI's speed with your taste — a strong idea plus edits only you would make.

### Module 5 · Responsible Art Rules (Very Important)
Great creativity comes with great responsibility. Follow these rules always:

- **Be honest.** If a school project must be your own original work, ask your teacher. Never pass off AI art as 100% yours without telling.
- **No real people's faces** without permission — especially classmates, teachers, or family. Never make embarrassing or scary images of anyone.
- **Don't copy a real artist's style with someone else's name** to pretend you made it.
- **AI learns from real artists' work.** Support human artists too — enjoy their work, credit them, and never steal their final art.
- **No violence, hurtful, or rude content.** Keep your art kind and safe for everyone.
- **Check with a grown-up** before using any new creative site or app and before sharing your work online.

**The honest-credit line:** If your school allows AI help, add a small note to your project: *"I used AI to help draft ideas and images; I chose, edited, and finished this myself."* That line shows maturity — and teachers love it.

### Module 6 · Your Finished Creative Project (Show & Tell)
When your project is finished, do these three things:

1. **Add your signature.** A hand-written name, a small doodle, or your initials — the human touch no AI can copy.
2. **Prepare one smooth sentence** about how you made it, for example: *"AI gave me the first draft, but I changed the colours, added my message, and chose the final version."*
3. **Share it safely.** Show your class, family, or teacher — no sharing online without a grown-up's permission.

Then reflect in your notebook:
- What was the first draft like?
- What did you change and why?
- What is the ONE part that is 100% you?

That reflection is the proof of the artist inside you.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 — The Book Week poster.** A student made two posters: one where she accepted the first AI image, and one where she edited the colours, added her own headline, and pasted her hand-drawn tree on top. Guess which one won the class vote.

**Example 2 — The science storybook.** For a younger class, a girl used AI to draft a 1-page story about plant life, generated two illustrations, then redrew the main character herself so it looked like "her" story. The younger students couldn't stop reading it.

**Example 3 — The remixed poem.** A boy asked AI for a poem about his town, then replaced half the lines with his own, made it rhyme differently, and read it at the school assembly. Everyone asked who wrote it — and the honest answer made him prouder.

---

## Key Takeaways

- AI is the brush; you are the artist — your ideas + edits make it yours.
- Creative prompts need detail: what, where, look, and mood.
- Improve in steps ("iterating") until the result is something you love.
- Share AI-assisted work honestly and never with anyone's face without permission.
- Support and respect human artists; never present stolen work as yours.
