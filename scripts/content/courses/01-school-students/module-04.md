### Module 1 · AI Can Create — But You Are the Artist

AI tools can create pictures, stories, poems, music, and even videos from just a prompt. This sounds like magic, but remember the golden rule: **AI is the paintbrush. YOU are the artist.**

The tool follows your instructions. Your imagination, your choices, and your edits make the project special. Two students with the same AI tool will make completely different projects - and both will be 'theirs' in different ways. Think of it like a cooking set: AI gives the ingredients, but you choose the recipe and decide how to serve it.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the golden rule: AI is the brush, you are the artist
- Describe how two students make different projects from one tool
- Connect AI art to the cooking-set idea in your own words
- Recognise that your choices make the artwork yours

## The Golden Rule of AI Art

- AI generates from a description - but your imagination drives it
### The one-line rule
- 'AI is the paintbrush. YOU are the artist'
- The tool follows your instructions - not the other way

## Same Tool, Different Art

- Two students with the same AI tool make different projects
### Why?
- Different ideas, different prompts, different edits
- Both results are 'theirs' - because both chose how to use the tool

## The Cooking-Set Lesson

### Ingredients vs recipe
- AI gives you the ingredients (drafts and ideas)
- You choose the recipe: what to keep, change, and present
```text
Your artist thermostat:
1. Pick the idea
2. Guide the prompt
3. Edit the result
4. Present it your way
```
- The dish is yours because of the choices *you* made

## Key Takeaways

- AI is the paintbrush; you are the artist
- Your imagination, choices, and edits make the project special
- Two students with the same tool make two different artworks
- AI gives ingredients; you choose the recipe
- Your choices are what make the final piece truly yours

### Module 2 · Writing a Great Creative Prompt

Creative prompts love **extra detail**. For pictures, use a four-part recipe: what it is, where and when, how it looks, and the mood. For stories, tell AI who it is for and what must happen. The more detail you give, the closer the result matches your imagination.

Notice what a great prompt contains: *what* (a robot watering flowers), *where* (a school garden), *style* (cartoon), *mood* (peaceful), and even a *shape* (square image). Every extra detail is a brush the AI uses. Keep it safe and kind - no scary or hurtful content.

## Learning Objectives

- Write a four-part recipe for an AI picture
- Write a story prompt with audience and surprise element
- Identify what, where, style, mood, and shape in a sample prompt
- Keep creative prompts safe and kind

## The Picture Recipe

### Four parts
- **What is it?** *'A friendly robot watering flowers...'*
- **Where and when?** *'...in a sunny school garden, in the morning...'*
- **How does it look?** *'cartoon style, bright colours, soft shading...'*
- **Mood?** *'peaceful and happy...'*
### A full example
- 'A friendly robot watering flowers in a colourful school garden, cartoon style, bright colours, soft shading, sunny morning, peaceful mood - square image'

## The Story Recipe

### A great story prompt
- 'Write a 3-paragraph story for a 12-year-old about a girl who finds a library that only opens at midnight. Include a surprise ending. Use fun and simple language'
### It carries all the details
- Who it is for (12-year-old), what happens (a midnight library), and how it ends (surprise)

## Every Detail Is a Brush

### Map the prompt
- What: robot watering flowers
- Where: school garden
- Style: cartoon
- Mood: peaceful
- Shape: square image
```text
Try it now:
- Invent your own picture idea
- Write all four parts
- Keep it safe and kind
```

## Key Takeaways

- Creative prompts need detail: what, where, look, and mood
- Add a shape (round, square) for precise results
- Story prompts need audience, plot, and a finishing element
- Every extra detail guides the AI like a brush stroke
- Always keep creative prompts safe and kind

### Module 3 · Editing & Iterating — Where Real Creativity Happens

The first result is rarely perfect. The best artists **iterate** - they improve step by step. 'Make the robot hold a book instead of a watering can', 'change the colours to purple and blue', 'make the story longer with a second character'. Each small change moves you closer to your vision.

You can also **remix** ideas: take a poem AI wrote, change the words you don't like, add your own, and make it yours. AI is a first draft machine - the final draft is always human.

## Learning Objectives

- Define iteration and why it matters in art
- Give three example edit commands
- Explain the artist's four-step checklist
- State why the final draft is always human

## Iterate, Don't Settle

- First results are starting points, not finished art
### Example edits
- 'Make the robot hold a book instead of a watering can'
- 'Change the colours to purple and blue'
- 'Make the story 2 paragraphs longer and add a second character'
- 'Give the ending a happy twist'

## Remixing Makes It Yours

### The remix idea
- Take a poem AI wrote, change words you don't like, add yours
- AI is a first draft machine - the final draft is always human
```text
The artist's checklist for every project:
1. Get a first draft from AI
2. Change at least 3 things
3. Add one thing AI can't (handwriting, photo, your name)
4. Decide why the final version is better
```

## You Chose, Therefore It's Yours

- When you can say 'I decided this and that', you've created something genuinely yours
### Try it now
- Take any AI draft you have and improve it 3 times
- Save 'before' and 'after' - you'll love the difference

## Key Takeaways

- Iteration = improve step by step until it's right
- AI gives first drafts; you shape the final art
- Change at least 3 things and add one thing AI can't
- Remixing turns AI output into your creation
- 'I decided this' is proof the work is yours

### Module 5 · Responsible Art Rules (Very Important)

Great creativity comes with great responsibility. Follow these rules always. They keep you honest, kind, and safe - and they protect real human artists too.

The honest-credit line is your friend: *'I used AI to help draft ideas and images; I chose, edited, and finished this myself.'* That small line shows maturity - and teachers love it.

## Learning Objectives

- List the responsible art rules
- Explain why real people's faces need permission
- Write your own honest-credit line
- Describe how to support human artists while using AI

## Be Honest and Kind

- **Be honest.** If a school project must be your own original work, ask your teacher. Never pass off AI art as 100% yours without telling
- **No real people's faces** without permission - especially classmates, teachers, or family. Never make embarrassing or scary images of anyone
- **Don't copy a real artist's style with someone else's name** to pretend you made it

## Respect Human Artists

- AI learns from real artists' work - support human artists too
### How to support them
- Enjoy their work and credit them
- Never steal their final art
- Keep your AI art kind, safe, and respectful
- No violence, hurtful, or rude content - ever

## The Honest-Credit Line

### Write yours
- 'I used AI to help draft ideas and images; I chose, edited, and finished this myself'
- That line shows maturity - teachers love it
### Check with a grown-up
- Before using any new creative site or app
- Before sharing your work online
```text
Before you share, ask:
- Is it honest about AI help?
- Are all faces allowed?
- Is it kind and safe for everyone?
```

## Key Takeaways

- Never pass off AI art as 100% yours without telling
- No real people's faces without permission - ever
- Support human artists: credit them, don't steal their art
- Keep your art kind - no violence, hurt, or rudeness
- Add an honest-credit line and check with a grown-up before sharing

### Module 6 · Your Finished Creative Project (Show & Tell)

When your project is finished, do these three things: add your signature, prepare one smooth sentence about how you made it, and share it safely. Then reflect - because that reflection is the proof of the artist inside you.

A hand-written name, a small doodle, or your initials is the human touch no AI can copy. And one calm, honest sentence about your process shows you own your work completely.

## Learning Objectives

- Add a human signature to your creative project
- Prepare one smooth sentence about how you made it
- Share your work safely with the right audience
- Reflect on what changed and what is 100% you

## Add Your Signature

- A hand-written name, a small doodle, or your initials
- This is the human touch no AI can copy
### Why it matters
- Your signature closes the loop: you launched it, you finished it

## One Smooth Scene

### Prepare one sentence
- 'AI gave me the first draft, but I changed the colours, added my message, and chose the final version'
- Practise it until it sounds natural and proud

## Share and Reflect

### Share safely
- Show your class, family, or teacher
- No sharing online without a grown-up's permission
### Reflect in your notebook
- What was the first draft like?
- What did you change and why?
- What is the ONE part that is 100% you?
```text
The artist's proof:
Signature + honest sentence + reflection
= a finished, truly yours project
```

## Key Takeaways

- Add a human signature no AI can copy
- Prepare one smooth sentence about how you made it
- Share safely: class, family, teacher - grown-up approves online sharing
- Reflect on what changed and what is 100% you
- Reflection is the proof of the artist inside you
