- Build a clear presentation outline before touching slides

### Module 1 · Your School-Life Superpower
Presentations, competitions, anchoring events, and group projects now happen in almost every class. AI can help you prepare faster and shine brighter — if you use it as a **coach and co-writer**, not a crutch.

> **The plan: AI drafts, you edit, you practise, you perform.**

Follow this order every time and you'll never be caught staring at an empty slide again:

1. **AI drafts** the outline and rough words.
2. **You edit** everything so it sounds like you.
3. **You practise** out loud — the real secret.
4. **You perform** with confidence.

The best presenters in the world aren't the ones with the smartest AI — they're the ones with the most practice. AI just makes the preparation faster.

**Try it now:** Think of your next class presentation or event. Write one line: *"I will let AI draft, but I will ______."* (you decide the blank!).

### Module 2 · Outline First — Never Slides First
Rushing to make slides is a classic mistake. First, build a **structure**:

**Prompt example:**
> "I have to present on [topic] for Class [class] for 5 minutes. Create an outline: 1 catchy title, 3 main points, and a strong ending. Keep each point to one line."

Then turn the outline into **slides**:
> "Take my outline and make slide-by-slide suggestions. Each slide: a short heading and under 20 words of text. Leave space for pictures."

**Golden rule of slides:** less text, more meaning. If the audience has to read your slide, you've already lost them. Slides are *your* cue cards made visual.

A great slide shows:
- one big idea,
- a short heading,
- a picture that supports the point,
- almost no full sentences.

**Try it now:** Open any school project and reduce one slide's text to under 15 words. Feel how much cleaner it looks.

### Module 3 · Script for Speaking
Now turn the outline into words you'll actually say out loud. Speaking words are different from reading words — they sound like talking, not like a textbook.

**Prompt example:**
> "Turn my outline into a 5-minute anchor script. Start with a friendly welcome, use short sentences, and add *[pause]* and *[show picture]* notes. Sound natural, like talking to friends."

Then comes the most important step of all: **rewrite it in your own words.**

- Change phrases you'd never say.
- Add your own jokes, examples, or stories.
- Read it aloud — if it sounds like a robot, fix it.

You should sound like YOU, not like a robot. Your friends didn't come to hear AI; they came to hear *you*, with your voice and your life in the words.

**Try it now:** Take one AI script sentence and rewrite it the way *you* would actually talk to a friend.

### Module 4 · Practise the Right Way
Practice is the superpower behind every confident presenter. Here's the routine:

- Read your script **out loud** 3 times — in front of a mirror or a family member.
- Time yourself. 🕐 Shorter is usually better.
- Circle words you stumble on and practise those separately.
- Ask AI: *"Here's my 5-minute script. Give me 3 tips to make it more fun for my classmates."*
- Practise a friend's role: *"Pretend to be my classmate. Ask me 3 questions about my topic so I can practise answering."*

**The 3-times rule:** once to hear it, once to smooth it, once to own it. By the third read, your brain knows the material so well that nerves can't shake it.

**The confidence trick:** nervous feelings feel exactly like excitement in your body. So before you go on, say to yourself: *"I'm excited, not nervous."* It sounds too simple — but it works.

### Module 5 · AI for Events & Competitions
Beyond homework, AI shines at school events:

- **Quiz master helper:** *"Write 10 fun quiz questions about [topic] for a Class [class] quiz, with answers."*
- **Team games:** *"Give me 5 team-building game ideas for our class party, no props needed."*
- **Anchor scripts:** *"Write a short welcoming script for our school function. Mention our teachers, and use a warm, cheerful tone."*
- **Competition prep:** *"My debate topic is [topic]. Give me 3 strong points on my side and 3 the other side might use."*

**Debate bonus:** after you get your points, ask the AI to attack them — *"Now give me the strongest counter-argument against each of my points."* Preparing for the other side makes your own side bulletproof.

Whatever the event, the same law applies: AI generates ideas, you pick the best, you make them yours, and you practise.

### Module 6 · Making Simple AI-Assisted Videos + Your Confidence Checklist
Videos for projects don't need fancy cameras. Try this safe, simple recipe:

1. **Script:** Write 4–6 short sentences (AI helps you draft).
2. **Slides/Images:** Create visuals in slides or with an image tool.
3. **Voice notes:** Read your script and record your own voice (with a grown-up's help and permission).
4. **Put it together:** Simple editing apps can combine picture + voice + captions.

> **Privacy rule:** School videos should be shared only with your teacher/family. Never post your face, name, school, or address online without a parent's permission.

**The Confidence Checklist** — check every box before you present:

- [ ] I have a clear outline.
- [ ] My slides have short text and good pictures.
- [ ] My script is in my own words.
- [ ] I practised out loud at least 3 times.
- [ ] I timed myself.
- [ ] I have one fun opening line and one strong closing line.

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1 — The 10-minute talk.** A student had a 10-minute project talk the next day. Using the outline-first approach, AI built her structure in 2 minutes, she spent the evening rewriting it in her own words, practised once with her mother, and presented without reading a single slide.

**Example 2 — The event anchor.** For the school annual day, two students asked AI for a cheering welcome script, then rewrote it with inside jokes their classmates would love. They practised the timing with a stopwatch and ran the event smoothly — crowd laughing, zero panic.

**Example 3 — The science video.** A group that couldn't meet after school asked AI to split their presentation into a 5-slide storyboard, recorded their own voice notes on the bus, and stitched it into a 2-minute video with captions. Their teacher showed it to the whole class.

## Key Takeaways

- Outline first, slides second, practise always.
- Short sentences on slides; real conversations in your script.
- Rewrite AI drafts in your own words — sound like you.
- AI can generate quizzes, games, anchor scripts, and debate points.
- Keep school videos and personal details private and grown-up-approved.

1. **Create an Outline:** Pick an upcoming class topic (or invent one). Use AI to build a 3-point outline with a title and an ending.
