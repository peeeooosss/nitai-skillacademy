### Module 1 · Your School-Life Superpower

Presentations, competitions, anchoring events, and group projects now happen in almost every class. AI can help you prepare faster and shine brighter - if you use it as a **coach and co-writer**, not a crutch.

The plan is simple: **AI drafts, you edit, you practise, you perform.** Follow this order every time and you'll never be caught staring at an empty slide again. The best presenters in the world aren't the ones with the smartest AI - they're the ones with the most practice. AI just makes the preparation faster.

## Learning Objectives

- **1. AI drafts** the outline and rough words
- **2. You edit** everything so it sounds like you
- **3. You practise** out loud - the real secret
- **4. You perform** with confidence
```text
The order that never fails:
Draft → Edit → Practise → Perform
```

### Module 2 · Outline First — Never Slides First

Rushing to make slides is a classic mistake. First, build a **structure** - an outline with a title, main points, and a strong ending. Slides are decoration for that structure, not the starting point.

Then turn the outline into slides with slide-by-slide suggestions. And follow the golden rule of slides: **less text, more meaning.** If the audience has to read your slide, you've already lost them. Slides are your cue cards made visual.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a presentation outline before making slides
- Use an AI prompt to create the outline
- Turn the outline into slide suggestions
- Apply the golden rule: less text, more meaning

## Structure First

### The outline prompt
- 'I have to present on [topic] for Class [class] for 5 minutes. Create an outline: 1 catchy title, 3 main points, and a strong ending. Keep each point to one line'
- The outline is your presentation's skeleton

## From Outline to Slides

- 'Take my outline and make slide-by-slide suggestions. Each slide: a short heading and under 20 words of text. Leave space for pictures'
### The golden rule
- Less text, more meaning
- If the audience reads your slide, you've lost them

## What a Great Slide Shows

### The recipe
- One big idea
- A short heading
- A picture that supports the point
- Almost no full sentences
```text
Slide test:
Can the class understand it in 5 seconds?
If not, cut the text.
```

## Key Takeaways

- Outline first - slides second
- Use AI to build: title, 3 main points, strong ending
- Turn the outline into slide-by-slide suggestions
- Golden rule: less text, more meaning
- Great slides: one idea, short heading, supporting picture

## Practice Challenge

3. Reduce one old slide's text to under 15 words and feel the change.

### Module 3 · Script for Speaking

Now turn the outline into words you'll actually say out loud. Speaking words are different from reading words - they sound like talking, not like a textbook. Your script should sound like a friendly chat, not a school essay.

Then comes the most important step of all: **rewrite it in your own words.** Change phrases you'd never say, add your own jokes and stories, and read it aloud. If it sounds like a robot, fix it. You should sound like YOU.

## Learning Objectives

- Turn an outline into a speaking script with AI
- Add pause and visual notes to the script
- Rewrite the script in your own voice
- Test the script aloud for a natural sound

## The Speaking Script Prompt

- 'Turn my outline into a 5-minute anchor script. Start with a friendly welcome, use short sentences, and add *[pause]* and *[show picture]* notes. Sound natural, like talking to friends'
- Short sentences sound clear when spoken
- Notes like '[pause]' tell you when to slow down

## Rewrite It in Your Own Words

### Why it matters
- Your friends came to hear YOU, not AI
### The fixes
- Change phrases you'd never say
- Add your own jokes, examples, or stories
- Read it aloud - if it sounds like a robot, fix it

## The Sound-Alive Test

### Try it now
- Take one AI script sentence and rewrite it the way you'd talk to a friend
### The test
- Does it sound like you? Does it flow?
```text
Speaking rule:
If it sounds like a robot, it isn't your script yet.
```

## Key Takeaways

- Use AI to turn the outline into a speaking script
- Add '[pause]' and '[show picture]' notes for delivery
- Rewrite the script in your own words - always
- Add your own jokes, examples, and stories
- If it sounds like a robot, keep editing until it's you

## Practice Challenge

### Module 4 · Practise the Right Way

Practice is the superpower behind every confident presenter. A simple routine - read aloud, time yourself, circle hard words, ask AI for tips, and rehearse questions - will take you further than any fancy tool.

The **3-times rule**: once to hear it, once to smooth it, once to own it. By the third read, your brain knows the material so well that nerves can't shake it. And the confidence trick: nerves feel exactly like excitement - so say 'I'm excited, not nervous.' It works.

## Learning Objectives

- Run the practice routine: read aloud, time, circle, ask AI
- Apply the 3-times rule for delivery
- Use the confidence trick before going on stage
- Rehearse answers with AI playing your classmate

## The Practice Routine

- Read your script **out loud** 3 times - in front of a mirror or a family member
- Time yourself. Shorter is usually better
- Circle words you stumble on and practise those separately
- Ask AI: *'Here's my 5-minute script. Give me 3 tips to make it more fun for my classmates'*
- Practise a friend's role: *'Pretend to be my classmate. Ask me 3 questions about my topic so I can practise answering'*

## The 3-Times Rule

### The three reads
- Read 1: hear it
- Read 2: smooth it
- Read 3: own it
```text
After read 3, your brain knows it
so well that nerves can't shake it.
```

## The Confidence Trick

- Nervous feelings feel exactly like excitement in your body
### Before you go on, say
- 'I'm excited, not nervous'
- It sounds too simple - but it works

## Key Takeaways

- Practise: read aloud 3 times, time yourself, circle hard words
- Ask AI for fun tips and rehearse as-classmate questions
- The 3-times rule: hear, smooth, own
- Nerves and excitement feel the same - choose excitement
- Reps, not tools, build presentation confidence

## Practice Challenge

1. Read your script aloud 3 times and time yourself.
2. Ask AI for 3 tips to make it more fun.
3. Say 'I'm excited, not nervous' before your next practice run.

### Module 5 · AI for Events & Competitions

Beyond homework, AI shines at school events: quiz master helper, team games, anchor scripts, and competition prep. Whatever the event, the same law applies: AI generates ideas, you pick the best, you make them yours, and you practise.

There is a debate bonus too: after you get your points, ask the AI to attack them. Preparing for the other side makes your own side bulletproof.

## Learning Objectives

- Use AI for quizzes, team games, and anchor scripts
- Prepare strong debate points with AI
- Attack your own arguments to make them stronger
- Pick the best AI ideas and make them yours

## Event Help from AI

- **Quiz master helper:** *'Write 10 fun quiz questions about [topic] for a Class [class] quiz, with answers'*
- **Team games:** *'Give me 5 team-building game ideas for our class party, no props needed'*
- **Anchor scripts:** *'Write a short welcoming script for our school function. Mention our teachers, and use a warm, cheerful tone'*
- **Competition prep:** *'My debate topic is [topic]. Give me 3 strong points on my side and 3 the other side might use'*

## The Debate Bonus

- After getting your points, ask AI to attack them
### The counter-attack prompt
- 'Now give me the strongest counter-argument against each of my points'
- Preparing for the other side makes your own side bulletproof

## The Event Law

### The same law every time
- AI generates ideas → you pick the best → you make them yours → you practise
```text
Event equation:
AI ideas + your choices + practice
= a confident, shining performance
```

## Key Takeaways

- AI writes quizzes, games, anchor scripts, and debate points
- Use specific prompts with topic, class, and tone
- Ask AI to attack your debate points to make them stronger
- Same law everywhere: generate, pick, make yours, practise
- The other side's arguments are your training ground

## Practice Challenge

1. Write 5 quiz questions for your favourite topic with AI.
2. Get 3 debate points (or event ideas) and then attack them with AI.
3. Practise one anchor welcome line aloud.

**Example Quiz**

**Answer:** A — Quizzes, games, scripts, and debates are all AI-ready.

2. The debate bonus is ________.
- A) asking AI to attack your own points

- D) hiding arguments

**Answer:** A — Counter-attacks reveal weak spots - strengthening your side.

3. The event law is ________.
- A) generate, pick, make yours, practise
- B) copy and submit
- C) skip and hope
- D) ask a friend to go

**Answer:** A — AI drafts ideas; you choose and own them.

4. 'Mention our teachers, and use a warm tone' controls ________.

### Module 6 · Simple AI-Assisted Videos + Your Confidence Checklist

Videos for projects don't need fancy cameras. Try this safe, simple recipe: write a short script, create visuals, record your own voice notes, and stitch it together in a simple editor. AI helps with the script - you supply the ideas and the voice.

Keep a privacy rule close: school videos should be shared only with your teacher or family - and never post your face, name, school, or address online without a parent's permission. Then finish with the confidence checklist before you present.

## Learning Objectives

- Describe the four-step video recipe
- State the privacy rule for school videos
- Use AI for script drafts without giving your voice to it
- Complete the confidence checklist before presenting

## The Four-Step Video Recipe

- **1. Script:** write 4-6 short sentences (AI helps you draft)
- **2. Slides/Images:** create visuals in slides or with an image tool
- **3. Voice notes:** read your script and record your own voice (with a grown-up's help)
- **4. Put it together:** simple editing apps combine picture + voice + captions

## The Privacy Rule

- School videos are shared only with your teacher or family
### Never post online without permission
- Your face, name, school, or address
- Ask a parent before any online share
```text
Video checklist:
[] Short script
[] Visuals made
[] My own voice
[] Edited simply
[] Private sharing only
```

## The Confidence Checklist

- [ ] I have a clear outline
- [ ] My slides have short text and good pictures
- [ ] My script is in my own words
- [ ] I practised out loud at least 3 times
- [ ] I timed myself
- [ ] I have one fun opening line and one strong closing line

## Key Takeaways

- Video recipe: script, visuals, your voice, simple editing
- Keep school videos private: teacher or family only
- Never post your face, name, school, or address without permission
- Record your own voice - that part is yours
- Complete the confidence checklist before every presentation

## Practice Challenge

3. Tick every box in the confidence checklist before you present.
