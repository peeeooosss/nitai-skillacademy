### Module 2 · What 'Artificial Intelligence' Really Means

Here is the big idea in plain words. **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** is when a *machine* does something that normally needs a human brain - like understanding your voice, answering a question, recognising your face, or playing a game against you.

Break the two words apart. **'Artificial'** means *made by humans* - not natural, but built by us. **'Intelligence'** means *the ability to think and learn*. Put them together and you get: a machine that is taught to think and learn, like a very fast helper with a huge memory.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain 'artificial' and 'intelligence' in your own words
- State one sentence that defines AI simply
- Describe why AI is training and practice, not magic
- Give an example of AI improving with practice

## Two Words, One Idea

- **Artificial** = made by people. Not grown or natural - built on purpose
- **Intelligence** = the ability to think and learn from experience
### Definition in one line
- 'AI is a machine that is taught to think and learn'
- It is like a very fast helper with a huge memory

## AI Is Training and Practice

```text
AI learns like a student:
1. See many examples (training data)
2. Guess the answer each time
3. Get corrected when wrong
4. Slowly improve with practice
5. Master one job after many tries
```
- Just like you practise maths sums or swimming, AI practises with examples
- The more examples it sees, the smarter it gets - but only at that one job

## Not Magic - Practice

### AI is not born smart
- No computer comes out of the box knowing cats, cricket, or songs
- Someone trains it first using lots of example data
### Practise in your own words

## Key Takeaways

- AI = machines that can think, learn, and do brain-jobs
- 'Artificial' means made by humans; 'intelligence' means thinking and learning
- AI learns from examples and practice - it is not magic
- The more examples an AI sees, the better it gets at one job
- You can explain AI in one friendly sentence to anyone

### Module 3 · How AI Learns: Training, Practice, Examples

So how does a machine learn to recognise a cat or understand 'what's your name?' It follows a simple recipe. First, feed it lots of examples. To learn what a cat is, the AI is shown *thousands* of cat photos - fat cats, small cats, cartoons, and real ones.

Then it guesses, again and again. Each time it sees a photo, the AI guesses 'cat' or 'not cat'. When it is wrong, it makes a tiny adjustment. After millions of guesses and corrections, the AI learns to spot a cat almost as well as you can. That is why people say AI is **trained**, not programmed.

## Learning Objectives

- List the three steps of training AI
- Explain why AI is trained, not programmed like a calculator
- Describe the difference between fixed rules and learned patterns
- Use the 'trained like a student' idea in conversation

## The Training Recipe

- **Step 1: Feed it examples.** Thousands of cat photos - different sizes, colours, and angles
- **Step 2: Guess and adjust.** AI guesses 'cat' or 'not cat'; a wrong guess makes a tiny fix
- **Step 3: Repeat millions of times.** Slowly the guesses become almost always right
### Analogies that click
- Like practising free throws in basketball - each miss teaches your hands
- Like learning spellings - you see the word, guess, check, and remember

## Trained, Not Programmed

```text
Calculator: fixed rules, same answer every time.
AI: learns patterns, handles NEW situations it never saw.
```
- A normal calculator follows rules you type in - *you* are the intelligence
- AI *practises* and improves - very much like a student
- That is what makes AI feel smart: it can handle new situations using what it learned

## The Computer vs Smart Idea

### Calculator
- Told exactly what to do every time
- Cannot handle anything outside its rules
### Learned AI
- Learns patterns from examples
- Can face a brand-new case and make a smart guess

## Key Takeaways

- Training = feed examples, guess, adjust, repeat millions of times
- AI is trained, not programmed with fixed rules
- A calculator follows fixed rules; AI learns patterns
- AI can handle new situations because it generalises from examples
- The more practice data, the better the patterns - just like you

### Module 4 · Strong AI vs Narrow AI: Specialists, Not Superheroes

There are two famous words you should know: **Narrow AI** and **Strong AI**. They help you understand what today's machines can and cannot do - and why you are still the most important person in the room.

Almost every AI today is narrow: a translator translates, a face-recogniser recognises faces, a chess AI plays chess, and a chat assistant chats. Strong (or general) AI - a machine that can do *many* different jobs like a human - **does not exist yet**. Knowing the difference keeps your expectations realistic and your decisions smart.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish narrow AI from strong AI with examples
- Explain why today's AI is a specialist, not a superhero
- Choose the right AI tool for the right job
- See yourself as the only 'general intelligence' in the room

## Two Kinds of AI, Two Meanings

- **Narrow AI** - very good at ONE job. Translator, face-recogniser, chess player, spam filter
- **Strong (general) AI** - can do many different jobs like a human being. Not built yet
```text
Today's reality:
Translator App: translates only
Chess Engine: plays chess only
Image Maker: makes pictures only
Each is narrow - super good at one thing.
```

## Specialists, Not Superheroes

- Your translator app can't also play chess
- The chess AI can't write your essay
- Each tool is a specialist with one superpower
### Why knowing this matters
- Use the right tool for the right job (translator for languages, image maker for pictures)
- Don't expect one AI to be perfect at everything

## You Are the Generalist

- You - a human - can translate, draw, play chess, write essays, and sing
- No single AI can match all of that - only you can
### The power of being human
- Strong AI does not exist yet, so YOU are the only one who does everything
- That puts you in charge of which tool to use for which job

## Key Takeaways

- Narrow AI = super good at one specific job
- Strong AI = many jobs like a human - not invented yet
- Today's tools are specialists; use each for its best job
- You are the only general thinker in the room - you decide the tool
- Realistic expectations = smarter, safer AI use

### Module 6 · Your First AI Safety Rules

Before you finish this mission, learn the **Three-Stop Rule** for using AI. Before you ask AI anything, stop and check. This little rhythm - Plan, Read twice, Check - is the foundation of safe, smart AI use.

And never forget the two golden rules of your whole journey: **AI is your helper, not your replacement** (AI should never do YOUR work for YOU), and **ask a grown-up** before using a new app or sharing details. You will practise these rules in every single mission from now on.

## Learning Objectives

- Recite the Three-Stop Rule with confidence
- Explain why you are the boss of AI, not the other way around
- Know when to involve a parent or teacher
- Apply the rules to your first AI sessions

## The Three-Stop Rule

- **Stop 1 - Plan:** Think about what you really need. Write your question clearly
- **Stop 2 - Read twice:** Never copy answers without understanding them. Ask AI to *explain*, not just give the answer
- **Stop 3 - Check:** Does the answer make sense? Is it correct? Ask your teacher or parents if unsure
```text
The rhythm: Plan → Read twice → Check
Use it before every AI question.
```

## Two Golden Rules

### Golden rule 1: AI is your helper
- You are the boss; AI is the assistant
- AI should never do YOUR work for YOU
### Golden rule 2: Ask a grown-up
- Tell a parent or teacher before using a new app or camera tool
- Especially anything that asks for your details

## Practising the Rules

- Each mission from now on will ask you to use the Three-Stop Rule
### A quick self-check
- Did I plan my question clearly?
- Did I read the answer twice and understand it?
- Did I check the answer with a trusted source?

## Key Takeaways

- Three-Stop Rule: Plan → Read twice → Check
- AI is your helper, never your replacement
- You are the boss; AI is the assistant
- Ask a grown-up before new apps or sharing details
- Practise these rules in every mission from now on
