### Module 2 · What "Artificial Intelligence" Really Means
Here is the big idea in plain words:

> Artificial Intelligence (AI) is when a **machine does something that normally needs a human brain** — like understanding your voice, answering a question, recognising your face, or playing a game against you.

- **"Artificial"** means *made by humans* — not natural, but built by us.
- **"Intelligence"** means *the ability to think and learn*.

So **AI is a machine that is taught to think and learn** — a bit like a very fast helper with a huge memory.

Now the most important part: **AI is not magic.** AI is **training and practice**. Just like you practise maths sums or swimming, AI learns from lots of examples. The more examples it sees, the smarter it gets — but only at that one job.

**Try it now:** Tell a friend or family member in one sentence: *"AI is a machine that learns from practice — just like I do."*

### Module 3 · How AI Learns: Training, Practice, Examples
So how does a machine learn to recognise a cat or understand "what's your name?" Let's see the recipe:

1. **Feed it lots of examples.** To learn what a cat is, the AI is shown *thousands* of cat photos — fat cats, small cats, cartoons, real ones.
2. **It guesses, again and again.** Each time it sees a photo, the AI guesses "cat" or "not cat". If it's wrong, it makes a tiny adjustment.
3. **Little by little, it gets better.** After millions of guesses and corrections, the AI learns to spot a cat almost as well as you can.

That's why people say AI is **trained**, not programmed like a normal computer. A normal calculator follows fixed rules you type in. AI *practises* and improves — very much like a student!

**The difference between a computer and "something smart":**
- A **calculator** is told exactly what to do every time — *you* are the intelligence.
- **AI** learns patterns from examples, so it can handle things it has never seen before — where the rule was never written down.

That is what makes AI feel "smart": it can **handle new situations** by using what it learned.

### Module 4 · Strong AI vs Narrow AI: Specialists, Not Superheroes
There are two famous words you should know:

- **Narrow AI** — AI that is very good at **one** job. Almost every AI today is narrow: a translator translates, a face-recogniser recognises faces, a chess AI plays chess.
- **Strong (or general) AI** — AI that can do *many* different jobs like a human being. This **does not exist yet**.

So today's AI is a **specialist**, not an all-rounder superhero. Your translator app can't also play chess — and the chess AI can't write your essay.

Why does this matter for you? Because:
- You should use the **right tool for the right job** (a translator for languages, an image maker for pictures).
- You shouldn't expect one AI to be perfect at everything.
- And *you* are the only one in the room who can do *everything* — that puts you in charge.

**Example Quiz**

**Answer:** B — Today's AI is narrow — super good at one specific job.

**Answer:** A — It is brilliant at chess and only chess — that is narrow AI.

### Module 6 · Your First AI Safety Rules
Before you finish this mission, learn the **Three-Stop Rule** for using AI. Before you ask AI anything, stop and check:

1. **Stop — Plan first.** Think about what you really need. Write your question clearly.
2. **Stop — Read twice.** Never copy answers without understanding them. Ask AI to *explain*, not just give the answer.
3. **Stop — Check.** Does the answer make sense? Is it correct? Ask your teacher or parents if you are unsure.

And two golden rules for your whole journey:

- **AI is your helper, not your replacement.** You are the boss; AI is the assistant. AI should never do YOUR work for YOU.
- **Ask a grown-up.** If you use a new app, camera, or online tool, tell a parent or teacher first, especially for anything that asks for your details.

You will practise these rules in every single mission from now on — they are the foundation of safe, smart AI use.
