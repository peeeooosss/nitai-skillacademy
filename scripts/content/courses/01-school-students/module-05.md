### Module 1 · Science + AI = Curiosity Accelerator

Science is all about asking 'why' and 'what if.' AI is brilliant at helping you explore - it explains ideas, suggests experiments, and never gets tired of your questions. But the best science habit is: **predict first, then ask.**

Before you ask AI anything, answer yourself: *'What do I THINK happens?'* Then compare with AI's answer and with your textbook. Your prediction + AI's answer + your teacher = deep learning. The moment you compare is the moment you truly understand.

## Learning Objectives

- Ask science 'why' and 'what if' questions to AI
- Predict your own answer before asking
- Run the science loop: predict, ask, compare, confirm
- Explain a science idea back to check understanding

## Curiosity Questions for AI

- 'Why is the sky blue?' → AI explains... but explain it *back* to check you understood
- 'What happens if I put salt on ice?' → an instant experiment idea!
- 'Explain photosynthesis like a football match'
- 'Give me 3 safe experiments to show what air pressure is'

## The Science Loop

- **1. Predict** - guess what will happen
- **2. Ask** - get AI's explanation
- **3. Compare** - your guess vs AI vs your textbook
- **4. Confirm** - check with your teacher when in doubt
```text
The loop in action:
Predict → Ask → Compare → Confirm
Your teacher is the final check.
```

## Predict First, Ask Later

- Prediction engages your brain before AI does the talking
### The deep-learning moment
- The moment you compare your guess with AI is the moment you understand
- Prediction makes you an active scientist, not a passive reader

## Key Takeaways

- AI turns curiosity into instant explanations and experiment ideas
- Predict your answer before you ask AI - every time
- The science loop: predict, ask, compare, confirm
- Explain AI's answer back to check you understood
- You, your textbook, and your teacher are the final judges

### Module 2 · The Explain → Test → Teach Method

This is a famous way to truly learn science - and AI makes it easy. Scientists say that explaining a topic to someone else is the fastest way to master it yourself. The method works whether the 'someone' is your little brother, your grandmother, or even a rubber duck!

Three simple steps: Explain so it's simple, Test yourself with quiz questions, then Teach it out loud. If you stumble while teaching, that stumble shows exactly what to revisit.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain a topic simply with AI's help
- Test yourself with AI-generated quiz questions
- Teach the topic aloud to a real listener
- Use a stumble while teaching as a signal of what to revisit

## Step 1: Explain

- Ask AI to explain a topic simply
- Example: *'Explain how magnets work in 4 steps'*
- Check the steps make sense to you

## Step 2: Test

- Ask AI for 3 quiz questions on the topic
- Answer them WITHOUT looking back
- Let AI mark you and explain mistakes
```text
The full cycle:
Explain → Test → Teach
Teach it to someone you know
Stumbling = exactly what to revisit
```

## Step 3: Teach

- Explain the topic out loud to a parent or sibling
### The mastery test
- If you can teach it, you know it
- A stumble while teaching marks the spot to revisit
- Even a rubber duck listener works!

## Key Takeaways

- Explain → Test → Teach is a proven learning method
- AI explains simply, quizzes you, and marks your answers
- Teach someone aloud - if you can teach it, you know it
- A stumble while teaching shows exactly what to revisit
- The method works with any listener - even a rubber duck

### Module 3 · Math + AI = Personal Coach

Math can feel frustrating - but AI turns it into a private coach. There are four ways to ask for help, from lightest to deepest: first step only, break it down, find the mistake, or make it real. Choose the level you need, and never ask for the full answer first.

There is also a 2-Second Rule (really 2 minutes): always try the problem yourself for at least 2 minutes before asking AI. That struggle is where your brain grows. AI is your coach, not your calculator slave - the more you try first, the stronger you get.

## Learning Objectives

- Use the four math help prompts with AI
- Always try a problem yourself before asking AI
- Explain the 2-minute rule and why struggle grows you
- Use 'make it real' to turn sums into real-life stories

## The Four Ways

- **Way 1 - First Step Only:** *'I'm stuck on this problem: [problem]. Do not solve it. Give me only the FIRST step and a small hint, then wait for my answer'*
- **Way 2 - Break It Down:** *'Solve this in 4 small steps, explaining each step in one easy sentence: [problem]'*
- **Way 3 - Find the Mistake:** *'Here's my working: [your working]. My answer is 24. Show me where I made a mistake - don't solve it fully'*
- **Way 4 - Make It Real:** *'Give me a real-life word problem version of this sum: [sum]'*

## The 2-Minute Rule

- Always try the problem yourself for at least 2 minutes before asking AI
### Why struggle matters
- Struggle is where your brain grows
- AI is a coach, not a calculator slave
- The more you try first, the stronger you get

## Coach, Not Calculator

### What a coach does
- Gives hints and steps so YOU learn the method
- Never rushes you - waits for your answer
### What a slave does
- Just gives the answer - you learn nothing
```text
In 2 minutes: try hard.
Then: pick a help level, not a full answer.
```

## Key Takeaways

- Four help levels: first step, break down, find mistake, make it real
- The 2-minute rule: try yourself before asking
- Struggle grows your brain - AI should coach, not replace
- Choosing a help level keeps you the thinker
- 'Make it real' turns boring sums into stories

## Practice Challenge

1. Pick one math problem you're stuck on and try it for 2 minutes first.
2. Use the 'first step only' method and solve the rest yourself.
3. Ask AI to turn one sum into a real-life story problem.

**Example Quiz**

- A) Try it yourself for 2 minutes

- D) Ask a friend to solve it

**Answer:** A — Struggle first - the 2-minute rule grows your brain.

2. Which way gives you only a hint, not the answer?

### Module 5 · Safety First + Checking Your Sources

Before you try any experiment, lock in these rules. Curiosity is great - safety is greater. Never run experiments you find online without asking a parent or teacher first, and keep home experiments to safe kitchen items.

And remember: AI is smart but can be wrong, especially with very new science. The detective habit is: every AI answer is a *suggestion* to check, not a fact to copy. You, your textbook, and your teacher are the final judges.

## Learning Objectives

- State the safety rules for home experiments
- Use safe kitchen items only for experiments
- Check AI answers against your textbook
- Ask AI to re-check itself when in doubt

## The Safety Rules

- **Never** run experiments you find online without asking a parent or teacher first
- Home experiments should use **safe kitchen items** (water, salt, vinegar, baking soda, paper) - no flames, no electricity, no chemicals
- If AI suggests an experiment, **check with a grown-up** before touching anything
- When in doubt: ask. Curiosity is great - safety is greater

## Check Your Sources

- Compare AI answers with your **textbook**
- **Ask 'Are you sure? Check your answer'** - many AI tools will re-check themselves
- Follow up with *'What is the evidence for that?'*
- When in doubt, ask your **teacher** - your best and most trustworthy source

## The Detective Habit

### Every AI answer is a suggestion
- Not a fact to copy - a lead to verify
```text
The verification chain:
AI answer → your textbook → your teacher
Only then, a checked fact.
```
- This habit protects you from confident mistakes

## Key Takeaways

- Ask a grown-up before any experiment - online or new
- Home experiments: safe kitchen items only
- Check AI answers against your textbook
- Ask AI 'are you sure?' and 'what is the evidence?'
- Every AI answer is a suggestion to verify, not a fact to copy

## Practice Challenge

2. Check one AI science answer against your textbook.
3. Ask AI 'are you sure? check your answer' and note what changed.

### Module 6 · Build a Mini Science Project with AI

Now let's make something real - a small, safe science project you can show off. With a grown-up's approval, pick a safe experiment like making a balloon inflate with baking soda + vinegar, watching salt melt ice, or making a paper boat that floats.

The magic is the sequence: predict first, do the experiment, compare and explain, then document it in a mini report. You will have done the full scientist's loop - predict, test, compare, confirm, explain - with AI as your lab partner.

## Learning Objectives

- Pick a safe, grown-up-approved experiment
- Predict the result before testing
- Compare your observation with AI's explanation
- Write a mini project report with five sections

## Pick and Predict

- **Pick a safe experiment** (with a grown-up's approval)
- Examples: baking soda + vinegar balloon, salt melting ice, a paper boat that floats
- **Predict first:** ask AI *'What do you predict will happen and why?'*
- Write YOUR prediction next to it

## Do, Compare, Explain

- **Do the experiment carefully** with your grown-up helper
- **Compare and explain:** ask AI *'Explain what we just saw in 3 simple sentences'*
- Does it match what happened?
- If not - wonder why. That wondering is real science

## Document It like a Scientist

### Your mini report
- Question
- My prediction
- What happened
- Why
- AI's explanation
- Add photos or drawings
```text
You now think like a real scientist:
Predict → Test → Compare → Confirm → Explain
AI is your lab partner, not your boss.
```

## Key Takeaways

- Pick a safe, grown-up-approved experiment
- Predict before you test - write your guess down
- Compare what happened with AI's explanation
- Document: question, prediction, result, why, AI's explanation
- You completed the scientist's loop with AI as your lab partner

## Practice Challenge

2. Predict the result, test it, and write what actually happened.
3. Write your mini report: question, prediction, outcome, why, AI's explanation.
