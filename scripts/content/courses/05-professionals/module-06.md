# Mission 6: Measuring and Presenting Your AI Gains

## Learning Objectives

- Measure AI gains honestly: time, quality, rework, emotional drain
- Use before-and-after data instead of feelings
- Build a one-page evidence story for your manager
- Present AI wins as role-level gains, not tool talk
- Convert one win into a next-90-days plan

---
### Module 1 · Measure, Don't Feel

Feelings are not evidence. 'I feel faster' does not survive a manager review; a time-and-quality number does.

For each task you automate, capture:

- **Before** time - how long it took last month.
- **After** time - how long it takes now.
- **Redo rate** - how often it came back for fixes.
- **Quality** - error rate or review rounds.

Even two weeks of honest numbers beat adjectives.

**Example Quiz**

1. Why does 'I feel faster' fail as evidence?
- A) Feelings are not measurable data
- B) It is too modest
- C) It is not English
- D) Managers dislike numbers

**Answer:** A — Managers act on numbers - feelings alone do not survive review.

2. What should you measure per automated task?
- A) Before/after time, redo rate, and quality
- B) Number of tools used
- C) Friends helped
- D) Keystrokes typed

**Answer:** A — Before time, after time, redo rate, and quality are honest measures.

3. How long of honest numbers is enough?
- A) About two weeks
- B) One day
- C) One hour
- D) A full year

**Answer:** A — Even two weeks of real numbers beat adjectives.

### Module 2 · The Before-and-After Table

Build a simple table - it is the clearest report you can give:

| Task | Before | After | Savings/wk | Redo rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Status email | 30 min | 5 min | ~2 hrs | down |
| Meeting summary | 45 min | 10 min | ~3 hrs | same |

Keep the table to your top 3-5 automated tasks. Managers read tables faster than paragraphs.

**Honesty rule:** include any added time (checking, fixing) in the 'after' number.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the clearest format to report time savings?
- A) A before-and-after table
- B) A long narrative
- C) A pie chart of tools
- D) A WhatsApp status

**Answer:** A — Tables are instantly scannable - managers read them faster than paragraphs.

2. What should the 'after' number include?
- A) Checking and fixing time too
- B) Only typing time
- C) Only the coffee break
- D) Zero

**Answer:** A — Include checking and fixing in 'after' - that is the honest number.

3. How many automated tasks should you highlight?
- A) Your top 3-5
- B) Every task ever done
- C) Only one
- D) None

**Answer:** A — A focused top 3-5 table is persuasive; a dump-room is noise.

### Module 3 · Quality and Consistency Gains

Time is only half the story. Measure quality too:

- **Review rounds** - how many edits before sign-off (was: 3, now: 1).
- **Format consistency** - same template every time (fewer exceptions).
- **Error catch** - AI catches typos and missing numbers before you send.
- **Availability** - templates are ready even when you are out.

'Better and faster' will always beat 'just faster'. Present both.

**Example Quiz**

1. Which quality metric matters?
- A) Review rounds before sign-off
- B) Total words
- C) Colour count
- D) Coffee consumed

**Answer:** A — Fewer review rounds mean cleaner, more consistent work.

2. Why present quality with time?
- A) 'Better and faster' beats 'just faster'
- B) Quality is unmeasurable
- C) Time is irrelevant
- D) Managers prefer adjectives

**Answer:** A — Quality plus speed is the full story - improvements to both impress.

3. What is an example AI quality win?
- A) Catching typos and missing numbers before sending
- B) Longer messages
- C) More meetings
- D) No changes at all

**Answer:** A — AI catching errors before send is a measurable quality gain.

### Module 4 · The One-Page Evidence Story

One page, four sections - that is all you need:

1. **Before** - what your day looked like (tasks and time).
2. **What changed** - the 3-5 workflows you run now.
3. **After** - measured savings in time and quality.
4. **Next 90 days** - the bigger things this unlocks.

Make AI the supporting character; **make your role's output the star**. Managers fund role outcomes, not tool enthusiasm.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the four sections of the one-page story?
- A) Before, what changed, after, next 90 days
- B) Intro, bio, contact, thanks
- C) Tools, prices, sales, goals
- D) Only numbers

**Answer:** A — Before, what changed, after, and next 90 days tell a complete story.

2. Who or what should be the star of the story?
- A) Your role's output
- B) The AI tool
- C) The subscription
- D) The vendor

**Answer:** A — Fund the role outcome, not the tool - make output the star.

3. Why close with 'next 90 days'?
- A) It turns a past win into a forward plan
- B) It fills space
- C) It is required by law
- D) It hides the numbers

**Answer:** A — A next-90-days plan converts the win into trajectory.

### Module 5 · Presenting It to Your Manager

When you present, follow the manager's language:

- **Lead with the measurable** - 'I found ~5 hours a week in [task].'
- **Frame as role-level** - 'Our [reports/emails] now go out with fewer review rounds.'
- **Be ready with the exit ramp** - 'I will not send anything automated without my review.'
- **Ask for the next authority** - 'I would like to extend this to [bigger task].'

The anti-hesitation point: remind that everything is checked by you; you are accelerating, not outsourcing decision-making.

**Example Quiz**

1. How should you lead your AI pitch to a manager?
- A) With a measurable saving and role-level framing
- B) With a list of tools
- C) With jargon
- D) With a demo only

**Answer:** A — Lead with measurable, role-level impact - not tool names.

2. What reassurance keeps managers comfortable?
- A) Everything is checked by you before it leaves
- B) Nothing is checked
- C) It is cheap
- D) It is a secret

**Answer:** A — Explicitly confirm human review - it removes the fear of blind AI output.

3. What should you ask for after sharing results?
- A) Authority to extend to a bigger task
- B) A promotion immediately
- C) A raise guaranteed
- D) More vacation

**Answer:** A — Ask for the next authority - it converts a win into a larger role.

---
### Module 6 · From One Win to the Next 90 Days

Close the loop by planning growth:

1. **Consolidate** - polish the workflows that are already saving time.
2. **Expand** - apply the pattern to the next 2-3 repetitive tasks.
3. **Share** - teach one colleague your best prompt (it multiplies the win).
4. **Upgrade** - add one harder task (reports, decks) to your toolkit.

Your goal is not to become 'the AI person' - it is to become the professional whose output is measurably better and faster. That is a promotion argument on its own.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does 'consolidate' mean in the plan?
- A) Polish workflows already saving time
- B) Buy new hardware
- C) Hire someone
- D) Delete everything

**Answer:** A — Consolidation locks in what already works before expanding.

2. How does teaching a colleague multiply the win?
- A) The pattern produces value beyond you
- B) It takes your credit
- C) It confuses the team
- D) It doubles your hours

**Answer:** A — A colleague using your prompt doubles real organisational impact.

3. What is the true goal of this journey?
- A) Output measurably better and faster - a promotion argument
- B) Owning every AI tool
- C) Automating everything instantly
- D) Working less than everyone

**Answer:** A — Measurable, role-level improvement - that is the sustainable win.

## Real-World Examples

**The honest time table —** A coordinator tracked status emails at 30 minutes, summaries at 45. After two weeks of reuse, the table showed about 5 hours a week back, and the redo rate on emails dropped - enough evidence to try the next workflow.
**The quality win —** A team reported fewer review rounds on its monthly report after prompt templates: sign-off went from 3 rounds to 1, because the structure arrived correct each time.
**The manager pitch —** One analyst led with 'I found about 5 hours a week in reporting and our decks now pass in one review round', reminded that everything is checked by them, then asked to expand to the quarterly budget narrative - and got the go-ahead.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Measure time, redo rate, and quality - not feelings.
- A before-and-after table is the clearest evidence.
- 'Better and faster' always beats 'just faster'.
- One page: before, what changed, after, next 90 days.
- Lead with measurable, role-level impact - not tool names.
- Everything leaves your desk checked; say so explicitly.
- Consolidate, expand, share, upgrade - next 90 days.
