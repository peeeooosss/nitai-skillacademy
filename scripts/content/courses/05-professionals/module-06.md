# Mission 6: Measuring and Presenting Your AI Gains

---
### Module 1 · Measure, Don't Feel

Feelings are not evidence. 'I feel faster' does not survive a manager review, but a time-and-quality number does. The first skill in presenting your AI gains is honestly measuring them - before data, before impact, before the story.

For each task you automate, capture a small set of numbers. Even two weeks of honest numbers beat adjectives in any conversation with a manager.

## Learning Objectives

- Capture before-and-after time for each automated task
- Track redo rate and quality alongside time
- Explain why numbers, not feelings, survive a manager review

## What to Capture

### Before time
- How long the task took last month, measured, not guessed
### After time
- How long it takes now, including checking and fixing
### Redo rate
- How often the output came back for fixes
### Quality
- Error rate, review rounds, or rework count

## Measuring, Not Guessing

- A reasonable estimate you can defend beats a perfect measurement you never take
- Log the times for a real week before and after, not a single lucky day
- Include every minute - especially the checking time you add on

## Enough Data in Two Weeks

- Two weeks of real numbers beats adjectives in any review
- A short runway keeps the honest habit alive instead of stalling on perfection
- Numbers also tell you when a workflow is actually failing - adjust early

## Key Takeaways

- Managers act on numbers - feelings alone do not survive review
- Capture before time, after time, redo rate, and quality
- Include checking and fixing time in the after number
- Even two weeks of real numbers beat adjectives
- Numbers reveal failing workflows early, so you adjust fast

## Practice Challenge

1. Pick three tasks you automated and note their before times from last month.
2. Measure their after times this week, including checking.
3. Add a redo-rate column and start the two-week log.

### Module 2 · The Before-and-After Table

The clearest report you can give anyone is a simple before-and-after table. Managers read tables faster than paragraphs, and numbers in rows carry more weight than the same numbers buried in prose.

Keep the table to your top three-to-five automated tasks, and include everything honestly - any time you added in checking or fixing belongs in the 'after' column.

## Learning Objectives

- Build a persuasive before-and-after table of your top automated tasks
- Include checking and fixing time in the after column
- Keep the table focused on three-to-five tasks, not a data dump

## The Table Shape

```text
Task           | Before | After | Savings/wk | Redo rate
Status email   | 30 min | 5 min | ~2 hrs     | down
Meeting summary| 45 min | 10 min| ~3 hrs     | same
```
- One row per task, one honest number per cell
- The savings column does the persuading - keep it defensible

## The Honesty Rule

- Include added checking and fixing time in the 'after' number - that is the honest cost
- Inflated savings collapse under one question from a manager
- Honest numbers also protect you from over-claiming to yourself

## Focused Beats Comprehensive

- Your top three-to-five tasks tell the story; a twenty-row table buries it
- Choose tasks with the largest, most defensible savings
- If a row is weak, leave it out rather than stretch the claim

## Key Takeaways

- A before-and-after table is the clearest report for time savings
- Include checking and fixing time in the after number - that is honest
- Top three-to-five tasks persuade; a data dump buries the story
- Tables are scannable - managers read them faster than paragraphs
- Defensible savings survive questions; inflated ones collapse

## Practice Challenge

1. Build the before-and-after table for the three tasks you are logging.
2. Recalculate every 'after' figure to include checking time.
3. Trim the table to your most defensible rows for the story you want to tell.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is the clearest format to report time savings?
- A) A before-and-after table

- C) A pie chart of tools

**Answer:** A — Tables are instantly scannable - managers read them faster than paragraphs.

2. What should the 'after' number include?
- A) Checking and fixing time too
- B) Only typing time
- C) Only the coffee break
- D) Zero

**Answer:** A — Include checking and fixing in 'after' - that is the honest number.

3. How many automated tasks should you highlight?
- A) Your top 3-5
- B) Every task ever done
- C) Only one
- D) None

**Answer:** A — A focused top three-to-five table is persuasive; a dump is noise.

### Module 3 · Quality and Consistency Gains

Time is only half the story. If all you did was produce the same work faster, the gain is modest. The stronger case is that the work is also better and more consistent - fewer review rounds, fewer errors, the same template every time.

'Better and faster' will always beat 'just faster'. Present both, with quality measured in review rounds, consistency, error catches, and availability.

## Learning Objectives

- Measure quality alongside time: review rounds, consistency, error catches
- Explain why 'better and faster' beats 'just faster'
- Turn quality improvements into the second half of your evidence story

## The Quality Metrics

### Review rounds
- How many edits before sign-off - was three, now one
### Format consistency
- Same template every time - fewer exceptions and surprises
### Error catches
- AI catching typos and missing numbers before you send
### Availability
- Templates ready even when you are out - work can proceed

## Why Quality Matters as Much as Time

- A manager funds faster good work, not faster sloppy work
- Fewer review rounds means your output frees up other people's time too
- Consistency makes your work predictable - and predictable earns trust

## Collecting the Evidence

- Note review rounds per document before and after your prompts
- Keep a small log of errors the AI caught before send
- These are everyday events - capture them as they happen, not at report time

## Key Takeaways

- Review rounds before sign-off are a hard quality metric
- 'Better and faster' beats 'just faster' every time
- Consistency - same template, fewer exceptions - builds trust
- Error catches before send are measurable quality wins
- Templates keep work available even when you are not around

## Practice Challenge

1. Log review rounds on your three most common outputs for two weeks.
2. Start a small log of errors AI caught before they were sent.
3. Compare consistency-of-format before and after your templates.

**Example Quiz**

1. Which quality metric matters?
- A) Review rounds before sign-off
- B) Total words
- C) Colour count
- D) Coffee consumed

**Answer:** A — Fewer review rounds mean cleaner, more consistent work.

2. Why present quality with time?
- A) 'Better and faster' beats 'just faster'
- B) Quality is unmeasurable
- C) Time is irrelevant
- D) Managers prefer adjectives

**Answer:** A — Quality plus speed is the full story - improvements to both impress.

3. What is an example AI quality win?
- A) Catching typos and missing numbers before sending
- B) Longer messages
- C) More meetings
- D) No changes at all

**Answer:** A — AI catching errors before send is a measurable quality gain.

### Module 4 · The One-Page Evidence Story

Everything you have measured comes together in one document: a one-page evidence story. Four sections - before, what changed, after, and next 90 days - are all you need. Anything longer gets skimmed; one page gets read.

The composition rule that matters most: make your role's output the star, and AI the supporting character. Managers fund role outcomes, not tool enthusiasm.

## Learning Objectives

- Structure your evidence as before, what changed, after, and next 90 days
- Put your role's output at the centre of the story
- Write a story that converts a past win into a forward plan

## The Four Sections

### 1. Before
- What your day looked like - the tasks and the time they took
### 2. What changed
- The three-to-five workflows you run now
### 3. After
- Measured savings in time and quality, from your table
### 4. Next 90 days
- The bigger things this unlocks if you continue

## Make Output the Star

- Frame the story around outcomes: reports out on time, decks passing in one review
- Mention AI only as the means - the role's output is the argument
- 'Our reports now go out with fewer review rounds' beats 'I use a new tool'

## The Forward Plan

- Closing with next 90 days turns a past win into trajectory
- Propose the next one or two tasks you will tackle with the same pattern
- It converts a report into a request - and requests get decisions

## Key Takeaways

- One page, four sections: before, what changed, after, and next 90 days
- Make your role's output the star and AI the supporting character
- Managers fund role outcomes, not tool enthusiasm
- Close with next 90 days to turn a past win into a forward plan
- A report that asks for something gets a decision; a report that does not gets a folder

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your one-page story in the four-section structure.
2. Re-read it and count how many lines mention your output versus the tool.
3. Rewrite the closing so it proposes the next 90 days explicitly.

**Example Quiz**

- A) Before, what changed, after, next 90 days
- B) Intro, bio, contact, thanks
- C) Tools, prices, sales, goals
- D) Only numbers

**Answer:** A — Before, what changed, after, and next 90 days tell a complete story.

2. Who or what should be the star of the story?
- A) Your role's output
- B) The AI tool
- C) The subscription
- D) The vendor

**Answer:** A — Fund the role outcome, not the tool - make output the star.

3. Why close with 'next 90 days'?
- A) It turns a past win into a forward plan
- B) It fills space
- C) It is required by law
- D) It hides the numbers

**Answer:** A — A next-90-days plan converts the win into trajectory.

### Module 5 · Presenting It to Your Manager

The document gets you credibility; the conversation gets you the next authority. When you present, speak your manager's language - measurable, role-level, and reassuring. Tool names are interesting; hours found and review rounds cut are persuasive.

The one reassurance to have ready: everything is checked by you before it leaves. You are accelerating work, not outsourcing judgement.

## Learning Objectives

- Lead the AI conversation with a measurable, role-level result
- Reassure managers that everything is reviewed before it leaves
- Close with a specific request for the next level of authority

## The Manager's Language

### Lead with the measurable
- 'I found about 5 hours a week in [task]' - the number first, always
### Frame as role-level
- 'Our reports now go out with fewer review rounds' - team output, not personal usage
### Ask for the next authority
- 'I would like to extend this to [bigger task]' - a forward request, not a lecture

## The Exit Ramp

- Be ready with the human-review line: 'I will not send anything automated without my review'
- This removes the unspoken fear of blind AI output escaping your desk
- Managers relax once they hear oversight, not tools

## Handling the 'Is It Safe?' Question

- Answer with your safety rules in order: no sensitive data, no auto-sending, policy checked
- Mention verification: numbers are checked against your systems every time
- Keep answers brief and concrete - a walkthrough of one workflow beats a slide of claims

## Key Takeaways

- Lead with a measurable saving and role-level framing, not tool names
- Explicitly confirm human review - it removes the fear of blind AI output
- Close with a request for the next level of authority
- Answer safety questions with your three rules, briefly and concretely
- One workflow walkthrough beats a slide deck of claims

## Practice Challenge

1. Draft your opening line: the measurable saving in one sentence.
2. Script your exit ramp - the human-review reassurance.
3. Write your closing ask for the next authority on a bigger task.

**Example Quiz**

1. How should you lead your AI pitch to a manager?
- A) With a measurable saving and role-level framing
- B) With a list of tools
- C) With jargon
- D) With a demo only

**Answer:** A — Lead with measurable, role-level impact - not tool names.

2. What reassurance keeps managers comfortable?
- A) Everything is checked by you before it leaves
- B) Nothing is checked
- C) It is cheap
- D) It is a secret

**Answer:** A — Explicitly confirm human review - it removes the fear of blind AI output.

3. What should you ask for after sharing results?
- A) Authority to extend to a bigger task
- B) A promotion immediately
- C) A raise guaranteed
- D) More vacation

**Answer:** A — Ask for the next authority - it converts a win into a larger role.

### Module 6 · From One Win to the Next 90 Days

The course ends where your own growth plan begins. One working workflow is a proof point; the next 90 days turn it into a pattern. The loop is simple: consolidate what works, expand to the next tasks, share with a colleague, and upgrade to one harder task.

Your goal is not to become 'the AI person'. It is to become the professional whose output is measurably better and faster - and that is a promotion argument on its own.

## Learning Objectives

- Plan a 90-day AI growth loop for your own role
- Explain how sharing a prompt multiplies the win
- Frame the true goal as role-level improvement, not tool ownership

## The 90-Day Loop

### Consolidate
- Polish the workflows that are already saving time - lock in the gains
### Expand
- Apply the same pattern to the next two or three repetitive tasks
### Share
- Teach one colleague your best prompt - it doubles the organisational win
### Upgrade
- Add one harder task - reports, decks, analyses - to your toolkit

## Why Sharing Multiplies the Win

- A colleague using your prompt produces value beyond your desk
- Teaching forces you to make the prompt clear enough to survive contact
- Organisational wins are the currency of promotion conversations

## The True Goal

- Not owning every tool or automating everything instantly - but measurably better output
- Each 90 days adds one or two solid workflows, not twenty fragile ones
- Sustainable growth beats a burst of enthusiasm

## Key Takeaways

- Consolidate what works before expanding to anything new
- Expand the pattern to the next two or three repetitive tasks
- Teaching a colleague doubles the real organisational impact
- Upgrade to one harder task each cycle to grow the toolkit
- Measurable, role-level improvement is the sustainable promotion argument

## Practice Challenge

1. Write your own 90-day plan with the four loop steps.
2. Name one colleague you will teach and one prompt you will share.
3. Pick the one harder task you will add to your toolkit next.

**Example Quiz**

2. How does teaching a colleague multiply the win?
- A) The pattern produces value beyond you

- D) It doubles your hours

**Answer:** A — A colleague using your prompt doubles real organisational impact.

3. What is the true goal of this journey?
- A) Output measurably better and faster - a promotion argument
- B) Owning every AI tool
- C) Automating everything instantly
- D) Working less than everyone

**Answer:** A — Measurable, role-level improvement - that is the sustainable win.
