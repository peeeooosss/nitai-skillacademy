# Mission 2: Automating Repetitive Tasks with AI

## Learning Objectives

- Identify the repetitive tasks in your role that AI can offload safely
- Learn the safety and privacy rules before automating anything
- Build reusable automation prompts for emails, summaries, and formatting
- Deliver your first automated workflow to a real work need
- Know which tasks should never be handed to AI automatically

---
### Module 1 · What 'Automate' Really Means Here

Automating with AI does not mean full robot workflows. For most professionals it means: **the AI does the drafting, formatting, and summarising; you do the deciding, checking, and sending.**

A realistic first step is a prompt you reuse: the same kind of email, summary, or update, produced from simple inputs each time.

**Keep expectations right:** you are removing the tedious 80%, not your judgement. The professional who automates best is the one who still checks everything.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does 'automate' mean for most professionals?
- A) Replace your entire job with robots
- B) AI drafts, formats, summarises - you decide and send
- C) Delete all manual work
- D) Let AI approve your emails

**Answer:** B — AI removes the tedious 80% - you keep deciding, checking, and sending.

2. What is a realistic first automation step?
- A) A prompt you reuse for the same kind of task
- B) A full software pipeline
- C) Buying expensive software
- D) Hiring a developer

**Answer:** A — Start with a reusable prompt for one recurring task type.

3. Who still checks the automated work?
- A) No one, it is automatic
- B) The professional
- C) The software vendor
- D) The AI itself

**Answer:** B — The professional always checks automated output - judgement stays human.

### Module 2 · Safety Rules Before You Automate

Before any automation, apply three safety rules:

1. **No sensitive data** - do not paste passwords, client financial details, medical records, or internal secrets into an AI tool unless your company has approved the tool for it.
2. **No auto-sending** - automate drafting, never blind sending. You approve before anything leaves.
3. **Check the policy** - your organisation may have rules about which tools are allowed; a two-minute check protects you.

These rules are not fear - they are what a professional does by default.

**Example Quiz**

1. Which inputs should you NOT paste into an AI tool?
- A) Bank account numbers and internal secrets
- B) Public company details
- C) Your name
- D) A product name

**Answer:** A — Passwords, financial details, medical records, and internal secrets stay out of AI tools.

2. What is the 'no auto-sending' rule?
- A) AI must never draft anything
- B) AI drafts, but a human approves before it is sent
- C) Send everything from a work account
- D) Always reply in under a minute

**Answer:** B — Automate drafting, never blind sending - you approve everything.

3. What should you check before using a tool at work?
- A) Your company's AI tool policy
- B) The tool's stock price
- C) Your colleagues' chat history
- D) Nothing

**Answer:** A — A quick check of company AI policy protects you and your work.

### Module 3 · Automating Routine Emails

Routine emails are the easiest first automation. Give the AI a reusable pattern with three inputs:

'You are a [role]. Given this situation, write an email to [audience]. Include [points]. Tone: [tone].'

Each time you write the same email type, you change only the situation and points - the structure stays.

**Try it now:** take a status-update email you send often and write its reusable prompt. That single prompt can save an hour a week.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the three inputs for the email prompt?
- A) Date, time, signature
- B) Situation, audience, points to include
- C) Subject, attachment, recipient
- D) Tool, model, version

**Answer:** B — Situation + audience + points lets one prompt serve many similar emails.

2. What changes each time you reuse the email prompt?
- A) The situation and points
- B) The tone and language
- C) The tool and account
- D) Everything

**Answer:** A — You swap in the new situation and points; the structure stays.

3. What is a realistic saving from one reused email prompt?
- A) About an hour a week
- B) Eight hours a day
- C) Nothing
- D) A full salary

**Answer:** A — One well-reused prompt for a frequent email type can save about an hour a week.

### Module 4 · Automating Summaries and Notes

Summarising meetings, articles, and reports is where AI shines. The frame:

'Summarise the attached notes into: 1) the key decisions, 2) the open actions with owners and dates, 3) what still needs discussion.'

Paste your raw notes (keep them free of sensitive data) and the AI gives you a structured summary in seconds - then you check it against the source.

**Why this matters:** a good summary turns a two-hour meeting into a two-minute read for everyone.

**Example Quiz**

1. What does 'summarise' via AI typically produce?
- A) Three sections: decisions, open actions with owners and dates, items to discuss
- B) A full transcript
- C) A video
- D) Nothing useful

**Answer:** A — AI structured summaries - decisions, actions, open items - are instantly usable.

2. What must you do with a summarised document before relying on it?
- A) Check it against the source material
- B) Delete the original
- C) Send it blind
- D) Print three copies

**Answer:** A — Check the summary against the source; AI can miss nuance.

3. What is a good frame for a meeting summary prompt?
- A) List, List, List
- B) Decisions, open actions with owners and dates, items to discuss
- C) Who said what, minute by minute
- D) A praise paragraph

**Answer:** B — Structured asks - decisions, actions, discussion points - give usable summaries.

### Module 5 · Automating Formatting and Repurposing

A huge hidden time cost is reformatting. AI is excellent at it:

- Turn a paragraph into five bullet points.
- Turn bullet points into a table.
- Turn a report table into a short narrative summary.
- Turn an internal note into a client-friendly version.

The frame: 'Convert the following into [format]. Keep the facts identical. Do not add information.'

**Rule:** reformatting must never change facts. Tell the AI explicitly, and check that numbers and wording survived.

**Example Quiz**

1. What is a strong rule when reformatting content?
- A) Keep the facts identical; never add information
- B) Improve the numbers a little
- C) Rewrite in a new language
- D) Summarise freely

**Answer:** A — Reformatting must keep facts identical - tell AI and check numbers survived.

2. Which of these is a valid repurposing task?
- A) Turning a report table into a narrative summary
- B) Deleting your drafts
- C) Changing account passwords
- D) Formatting a hard drive

**Answer:** A — Repurposing - table to narrative, bullets to slides - is a fast, real win.

3. Why is reformatting such a large time cost to professionals?
- A) It repeats daily across formats and audiences
- B) It requires special hardware
- C) It cannot be delegated
- D) It is the hardest skill to learn

**Answer:** A — Reformatting repeats daily - once a prompt is saved, it takes seconds instead of minutes.

### Module 6 · What NOT to Automate

Some things should never run on autopilot:

1. **Anything confidential** - contracts, unannounced financials, personal data.
2. **Anything you cannot verify** - if you cannot check the output against a source, do not ship it blind.
3. **Anything with legal or reputational weight** - final external claims, regulatory filings, high-stakes communications.
4. **Decisions you must own** - firing, hiring-shortlists, pricing; AI can help draft, but the decision is yours.

**The professional line:** automate the tedious and reversible; keep the consequential and irreversible human.

**Example Quiz**

1. Which task should you NOT automate?
- A) Drafting a routine status email
- B) A confidential contract draft
- C) Meeting minutes summary
- D) A slide outline

**Answer:** B — Confidential, irreversible, and unverifiable tasks stay human-managed.

2. What makes a task safe to automate?
- A) It is tedious, repeatable, and easy to verify
- B) It is confidential
- C) It is brand-new
- D) It is urgent right now

**Answer:** A — Automate tedious, repeatable, verifiable tasks; keep consequential ones human.

3. Who decides in decisions like hiring and pricing?
- A) The AI tool
- B) The data
- C) The professional - AI only drafts
- D) The software vendor

**Answer:** C — AI can help draft and analyse, but decisions with real consequences stay with people.

## Real-World Examples

**The weekly status email —** A project coordinator wrote the same weekly status email for six teams. One reusable prompt now turns their bullet notes into a structured email each Friday - saving about 40 minutes weekly with the same quality.
**The two-hour meeting summary —** Instead of writing meeting minutes from raw notes, a manager asks AI to extract decisions, actions with owners and dates, and open items. The summary is circulated in minutes and stays consistent.
**The report repurposing —** A finance analyst repurposes the monthly dashboard table into a one-paragraph narrative for a leadership email and a bullet set for the team chat - one prompt, two formats, zero retyping.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Automation means AI drafts, formats, and summarises - you decide, check, and send.
- Start with reusable prompts for one recurring task type, not full robot workflows.
- Apply three safety rules first: no sensitive data, no auto-sending, check company policy.
- Reformatting must keep facts identical - always check numbers and wording survived.
- Keep confidential, irreversible, and unverifiable tasks human-managed.
- Each saved prompt is a time asset - add it to your toolkit as you go.
