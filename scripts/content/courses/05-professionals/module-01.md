### Module 1 · AI Is a Fast Assistant, Not Magic

AI tools are best understood not as magic but as a very fast assistant who has read a great deal and follows instructions carefully. Your job is to give clear instructions, check the output, and improve it. Nothing in this course needs a technical degree - only a real work task and a habit of checking.

The simple principle that powers everything: **input quality decides output quality.** The better you describe your situation, audience, format, and constraints, the more useful the answer.

**The golden rule:** AI drafts; you decide. You are responsible for the final result that carries your name.

### Module 2 · The Four-Step Loop: Ask, Check, Improve, Save

Every activity in this course follows the same loop, and by the end of this mission it will feel automatic:

1. **Ask** - write a clear prompt describing the task.
2. **Check** - read the output for accuracy, tone, and omissions.
3. **Improve** - request revisions or correct it yourself.
4. **Save** - keep good prompts and good outputs for reuse.

Doing all four turns AI from a toy into a workflow. Most people only do step one and ship the raw output; professionals do all four.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the four steps of the loop?
- A) Ask, Read, Reply, Save
- B) Ask, Check, Improve, Save
- C) Type, Copy, Paste, Delete
- D) Plan, Draft, Send, Forget

**Answer:** B — Ask, Check, Improve, Save is the loop used in every mission.

2. Why is the 'Check' step important?
- A) It makes the answer longer
- B) It catches errors, wrong tone, and missing detail
- C) It impresses the AI
- D) It is optional for experts

**Answer:** B — Checking catches errors, wrong tone, and omissions before the work is used.

3. What should you 'Save' each time?
- A) Only the AI tool name
- B) Good prompts and good outputs
- C) Your passwords
- D) Your screen time

**Answer:** B — Saving good prompts and outputs gives you a reusable toolkit.

### Module 3 · The Context - Task - Format Frame

Most professional prompts fit one simple frame with three parts:

- **Context** - who you are, your audience, the situation (e.g., 'I am a finance analyst preparing a quarterly review for non-finance managers.').
- **Task** - exactly what you want done (e.g., 'Summarise these 20 expense lines into the top 5 exceptions and what they mean.').
- **Format** - how to deliver it (e.g., 'A one-page memo with a short paragraph and a table.').

Master this frame and you can apply it to almost every other mission in the course.

**Try it now:** Write a Context - Task - Format prompt for one real task you did this week.

**Example Quiz**

1. What are the three parts of the prompt frame?
- A) Hello, Please, Thanks
- B) Context, Task, Format
- C) Noun, Verb, Adverb
- D) Intro, Body, Conclusion

**Answer:** B — Context - Task - Format is the master frame used across all missions.

2. What belongs in the Context part?
- A) The exact task wording
- B) Your role, audience, and situation
- C) The output file name
- D) The number of words

**Answer:** B — Context gives the AI your role, audience, and situation so the answer fits.

3. Why do you specify a Format?
- A) So the answer is usable without extra edits
- B) Because AI requires it
- C) To make prompts longer

**Answer:** A — Formatting (memo, table, bullets) makes the output directly usable.

### Module 4 · First Workflow: Your Own Real Task

Pick one small, real task you actually did this week - a routine email, a meeting summary, a short report section. Run it through the loop:

1. Write a Context - Task - Format prompt for it.
2. Get the draft and **check** it.
3. Ask for one revision: 'Make it shorter and more practical.'
4. **Save** the prompt and the final output.

This is the point of the whole mission: you are not learning about AI, you are building your first workflow. The task is real, so the result is real.

### Module 5 · Where to Find Your First Hour Back

In one working day you likely lose time to small repetitive tasks. List them mentally: drafting emails, summarising notes, structuring plans, reformatting documents. These are exactly what AI is best at.

A quick audit: for one day, note anything you did that took more than five minutes and felt mechanical. Most people find 3-5 such tasks - that is your automation list for this course.

**The payoff:** turning a half-hour of routine drafting into five minutes of checking and editing is the fastest credibility win you can show a manager.

## Real-World Examples

**The busy-work problem —** A typical day is full of small repetitive tasks - drafting emails, summarising notes, structuring plans. With one well-worded prompt, a participant turns a half-hour of routine drafting into five minutes of checking and editing.
**The blank-page problem —** Staring at an empty document is common and costly. A professional uses AI to produce a first draft or outline in seconds, then spends energy improving the draft instead of starting from nothing.
**The consistency problem —** When the same style or format is needed repeatedly - a report layout, a newsletter structure, a lesson plan - the saved prompt applies the pattern every time, making consistency easy.
